7 Places on the Body That Hidradenitis Suppurativa Can Appear

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Key takeaways

  • Hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is a chronic, progressive skin condition that causes boil-like lumps to form in areas with hair follicles and apocrine glands. These lumps may become inflamed, infected, and painful, though HS itself isn’t an infection.
  • HS commonly appears in areas prone to sweat and friction, including the underarms, breasts, groin, inner thighs, buttocks, nape of the neck, and waistband area. Friction from skin rubbing on skin or clothing may trigger flares.
  • Genetic, environmental, and hormonal factors may play a role in HS, though the exact cause isn’t known. If you suspect you have HS, talk with a dermatologist who can provide a proper diagnosis and treatment plan.

Despite what Instagram and photo filters would have us think, no one has perfectly smooth skin over the entirety of their body. We’re all prone to the occasional bump, rash, zit, or ingrown hair. These issues generally go away after the blocked follicle, clogged pore, or minor skin irritation resolves.

But hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is different. HS is a chronic, progressive skin condition that typically appears as boil-like lumps in some of your most sensitive regions (and beyond).

HS lumps or bumps can form interconnected tracts underneath the skin that may become inflamed and infected, causing discomfort and pain. Although infections are often a part of HS, the disease itself is not an infection — it’s due to an ongoing inflammatory response in the body.

In addition to causing pain and discomfort, HS can be extremely frustrating and impact your self-esteem. Identifying HS correctly and early is crucial for getting optimal care, easing symptoms, slowing disease progression, and improving your mental health.

While we don’t know what exactly causes HS, genetic, environmental, and hormonal factors may play a role. Skin regions prone to HS include those where we sweat often and where friction is super common. Friction can occur from skin rubbing on skin, or where clothing can chafe or rub.

Here are seven areas of the body that are often impacted by HS.

Armpits

Our underarms are packed with hair follicles and apocrine glands (aka, sweat glands). With HS, these follicles and glands may become clogged, and bacteria can start to grow, leading to the inflamed boil-like bumps of HS.

If you’ve ever gone for a run and come back with the dreaded armpit chafing, you’re all too familiar with how this area of the body can be prone to skin-on-skin friction or excessive rubbing from clothing. (Lookin’ at you, sports bra!) Friction and chafing can also cause an HS flare-up.

Complicating matters is that some people shave or wax their armpits, and many understandably slather this region with antiperspirants or deodorants. These factors can also clog or irritate sweat glands and hair follicles.

Breasts

The breasts, including underneath and even on the areolae (the area around the nipple), are a common place for HS to show up.

The breast area is a frequent spot for chafing from bras or other clothing. This chafing not only causes skin irritation — it can also trap sweat and bacteria on the skin. The areolae contain apocrine glands and hair follicles that can easily become infected. All these factors can contribute to an HS flare. 

Research also points to sex hormones playing a role in HS, so a hormone shift or imbalance may also make breast tissue more prone to HS lesions. However, more research is needed on the connection between HS and hormones.

Groin

No one can deny that the crotch becomes a sweaty area when life gets steamy, either from the weather, a workout, or both. It’s also a common area for clothing to rub against just from walking and everyday movements. 

As with other HS-prone areas, the groin and genital areas contain apocrine glands and oodles of hair follicles, all of which can become blocked as part of HS, leading to a flare. Speaking of hair down there, many people groom their pubic region, which can also lead to blocked hair follicles.

Again, hormonal shifts or imbalances may also play a role in why these sensitive areas can become hot spots for HS.

Inner thighs

The inner thighs are an extension of the groin area. Similarly, sweat also hangs out here, and it’s a common spot for chafing to occur from skin-on-skin friction, or from clothing rubbing and irritating the area. 

Buttocks

The entire buttocks area is also prone to HS in many people. Areas commonly affected include the anus, the butt crack, and the gluteal fold — or the crease where the buttocks meet the backs of the upper thighs. 

Apocrine glands and hair follicles are abundant here, too. Sweat and other bacteria sources can also lead to HS infections. The buttocks are also subjected to pressure from sitting, which can lead to further irritation.

Although more research is needed, some studies suggest a link between HS and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). IBD is an umbrella term for conditions such as Crohn’s and ulcerative colitis. The potential links between HS and IBD may play a role in why the buttocks area is a common site for HS.

Nape of the neck

Although less common than in other areas, HS can also show up at the nape of the neck. This might be due to friction from clothing collars, jewelry, and other clothing or accessories. The neck is also a common site for sweat, which could block hair follicles in this area.

Waistband area

If skin folds at the waist, or clothing waistbands (especially tight ones!) cause friction, it can lead to HS in the waistband area. As a result, HS lesions may show up on the abdomen, back, or sides. 

tl;dr

HS tends to appear on some of the body’s most sensitive places, including the underarms, breasts, groin, and buttocks. These areas are prone to sweat, friction, and irritation from hair removal — all of which may contribute to an HS flare.

If you suspect you have HS, talk with a knowledgeable dermatologist who can give you a proper diagnosis and help you develop the right treatment and self-care plan to combat irritation and flares so you can feel more comfortable in your skin.

DANBURY - When it comes to girls soccer in the SWC, one team stands above the rest. 

Notre Dame Prep successfully defended its conference title on Sunday, defeating Pomperaug 4-0 at Western Connecticut State University for its fifth SWC title in six years. 

"We just keep getting good players that come to the school, and it is all about the players," Notre Dame Prep coach Wayne Mones said. "They want to play for us, they want to learn and they want to get better. If they want to be a champion, they come to Notre Dame."

Notre Dame Prep has reached the SWC championship game in six straight seasons, only losing in 2024 to Brookfield. 

Lancers goalie Cara Williams was named the tournament MVP after posing three shutouts, not allowing a goal in the tournament. Notre Dame Prep outscored opponents 11-0 in the three games. 

"I feel really great, I honestly couldn't do this without my teammates," Williams said. "They always help me get better and this just feels really good.

"This really shows how great we are. Wayne (Mones) is a great coach, he strives to win. And all of these girls are so determined, from my freshman year to now it is amazing how much we have succeeded."

Junior forward Kolbe McCray scored a pair of goals in the championship game, while Maria Rocha Reaes and Truth Houston also scored. 

McCray scored the second and final goal for the Lancers.

"It is fun, being a forward and getting to score (in a championship game), I enjoy it," McCray said. "My team makes it fun for me too, they know how to move the ball and they always find me. I am proud of everybody, and I am proud to do it for my team."

Pomperaug reached its first SWC championship game since 2018 by defeating Newtown in penalties in the semifinals. 

Notre Dame Prep will now turn its attention to the CIAC Class LL tournament, hoping to reach its first final since 2023.

As for the Browns' defense, they were tasked with being on the field more than usual due to turnovers and long drives, which may have affected their gas tanks.

There is a lot to learn and adjust as they get ready for the New Orleans Saints in Week 9. Before then, we have some report card grades to share from the game.

Quarterback: D

Deshaun Watson did not look like he was crafted for the moment that the Browns found themselves in during Week 8. Has he looked much better? Yes. Should that excuse him from the overall poor play outside of five quarters we have seen this year? Absolutely not. Back to square one for Watson heading into Week 9.

Running Backs: B-

Quinshon Judkins looked explosive at times, but a combination of his offensive line and vision let him down. He just struggled to help this offense much, but I saw enough flashes of promise to give a slightly higher grade.

Wide Receivers and Tight Ends: B

The wide receivers and tight ends had moments on Sunday. Harold Fannin Jr. specifically looked like he can get back into a groove when given targets in this offense. Denzel Boston and KC Concepcion also got open and had chances, but quarterback play let them down.

Offensive Line: F

This was the worst offensive line performance from the Browns all season long. Between the penalties and eight sacks, it was a performance so bad that you burn the film and move on.

Defensive Line: C

Early reporting on the pressures has the Browns at just three, with one resulting in a sack. In their first matchup, they had a total of 15 pressures and five sacks, so what happened? The Steelers made adjustments while the Browns rested on their laurels.

Linebackers: D-

The Browns' linebackers have been brilliant this year, but Week 8 was not so much their best performance. Carson Schwesinger and Quincy Williams were negated, especially in pass coverage, but also in the run game.

Secondary: C

The secondary had moments of brilliance, but the chunk plays were there for the Steelers. Darnell Washington was beating cornerbacks and safeties for chunk plays, which should never happen. Denzel Ward continued to be the Lamborghini in the used car dealership that was their secondary in Week 8.

5 momentous plays from Week 8: Lamar Jackson's shocking injury and Jim Harbaugh's passivity

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Lamar Jackson was back on Sunday. For one play.

His return was marred by a shocking injury one play into Sunday's game, and his injury figures to have massive long-term ramifications for the Ravens.

That was clearly the most momentous play of Sunday's early and afternoon action, although we also saw a struggling kicker miss again, threatening his career with the only franchise he's known.

We also witnessed the Cowboys melt down in real time and the Buccaneers somehow win a game without completing a forward pass.

The Chargers also have gone this season without a win, and it's easy to see why, given their passive approach.

Here are the five most momentous plays from Sunday's action:

1. Lamar Jackson's shocking thumb injury

Momentous? It could derail the Ravens' entire season.

On Jackson's first snap of Sunday's big showdown against Buffalo, he suffered a thumb injury after being hit by the Bills' Greg Rousseau.

Lamar Jackson heads to the locker room after his first pass attempt with an apparent hand injury. pic.twitter.com/JVUQYjJV8H

Jackson was ruled out shortly thereafter and will be placed on injured reserve. Backup Tyler Huntley later was also knocked out of the game, replaced by undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano in his NFL debut. The Ravens hung around until the third quarter, but the Bills won going away, 36-10.

Fagnano was thrown into a tough spot, throwing a pick and losing a fumble. The Ravens' season is now very much in question, in spite of a 4-4 record. 

There was no mystery with this play. No second-guessing strategy. Just terrible luck in Jackson's return for the first time in nearly a month. His stay lasted exactly one play.

2. Bucs win going away … without completing a pass

Let's call the Buccaneers' third-down play on their opening possession of the second half their most momentous snap, just for laughs. Why? Because it was the final forward pass the Bucs attempted all game. 

The Bucs led, 9-0, at that point. Baker Mayfield's third-down pass in the deluge that was Tampa on Sunday fell incomplete. The Bucs would run 28 more plays from that point on. None of them would be thrown passes.

It was the first time since 1974 that an NFL team completed zero passes and won a game. The Bucs actually finished with negative pass yards thanks to a 2-yard sack on Mayfield.

Baker Mayfield completed zero passes, and the Tampa Bay Buccaneers still won. (Photo by Mike Stobe/Getty Images) Mike Stobe via Getty Images

Mayfield finished the game 0-for-3 passing in the rainy conditions. He also had three fumbles, but he recovered them all. 

He still had a better game than Falcons QB Michael Penix Jr., who attempted 24 passes, completed eight to his team and four to the Bucs. Penix's four INTs were a career high and were as many as he'd thrown in his previous 14 games combined.

But holistically, I’ve liked what I’ve seen from him thus far.

He’s third on the team in scoring at 16.3 points per game, leads the Suns in blocks, and is their top rebounder. Yes, that 22.2% shooting from beyond the arc sucks, and it certainly doesn’t help the argument I made earlier about the team’s three-point shooting. My assumption, however, is that those numbers will begin to normalize with time.

What I’ve appreciated most about Miles Bridges is that he reminds me a little bit of Shawn Marion, somebody you don’t necessarily have to draw up offensive plays for. His offense is generated through effort, whether that’s putbacks, hustle points, or simply finding opportunities within the flow of the game. Every roster needs somebody like that. Somebody who can generate those junkyard points without demanding the basketball, yet still provide meaningful offensive production.

Think about it. Dillon Brooks is averaging 16.5 points per night, but he has to be incredibly ball dominant for those points to materialize. Those are proactive points, whereas much of what Bridges provides comes reactively, taking advantage of opportunities that develop around him. And through that lens, I can see the vision of what Miles Bridges is designed to be within the context of this Suns lineup.

The shooting absolutely needs to improve, as does the defense, but there’s something valuable about having a player who can contribute offensively without requiring the entire offense to revolve around him.

3. The Bench Unit

Without a doubt, the bench has been the bright spot for the Phoenix Suns. The connectivity that this group possesses is something the starting unit should envy, and a big part of that comes from the continuity the organization talked about throughout the offseason. Much of that continuity has manifested itself within the second unit, where the only new addition to the rotation has been Luke Kennard, who has stepped into the Grayson Allen role pretty seamlessly.

The Phoenix Suns’ bench ranks 10th in the league with a +2.2 net rating, third with a 72.5% assist percentage, and fourth with a 1.94 assist-to-turnover ratio. And that’s where, as a Suns fan, you begin to realize that this team might not be as far away from being successful as it feels after watching those first four games.

The starting unit certainly needs to pick up the slack, and those players need to learn how to play together. But with 40% of that group being new to the lineup, it’s going to take some time for that chemistry to develop. Meanwhile, the backbone of this team has been the bench and the way it operates.

So even as I sit here pounding the table for Collin Gillespie to become a starter, perhaps the stability he provides to the second unit is something that needs to be considered as part of that equation. Because if the starting unit can figure things out, the bench can continue delivering punch after punch after punch when it enters the game, beating the opposition into submission.

Of course, there are multiple issues with that starting unit, and we don’t know whether time will ultimately solve them. But you know it, and I know it, because we’ve seen it with our own eyes while watching these games. When the second unit checks in, you immediately feel a sense of responsibility entering the game.

The Suns have legitimate problems, particularly defensively, and those aren’t going to disappear because I decided to write something positive. But the shooting is encouraging, Bridges is finding ways to contribute, and the bench has established an identity worth building upon. There’s work to be done, plenty of it. But even with the clouds hanging over the Valley, there’s still a little sunshine breaking through.
