My Chemical Romance Earns First No. 1 Album on Billboard 200 After ‘The Black Parade’ Reissue

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

My Chemical Romance triumphs on the Billboard 200 chart, scoring its first No. 1 album, as The Black Parade jumps from No. 139 to No. 1 on the Nov. 7-dated chart, following the 20th anniversary deluxe reissue of the 2006 album. The set originally debuted and peaked at No. 2 upon its release and remained the band’s highest-charting album until this week.

The album was reissued in an array of configurations, and was available across more than 20 physical variants, with the expanded editions including bonus tracks. All versions of the album are combined for tracking and charting purposes. In the week ending Oct. 29, it earned 78,000 equivalent album units in the United States (up 653%) according to Luminate — largely driven by pure album sales (68,000, up 2,082%).

The group has celebrated the album on tour since July 2025, with a trek that is slated to wrap on Nov. 22 in Indonesia. Among the stops was a five-night stand at the Hollywood Bowl in Los Angeles this October.

The Black Parade’s 20-year-wait to reach No. 1 is the longest ever for an album, surpassing the 15-year and nine-month wait for Toby Keith’s 35 Biggest Hits, following his death in 2024.

My Chemical Romance is the third act in 2026 to wait at least 10 years from a Billboard 200 chart debut to a first No. 1. The band went 22 years and four months from its debut with Three Cheers for Sweet Revenge (June 26, 2004-dated chart) until its first leader. In September, Jhené Aiko got her first No. 1 with Westside Whimsy — 12 years and 10 months after her debut in 2013. In February, Megadeth landed its first No. 1 with its self-titled set — nearly 40 years after the metal act’s chart debut in 1986.

The Billboard 200 chart ranks the most popular albums of the week in the U.S. based on multi-metric consumption as measured in equivalent album units, compiled by Luminate. Units comprise album sales, track equivalent albums (TEA) and streaming equivalent albums (SEA). Each unit equals one album sale, or 10 individual tracks sold from an album, or 2,500 ad-supported or 1,000 paid/subscription on-demand official audio and video streams generated by songs from an album. The new Nov. 7, 2026-dated chart will be posted in full on Billboard‘s website on Nov. 3. For all chart news, follow @billboard and @billboardcharts on both X and Instagram.

Of The Black Parade’s 78,000 equivalent album units earned in the latest tracking week, album sales comprise 68,000 (up 2,082%, it surges 29-1 on Top Album Sales for the group’s first week at No. 1), SEA units comprise 10,000 (up 34%, equaling 10.14 million on-demand official streams of the set’s tracks) and TEA units comprise a negligible sum. Vinyl purchases accounted for 42,000 of the album’s sales for the week — the band’s best week ever on vinyl. The album benefits from its availability across more than 20 physical variants on vinyl, CD and cassette, including signed editions.

The Black Parade houses My Chemical Romance’s highest charting hit on the Billboard Hot 100, “Welcome to the Black Parade,” which peaked at No. 9 in January 2007. The track is also the band’s sole No. 1 on the Alternative Airplay chart, among seven top 10s; it spent seven weeks atop the list in late 2006.

Notably, The Black Parade’s 139-1 vault is the largest jump to No. 1 since Taylor Swift’s Fearless (Taylor’s Version) returned to the top, soaring 157-1 on the Oct. 16, 2021-dated chart after the release of a signed CD and vinyl versions.

The Black Parade is also the latest reissue of 2026 to make a splash in the top 10, following The Beatles’ Rubber Soul (No. 4 reentry, Oct. 17 chart), Mac Miller’s The Divine Feminine (bounding 159-10, also Oct. 17) and Beyoncé’s B’Day (No. 8 reentry, Sept. 19).

The Nos. 2-6 titles on the latest Billboard 200 are all former No. 1s. Olivia Rodrigo’s you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love falls a rung to No. 2 (64,000 equivalent album units earned, down 15%); Morgan Wallen’s I’m the Problem is steady at No. 3 (53,000, down 3%); Ella Langley’s Dandelion is stationary at No. 4 (47,000, down 4%); Noah Kahan’s The Great Divide rises 6-5 (39,000, down 3%); and Swift’s The Life of a Showgirl dips 5-6 (35,000, down 18%).

Zach Bryan’s Young Manhood and Other Donatable Organs falls 2-7 in its second week (33,000 equivalent album units, down 49%), Kahan’s Stick Season is a non-mover at No. 8 (32,000, down 3%), Drake’s HABIBTI descends 7-9 (31,000, down 10%) and Olivia Dean’s The Art of Loving falls 9-10 (30,000, down 7%).

Strategy Reports 848,517 Bitcoin as Q3 Gains Put Cash Coverage in Focus

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Strategy reported 848,517 BTC as of October 25 and large third-quarter digital-asset gains. Cash coverage and financing obligations remain central to assessing the results.

Strategy’s latest results show how far its financial profile has moved beyond the size of its Bitcoin holdings. The company reported 848,517 BTC as of October 25, alongside a quarter in which digital-asset valuation gains dominated operating income.

The October 29 release also emphasized dollar liquidity and the obligations attached to its financing securities. Together, those figures offer a more useful picture than a single profit number.

Bitcoin valuation drove the earnings headline

Strategy’s third-quarter announcement reported $20.91 billion of gains on digital assets and approximately the same amount of operating income. Net income attributable to common stockholders was $19.12 billion, with diluted earnings of $44.75 per share.

The company also reported $5.07 billion of USD assets and described those assets as covering 3.4 years of dividend and interest obligations on its stated measure. These are company-reported figures with different measurement purposes.

The holdings snapshot is dated October 25. It should not be silently treated as a live wallet balance or substituted for the September 30 financial reporting date.

An accounting gain is different from spendable cash

When the market value of an asset rises, the resulting accounting gain can increase reported income even if the asset has not been sold. That does not make the gain meaningless: it reflects a change in economic value. It does mean the income statement and cash position answer different questions.

Dividend payments, interest and operating bills ultimately require resources that can be delivered when due. A strong quarter for a treasury asset may therefore coexist with a need to manage funding carefully.

The reverse also applies. A subsequent valuation decline can weigh on earnings without establishing that an immediate cash payment of the same size is required.

Comparisons across quarters should preserve that distinction. Stronger reported profit may reflect a larger asset position, a greater price increase, or both, without saying much about the underlying software operation. A useful review therefore separates changes in the quantity of Bitcoin, changes in its valuation and cash flows generated by the rest of the business.

The financing stack matters to common shareholders

Strategy’s business has to be assessed across the securities used to finance it. The SEC’s investor education material explains the general difference between common and preferred stock, including preferred shareholders’ usual priority over common shareholders for dividends and liquidation proceeds.

That ordering helps explain why gross Bitcoin value cannot be read directly as value available to common stockholders. Each layer can have its own payment rights, conversion provisions and sensitivity to market conditions.

New financing may increase the asset base while also changing the claims against it. The relevant comparison is the resulting position for each class of investor, including dilution and ongoing costs.

Liquidity coverage deserves a stress test

A coverage period is a useful starting point, but its assumptions deserve attention. It depends on which obligations are included, how liquid the supporting assets are and whether future commitments change. It is not a promise that every possible financing need has already been funded.

For investors, a practical reading of the quarter separates three questions: how much Bitcoin the company owns, how its securities distribute gains and risks, and how it meets cash commitments through different market conditions.

The latest figures make the scale of the treasury clear. They also reinforce why evaluating Strategy requires attention to cash and capital structure alongside Bitcoin conviction. A rising asset price can improve several measures at once, while still leaving those measures conceptually distinct.

Investors see a Republican-controlled Congress as Bitcoin’s (BTC) best shot at ending 2026 above $100,000, a Bloomberg survey of 301 traders, portfolio managers and other investors shows. Twenty-nine percent expect that outcome under Republican control, against 8% if Democrats win.

For Bitcoin holders, the gap runs on the downside too. Sixteen percent expect a drop below $60,000 under Democrats, against 5% under Republicans.

Why Does Congress Control Matter for Bitcoin Holders?

Americans vote on Tuesday, November 3, to decide which party controls Congress. Lawmakers there write the rules for crypto markets.

Those rules are still missing. On September 15, the Senate voted 49-50 on whether to open debate on the CLARITY Act, a bill setting federal rules for crypto markets. It needed 60 votes.

Markets punished the CLARITY Act failure. Bitcoin fell more than 5% that day, while Coinbase and Circle shares dropped as much as 10%.

Dennis Porter, co-founder of the Bitcoin policy group Satoshi Action Fund, told BeInCrypto that clear rules could draw larger institutions into long-term crypto investments. He expected that effect to build slowly.

Bloomberg’s survey framed its Bitcoin question around that defeat:

  • Under Democratic control, 71% of respondents expected Bitcoin to finish the year lower.
  • A split Congress drew 47%, and
  • A Republican sweep 27%.

Follow us on X to get the latest news as it happens

Why Might a Republican Win Fall Short of $100,000?

Even in the bull case, about 70% did not see Bitcoin clearing $100,000. That level sits roughly 20% above Bitcoin’s late-September price near $83,600.

The 60-vote bar is the hurdle. The September bill drew no Democratic votes, and Republican Sen. Josh Hawley also opposed advancing it, citing risks to community banks.

The poll also has limits. Respondents opted in through Bloomberg links from October 2 to 17, and results were not weighted.

What Do Investors Expect for Stocks and Bonds?

Under Republican control, 65% expect S&P 500 gains to speed up or hold their current pace. Under Democrats, 63% expect gains to slow or turn into losses.

Bonds tell the opposite story. More than half expect falling Treasury yields, which lift bond prices, under Democrats. Only 26% expect that under Republicans.

Separately, 62% said stricter artificial intelligence (AI) rules would hurt US stocks. Still, 55% said no election outcome would push them to consider selling.

Any bill still pending when this Congress ends in January must start over. That leaves the CLARITY Act’s next attempt to the lawmakers chosen on Tuesday.

In a game they probably shouldn't have won, the Broncos beat the Chiefs with a goal-line stand in the final seconds.

Denver stopped Kansas City four times inside the 10-yard line, winning 28-24 for Sean Payton's 200th career victory, including the postseason. The Broncos lead the AFC West at 6-2, while the Chiefs fell to 5-2 with a second consecutive loss.

Denver trailed 24-13 in the fourth quarter before scoring 15 consecutive points for its fourth comeback win of the season.

The Chiefs uncharacteristically fell apart when it mattered most.

They had a chance to pad their lead, but Harrison Butker missed a 54-yard field goal wide right with 2:31 remaining. It came after Alex Singleton sacked Patrick Mahomes for 6-yard loss.

Butker is in danger of losing his job after the Chiefs signed Justin Tucker to the practice squad last week. The 10-year veteran has missed 6 of 17 field-goal attempts this season, including two misses in last week's loss to the Seahawks.

Two plays, 56 yards and 35 seconds later, the Broncos led for the first time. Jonah Coleman had a 51-yard catch-and-run before a 5-yard touchdown run. The Broncos led 28-24 with 1:56 left.

Emmett Johnson, the star for the Chiefs on Sunday, returned the kickoff 44 yards to the Kansas City 44, and two plays later, Mahomes found Travis Kelce for a 32-yard gain to the Denver 18.

The Chiefs had first-and-goal at the 4 with 35 seconds left. Kenneth Walker ran for 3 yards to the 1 before Patrick Surtain tackled him; Mahomes threw incomplete; and Walker was stopped for no gain by safety JL Skinner. That set up a final play with four seconds left, and after a false start, Malcolm Roach sacked Mahomes to end the game.

The Chiefs outgained the Broncos 399 to 294 as Mahomes went 30-of-41 for 321 yards and three touchdowns. Johnson caught five passes for 128 yards and a touchdown and had a 15-yard run. Kelce added five catches for 67 yards.

Hypnosis. Fact or fiction? Who knows. Some claim it works wonders, forcing them to quit habits they’ve had for decades or to pick up things they always wanted to do but never could. Others say it’s complete hogwash—a snake oil sale reliant on placebo and your own convictions.

Whether it’s true, I don’t know, but a movie theater seems to believe in its powers to the point of wanting to hypnotize viewers into forgetting their favorite movie.

The reason? You get to watch it for the very first time—again. As reported by Dexerto, a Dutch cinema chain called Pathé is hosting an experimental project called REWIND, which would theoretically allow viewers to mind-wipe and memory-hole their favorite movies and then experience them as if they had never seen them before. The project is taking up 30 moviegoers and kickstarting on Nov. 9 at its Buitenhof venue in The Hague.

It seems to work by bringing people into a session with a hypnotherapist—in this case, Victor Kraemer who apparently has decades of hypnotic experience under his belt—who would then, well, hypnotize you into forgetting. However, Pathé seems to maintain the procedure isn’t 100 percent certain, since it is unclear whether hypnosis can actually make you forget an entire movie. I’m positive it’s even more difficult if you’ve seen the thing dozens of times, as many do with their favorites.

The perfect game. Image via CD Projekt Red

If it does work, however, I would love to memory-hole The Witcher 3 from my mind and get to play that masterpiece for the first time again. In 2015, I had a HD 6670 and a dualcore Pentium G2030 supported by six gigs of DDR3 memory, running TW3 at a 12-15 FPS average. It was a horrible experience overall, but even as such, it became my favorite game of all time, to the point where my family invested a lot in a new machine that I bought the year after just so I could play TW3 on high graphics and a great FPS.

The story was just so gripping, so enticing, enthralling even that no amount of poor performance or lag or slow-motion could deter me from playing through it. Between 2015 and 2016 I probably put in over 300 hours in the game on that original hardware, and have come back to replay it time and again over the last 11 years. Blood and Wine is especially close to me, and I can’t even remember (pun intended) how many times I had seen that TW3 trailer with Orianna in it.

It’s the perfect game in almost every way, and I envy those who are yet to try it.
