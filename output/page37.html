Next Technology Authorizes Sale or Pledge of 5,833 Bitcoin After Risk Review

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Next Technology has authorized selling or pledging its reported 5,833 BTC. The decision creates flexibility; it does not confirm that a transaction has occurred.

Next Technology Holding has given management permission to reduce or finance its Bitcoin position after a board review of custody and market risks. The company reported approximately 5,833 BTC when announcing the decision on October 30.

For the wider market, the important distinction is between authorization and execution. A board can create the option to sell an asset without deciding when, at what price or through which counterparty a transaction will happen.

The board has approved several routes

The company’s announcement, distributed through GlobeNewswire, permits transactions in one or more tranches. The stated options include exchange sales, negotiated block trades and pledging Bitcoin to obtain financing.

Next Technology cited the recent Binance security incident and broader technological concerns in explaining the review. It specifically said none of its Bitcoin was held on or through Binance.

The release does not establish a completed disposal or a signed financing agreement. It also gives no assurance about the timing, price, amount or completion of a future transaction. Those qualifications belong at the center of any interpretation of the announcement.

A sale and a pledge have different consequences

Selling Bitcoin exchanges price exposure for cash. Once a sale settles, subsequent gains or losses on the disposed coins no longer accrue to the company. The trade-off depends on the price received and how the proceeds are used.

A pledge instead uses the coins as collateral. That can provide liquidity while retaining some economic exposure, but the borrower also takes on contractual obligations. Interest, collateral requirements, maturity and a lender’s enforcement rights can become as important as the asset’s market price.

Without those terms, it is impossible to treat a potential borrowing as equivalent to a sale, or to determine which route would leave shareholders with greater flexibility.

The sequence could matter too. A company might use financing to meet a near-term need and later reduce that borrowing through asset sales. Alternatively, it could sell only enough to fund a particular commitment. Neither sequence is announced here, but both illustrate why the full authorized balance should not be mistaken for a fixed disposal schedule.

The equity story extends beyond coin count

A corporate Bitcoin holding is only one part of an investment in the company that owns it. As Investor.gov explains, shares represent ownership in a business. They do not give each shareholder direct control of particular treasury assets.

The value reaching ordinary shareholders depends on liabilities, operating expenses, financing terms and the number of shares outstanding. A headline multiplying a reported coin balance by a market price therefore cannot settle the investment case.

Investors would need to know what restrictions already apply to the assets, whether proceeds are earmarked for specific commitments and how any financing changes the company’s overall capital structure.

Execution disclosures will carry more weight

The next meaningful development would be a disclosure identifying a completed sale, an executed pledge or a decision to retain the position. Price, quantity and resulting cash or debt balances would turn the board’s general authority into something that can be assessed.

The company’s reference to quantum computing is a statement of its risk considerations, not evidence that Bitcoin’s cryptography has been broken. A corporate risk review and a demonstrated technical exploit require different standards of proof.

Until transaction details emerge, treating all 5,833 BTC as immediate market supply would go beyond the announcement. The present news is that management has more options, with the consequences still dependent on what it actually does.

America’s EV market has made progress, but convincing traditional car buyers to switch remains difficult. As we’ve discussed before, electrification depends on more than adding vehicles to dealership lots. Charging access, affordability, and consumer confidence also shape demand.

A study from HERE Technologies and SBD Automotive puts that challenge into perspective. Its extensive report found that just 11% of surveyed US owners of gasoline and diesel vehicles consider an EV as their most likely next purchase or lease. The result suggests that growing infrastructure and wider model availability have yet to translate into strong buying intent.

Existing EV Owners See Things Differently

The study surveyed 4,058 drivers across eight markets and found a difference between current EV owners and people who have not made the switch. In the US, 85% of EV owners rate charging coverage as good or very good, up from 72% in 2025. That is the highest satisfaction figure among the surveyed markets.

Only 42% of non-EV owners give charging coverage a positive rating. The 43-point gap suggests that direct experience may make drivers more comfortable with the network than they were beforehand.

And that’s despite the massive infrastructure progress we’ve seen this year. US public charging points grew by more than 14% between 2025 and 2026, while average charger power increased from 65kW to 83kW, a 28% rise.

In terms of costs, well, that’s a different problem. Just 34% of US respondents believe EVs are cheaper to own than gasoline-powered vehicles. Another 29% say costs are about the same, while 28% consider EVs more expensive.

There is a brighter picture among people who already own electric cars: 77% of US EV owners say they are likely to buy another one. The report also pointed out that the end of purchase incentives in late 2025 contributed to dampened EV sales into 2026.

Better Infrastructure Isn’t Enough

Automakers have two jobs: keep current EV owners happy, and somehow win over the skeptics who still worry about price, charging, and whether an EV fits their daily grind. Sure, faster chargers and more plugs make life easier for EV owners, but that doesn’t mean gas-car drivers are suddenly sold. Carmakers still need to prove EVs make sense for the wallet and won’t mess up anyone’s routine.

Right now, America’s EV market has a first-timer problem. Existing owners are mostly on board for round two, but getting more gas-car drivers to take the plunge is still the real challenge.

AC Milan 3-1 Inter: Five things we learned – individual and tactical success

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

By: Ivan Stoev

AC Milan clinched a massive victory in their home derby against Inter on Saturday, with several positives to take from the game as a result. 

There wasn’t exactly an excess of optimism before the game among the Milan fans, following what was a poor mid-week defeat and a so-so start to the season. However, we didn’t see much of this in the big game, with two goals scored after just ten minutes.

It’s almost as if Gerry Cardinale manifested the win before the game and he certainly got to celebrate afterwards, even brought to tears in the mixed zone. He probably learnt a lot from this game and so did we, with several positives to highlight.

1. Moreira steals the show

Without Diego Moreira, Adrien Rabiot and Mario Gila, Milan and Ruben Amorim struggled a lot in the mid-week clash against Bologna. Fortunately, the trio was back against Inter and the difference was clear as night and day.

All three played a big role, but the true difference-maker was Moreira, who was elctric from the start after bagging an early and lovely assist for Christian Pulisic. Six minutes later, in the 10th minute, he picked up a second from a corner kick, setting up Rabiot.

Inter got back into the game a little with their goal, making it 2-1, and it was a delicate moment in the game when Milan needed to stay focused. And yet again, it was Moreira who brought them out of trouble with a fantastic free-kick just before half-time.

Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images

San Siro erupted and the Belgian certainly showed that he has several tricks up his sleeve. On top of the attacking contribution, the Belgian was relentless throughout the game as he helped a tonne defensively until the final whistle.

Milan seem to have a game changer in their hands and it was fitting that he delivered this type of performance in his first Derby Della Madonnina. Especially since Ricky Kaka was in the stands, the player who made Moreira choose the No.22 shirt.

2. You shall not pass

Milan did an outstanding job up front, being as clinical as they could be, but Inter were always going to create chances on the other end. So, it was even more impressive how the Rossoneri managed to contain them, especially in the second half.

There were some struggling moments in the first half, but in that period, Mike Maignan came up big with multiple good saves. One of them was just phenomenal, as he had little to no time to react and it was all about the Frenchman’s reflexes, which didn’t let him down.

That was a crucial moment in the game, with the result being 2-1 for Milan and with an equaliser there, the game could’ve been a lot different.

Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images

In the second half, the Rossoneri were a lot more compact and it was more about the players in front of Maignan. Strahinja Pavlovic stepped up a tonne after the half-time mark as he was a tad shaky in the opening 45 minutes, especially against Spence who was bursting down that flank.

Gila was also very solid on the right-hand side and especially when he was on the ball. It’s a completely different team when he’s on the pitch.

Diawara, Musah and Modric also did a very fine job closing down the spaces and handling Inter’s press well, with the Croatian rolling the years back as he was relentless throughout the game.

It was a very impressive defensive performance and especially in the second half of the game, as it seems Amorim’s instructions at the break limited Inter even further.

3. De Winter bullies Thuram

I purposefully left out Koni De Winter from the previous point because I think the Belgian deserves a section all for himself.

In the absence of Lautaro, Thuram was always going to be the main threat. Not just because he has five goals and four assists in the opening eight games, but also because he has done very well in the derby before.

So naturally, if Milan were to win the game, they had to neutralise the Frenchman and it was clear from the beginning that De Winter’s instructions were to stay close to the striker and not give him breathing space.

Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images

De Winter deserves all the praise here because he won almost all of the duels against the striker, effectively frustrating him and getting him out of the game completely. The only memory I have from Thuram was when he dropped almost into his own half towards the end of the game to try and make something happen, but that was not nearly enough.

Aside from his battle with the Frenchman, De Winter also did well with his passing and overall game and was arguably the best Milan defender on the night.

Really positive performance and he needs to keep it up, because conceding silly goals has been a problem for Milan and they clearly have the quality to prevent them.

4. Clinical precision 

We already mentioned the brilliant work at the back and the saves Maignan made, but it’s only fair to praise the attack too as they also did a fine job. Milan had three shots on goal until the 90th minute and had scored three goals, which is impressive considering how wasteful the Rossoneri have been in attack in the last few games.

The team started off with the right mindset and put pressure on Inter early on, with some great individual runs such as the one from Moreira. All of the chances were finished with clinical precision, which is exactly what you need to win in the big games.

We already praised Moreira, but Pulisic was also very good as he found the empty space on the opener and buried it to give his side the lead. He then continued playing well between the lines and he was smart about controlling the game after his side was ahead.

Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images

Rabiot came in clutch again with yet another goal and a positive performance overall, even if he had to do a bit more running in the second half and focus on the defensive aspect of the game.

I know he failed to score or assist again, but that doesn’t mean Goncalo Ramos was necessarily bad. He pressed quite a bit and he opened up spaces for his teammates. You can clearly see on Milan’s second goal how he pushes Sucic towards the byline so he can’t charge towards Rabiot after Moreira’s corner.

Again, he absolutely needs to be more involved in the box but he’s still a very valuable player on the pitch.

5. Amorim masterclass

There was obviously a bittersweet feeling coming into the derby after losing to Bologna in such a frustrating manner, but the availability of Moreira, Rabiot and Gila gave Amorim and the fans some enthusiasm prior to the game.

To Amorim’s credit, he picked the correct starting eleven and his team had the right mindset from the start. The coach was aware of Inter’s fragile defending and instructed his team to raise the intensity from the start and apply pressure, which worked a treat with two goals in the opening ten minutes.

Inter then tried to come back into the game and created a few chances, it’s fair to say, but Amorim again tweaked something at the half-time break. His team was more compact and more aggressive after that, which almost completely neutralised Inter.

Photo by Antonino Lagana/Getty Images

The coach also waited with his subs, which I think was correct given the team was playing well and there weren’t obvious signs of fatigue. When the changes did come, I think they were spot on.

More importantly, the whole game management was impressive. The game started off with Milan playing in Amorim’s style, which resulted in the goals but also exposed his team and then in the second half, Milan were more compact and not eager to press or attack as much.

On top of that, there were numerous times when the Rossoneri kept the ball, were patient and avoided risk and I think the tempo was dictated by Milan despite Inter having a bit more possession towards the end. Excellent from the coach.

The First Billboard: All That Was ‘New, Bright & Interesting on the Boards’

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

On Nov. 1, 1894, Billboard published its first issue, one that’s vastly different from today’s magazine, as well as Billboard’s expanded platforms. The premiere issue wasn’t even focused on the music industry; Billboard’s eventual trademark concentration on music, and, of course, charts, would develop over decades.

The First Issue

Before it became a weekly publication, and a 24/7 presence online, Billboard launched as Billboard Advertising. Its original mission? One that helps explain its name: “A monthly résumé of all that is new, bright and interesting on the boards.” (Circus promoter Jared Bell is widely credited as a pioneer of 19th century marketing through billboards, beginning in the 1830s.)

Upon its premiere, the eight-page magazine (priced at 10 cents an issue, or, 99 cents per year), was “devoted to the interests of advertisers, poster printers, bill posters, advertising agents & secretaries of fairs.”

The magazine’s first cover subject was R.C. Campbell, then-president of the Associated Bill Posters’ Association. “No more fitting tribute can be paid to Mr. Campbell than to state that he is a man of infinite resource, progressive ideas and tireless industry,” Billboard noted in the inaugural issue.

“In selecting his photograph for the first number of this magazine, the Editor was actuated by the fact that he is the acknowledged leader, the first and foremost and most eminent man in the field which we aim to cover.”

The Charts

As 132 years have passed (and Campbell has ceded the cover to, most recently, Pitbull and Fuerza Regida), Billboard’s mission to report on and analyze the entertainment business remains on point, although with, for the past eight-plus decades, a more specific focus on the music segment of the industry.

The magazine’s first national music chart, the 10-position National List of Best Selling Retail Records, appeared in the July 27, 1940, issue. Previously, Billboard had highlighted the national Sheet Music Best Sellers, Records Most Popular on Music Machines (compiled from national reports from phonograph operators), and Songs With the Most Radio Plugs on a handful of New York radio stations. The National List of Best Selling Retail Records, however, was the first to poll retailers nationwide on record sales.

Tommy Dorsey crowned the first retail list with “I’ll Never Smile Again.” The eventual standard, featuring vocals by Frank Sinatra, would total 12 weeks at the summit. The scorecard paved the way for the industry-standard Billboard 200 albums chart (which became a weekly chart on March 24, 1956) and Billboard’s present-day menu of Luminate data-based format-centric song and album rankings and more. The Billboard Hot 100 songs chart premiered Aug. 4, 1958, with Ricky Nelson’s “Poor Little Fool” the first of the tally’s 1,195 No. 1s and counting — right through Ella Langley’s current reign with “Choosin’ Texas,” reflecting that, 132 years on, historic feats can still be achieved in any week.

In more recent years, Billboard’s charts offerings have expanded to include surveys covering social and streaming activity, with streams added to the Hot 100’s weekly tabulation. Billboard has also launched the weekly Billboard Artist 100, expanded touring tallies, weekly surveys ranking songwriters and producers, the Billboard Global 200 and Billboard Global Excl. U.S. lists and more.

‘Let Its Growth Be Natural’

Meanwhile, Billboard has grown to encompass multiple platforms, including the top-rated Billboard Music Awards, industry summits such as Women in Music and Latin Music Week, podcasts and chart-based Billboard countdowns and channels on SiriusXM, in addition to the weekly magazine and more.

“Although only in its swaddling clothes, [our] success is already absolutely assured,” Billboard proclaimed on page four of the maiden issue. “The publishers aim to have it always newsy and to maintain a high and exacting standard of excellence in all articles appertaining to the interest of its readers.”

As a “What They Think of Us” feature reflected, “Responses to the very modest prospectus demonstrated that beyond all doubt or peradventure … surely a journalistic youngster was never started under such auspicious circumstances.”

“Start the new paper in a small and inexpensive manner, and let its growth be natural. Do not force it. You will not need to. There is a field for it,” advised Col. Burr Robbins.
