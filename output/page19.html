Linus Torvalds says Linux kernel is out of ‘stabilization phase’ and using ‘much healthier model’ for AI contributions

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Linus Torvalds has posted the first release candidate for version 7.4 of the Linux kernel and offered his opinion that the project has found a “much healthier model” for using AI.

“This is a *BIG* merge window,” he wrote in his weekly state of the kernel update, as it contains almost 20,000 commits compared to the usual ~15,000 that developers submit for a new cut of the kernel.

“I think the reason is the by now obvious one, but I'm actually taking it as a good sign: I'm *much* happier with the flood of AI triggered work going into the merge window than it showing up mainly as extra fixes during the -rc period.”

He therefore hopes that the unusually large number of commits “is a sign that the AI fixes are becoming more part of the ‘normal development’ than a flood of fixes that go into the whole stabilization phase.”

“I think that's a much healthier model,” he added, before hedging with the observation that “people are expecting 7.4 to be the next LTS [long-term support] release, which is another independent reason why some releases tend larger than others.”

“So it may not *just* be the flood of AI patches.”

Torvalds also seems not to be completely convinced that all AI submissions are entirely helpful, by describing this release as “Large, and full of fixes for issues found by AI (and more and more the patches have been suggested by AI too, although they often get rewritten).”

AMD GPU register offsets and bit masks make up a third of the changes this time around.

“We've been here before, and I'm sure we'll see it many times again in the future,” Torvalds wrote. “Just ignore the noise.”

The kernel-watchers at Phoronix rate the major inclusions in Linux 7.4 as improved support for processors from AMD, Apple, and Google. Other additions include some handy tweaks to improve security for virtual machines, support for the Raspberry Pi 10-inch monitor and more work to get Linux running happily on Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X2 SoC for PCs. ®

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is actually managing U.S. debt costs not inflation, economist says, as interest rates threaten to top GDP growth soon

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Trying to pin down Kevin Warsh as a hawk or a dove has been tricky with his statements and actions puzzling Wall Street.

As President Donald Trump’s hand-picked Federal Reserve chairman, Warsh was expected to lean toward looser monetary policy. Then he declared that inflation was a “choice,” suggesting a tougher approach.

Over the summer, he suggested looking at alternative inflation gauges and that the bond market would do some of the Fed’s tightening work, hinting as less aggressive policy.

But he delivered a hawkish speech at the Jackson Hole conference in August, and led the Fed to a unanimous rate hike in September.

Last week, he held off on backing another rate hike, despite inflation heating up again, with his lack of explanation and refusal to offer any hints about the next policy decision producing an overall dovish effect.

But Robin Brooks, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution, said asking whether Warsh is a hawk or a dove is the wrong question. Instead, he views all the mixed messages from the Fed chair through a different lens, arguing the hot inflation readings can’t rationalize Warsh’s statements.

“We’re not in hawkish or dovish land. What’s happening is yield curve management, which itself is a function of how precarious the U.S. debt picture is,” Brooks wrote in a Substack post on Thursday.

That’s because the surge in Treasury yields this year threatens to worsen the already-grim outlook for U.S. debt, which is now above $40 trillion in gross terms with publicly held debt at 100% of GDP.

In fiscal year 2026, which ended in September, the federal government’s debt-interest costs topped $1 trillion, exceeding defense spending.

“The bottom line is that inflation and other macro data are secondary,” Brooks added. “The twists and turns in Fed policy are about what’s going on with long-term yields.”

He suspects the Fed and Treasury Department are in a de facto accord to keep borrowing costs stable. Brooks pointed to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s surprise announcement to buy back more long-term bonds, briefly lowering yields.

Bessent also intervened in the currency market to prop up the yen without Japan having to sell its holdings of U.S. debt, which would have lifted yields.

The actual effectiveness of these attempts at managing bond yields is a different story. Expectations for the Iran war to re-escalate have sent oil prices and yields higher. Signs that the U.S. economy is running hot have also raised inflation and rate forecasts.

But the U.S. also caught some breaks too. Fears that the AI boom, a major driver of GDP growth, will go bust have taken pressure off yields. France’s political turmoil and fiscal dysfunction have sparked worries that it will trigger a debt crisis that spreads to other parts of the eurozone.

Investors have since embraced Treasury debt again as a safe have asset, and the 10-year yield has dipped back below 5% again.

“Warsh is neither hawk nor dove,” Brooks declared. “Instead—like Bessent—he’s just trying to keep long-term yields manageable.”

Such management risks putting the U.S. on the path toward so-called fiscal dominance, where high debt and deficits override the Fed’s ability to independently manage monetary policy and fight inflation.

Bank of America

Economists have pointed out that as long as nominal GDP growth remains above interest rates, then the U.S. can avoid a debt spiral where borrowing costs pile up faster than the economy expands.

But bond yields have risen so high and so quickly this year that they have blown past long-term estimates, raising alarms about the debt trajectory. In fact, the Congressional Budget Office was asked to assess scenarios where yields are 150 basis points and 50 basis points higher than the watchdog’s baseline of 4.3%.

Under the 150-point scenario, net interest costs go up by $4.9 trillion over the next 10 years, and publicly held debt reaches 133% of GDP by 2036. While yields have pulled back recently, they jumped more than 100 basis points over the CBO’s baseline earlier.

Analysts at Bank of America looked at the CBO’s numbers and estimated that in the alternate scenario, the effective interest rate on government debt will exceed long-term nominal GDP growth (assumed to be a 4% pace) as early as fiscal year 2028—which begins October 2027.

For now, nominal growth remains robust and was 5.6% in the third quarter, helped by the AI boom and high inflation.

“However, both forces are likely to prove more temporary than permanent unless AI adoption ultimately delivers substantial productivity gains,” BofA said in a note on Oct. 19. “Should that occur, concerns around fiscal sustainability would ease materially. Even so, we believe it is optimistic to assume the United States can simply grow its way out of its mounting debt burden.”

A six-year-old flipped over a rock in a Nebraska park and found a kind of fossil not recorded in the state for more than 55 years

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

During a late-summer Rafting and Rocks event at Schramm Park State Recreation Area near Gretna, Nebraska, six-year-old Leo Youngdahl turned over a rock at an outcrop of exposed bedrock. The boy from Eagle hoped to find one of his favorite fossils on its underside.

He was looking for a crinoid. Instead, the rock held a 2.75-inch (7-centimeter) conulariid, an ancient marine animal that rarely survives as a fossil.

The only other known Nebraska specimens turned up more than 55 years ago, in the towns of Louisville and Weeping Water.

Unusual to find in limestone

Kelly Ekue, an outdoor education specialist at the Schramm Education Center, leads fossil programs at the park for the Nebraska Game and Parks Commission.

“I’ve been leading programs at the outcrop for over seven years, and there are six main types of fossils we find,” Ekue said.

“This fossil didn’t look anything like any of the species we see,” she added.

“It was larger and seemed fish-like,” she explained. That stood out because the outcrop’s rock layers contain only invertebrate fossils.

Ekue emailed a photo to a geologist she knew, who passed it along. It reached Shane Tucker, a paleontologist with the University of Nebraska State Museum, and Bob Diffendal, a geologist who curates the museum’s invertebrate fossils.

The two experts identified the animal from a herringbone-style imprint on the limestone. That imprint is the only part of the conulariid that fossilized.

What conulariids were

Conulariids were ancient cnidarians, members of the animal group that includes jellyfish. Park staff describe them as jellyfish-like creatures shaped like an upside-down pyramid.

A 2022 study of a Brazilian conulariid classifies them as an extinct order of marine cnidarians. Their outer shell, or periderm, was steeply pyramidal or conical, and their bodies showed four-part radial symmetry.

Their closest living relatives may be crown jellyfish of the order Coronata, the authors suggest.

The oldest conulariids known before that work date to the late Cambrian. The Brazilian fossil comes from the final stage of the earlier Ediacaran Period, and the team calls it the oldest documented conulariid.

Unlike later conulariids, the Brazilian specimen shows no calcium phosphate in its shell.

The last conulariids lived in the Norian Stage, near the end of the Triassic Period. For most of that history, between 200 million and 500 million years ago, conulariids lived in eastern Nebraska and around the globe, the commission said.

What rare conulariid fossils reveal

Nebraska has few conulariids preserved as fossils. Their soft bodies and thin skeletons usually broke apart and disintegrated too quickly to fossilize.

Those skeletons were made of carbonate-rich apatite, a phosphate mineral, according to a 2023 micro-CT study of conulariids.

That work scanned unusually well-preserved specimens from Pennsylvanian rocks in Texas and Oklahoma, in the North American Midcontinent. Conulariids have been studied for more than two centuries, the authors note.

The X-ray images showed structures the team identified as longitudinal muscle bundles, along with a possible gastric cavity. The bundles often joined in V-shaped pairs and ran toward the animal’s opening.

Preservation varied from one fossil to the next. Some showed both features, while others kept only the muscle bundles.

Such internal structures had never before been shown with micro-CT. Leo’s specimen preserves far less, only the patterned surface pressed into stone.

Traces of Nebraska’s ancient sea

Fossils from the outcrop date from a time when Nebraska was covered by a saltwater sea, the commission noted.

A geologic display at the park’s east entrance highlights limestone and shale from the Pennsylvanian Period. Visitors may also spot Paleozoic marine fossils such as brachiopods or corals, the park’s official page notes.

The Rafting and Rocks program pairs round-raft floats on a pond with an outcrop visit. There, participants explore limestone layers and look for fossils.

Schramm Park, midway between Lincoln and Omaha, is home to Nebraska’s oldest state fish hatchery. In 1937, the hatchery became the state recreation area.

The park takes its name from geology professor E.F. Schramm, who gave land so it could be used for teaching.

The fossil stays at Schramm

“It is really amazing that among all those rocks in the Schramm outcrop, Leo not only picked up this one, but recognized it as a fossil and thought to ask about it,” Ekue said.

Leo said he had been told the conulariid was rarer to find than a mammoth bone. He plans to show his finds to naturalist Heidi Swanson at Platte River State Park, near Louisville.

He doesn’t mind leaving the fossil behind. Park staff will use it in educational programs, continuing the tradition E.F. Schramm started.

“Delight in all discoveries, not just the big ones like a rare fossil, but the little ones, too,” Ekue advised.

State collecting laws bar visitors from taking fossils, feathers or nests, so Ekue suggests photographing finds instead. Picks and shovels are not allowed at the outcrop, and visitors should stay out from under overhanging rocks.

The information was obtained from the Nebraska Game and Parks Commission.

KuCoin Announces Russia and Belarus Exit With Individual Account Deadlines

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: KuCoin says it is discontinuing all services for users in Russia and Belarus. Affected accounts will receive their own deadlines for closing positions and withdrawing assets.

KuCoin has announced an immediate end to services for users in Russia and Belarus, while saying it will provide an orderly process for affected customers to close positions and withdraw assets. The October 29 notice applies across the exchange’s products and features.

The announcement leaves an important operational detail at account level. It does not publish one universal withdrawal deadline, so users should not assume a date circulating elsewhere applies to their circumstances.

The restriction covers the whole platform

KuCoin’s official notice attributes the decision to applicable legal and regulatory requirements. It says affected customers will receive notifications containing closure instructions and relevant deadlines. Customers who have not received a notification are told that no action is required on the basis of this notice.

The statement does not identify one particular enforcement order as the sole trigger. It should therefore not be expanded into a claim that a named government compelled the exit unless further documentation establishes that connection.

The published decision is nevertheless broad: the withdrawal from the two markets is not limited to a particular token listing or an individual trading product.

Closing exposure is different from transferring a balance

An account can contain more than assets that are immediately available to send elsewhere. A trader may have open orders, borrowed funds or positions that need to be closed before the remaining balance can be withdrawn. The exact procedure depends on the product and the account’s state.

That is why account-specific instructions matter. A simple transfer deadline would not explain how to deal with every possible obligation. It also would not identify which assets or networks remain available during a wind-down.

Customers should use the official account channel to establish those details. The exchange’s terms of use provide the wider contractual context, but a general terms page is not a substitute for the notification governing a particular closure.

Global brands still operate through regional boundaries

A blockchain transaction can cross borders without changing its basic technical format. An exchange’s relationship with a customer is different. Access depends on the legal entities, services and jurisdictional restrictions under which that relationship operates.

This creates a practical difference between ownership of a token and access to one venue that supports it. Losing access to a platform does not itself change the token’s network rules, but it can change how a customer trades, converts or withdraws that holding.

For businesses integrating exchange services, jurisdiction checks cannot be a one-time onboarding exercise. A later restriction can affect treasury operations, recurring transfers and the availability of a previously convenient source of liquidity.

Clear notices matter during an exchange exit

An orderly closure should make the remaining steps understandable: which positions must be settled, which transfers can still be requested and how exceptions are handled. Ambiguity can turn a manageable deadline into a rushed operational decision.

It can also create an opening for impersonators. KuCoin’s announcement specifically directs users to official channels. A message offering to accelerate an account exit should not be trusted merely because it references the real withdrawal from Russia and Belarus.

The relevant next evidence is the implementation of the closure process. The notice establishes the policy; account communications establish the individual timetable. Until those are read together, it would be misleading to describe every affected user as already unable to retrieve funds or to promise that all accounts will follow the same path.

For market observers, the story is another example of access risk at the service layer. The underlying assets can remain transferable while a particular commercial gateway changes the countries it serves.
