Jude Bellingham eclipses Zinedine Zidane Real Madrid feat in 73 fewer games

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Jude Bellingham scored his 50th Real Madrid goal in their 3-0 win over Racing Santander on Sunday.

Jose Mourinho’s side cruised to a dominant away victory after scoring three first half goals at El Sardinero.

Bellingham scored his 50th Real Madrid goal on Sunday, doing so much quicker than football icon Zinedine ZidaneGetty

Kylian Mbappe and Yan Diomande were also on the scoresheet either side of Bellingham’s 22nd minute strike.

The win leaves Real Madrid third in LaLiga, three points behind leaders Barcelona.

Bellingham makes history

Their victory also saw Bellingham reach an historic milestone in his Los Blancos career.

The England midfielder has now scored 50 goals in 154 matches for the Spanish giants since signing from Borussia Dortmund in 2023.

In doing so, he becomes the 50th player in Real Madrid’s history to reach the tally.

Bellingham is also the only player to score 50 goals since his debut season in 2023/24.

His latest effort was already his fourth in 11 LaLiga appearances under Mourinho this season.

Meanwhile, he has overtaken Zinedine Zidane’s tally for the club in 73 fewer matches.

The Ballon d’Or winner scored 49 goals altogether in 227 appearances between 2001 and 2006.

Zidane, who also wore the No.5 shirt for Real Madrid, won six trophies during his time at the Santiago Bernabeu.

Zidane’s incredible volley in the 2002 Champions League goal was surely his best oneAlamy

Those included a LaLiga title and the Champions League, honours which Bellingham won in his debut season in Spain.

The 23-year-old has also won the UEFA Super Cup, FIFA Intercontinental Cup and Spanish Super Cup in the last three years.

LaLiga title race

Real Madrid’s latest success has seen them maintain their three-point deficit to bitter rivals Barcelona.

It follows Barca’s 6-1 home win over Alaves on Saturday, which temporarily saw them move six points clear of Los Blancos.

Whilst neither side has drawn in LaLiga this season, Real Madrid have suffered two defeats to Barca’s one.

The leaders’ only setback came in El Clasico last weekend as they were beaten 2-1 at the Camp Nou.

Bellingham is the 50th player to score a half century of goals for Real MadridAFP

Real Madrid resume their league campaign with a trip to Valencia on Saturday.

It follows a Champions League clash with Greek champions AEK Athens in midweek.

Soaring health insurance costs are quietly preventing wages from rising higher, while workers also shoulder a larger share of premiums, economist says

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Open enrollment season for health care plans is about to begin, and many Americans may get sticker shock after seeing how much premiums will rise.

But the rising cost of insurance is also quietly eroding wage gains, preventing workers from getting bigger raises than they would otherwise receive.

Health insurance costs are a major issue in the midterm election. In addition to higher gas prices and utility bills, insurance has stoked voter anxiety about the rising cost of living.

So despite consistently solid wage gains and a labor market that’s at full employment, consumer confidence gauges have tumbled as overall affordability deteriorates. Fresh economic data illustrate why workers aren’t feeling upbeat about more pay.

In the third quarter, wages and salaries increased 3.1% from a year ago, according to the employment cost index released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics on Friday.

But at the same time, employer health insurance contributions jumped 6.4%, matching the fastest pace in two decades, according to economist Guy Berger, senior fellow at the Burning Glass Institute.

In a Substack post, he calculated that if employer contributions had instead grown at 3%, which was the pace a few years ago, wage growth would be about 0.2-0.3 percentage points faster today.

“That’s not a lot, but not nothing either,” Berger wrote.

With many Americans resorting to buy now, pay later plans to buy essentials, even those incremental gains could make a big difference.

Berger added that the data likely understates the squeeze workers a facing, as employer contributions might not be keeping pace with health insurance costs.

“In other words, employees are paying a bigger share of premiums,” he explained.

On top of that, the insurance plans themselves are covering less, forcing workers to pay more expenses out of pocket, Berger said.

Bureau of Labor Statistics

Americans who get health insurance through their employers aren’t the only ones feeling the pain. The cost of coverage via Affordable Care Act plans has also be surging.

In 2027, premiums will rise by 13%, according to health-research nonprofit KFF, which drew on federal data released Friday. That’s the second biggest hike since 2018 and follows a 26% jump in 2026.

The latest increase translates to a $704 average monthly premium for a 40-year-old purchasing a mid-tier Obamacare plan. Such “benchmark” premiums were $624 in 2026 and $497 in 2025.

While spending on drugs and hospital care is going up, the ACA market has come under particular strain since 2025, because Congress didn’t renew the enhanced subsidies that were enacted during the pandemic.

As a result, ACA enrollment has dropped by millions as people balked higher premium expenses. Since most who opted out were healthier, those remaining tend to be sicker and drive up premiums.

As of February, enrollment in ACA plans had plunged by 12% from a year earlier, or around 19.2 million people, and the industry has indicated more have dropped out since then.

Meanwhile, insurers are choosing not to participate in ACA marketplace, narrowing the choices for consumers. According to KFF, there were 31 state-level departures by insurers as of late October versus eight additions.

“Looking ahead to 2027, several insurers have said they expect the market to continue to deteriorate in 2027 as a continued result of the enhanced premium tax credits ending,” KFF said on Friday. “Some enrollees who were able to keep coverage in 2026 may no longer be able to make ends meet.”

Which Tech Companies Started Using Trump's Phrase 'Super Intelligence'?

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
CNBC reports: A month after President Donald Trump ordered that "super intelligence" replace the term artificial intelligence within executive branch departments and agencies, his leading tech allies, Elon Musk and Mark Zuckerberg, followed suit on earnings calls. Their fellow tech CEOs were less eager to do the same... SpaceX and Tesla adhered to Trump's wishes on earnings calls last week after Musk, earlier in October, changed the name of his rocket company's AI division to SpaceXSI. Speaking on Tesla's call about his work on humanoid robots, Musk said, "Some of them look very impressive, but they're not really using any kind of advanced AI or SI." Zuckerberg was equally compliant on Meta's earnings call on Oct. 21, emphasizing his company's "goal of delivering personal superintelligence to everyone." However, Meta was already using the term, having created a unit called Meta Superintelligence Labs last year.... [Some other tech CEOs are holding out.] Amazon CEO Andy Jassy peppered his call with use of "AI" on Thursday, uttering the abbreviation well over a dozen times in his introductory remarks. A day earlier, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella said one of the company's three points of focus is "designing AI to empower people." And Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai was seconds into his quarterly earnings commentary when he said the search giant's "AI investments are driving increased usage and overall user growth...." Trump's preferred term is seeping into other correspondence and documents. Microsoft's annual report, filed last week, included eight mentions of "super intelligence," starting at the top of the letter to shareholders... IBM CEO Arvind Krishna, who didn't attend Trump's September lunch, used "SI" alongside "AI" during his company's earnings call last week.... Lip-Bu Tan, CEO of chipmaker Intel, said Thursday the supply shortage the chip industry is navigating has been "driven by the unprecedented demand for super intelligence." Some in the tech industry have been explicit in their support of Trump's terminology ahead of their forthcoming earnings reports next month. Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora repeatedly referred to "SI" on CNBC, and posted on X on Sept. 30, "The meeting at the WH on SI was an epic moment...." Like Arora, [Nvidia CEO Jensen] Huang took to X after last month's AI meeting to praise the "White House Accord on Super Intelligence."
