Religious Exemption from Using AI in Workplace Granted to Humanist

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
"David Durman, a nontheistic humanist, recently secured a religious exemption from using AI in the workplace," reports TechCrunch. Durman, a University of Kentucky administrator, told TechCrunch that humanism centers on "human well-being and flourishing, both individually and collectively ... and implores a person to try to improve both their well-being and their flourishing as well as the others around them." He believes that using the technology runs counter to his beliefs. "The way we become moral actors for ourselves and for others is through the active application of our minds, and the diminishing of that [through AI use] is going to necessarily have a negative impact on our ability to do so," he said, reflecting on how overreliance on AI might affect critical thinking...." [Humanism] tends to appeal to people who are skeptical of a god or other supernatural power yet still seek a scientific and moral framework to ground themselves in. [Alexandria Huiskens, staff attorney at the American Humanist Association, told TechCrunch] "Concern about AI and human dignity can run through several different faiths and traditions, whether that's Catholic teachings, or Universalism, or Humanism, and we want Humanists or nontheists to know that they fall into these categories — that they are also protected by the law at work...." In Durman's administrative role, in which he supports researchers applying for grants, he has not been required to use AI at work. Like many workers across fields, some of his colleagues and leadership have recommended that employees try out these tools, so he sought to secure his exemption now, in case the suggestion becomes a mandate in the future. "I hope that if [others] have similar feelings, that they feel encouraged to pursue some kind of resolution," Durman said. A University of Kentucky spokesperson told TechCrunch they "determined that an accommodation could be provided while allowing the employee to continue performing the essential functions of their position." (They added that "As technology and job responsibilities evolve, accommodations may be reviewed to ensure they remain reasonable and workable.")

Sean Payton is the sixth-fastest coach to 200 wins

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Broncos coach (and long-time Saints coach) Sean Payton was going for his 200th career win on Sunday. And he got it.

It's one thing to get to 200. It's another thing to get there quickly, relative to others.

Payton is the sixth-fastest to 200 wins. He got there in 320 games.

The other five are Hall of Famers, with the exception of one who should be a Hall of Famer. Don Shula got there in 286 games. George Halas made it in 287 games. Curly Lambeau made it in 300 games. Bill Belichick made it in 307 games. And Paul Brown made it in 309 games.

Behind Payton is another Hall of Famer — Tom Landry. He got there in 325 games.

The other coaches with 200 or more wins are Andy Reid, Chuck Noll, Marty Schottenheimer, Mike Tomlin, and Dan Reeves.

Payton will now chase Reeves and Tomlin, who have 201 wins each, Schottenheimer, who has 205, and Noll, who has 209.

After that, it's Brown with 222, Lambeau with 229, and Landry with 270.

Andy Reid: I didn't see a false start on Jaylon Moore at end of game

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Chiefs blew an 11-point lead in the fourth quarter of Sunday's game against the Broncos, but they were able to drive back into position to try for a game-winning touchdown in the final seconds.

That effort looked like it was successful when Patrick Mahomes flipped a pass to wide receiver Rashee Rice in the end zone with four seconds to play, but the play had been whistled dead due to a false start on right tackle Jaylon Moore. Mahomes was sacked on the next snap and the Broncos celebrated a 28-24 win.

Moore's movement was minimal enough that Chiefs head coach Andy Reid said at his postgame press conference that he thought the call was incorrect.

“I didn’t see it," Reid said. "That’s one man’s opinion. I saw him move his head, but I didn’t see a false start.”

Moore also appeared to twitch his leg prior to the snap, which may not change Reid's opinion but it was enough for a costly penalty that helped send the Chiefs to a second straight loss.
