McMurtry's 1,000-HP Fan Car Still Sucks After 10 Years

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

McMurtry Automotive is officially celebrating its tenth anniversary, and the high-performance world is far better for it. What began in 2016 as a tiny team of ambitious engineers in rural Gloucestershire, England, has transformed into the manufacturer of arguably the most violent and capable track car ever conceived. Over the last decade, the boutique brand expanded to nearly 100 employees and recently opened a state-of-the-art production facility in the Cotswolds, proving that a small, independent automaker can still disrupt the global hypercar establishment.

The company’s original goal was simple but seemingly impossible: build the ultimate lightweight driver’s vehicle. When their initial superbike-on-four-wheels concept didn’t commercially pan out, the late engineering pioneer and co-founder Sir David McMurtry offered a delightfully blunt solution to solve their traction limitations: “just suck it to the ground.” Inspired by banned 1970s racing technology, the team pivoted to fan-based downforce, creating a platform that would fundamentally change the performance landscape.

The Fan-Powered Phenomenon

By engineering a proprietary “Downforce on Demand” system, McMurtry created a vehicle that literally vacuums itself to the tarmac. Twin electric fans pull air from underneath the chassis, generating a staggering 4,400 pounds of downforce while the vehicle sits at a complete standstill. This allows the almost production-ready electric drivetrain to deploy its 1,000 horsepower instantly, launching the ultra-lightweight carbon fiber chassis from zero to 60 mph in a brain-scrambling 1.55 seconds.

While Tesla promises their upcoming Roadster will use cold-gas thrusters to literally blow the competition away, McMurtry took the exact opposite approach; they built a hypercar that simply sucks. The on-track results of this extreme vacuum engineering have been nothing short of historic. The Spéirling shattered the outright hillclimb record at the Goodwood Festival of Speed in 2022, then smashed the long-standing Top Gear Test Track record. It generates so much instantaneous cornering grip that it even leaves veteran racing drivers speechless, with extreme sports legend Travis Pastrana claiming it is the fastest they’ve driven.

Ready for the Track

After a decade of relentless prototyping and high-speed testing, the pure fan car is finally transitioning from a wild concept into a tangible reality for well-heeled enthusiasts. Production of the Spéirling PURE is officially underway at the brand’s new UK facility, with the very first customer deliveries slated to begin later this year. Production is strictly capped at just 100 units worldwide, ensuring the vehicle remains an incredibly rare sight at local track days.

From an editorial perspective, McMurtry’s ten-year journey is a massive victory for independent automotive engineering. In an era where legacy manufacturers are constantly adding weight and complexity to their vehicles, McMurtry took a massive financial risk to build a pure, single-seat track weapon. Access to this unparalleled, Formula 1-level performance obviously comes at an astronomical cost, carrying a massive $1.3 million sticker before personalization. However, for the lucky few who can afford it, they are buying a literal piece of physics-defying history.

The 2026-27 Premier League season has already given us some absolute beauties. And we’re not even close to the halfway point yet.

We’ve already witnessed some extraordinary strikes, with long-range rockets, outrageous individual efforts and slick team moves all catching the eye.

But which have been the very best? We’ve ranked our 10 favourite Premier League goals of the season so far.

10. Keane Lewis-Potter vs Tottenham Hotspur

Team moves can be just as pleasing as solo efforts when it feels like the shot is the end of a beautifully orchestrated move.

Such an example came in Brentford’s 3-0 beating of Tottenham and the Bees opening the scoring via Lewis-Potter who was the final cog in a well oiled machine.

Brentford cut Spurs’ defence apart. Mamadou Sangare picked out Dango Ouattara who returned the pass and by the time he had done, Sangare had got into the box without a single Spurs player following him.

Sangare then had the presence of mind to look up and pick out a completely unmarked Lewis-Potter who finished off the team move by rolling the ball into the net.

9. Emi Buendia vs Tottenham Hotspur

Spurs certainly gave Buendia a helping hand as they opted to give him all the time in the world on the edge of the box but they were probably not anticipating such a sweetly hit strike.

Given that freedom, Buendia picked out the top corner but what made it especially satisfying was that it was the opposite corner to where he was facing.

Kinsky did not stand a chance.

8. Kevin Schade vs Leeds United

It was a wonderfully inventive finish from Schade who had such little space to work with.

Away at Leeds, the ball was crossed in at pace and all Schade could do was poke a foot at it.

The mere mortals of us would probably have got our toe on it and sent it out for a goal kick but Schade was able to give it a cushioned glance, allowing it to go over the keeper and in at the far post.

7. Rayan Cherki vs Crystal Palace

Not every beautiful goal has to be a long-range screamer and it comes as little surprise that Rayan Cherki is the author of the most difficult technical skill so far this season.

Cherki has scored plenty of goals with his quick feet over the years but his best of the campaign was in City’s 4-0 thumping of Crystal Palace.

Cherki gets the ball on the edge of the box, dribbles past one slide tackle and then holds off the pressure of Chris Richards to work a way to goal.

His shot is actually very slow but it was perfectly placed as Dean Henderson had already committed to going to the near post, the most logical place of where Cherki would try to shoot.

6. Bruno Guimaraes vs Sunderland

The angle of Bruno Guimaraes’ body when he hits this ball makes no sense.

He is completely leaning back, so much so that he is not that far from sitting down and by the time he is done swinging, his right foot has crossed over past his left.

And yet, it is a perfectly hit shot into the top corner. It is extra enjoyable as it comes off the bar on the way.

The fact there is the added needle of an ex-Newcastle man scoring against Sunderland also improves it.

5. Julio Enciso vs Hull City

There’s a lot to love about Enciso’s effort against Hull.

Firstly, there’s the fact he does not go down despite an Oli McBurnie shove.

Then, he has the footwork and the presence of mind to skip forward, move the ball onto his left and then flick it past the closing defender.

4. Harvey Barnes vs Bournemouth

There is something about a perfectly placed curled effort that is just very aesthetically pleasing.

3. Ainsley Maitland-Niles vs Manchester United

Goals from distance are always enjoyable but when it rescues a last-minute point against Manchester United, it’s even better.

Maitland-Niles had only been on the pitch for 13 minutes when the ball rolled to him just outside the penalty area. He did not take a touch before as he shot without even breaking stride.

Senne Lammens could only watch as it sailed into the very top left corner.

2. Diego Gomez vs Crystal Palace

You can tell a player and team is feeling confident when they are pulling off goals from the halfway line.

Brighton were already leading 1-0 after five minutes when Gomez gets the ball inside his own half. Instead of passing to a teammate, he immediately looks to see where goalkeeper Walter Benitez is and, spotting he was off his line, goes for it.

One of the most pleasing aspects of the shot was that it was not a high lob into the air then bounced on the way into the goal. This was a shot that stayed the same height in the air until the exact moment it needed to drop for it to creep in under the bar.

His halfway line hit the net before the ground which is a sign of how good a shot it was and there was no one on the planet happier than him at that moment.

1. Lewis Dunk vs Coventry City

This would already be the best goal of the season but something about it coming from a 6ft 4’ centre back who had previously scored one goal in the past two seasons makes it all the better.

Dunk picks the ball up from 30 yards and decides to just have a crack. What he produced was the most perfect, dipping shot that a full-stretch Carl Rushworth didn’t even get close to.

It had an xG of 0.03 and is pleasingly the only shot he has taken all season.

It just beats Gomez’s effort. Just.

A scorching planet that should have lost its atmosphere billions of years ago still has one

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

A rocky planet orbiting extremely close to its star might be expected to be bare, scorched stone, its atmosphere stripped away long ago by intense radiation.

Yet new observations from the James Webb Space Telescope suggest that a lava planet called TOI-561 b still has a thick atmosphere.

Something in that atmosphere reflects most of the starlight that hits it.

If that reading holds up, a small rocky planet can keep its gas in a place where astronomers expected only bare rock. The planet is about 280 light-years away, and its atmosphere is not livable.

Anjali Piette, an assistant professor of astronomy at the University of Birmingham in the U.K., is part of the international team behind the work.

“Our discovery challenges current theories,” Piette said. “We would expect a heavily irradiated rocky planet like TOI-561 b to have lost its atmosphere billions of years ago.”

The study was led by Samuel Boucher of the Université de Montréal, with Lisa Dang of the University of Waterloo in Canada as project lead.

The planet should be bare rock

TOI-561 b is a little more than twice Earth’s mass and about 1.4 times as wide. It circles its star in about 10.7 hours, so a full year there passes in less than half an Earth day.

At that distance, one side of the planet always faces the star and the other always faces away.

The day side is likely covered by an ocean of molten rock, which is why astronomers call it one of the lava planets.

TOI-561 b is also lighter for its size than most planets in such tight orbits, which tend to match Earth-like rock. Its star is unusual too: at least 10 billion years old, more than twice the age of the sun, and poorer in heavy elements.

Watching a whole orbit

The first hint of air came from a 2025 study led by Johanna Teske. Her team measured heat from the planet’s day side each time it passed behind its star.

It was too cool for bare rock, and too cool even for an atmosphere made only of rock vapor.

The heat measurements came from a single 37-hour stretch in one Webb observing program.

During that time, the planet passed behind its star four times. Boucher’s team analyzed the whole stretch, night side included.

The long watch also let the team separate the planet’s signal from slow flickering on the star’s surface, where hot gas rises and sinks.

The day side ran cool

The researchers calculated how hot the day side would get if the planet were dark, bare rock with no air to carry heat around. The answer was about 5,300°F (3,200 K). Webb measured about 3,200°F (2,050 K) instead.

The missing heat could have gone one of two ways. Air could have carried it around to the night side, or the planet could have reflected it away before absorbing it.

The night side came in at about 1,500°F (1,100 K). That’s warm for a side that never sees its star, so some heat is reaching it. It isn’t warm enough to explain the gap, though.

Reflection has to cover the rest. The team estimates the planet reflects about 74% of the starlight that hits it, close to the share reflected by Venus, the brightest planet in our night sky.

Lab tests show that molten-rock surfaces reflect very little light, so something above the surface has to be doing the reflecting.

Rock clouds could reflect light

To find out what that could be, the team compared Webb’s readings with computer simulations of the planet’s climate.

Heat moving around the planet couldn’t explain the readings alone. The best matches came from a thick atmosphere, with surface air pressure between one and 10 times Earth’s, holding something that reflected a lot of light.

The leading candidates are mineral clouds, made from rock that evaporates off the molten surface.

In the simulations, clouds of silicon dioxide, the mineral in quartz and most sand, and magnesium silicate could form on the day side.

They would gather near the edge where day turns to night, where the air cools enough for those minerals to condense.

Clouds like that would work as a sunscreen, sending starlight back out before it can heat the planet.

Venus or a hotter Io?

The gas that makes up most of the atmosphere is still unknown. It could be a thick layer of carbon dioxide, like a much hotter version of Venus.

Or it could be rich in sulfur dioxide, a gas released by the volcanoes on Io, one of Jupiter’s moons.

Another lava planet, 55 Cancri e, is also thought to have a substantial atmosphere, probably carrying carbon dioxide or carbon monoxide.

Earlier work by Dang, Piette and their colleagues pointed to gases from TOI-561 b’s interior refilling its air.

How the atmosphere is still there at all is the bigger puzzle.

“This raises major questions such as whether the planet started with an unusually large supply of gases or if the ocean of molten rock covering its surface acts as a planet-wide volcano that is constantly feeding its atmosphere,” Piette said.

Webb has more to look at

The temperatures are best estimates. Webb watched the planet in a narrow band of infrared light, and that band misses the wavelengths where the planet gives off the most heat.

The two detectors inside Webb’s instrument also didn’t agree perfectly once their data were compared with the climate simulations.

The difference could come from the instrument, from the star, or from clouds or gases that look different at different wavelengths, and the team can’t yet say which.

Webb also recorded the planet passing in front of its star during the same observation.

Those data will get a paper of their own, which should help show how thick the atmosphere is and whether clouds are really there.

“The next big question we will seek to answer is whether TOI-561 b’s atmosphere resembles Venus or an extreme version of Io,” Dang said.

“Observing the planet at longer wavelengths with JWST could give us insights and help explain how a planet this hot has held on to an atmosphere for billions of years.”

Carbon dioxide absorbs light at one of those longer wavelengths. A full orbit watched there with Webb’s mid-infrared instrument could show whether the sky over TOI-561 b looks more like Venus.

The full study was published in the journal The Astronomical Journal.
