On the surface, Contacts appears to be a basic app that stores names, numbers, and other things you want to remember about people or businesses. However, it has some great features hidden under the surface, such as the ability to quickly delete duplicate contacts, smart lists, and faster ways to bulk share or delete contacts. Use this hidden search feature to delete duplicate contacts

Credit: Pranay Parab

If you have the same number or email address saved under multiple contacts, you can easily clear these from your iPhone. iOS shows you a prompt at the top of the list of contacts that lets you remove duplicate contacts easily. The same feature is hidden in the Mac version of Contacts. To try it, open Contacts on the Mac, and select the Card menu at the top of the screen. Then, click Look for Duplicates. This will identify duplicate contacts for you, and you can quickly merge or delete them.

Try the two-finger swipe gesture to select multiple contacts

It's pretty easy to select multiple entries when you use the Contacts app on the Mac. You can hold the Shift or Ctrl buttons and use arrow keys or click multiple contacts to select them all. On your iPhone or iPad, this feature is available via a hidden gesture. You need to use two fingers and swipe upwards or downwards on the list of contacts. Once you get the gesture right, you'll be able to select multiple contacts quickly. After you select one batch of contacts, you can scroll up or down and use the same gesture to add more contacts to your selection. This is the fastest way to execute bulk actions in your iPhone's Contacts app.

Copy a contact and paste it in Messages to share phone numbers quickly

When you share a contact, iOS shows you a prompt to select the fields you wish to send. This is great for privacy because you don't want to send your partner's home address, birthdate, your anniversary, nicknames, or other personal details by default. However, this extra step adds a little bit of friction while sharing contacts. If you want a faster way to send a contact on your iPhone, you can copy and paste it in the Messages app. To try it, open the Contacts app and scroll till you see the contact's name. Hold the contact's name and press Copy. Open Messages, paste it in any chat, and send it. Unfortunately, this doesn't work with third-party messaging apps like WhatsApp, but it's great for texts and iMessage.

Hold the message button in a contact card to switch to a different messaging app

On your iPhone, you can use Contacts to message people on WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, or other apps. Just open a contact and hold the messaging button. This will reveal a list of apps where you can ping that person. WhatsApp doesn't have access to my contacts, which makes it a bit annoying to message people I know. Since I started using the Contacts app to start chats on WhatsApp, the process of pinging new people has become a lot faster.

Hold the call button to change the app used to call a person

Just as you can use Contacts to message people on third-party apps, you can also use it to call them on those apps. Go to any contact in the Contacts app on your iPhone, and hold the call button. This will let you call them on WhatsApp, Signal, or any other apps where their phone number is linked.

Delete a contact quickly by long-pressing its name

Credit: Pranay Parab

iOS makes it hard to delete contacts quickly. This prevents accidental deletions, but it can be annoying when you just want to remove people who you last spoke to in high school. To speed this up, open Contacts on your iPhone and hold any contact's name. In the popover menu, select Delete to remove the contact. To remove multiple contacts quickly, use the two-finger swipe gesture to select multiple contacts, and hold any of the selected contacts. In the popover menu, you can select Delete X Contacts, where X is the number of contacts you've selected.

Use the Mac app to export your contacts and back them up offline

To export your entire address book, you can use the Contacts app on your Mac. Once you have it open, go to File > Export > Export vCard. This will save all your contacts in a vcf file, and you can import that into Google Contacts or other apps or services. You also have the option to export contacts as a PDF file. Just select File > Export as PDF to get it done.

Remove fields you don't use when adding new contacts on Mac

When you create a new contact on your Mac, the app shows multiple fields that you may never use. If you just need to save phone numbers and email addresses, you can delete all the other blank fields from the template. To do this, open Contacts on your Mac, and go to Card > Add Field > Edit Template to add or remove fields from the default contact template.

Use smart lists to filter contacts on your Mac

Credit: Pranay Parab

If you added a number recently, such as a salesperson at a car dealership, but forgot their name, the Contacts app doesn't make it easy to find the contact. Instead of scrolling through the entire list of contacts and trying to guess which one could be the person you spoke to, you can use the Contacts app on your Mac to locate them quickly. It lets you create smart lists to sort contacts by recently added. Open Contacts on your Mac, and go to File > New Smart List. Now select Card in the first drop-down menu, followed by has changed in for the second one. You can then add the number of days, such as 10 days, in the final field, and click OK. Click the newly created smart list in the left pane, and you'll see all contacts that were either created or updated in the last 10 days, which will include the hypothetical car salesperson.

Smart lists also allow you to quickly create groups of related contacts. For instance, I've saved all my former colleagues' details with the company name in the contact. To find them all quickly, I created a smart list that groups all contacts with that company's name.

Optimize your contact card to avoid sharing unnecessary information

NameDrop is one of my favorite Apple features. It lets you bump two Apple devices together to exchange contact information quickly. Try holding your iPhone near another person's iPhone, and you'll see a full-screen pop-up asking if you want to share your contact with someone, or if you'd just like to receive their contact information. To protect your privacy, you should limit sending information to others. Updating your contact card is the best way to do this. Open the Contacts app on your iPhone, and tap your name up top. Hit the Edit button and remove information you don't wish to share. I recommend updating your profile picture too. Instead of sharing a candid photo, you can use an emoji or a professional headshot that you're comfortable sharing in public.

EA Reportedly Chose An Unannounced Star Wars Game Over BioWare’s Next Mass Effect

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

EA recently announced it was shutting down BioWare, ending a studio responsible for multiple hit games over 30 years. According to a new report, EA's decision was driven by economics, as it had to choose between funding a new Mass Effect and backing the safer bet, Star Wars: Battlefront 3.

Bloomberg's Jason Schreier reported that EA had to choose between two costly projects, and with the added pressure of paying off $18 billion in leveraged debt after its sale to a business consortium and Saudi Arabia's investment fund, Battlefront 3 made more financial sense. To add insult to injury, Battlefront 3 isn't even in development yet, and it hasn't been officially announced.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dx0b2Xbnnrc

"They chose to invest in Star Wars and shut down BioWare as a result, said the people familiar, speaking anonymously because they were not authorized to talk to the press," Schreier wrote. "Star Wars: Battlefront 3 has not even begun development yet, but EA executives saw it as a less risky, more predictable bet than a new Mass Effect--and the publisher behind Battlefield has always been more comfortable with shooters than RPGs."

Schreier added that "corporate politics" may have also influenced the decision, as one of the studio's key advocates, senior EA exec Laura Miele, was reportedly sidelined when EA Sports veterans David Tinson and Cam Weber were promoted. BioWare also experienced several flops over the last decade, with Mass Effect: Andromeda, Anthem, and Dragon Age: The Veilguard failing to meet sales expectations, which further influenced EA's decision to shut down the studio.

After BioWare's closure, more details on its Mass Effect project have emerged. A trailer for Mass Effect: Universe was posted online last week; the game's details made it sound like a sci-fi version of John Wick, and video game composer Austin Wintory shared an experimental score as part of his Mass Effect audition tape.

Spanish Team Bags First Ascent of Jugal II

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Mikel Zabalza, Ruben Perez, and Mikel Saez de Urabain have completed the first ascent of 6,518m Jugal II.

According to Spanish media, the team climbed via the east-southeast side and reached the summit on October 26. One day later, they returned to base camp, their home team told Diario de Navarra. Zabalza and Urabain had explored the area in the spring of 2024, but bad weather prevented them from attempting the peak. The team will share more details when they're back home.

Two attempts, one success

Jugal II was also the planned goal for Italians Nives Meroi and Romano Benet, who left for the area three weeks ago. Originally, the team wanted to climb Tilicho Peak in the Annapurna region. Recent storms and floods had cut trails and swept away bridges in that area, so they had to seek an alternative.

ExplorersWeb caught up with Meroi as she and Benet reached a lodge to seek shelter from the heavy rain.

"We're on our way to Kathmandu, and we are with the Spaniards," she confirmed. As Nives explained, they all attempted the climb, and the Spanish team reached the summit, while the Italians retreated on the previous day. Until now, there has been no news from the Italians since Tine Cuder of Slovenia, the third team member, fell ill and left the expedition two weeks ago.

Left to right, Nives Meroi, Tine Cuder, and Romano Benet in Kathmandu. Photo: Romano Benet

Open since 2023

The Jugal Himal is just northeast of Kathmandu, on the Tibetan border. Surprisingly accessible for a previously unclimbed peak, it’s reachable by bus and a few days’ trekking. Jugal I and II opened to foreign expeditions in 2023. In spring 2024, veteran South Korean climber Um Hong-gil led a Korean-Nepalese expedition featuring Sherpa celebrities Dawa Yangzum and Lakpa Dendi to both unclimbed Jugals. They successfully summited Jugal I, but not Jugal II. According to The Himalayan Database, Jugal II remained unclimbed.

Route 2 FI’s math holds up. Arkham’s Binance Hacker entity shows 18,270 BTC, worth $1.42 billion at Friday’s prices. SoSoValue figures show US spot Bitcoin ETFs took in about $440.7 million across four straight days of inflows from October 26 to 29. That makes the hacker’s Bitcoin about 3.2 times a week of ETF demand.

Binance hacker holdings on October 30, 2026 vs spot Bitcoin ETF inflows for October 26 to 29. Sources: Arkham, SoSoValue.

The hacker hasn’t sold any of the Bitcoin yet. The selling so far has been in smaller tokens: Lookonchain tracked the attacker dumping UNI, PEPE, ENA, LINK and others for 70,480 ETH, worth about $158 million.

On the other side, Binance is already buying. The exchange told CoinDesk it has set aside more than $6.34 billion to cover the losses, $5 billion of it already held in the same tokens the attackers took, and is buying the rest on exchanges and over the counter. About $3 billion of the stolen funds is frozen and doesn’t need replacing.

Co-founder Yi He laid out the plan on X:

“Binance’s SAFU fund and Binance’s holdings include a large amount of assets such as BTC and ETH. We are currently buying, through OTC and on-exchange, all the stolen altcoins in fully matching amounts.”

That matters for Bitcoin. If Binance already holds enough BTC to cover customers, its buying lands mostly on altcoins, which leaves the hacker’s 18,270 BTC as a seller without a matching buyer.

What the Bybit Hack Says About Buybacks

Route 2 FI pointed to the Bybit hack as the template. Attackers took about $1.4 billion in ether from Bybit on February 21, 2025, and the exchange replaced it within days through loans, deposits and market purchases.

The buyback gave prices only a short lift. Measured from the February 20, 2025 close on Binance:

Asset Two days after the hack Low within 30 days ETH +3.0% ($2,819.69) −35.9% ($1,754.28 on March 11) Bitcoin −2.1% ($96,258) −22.1% ($76,606 on March 11)

Ethereum bounced while Bybit bought, then lost more than a third of its value within three weeks. The broader market sold off over that stretch for reasons beyond the hack, so the table shows that buyback demand fades fast. It doesn’t show that the hack caused the slide.

Route 2 FI also argued that the hack, coming a year after the October 10, 2025 crash, gives traders another reason to keep less money on centralized exchanges. Binance paid out more than $283 million in compensation after that crash, when some assets lost their pegs on its platform.

The trader cited the jump in Hyperliquid’s HYPE token as proof. The timing doesn’t fit, though. HYPE rose from $79.38 to a $97.09 high between 10:00 and 16:00 UTC on Thursday, after ICE and Trade.xyz announced plans for a regulated US perpetuals venue on Hyperliquid. That was about ten hours before the Binance drain began at 20:04 UTC.

After the hack, HYPE fell to $88.55 and trades at $90.83.
