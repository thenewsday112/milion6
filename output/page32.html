As Trump eyes new attack, Iran vows to retaliate beyond the Mideast and target financial assets, while oil markets brace for generational damage

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The clock is ticking toward a new round of fighting in the U.S.-Iran war, but this time the damage could be much more widespread, stretching to new regions and different types of targets.

President Donald Trump has said he’s contemplating “another little Epic Fury” after the U.S. midterm elections on Tuesday.

The U.S. has already sent a third aircraft carrier and Marine expeditionary unit to the Middle East, and told allies in the region to prepare for a possible short but intense renewal of fighting, according to the Wall Street Journal.

The Iranian regime similarly stepped up its threats on how it would respond, seeking to deter an attack by threatening disproportionate and no-holds-barred retaliation.

On Saturday, Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei warned Iran’s response would be “without any geographical limitations,” adding that the “data bank of targets should be updated so that the armed forces could hit all kinds of financial and non-financial assets.”

In recent weeks, Iran has warned undersea internet cables and data centers would be targets. The foiled attack on a British air base used by U.S. forces has also been attributed to Tehran.

And while North Koreans have been blamed for the $6.3 billion crypto heist at Binance, it shows how vulnerable such assets are to Iran, which is also adept at cyberattacks. At the same time, the Pentagon is investigating whether hackers hit freezers on U.S. military bases.

The RAND think tank pointed out last week that hostile Iranian activity “spans the globe,” with proxy Hezbollah in Latin America and reported plots against civilians in Europe.

“Unbound and bent on revenge, Iran is unlikely to be contained to just one corner of the globe,” analysts wrote, arguing that the least bad option would be for the U.S. to continue its naval blockade and not launch new attacks.

Diplomacy is ongoing with the U.S. and Iran exchanging messages via intermediaries, but neither side has budged from their initial demands, dampening hopes for a breakthrough in the next few days.

Given the hardened rhetoric from the regime with threats of unrestricted retaliation, experts now detect a change in Iran’s tactics against the U.S. and see greater odds of the war returning.

For Hamidreza Azizi, senior Iran analyst at the Crisis Group, the most important development was the message from Khamenei.

“This language suggests a further evolution in Iran’s military strategy,” he posted on X on Saturday. “Since the beginning of the war, Tehran has increasingly moved away from its traditional emphasis on proportionate response and retaliation toward a more offensive approach aimed at imposing costs on adversaries. The latest statements appear to take this logic a step further.”

Azizi also cited remarks from another top official who indicated Iran could stage a preemptive strike if preparations for hostile action were confirmed. In addition, Khamenei insisted Iran would determine when and how any renewed conflict ends.

Iran is also wary of further diplomatic efforts, especially since previous attacks came even in the middle of talks with the U.S. early this year and last summer.

“Iran appears increasingly determined to prevent a repetition of previous scenarios in which negotiations or temporary ceasefires were followed by renewed military conflict,” Azizi added. “From Tehran’s perspective, the objective is to make another attack so costly and unpredictable that Washington would have to reconsider the option altogether.”

Meanwhile, Iran still has thousands of missiles and drones in its arsenal that can inflict catastrophic damage to its neighbors in the Persian Gulf area.

That’s as stockpiles of U.S. interceptors have already been depleted to critically low levels, while newer Iranian missiles has demonstrated the ability to evade air-defense systems.

Considering how much damage the region’s energy infrastructure sustained earlier in the war, it can’t absorb another round of fighting, according to Amir Handjani is a board member at the Quincy Institute for Responsible Statecraft.

Indeed, even U.S. military bases across the Middle East have been heavily damaged by Iranian drones and missiles.

“Gulf producers spent five decades persuading the world that their supply could withstand regional wars. Eight months of fighting on two fronts have badly weakened that case. A negotiated end to the war would give the region time to rebuild it,” Handjani wrote.

“Another round, on top of the Saudi-Houthi war, would leave damage to infrastructure and to importers’ confidence that markets are likely to carry for a generation.”

Rawayana Throws Venezuelan-Style Halloween Party in NYC, Feat. Guests Cheo Pardo & Yorghaki

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Rawayana brought a Venezuelan-style Halloween party to New York City’s Barclays Center on Saturday night (Oct. 31), entertaining a sold-out crowd with hits like “Uber,” “Feriado” and “Hora Loca.” The band members joined in the festivities in costume, with Beto sporting dreadlocks, Fofo wearing a short, sleek bob wig, Abeja rocking an ’80s-style hair-metal wig and Tony dressed as a woman.

The beloved trippy-pop band started their performance on the main stage, featuring a backdrop screen alternating tropical visuals with imagery inspired by the songs they were performing, accompanied by smoke columns and confetti. They later moved to a second stage in the middle of the audience, truly igniting the party with tracks like “Si Te Pica Es Porque Eres Tú,” “Que Rico PR” and “Veneka,” undoubtedly among the most enthusiastically sung along tracks of the night. For the latter, in collaboration with Akapellah, Beto proudly carried the Latin Grammy for best Latin electronic music performance they received in 2025.

As part of this set, two special guests thrilled the crowd: Cheo Pardo, a former member of Los Amigos Invisibles whom Beto introduced as “one of our great heroes, great influencers in our musical history,” delivered a masterful DJ set dressed as a priest, keeping the crowd’s energy at its peak with classics like “Qué Es Lo Que Tiene Esa Nena” by El General and “El Tiburón” by Proyecto Uno. Then there was Yorghaki, who initially went unnoticed wearing a Spider-Man costume until he removed his mask to reveal his identity, before performing his collab with Beéle “abusadOra.”

Among the close to 12,000-people crowd, Venezuelan flags were prominently waved, with many fans wearing outfits referencing the South American country, such as jerseys from the Leones del Caracas baseball team as well as from the national soccer team, known as La Vinotinto. Flags from Mexico, Puerto Rico and the Dominican Republic, among other Latin American nations, were also spotted.

But it wasn’t all about the party. In a particularly heartfelt moment, Beto sat among the crowd with his guitar to perform “Tonada Por Ella,” dedicating the moment to those affected by the twin earthquakes that struck Venezuela last June. He invited attendees to donate to Fundación El After, a nonprofit organization Rawayana established to aid long-term recovery efforts in their homeland. QR codes appeared on the screens, directing fans to the foundation’s website, with the message: “Donate with El After. Your donation will help bring kids back to school,” accompanied by images of school desks.

Saturday’s show was part of Rawayana’s ¿Dónde Es El After? World Tour, in support of their album of the same name released on Jan. 1 which earned them their first No. 1s on the Billboard charts with “Inglés en Miami.” The song, a collaboration with Manuel Turizo, topped the Latin Airplay and Latin Rhythm Airplay rankings last May after going viral on social media with a dance challenge.

The New York City show also came just one day after the state of Massachusetts officially proclaimed Oct. 30, 2026 as “Rawayana Day,” following their performance at the MGM Music Hall at Fenway in Boston.

Promoted by Live Nation, the sold-out U.S. leg of the ¿Dónde Es El After? World Tour continues Sunday night (Nov. 1) in Philadelphia, with upcoming stops in Washington, D.C.; Chicago and Los Angeles, among other cities, before wrapping up on Dec. 6 in Miami.

Rawayana graced recently the cover of Billboard Español and participated in a panel during Billboard Latin Music Week in Miami Beach alongside Hans Schafer of Live Nation, who was recognized as Billboard’s Latin Power Players 2026 Executive of the Year.

Cristiano Ronaldo vs Lionel Messi: The post-2018 stats behind Erik ten Hag’s explosive claims

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Erik ten Hag has launched a scathing attack on Cristiano Ronaldo, claiming the Portuguese superstar has been in decline since leaving Real Madrid for Juventus in 2018.

But does the former Manchester United manager have a point? What better way to assess Ronaldo’s supposed decline than by comparing his numbers with those of his old rival Lionel Messi?

The summer of 2018 feels a lifetime ago. Ronaldo had just inspired Real Madrid to a third straight Champions League triumph, the pair were tied on five Ballon d’Ors, and Messi was licking his wounds after a disastrous World Cup with Argentina. The GOAT debate felt a lot closer back then.

“Every top team must embrace the top player, but the top player must also make way for the team. That was simply the problem with Ronaldo,” Ten Hag told ESPN.

“The players wanted to work for him and they looked up to him because let there be no misunderstanding about that. He is of course a tremendous player, one of the all-time greats.

“But that is also the reality over the last seven, eight years, he simply can no longer deliver. From Juventus onwards it’s simply over.

“That is actually the great gift of Real Madrid. They always know when to say goodbye to players at the right moment. They recognise when a player’s level is declining. And they say goodbye at a moment when to the outside world, they still appear to be at their peak, but they can no longer actually deliver.”

Ten Hag’s arrival at Manchester United effectively spelt the beginning of the end for Ronaldo, whose relationship with the Dutchman quickly turned sour.

“When I started at United, and I explained to him how we were going to play, what his role in that would be, and what he could expect from me and from the team. But ultimately, you also have to deliver yourself,” Ten Hag added.

“He could no longer bring himself to push through the pain and to give everything every single day and to train at his maximum in order to get fit.

“If you’re not fit in the Premier League, Champions League, or with the Portuguese national team, well, then you simply have no chance.”

Juventus broke the bank to sign Ronaldo in 2018, hoping he’d finally deliver the Champions League. He scored plenty and won two Scudetti, but their European fortunes deteriorated and their stranglehold on Italian football came to an end.

His Manchester United homecoming proved even more disastrous. The Red Devils missed out on Champions League qualification, Ronaldo’s relationship with Ten Hag completely broke down, and he ended up leaving European football altogether for Saudi Arabia.

Messi’s club career hasn’t exactly been plain sailing either. His Barcelona exit was desperately sad, albeit after another Ballon d’Or, while his time at PSG was largely forgettable. The star-studded attack alongside Neymar and Kylian Mbappe never quite clicked, and he’s since wound down in MLS.

But that’s in stark contrast to his international career, where he’s arguably enjoyed his greatest years, leading Argentina through a golden era of success.

The numbers make for interesting reading. Ronaldo has scored eight more goals than Messi since 2018, but 80 have come from the penalty spot compared to Messi’s 37. The Argentine has also registered more than three times as many assists.

But the greatest difference is in what they’ve won. Messi has added 14 trophies, three Ballon d’Ors and the crowning glory of the World Cup. It’s difficult to argue that Ronaldo has enjoyed anything like the same late-career success.

Sen. Chris Murphy, D-Conn., was hospitalized after suffering a stroke Saturday afternoon while attending his son’s hockey game, his office announced Sunday.

The 53-year-old senator began experiencing numbness in his arm and was taken to a local hospital, where doctors determined he had suffered a stroke. He was subsequently transferred to Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore for further monitoring.

“He was transferred to Johns Hopkins for further monitoring and is responding well to treatment,” Murphy’s chief of staff, Allison Herwitt, said in a statement.
