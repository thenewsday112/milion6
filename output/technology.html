This Is How Fast You Should Walk to Help Lower Your Risk of Death

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
People who walk faster may have better underlying health. Lol2368/E+/Getty Images
  • A study found that walking speed can help predict people’s risk of dying from any cause.
  • Both a clinical test and a test done in daily life were able to predict mortality risk.
  • Experts say walking speed predicts death risk because it’s an indicator of overall health.
  • If your walking speed slows, it’s a good idea to talk with your healthcare professional.

New research shows that how fast you walk, whether measured in a clinical test or through daily activity, can help predict your risk of dying from any cause.

The study compared two ways of measuring walking: a short walking speed test conducted in a clinic and the step rate people take in their everyday lives, measured with a wrist-worn device.

Both measures were linked to mortality risk and showed similar ability to predict it.

The findings, published on October 23 in JAMA Network Open, could help doctors and researchers choose the best method for assessing health risks in different settings.

Clinical walking speed vs. everyday walking

The study was part of the Whitehall II accelerometer substudy in the United Kingdom.

It included 4,067 participants, mostly males, with an average age of about 69 years. About one-third of the subjects were female.

The researchers collected data from January 2012 to December 2013 and followed up through August 2024.

To measure clinical walking speed, participants completed a standard walking test. They walked a marked 2.44-meter course at their usual pace. The test was repeated three times, and the average speed in meters per second was used for analysis. Participants who normally used a cane were allowed to use it during the test.

For free-living walking measurements, participants wore a wrist-worn GENEActiv accelerometer for 9 consecutive days. This device recorded movement at a high frequency and used special software to detect walking episodes and count steps.

From this data, two key measures of walking cadence were calculated: the median walking cadence (the middle value of steps per minute during walking periods) and the 95th-percentile walking cadence (a higher step rate indicating brisk walking). The study also calculated the average number of daily steps.

The researchers additionally gathered information on factors known to affect mortality, such as:

  • age
  • sex
  • education
  • race and ethnicity
  • marital status
  • smoking
  • alcohol use
  • diet
  • body mass index (BMI)
  • hypertension
  • chronic diseases

National death records were used to identify deaths during the follow-up period.

Statistical methods were then used to estimate the risk of death associated with walking speed and cadence while accounting for other risk factors.

The models were adjusted step-by-step: starting with no adjustments, then adding sociodemographic factors, behavioral factors, and finally health-related factors.

The ability of walking measures to predict mortality was assessed using a statistical measure.

The study also examined differences between males and females.

The researchers additionally conducted analyses to test how well the results held up when they excluded early deaths, looked at subgroups by age and health status, and used different definitions of walking cadence.

Faster walking speed linked to 23% lower risk of death

During a median follow-up of nearly 12 years, 739 participants died.

The average clinical walking speed of participants was 1.2 meters per second. The median walking cadence during daily life was about 75 steps per minute, and the 95th percentile walking cadence was about 112 steps per minute.

The study found only a weak correlation between clinical walking speed and free-living walking cadence, meaning the two measures do not strongly reflect the same walking behavior. However, both measures were independently associated with a lower risk of death.

After adjusting for age, sex, lifestyle, and health factors, for every standard increase in clinical walking speed, the risk of death was 23% lower.

Similarly, higher median and 95th-percentile walking cadences were associated with 17% and 18% lower risks of death, respectively.

When examining how well these walking measures predicted mortality, each on its own showed modest predictive ability.

Adding clinical walking speed to a model with other known risk factors slightly improved mortality prediction, while adding free-living walking cadence also improved prediction, albeit to a similar degree to that of clinical walking speed.

Combining clinical walking speed with free-living walking parameters provided a small boost in predictive ability, but the effect was not significant after accounting for detailed health factors.

The study also found that walking speed and cadence had similar predictive power for both men and women, although the overall prediction models performed slightly better for women.

Several sensitivity analyses supported the main findings. Results were consistent when excluding early deaths, focusing on healthier or older participants, and using different ways to define walking cadence.

Overall, this study suggests that clinical walking speed tests and free-living walking cadence measurements from wrist-worn devices are both useful in predicting all-cause mortality risk. They provide similar information but reflect different aspects of walking behavior.

The choice between methods may depend on the purpose of the assessment, available resources, and the context in which the measurement is taken.

Faster walkers may live longer because they are healthier

Neha Sabharwal, PT, DPT, a physical therapist and director of rehabilitation specializing in geriatric mobility and functional decline at Vintage Faire Nursing and Rehabilitation Center in Modesto, CA, said that walking speed helps predict longevity because it’s an indicator of someone’s overall health.

“Walking is something most of us do without thinking about it, but it requires several body systems to work together,” she told Healthline. “Your heart and lungs have to keep up, your muscles need to produce enough force, and your brain has to coordinate each step while keeping you balanced.”

Sabharwal, who wasn’t involved in the study, said that in her work, she pays attention to when someone starts walking more slowly than before.

“It is easy to dismiss that change as part of getting older, but there may be something else going on,” she said. This could include muscle weakness, pain, reduced endurance, balance problems, or underlying medical conditions.

According to Sabharwal, the current study is valuable because it suggests that useful information about people’s movement can be obtained outside a clinical setting.

However, she cautioned against misinterpreting the findings to mean that simply walking faster will make you live longer.

“People who naturally walk faster may already have better underlying health,” said Sabharwal.

“I would be less concerned about whether someone walks faster than their neighbor and more interested in whether they are walking slower than they did six months ago,” she said.

What to do if your walking speed slows

Theodore Strange, MD, chairman of medicine at Northwell Staten Island University Hospital and senior vice president of the Metro Market Region at Northwell Health, shared some insights for those who may notice their walking speed has declined. Strange wasn’t involved in the study.

“I mean, if it’s slowing down because a week or two ago, you were walking up a flight of steps with no problem, and now, you’re having some shortness of breath, I think it should be evaluated,” he told Healthline.

Strange added that if you’re experiencing aches or pains that weren’t there before, you should consult a healthcare professional, as they can make exercise more difficult.

It’s also essential to manage any chronic diseases that you have so that you can exercise as well as possible within the constraints of your condition, he said.

“And I think slow and steady wins the race here,” said Strange. He suggests monitoring your walking with a device like a pedometer and progressively increasing your steps.

“Everybody talks about 10,000 steps a day. I think the most recent studies show probably 7,000 to 8,000 is adequate,” he said. You can start where you are and work your way up gradually.

Strange further noted that physical therapy can sometimes be helpful if you have issues with gait mechanics, balance, or strength. Additionally, you might benefit from exercise that is tailored to your condition, for example, if you’ve had a stroke.

Finally, Strange advised stretching before and after exercise to keep your muscles moving.

“I go back to adequate nutrition and hydration being important,” he said. You want to make sure you are adequately supporting your body’s ability to exercise, said Strange.

PCBA Burn-In Testing: What to Log When an Embedded Board Runs Overnight

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Consider a hypothetical embedded terminal left running overnight. In the morning, the display is on and the application responds. That looks encouraging, until the logs show that the application restarted during the run. Did the board remain stable, or did automatic recovery hide a failure?

For developers, PCBA reliability testing includes deciding what evidence the software should produce while the hardware is under test. A printed circuit board assembly can remain powered without completing the operations its application depends on. The test needs a way to distinguish those states.

Burn-in is a screening activity in which hardware operates for an extended period, often at elevated temperature, to expose latent defects. The electrical and environmental conditions need their own engineering justification; an overnight run at room temperature does not reproduce an elevated-temperature profile. The software has a narrower job: run the planned workload and preserve evidence of interruptions.

Define what one completed test cycle means

For the terminal, suppose normal operation includes updating a display, storing a local record, and exchanging data over Ethernet. These are proposed test activities for this example, not results from a particular board.

A CPU workload alone leaves some of that path untested. Give the test application a repeatable cycle with observable results. It might generate a record, write it to a dedicated test area, read it back, compare the contents, and send a sequence-numbered message to a test server. Specify whether the storage check must include durable persistence; a read served from cache tests a different path.

Keep storage activity bounded by the test plan. Writing continuously at the maximum possible rate changes both the workload and the wear imposed on flash. Also distinguish a rendering operation reported as complete from an image verified at the panel. Software counters cannot prove that the physical display remained correct.

Count completed cycles and record the first failed operation. A rising counter is useful only if it advances after the required checks succeed. A timer that increments independently of the work can keep reporting progress after the worker has stalled.

Observe the board from another machine

An external collector can preserve evidence when the board loses power or its application stops. Have it record incoming progress messages, missed-message intervals, reconnects, and test-session identifiers.

Use a monotonic clock to measure elapsed intervals within each running system. Add wall-clock timestamps for correlation with instruments, and document clock synchronization where timing matters. After a board restart, begin a new timing segment instead of assuming its local elapsed time continues across boots.

A heartbeat should carry more than an indication that a process exists. Include the last completed cycle, current phase, and recent error counts. This lets the collector distinguish an active test from a responsive application whose storage worker has stopped progressing.

Loss of communication is still ambiguous. It could involve the board, cable, switch, server, or collector. Record it as an observation until other evidence supports a cause. External collection also cannot guarantee delivery of the final message before a sudden failure.

Keep recovery visible

A watchdog or service supervisor can return a failed system to operation. That is useful product behavior, but the recovery must remain visible in the test record.

Record application startup separately from system boot. Use a per-launch identifier for the test process and a boot identifier where the platform provides one. Correlate them with serial-console output or other independent observations. A changed process identifier alone does not establish that the whole board rebooted.

If the platform exposes a reset cause, capture it early and verify how that field behaves on the selected hardware and firmware. Do not assume every watchdog driver reports the same information.

Define what happens after recovery. The collector might retain the failed session and open another session for diagnostic continuation. Whether that continuation satisfies acceptance requirements is a separate decision. Automatic restart should never silently turn an interrupted run into an uninterrupted pass.

Put electrical and thermal context beside software events

When a timeout occurs, the surrounding conditions matter. Preserve the workload phase, connected peripherals, cooling arrangement, software build, and available instrument readings with the event.

Measure supply voltage at the board input when checking for lead-related voltage drop. A bench supply's output reading may not represent the voltage reaching the connector under load. Similarly, label temperature readings by their source: chamber air, package surface, board location, or an internal sensor. Those readings are not interchangeable.

Sampling also limits the conclusion. A slow current log can show a sustained change while missing a brief disturbance. Choose instruments and acquisition settings for the event being investigated; an uneventful low-rate trace cannot rule out every transient.

Make each failure record useful for the next investigation

The collector should retain enough context to guide the next measurement. For the example terminal, these are useful starting points:

Observation Evidence to preserve Question to investigate Progress counter stops Last completed cycle, active phase, application messages Which operation stopped completing? Application relaunches Launch identifier, boot identifier, exit information Did the process restart or the board reboot? Received data differs Expected payload, actual payload, sequence number Where did the mismatch first appear? Board disconnects Link events, collector status, console output, supply trace Is the interruption local to the board?

Avoid assigning a root cause from a single symptom. For example, a network timeout during a storage operation establishes timing, not that storage caused the timeout. Preserve the original run, then change one relevant condition for the diagnostic rerun.

Check the observer before trusting the result

Before starting a batch, deliberately stop the test worker and confirm that the collector detects stalled progress. Restart the application and verify that the new session is visible. Disconnect the communication path and check that the missing interval remains in the record after reconnection.

At the end, reconcile requested exposure, observed runtime, completed work, and interruptions. A passing software workload is evidence for the functions and conditions exercised. It does not replace environmental qualification or establish service life. Its practical value is that a board can no longer appear stable simply because the application recovered before anyone looked.

Two-Factor Authentication: Why SMS Codes Are the Weakest Option

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Short answer: SMS codes are the weakest common form of two-factor authentication because a text message can be redirected to a scammer through a SIM swap, where your phone number is moved to their SIM card. Authenticator apps, security keys and passkeys are stronger, but SMS 2FA is still far safer than a password alone.

Two-factor authentication is one of the single most effective things anyone can do to protect an online account, and turning it on for anything important is genuinely good advice. But not every 2FA method offers the same level of protection, and the most common one — a code sent by text message — happens to be the weakest widely available option.

How much does a second factor help? Microsoft has reported that multi-factor authentication can block over 99.9 percent of account compromise attacks, because a stolen or cracked password on its own is no longer enough to sign in.

How do the main 2FA methods compare?

  • SMS codes — a one-time code sent by text message, widely supported but the weakest method available
  • Authenticator apps — generate a new time-based code on your device every 30 seconds, without needing a phone signal
  • Hardware security keys — a physical device you tap or plug in, resistant to phishing since the credential is tied to the specific site
  • Passkeys — a newer, passwordless approach that also functions as strong two-factor-style protection by design

Why are SMS codes the weakest 2FA option?

SMS messages aren't encrypted the way app-based codes or hardware keys are, and phone numbers can be hijacked through SIM swap attacks — where an attacker convinces a mobile carrier to move your number to a SIM card they control, then receives your codes instead of you. This doesn't require access to your actual phone, just a successful social-engineering call to a carrier's support line.

What should you switch to instead of SMS?

Most major services now support authenticator apps as an alternative to SMS, and it's usually a quick change in account security settings. For accounts that matter most — email, banking, primary cloud storage — a hardware security key or passkey offers noticeably stronger protection, since the underlying credential can't be phished the same way a six-digit code can.

Is SMS 2FA still better than no 2FA?

Absolutely. The comparison here is between different levels of good, not between good and bad. If SMS is the only option a service offers, using it is still far better than leaving an account protected by a password alone. But where a stronger option exists, it's worth the few extra minutes to switch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Isn't SMS-based 2FA still much better than no 2FA at all?

Yes, significantly better. The concern with SMS codes is relative to other 2FA methods, not compared to having no second factor at all — any 2FA meaningfully reduces the risk of a stolen password being enough to break into an account.

What exactly is a SIM swap attack?

It's when an attacker convinces your mobile carrier to transfer your phone number to a SIM card they control, often through social engineering or a compromised support agent. Once that happens, they receive your SMS codes instead of you, without needing physical access to your phone.

Are authenticator apps hard to set up?

Not really — most involve scanning a QR code once during setup, after which the app generates a new code every 30 seconds without needing an internet or phone signal connection at all, since the codes are generated locally on the device.

What's the strongest 2FA option available today?

Those are different observations. If the adapter merges them, the next person debugging the request has to guess what actually happened.

Save a small diagnostic record before choosing a remedy

For an error response, start with four fields:

  • The HTTP status returned by your HTTP client.
  • A numeric error code found in the JSON, if present.
  • A server request ID, when available.
  • Whether the response body was a JSON object, another JSON value, invalid JSON, or too large for this example to parse.

RouterBase's error reference documents both nested and top-level JSON error shapes, along with x-request-id for correlating a request. Keep the transport status in its own field rather than replacing it with a value from the body.

A Python example you can run without calling a model

This function summarizes response data you supply. It makes no network requests and needs no API key. The example response is synthetic.

The output keeps http_status and json_numeric_code as separate values. In the synthetic example both are 402. If you supply HTTP 200 and a body containing {"code":402}, it preserves both values; that is an adapter test case, not a claim that RouterBase returns that combination.

The function deliberately omits error messages, prompts, request bodies, and authorization headers. It accepts only numeric codes in the 100–599 range and UUID-shaped request IDs. String error categories are left out of this small example. A missing value remains None; it is not replaced with a guessed cause.

This is a limited diagnostic summary, not a general redaction system or a complete response validator. Pass the actual integer status from your HTTP client, string header names and a text body. Bound the response read in your HTTP client too: the length check here happens after the body has reached the function.

UV Bounds Do Not Prove Independent Texture Regions

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AI assistance disclosure: AI assisted with research, drafting, and execution of the Python example below. The results come from constructed UV fixtures, not an imported vehicle asset or a browser-rendering experiment.

A UV range check answers a useful but narrow question: are these coordinates inside the intended texture tile?

It does not answer whether two mesh faces can be textured independently. Even adding “no zero-area triangles” and “total UV triangle area equals one” leaves a simple failure case.

Here is a reproducible counterexample. Two layouts both contain two nondegenerate UV triangles, span exactly [0, 1] × [0, 1], and have a summed triangle area of 1. One covers the square without interior overlap. The other maps both faces onto the same half of it.

That distinction matters when a mesh will receive separate labels, decals, or artwork. A clean range report is useful evidence. It is not a certificate of region isolation.

Two layouts that pass the same simple checks

Define these triangles in normalized UV space:

lower = (0,0), (1,0), (0,1) upper = (1,1), (0,1), (1,0)

The partitioned layout uses lower once and upper once. The stacked layout uses lower twice, assigning the same UV triangle to two faces. The example concerns UV assignments; it does not create a mesh file or specify physical dimensions.

The signed double area of a triangle is the two-dimensional cross product:

(bx - ax) * (cy - ay) - (by - ay) * (cx - ax)

Take its absolute value and divide by two for ordinary area. Each triangle here has area 1/2.

A third fixture reverses the vertex order of the second stacked triangle. This checks that the duplicate diagnostic below does not depend on winding order.

Area sum counts repeated coverage

For the partitioned layout, the two triangle interiors are disjoint and their union is the square. They share a diagonal, which has zero area.

For either stacked layout, both triangles occupy exactly the same lower half-square. Adding their areas counts that region twice. The upper half remains unused.

These are exact geometric consequences of the listed coordinates:

Layout Union area Area covered by both triangles Uncovered area in the unit square Partitioned 1 0 0 Either stacked fixture 1/2 1/2 1/2

This second table is derived directly from the complementary or identical half-squares. The code below does not implement a general polygon-union or intersection algorithm.

The useful observation is that sum(triangle areas) == 1 is compatible with both complete coverage and substantial overlap. An area sum is not a union-area calculation. Checking that sum more precisely would not resolve the ambiguity.

Reproduce the checks with the Python standard library

Save and run the following with Python 3. It uses integer-coordinate fixtures and exact rational arithmetic for their areas. No renderer, model download, or third-party package is required.

from collections import Counter from fractions import Fraction def twice_area(t): (ax, ay), (bx, by), (cx, cy) = t return (bx-ax)*(cy-ay) - (by-ay)*(cx-ax) def audit(triangles): points = [p for t in triangles for p in t] keys = Counter(tuple(sorted(t)) for t in triangles) return { "bounds": [min(p[0] for p in points), min(p[1] for p in points), max(p[0] for p in points), max(p[1] for p in points)], "in_unit_square": all(0 <= x <= 1 and 0 <= y <= 1 for x, y in points), "sum_area": sum((abs(Fraction(twice_area(t), 2)) for t in triangles), Fraction()), "degenerate_count": sum(twice_area(t) == 0 for t in triangles), "duplicate_pairs": sum(n*(n-1)//2 for n in keys.values()), } lower = ((0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1)) upper = ((1, 1), (0, 1), (1, 0)) fixtures = {"partitioned": [lower, upper], "stacked": [lower, lower], "stacked_reversed": [lower, tuple(reversed(lower))]} for name, triangles in fixtures.items(): r = audit(triangles) assert r["bounds"] == [0, 0, 1, 1] assert r["in_unit_square"] and r["sum_area"] == 1 assert r["degenerate_count"] == 0 assert r["duplicate_pairs"] == (0 if name == "partitioned" else 1) print(name, r)

The assertions check the reported bounds, range, total area, degeneracy, and duplicate-pair counts. The printed sum_area is Fraction(1, 1) in every row. The duplicate count is 0, 1, and 1 respectively.

Sorting each triangle's three coordinate pairs creates an order-independent key. This is deliberately a small diagnostic, not an overlap detector for arbitrary assets. It misses partial intersections, near-equal floating-point coordinates, and two different triangulations of the same covered region. It also does not interpret a glTF index buffer, a material, a texture transform, or a UV channel.

Do not round imported UVs until they match these keys and treat the result as a rigorous intersection test. A real asset audit needs an explicit numerical tolerance policy and a suitable geometry method.

Why this is an editing requirement, not a glTF validity rule

The glTF 2.0 specification defines texture coordinates as TEXCOORD_n attributes. Its wrapping section describes how samplers handle coordinates outside the normalized image interval.

A unit-square rule can be an application requirement for a particular atlas. It should not be presented as a universal glTF requirement. Similarly, sharing texture space may be intentional when multiple faces should use the same artwork.

The problem appears when the requested operation requires different artwork on those faces. If they use the same texture binding and the same UV region, editing that shared texture region cannot independently target only one of them through UV coordinates alone. That conclusion follows from the mapping; this example does not measure the behavior of a particular editing application.

Blender's UV selection documentation lists a Select Overlap operation. That is a relevant inspection aid, but the documentation reference is not a claim that this example was opened or tested in Blender.

A report that is more useful than one green checkbox

For an asset intended for local artwork placement, record several separate findings:

  1. Which coordinates are actually used. Record the target material, texture, and UV set. In Three.js, Texture exposes the coordinate channel and UV transformation matrix. Inspect the binding used by the operation rather than assuming the first UV set is the relevant one.
  2. Whether the intended regions share texture space. Check interior overlap between faces that must be independent. Shared boundaries and positive-area overlap are different cases. Record intentional sharing explicitly.
  3. Which faces belong to each editable region. Non-overlapping UVs still do not identify a door, a hood, or a rear panel. That requires a separate semantic assignment and inspection of the corresponding geometry.
  4. What the actual editing path does. Exercise a distinctive, asymmetric label or image on the intended region and inspect other regions and multiple views. Test intermediate and completed states if they follow different rendering paths.

Those are proposed acceptance steps, not results produced by this numerical example. Filtering near island borders, padding, texture resolution, orientation, distortion, and physical scale also remain separate concerns. Passing the checks here establishes none of them.

The next time an asset report says “UVs are valid,” ask what was checked. “All coordinates are in the intended tile” is a clear and useful result. “The intended faces can receive independent artwork” is a different claim, requiring different evidence.

Bitcoin Core 32.1 Arrives With Privacy Fixes and New Compatibility Checks

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Bitcoin Core 32.1 introduces network, privacy and interface changes, while 31.2 and 30.4 provide maintenance updates. Operators need to check integrations as well as version numbers.

Bitcoin Core’s late-October releases give node operators several upgrade paths, but the details deserve more attention than the version labels. The 32.1 release notes explain that 32.0 was tagged but never fully announced or released because of last-minute issues.

That makes 32.1 the relevant new major-version release to examine. Separate 31.2 and 30.4 updates address maintained earlier branches, with their own change lists and compatibility considerations.

Privacy and relay behavior are part of the update

The 32.1 release notes describe a fix for a possible IP-address exposure in a particular private-broadcast fallback configuration. They also describe changes to outgoing transaction relay limits and additional controls around private-broadcast queues.

These are specific implementation changes, not a claim that all prior Bitcoin transactions exposed their senders or that the new release guarantees anonymity. The circumstances described in the notes matter when assessing who is affected.

Node software manages connections and message handling as well as checking blockchain rules. Improvements to those functions can therefore be useful even when they do not create a new transaction type or alter Bitcoin’s supply.

Applications need to inspect interface changes

The same release changes some defaults and removes previously deprecated fields from certain interfaces unless compatibility options are requested. An application that assumes a field always exists can fail even when the node itself starts and synchronizes correctly.

For an exchange, wallet service or payment processor, an upgrade is consequently an integration task. The important question is whether the software calling the node still interprets responses correctly and handles the outputs it receives.

A version number alone cannot answer that question. The exact interfaces used by the application determine which release-note items need testing, while unrelated changes may have no effect on that deployment.

Earlier branches have separate maintenance releases

Bitcoin Core also publishes release notes for 31.2 and 30.4. Both describe bug fixes and performance improvements, providing a route for operators who need to maintain an existing major-version environment while planning a broader migration.

That does not make every branch interchangeable. Support status, available fixes and application compatibility should be checked together. Remaining on an old branch indefinitely is different from using a maintained branch as part of a deliberate upgrade plan.

The project’s lifecycle information is especially relevant to that distinction. A release once considered stable can eventually move beyond the period in which its maintainers expect to provide further updates.

Operational verification is the practical milestone

A successful upgrade should leave the node on the intended version, connected to the correct network and serving its dependent applications as expected. Merely installing a binary does not establish those outcomes.

Operators also need to preserve the information required to recover their service if an integration issue appears. Where a node manages wallet data, that includes understanding the compatibility of the wallet format before attempting any rollback. The release notes, not assumptions based on a previous upgrade, should guide that assessment.

For ordinary Bitcoin holders who do not operate their own node, these releases are infrastructure news rather than a requirement to move coins. A node-software update is not a token migration.

The significance of 32.1 lies in the accumulated work behind reliable validation, private transaction handling and maintainable interfaces. Those improvements are less visible than a price move, but they affect the systems on which wallets, exchanges and independent verification depend.

Don't make these time-change mistakes when planning travel

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A few years ago, on a damp evening in late March in London, I learned the hard way that countries don't all change their clocks for daylight saving time simultaneously. After ordering a glass of white wine at a cozy bar in Bermondsey, I mindlessly scrolled on my phone while the bartenders chatted about the clocks jumping forward that coming Sunday. I nearly spit out my Chablis.

Back home in New York City, I had already skipped ahead one hour on the second Sunday in March. As I frantically Googled, I learned that in the United Kingdom, the clocks go forward one hour on the last Sunday in March (and back one hour on the last Sunday in October).

Don't be like me and lose two hours of sleep in one month. When you travel, remember the following to avoid daylight saving time-related snafus and other time zone mistakes — and if you're in the U.S., most states fell back one hour on Sunday, Nov. 1.

Daylight saving time happens on different days around the world — or not at all

Egypt changes its clocks on the last Friday in April and the last Thursday in October. KITTI BOONNITROD/GETTY IMAGES

The U.K. isn't the only country that changes its clocks on a different weekend than we do in the U.S. For example, most European countries follow the same calendar as the U.K., moving clocks forward one hour on the last Sunday in March and back one hour on the last Sunday in October.

Generally, when traveling in the spring and fall, it's always best to check when the country you're going to falls forward or backward — and also whether that country observes daylight saving time at all. In fact, according to the Pew Research Center, about 66% of the world doesn't.

Though daylight saving time is commonly observed in North America and Europe, many non-European Union countries in Europe — including Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Georgia, Iceland, Russia and Turkey — stay on standard time year-round.

In 2023, Egypt became the only country in Africa to change its clocks when it reintroduced daylight saving time for the first time in seven years. Now, each year, its clocks move forward one hour on the last Friday in April and backward one hour on the last Thursday in October.

Not all states within the same country follow daylight saving time

Arizona doesn't observe daylight saving time. KONOPLYTSKA/GETTY IMAGES

It's also worth double-checking these things even if you're traveling within the U.S. In 48 states, daylight saving time runs from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November. Arizona and Hawaii are the outliers that stay on standard time year-round. While it's less of an issue on an island like Hawaii, Arizona shares enough borders with other states that do observe daylight saving time that it can be problematic.

"Always double-check timing when flying in/out and on road trips through the state," Becky Blaine, TPG's newsletter managing editor and former Arizona resident, said. Otherwise, you can risk missing a flight or a reservation.

To keep it simple, remember that Arizona is in the Pacific Daylight Time zone from mid-March to early November, and the rest of the year it's in the same time zone as other states in Mountain Standard Time.

It's a similar situation in Canada. Generally, Canada follows the same daylight saving schedule as the U.S., but most of Saskatchewan, the Yukon and some parts of Quebec and British Columbia stay on standard time year-round.

In Australia, daylight saving time starts the first Sunday in October and ends the first Sunday in April in the Australian Capital Territory, New South Wales, South Australia, Tasmania and Victoria; meanwhile, the Northern Territory, Queensland and Western Australia don't observe it.

Private island resorts sometimes create their own time zones

Many private island resorts in the Maldives are in a different time zone from the airport in the capital. CAVAN IMAGES/KONSTANTIN TRUBAVIN/GETTY IMAGES

Outside of daylight saving time changes, there are some general time zone issues to be aware of while traveling.

In countries as large as the U.S., Canada and Australia, it's easy to see why there are so many time zones. But Eric Rosen, TPG's director of travel content, also pointed out that the airport in the Maldives is in a different time zone than some resorts in the tiny island nation.

Technically, the Maldives has only one official time zone: Maldives Time, which is five hours ahead of Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). However, some of the archipelago's private island resorts add an hour to Maldives Time to maximize their guests' evening light.

For example, The St. Regis Maldives Vommuli Resort sets its clock one hour ahead of the official clock to what it calls "Island Time," but also provides guests with butlers who coordinate transfers to and from the airport so nobody accidentally misses a flight.

Before you go to the Maldives, be sure to check if your resort also does this.

Some places have half-hour time zones

The Newfoundland Time Zone is 30 minutes ahead of the Atlantic Time Zone in Canada. FEDEVPHOTO/GETTY IMAGES

Due to its geographic location, the Canadian island of Newfoundland is 90 minutes ahead of Eastern Time instead of just 60 minutes ahead, as in the rest of Atlantic Canada. On a July trip, Nick Ewen, TPG's editor-in-chief, found this challenging for calendar purposes.

While traveling in Newfoundland, he received an Outlook invitation to a 7:30 p.m. dinner meeting the following week and accepted it without much thought. However, the actual dinner back home in the U.S. started at 6 p.m. — a detail he completely missed until the reminder popped up 15 minutes before. At that point, he realized he would be late.

Other places offset from UTC by 30 minutes include India, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, Iran and some states in Australia. Want to be confused even more? Nepal's time zone is offset by 45 minutes due to its geography.

Mind your digital calendars

Even when not dealing with half-hour time zones, it can be easy to make similar mistakes when adding events back home to your digital calendar while traveling in a different time zone.

"Last year, I brought my middle son to a birthday party. We were running a few minutes late, but when we arrived, it seemed like the party was almost over," Tarah Chieffi, a senior writer at TPG, said. "While the kids were eating cake, I double-checked my calendar to make sure we had gotten the time right, and it seemed like we had. But then I went to check the invitation, and we had, in fact, arrived one hour late."

Her mistake? When she entered the party into her calendar while traveling, her calendar app defaulted to the time zone she was in and showed her the incorrect time back home.

"Now, I always toggle the time zone to my home time zone if I enter appointments into my calendar while I'm traveling," she said.

It's possible to add time zones to Outlook to avoid mistakes when creating calendar invites while traveling. LYNDSEY MATTHEWS/THE POINTS GUY

Another trick?

"Outlook lets you have up to three time zones showing on the calendar," Madison Blancaflor, a managing editor at TPG, said.

Go to "Settings," then "Calendar," then "Time zones." Then, check the box for "Show second time zone in Day and Week view" underneath your default time zone. If you need a third one, you can also check that box.

Cruise ships often sail through multiple time zones

It's also important to remember that it's possible to quickly encounter multiple time zones on cruises — especially on transoceanic cruises, where you'll change time zones daily or almost daily. And if you're on a cruise traversing the Pacific Ocean, you could lose an entire day if you cross the international date line, like Clint Henderson, TPG's principal spokesperson, did when he sailed from Seward, Alaska, to Tokyo last year.

Even on shorter cruises, check if you'll have to change time zones. When I was on a five-day Alaska cruise earlier this summer, I changed time zones twice as we moved in and out of Alaska Daylight Time on a round-trip sailing from Vancouver (which was one hour ahead, on Pacific Daylight Time).

Cruises will remind guests of time zone changes each night if needed. CLINT HENDERSON/THE POINTS GUY

Cabin stewards usually leave notes on your bed at turndown to remind you of upcoming time changes. Clint said he had to set his clocks back between one and two hours each night on his cruise. But you can't always rely on your cellphone to display the correct time when it's in airplane mode and using satellite-based internet. Bring an analog watch or an alarm clock to ground you if you're unsure whether your device will update properly.

However, some cruise ships may ignore time changes at select ports. "I've had that happen on Royal Caribbean ships in the Caribbean, for example, to avoid the ship time changing three days in a row," said Christine Gallipeau, a senior editor on TPG's points and loyalty team. "So if you're doing shore excursions, make sure you know what time the ship is following, as it may not necessarily match the local time."

Bottom line

When traveling, don't assume every country or state follows the same daylight-saving-time rules and schedules as where you live, and be mindful of time zone anomalies. Losing or gaining an unexpected hour can lead to lost sleep, arriving late to an event and, worst of all, missed flights.

The anti-Skydance: How a Nike billionaire’s backing lets Laika spend a reported $100 million on ‘Wildwood’

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Laika, the Oregon studio behind Coraline and ParaNorman, has something close to a hit on its hands, at least by its own standards. Wildwood, the company’s sixth feature and, like all of its films, made entirely in stop-motion animation, opened in third place on Oct. 23 with roughly $12.3 million at the domestic box office. Directed by Laika president and CEO Travis Knight, the film held up over Halloween weekend, slipping just 39% to an estimated $7.5 million and lifting its domestic total to $25.9 million, according to the Associated Press. It again finished third, behind A24’s Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything and DC Studios’ Clayface.

In an animated landscape dominated by billion-dollar sequels like Toy Story 5 and The Super Mario Galaxy Movie, those are modest numbers. For Laika, they’re a meaningful rebound. Its previous film, 2019’s Missing Link, opened to just $5.9 million and finished with $16.6 million domestically. Wildwood has already passed that film’s entire global run, reaching nearly $36 million worldwide as it rolls out across dozens of international markets, per Deadline.

Still, the film is a long way from profitable. Laika hasn’t disclosed Wildwood’s budget, but it has been widely reported at about $100 million, more than Illumination spent on Minions & Monsters ($85 million) or DreamWorks spent on Forgotten Island ($80 million), an acclaimed original that opened to a similar $13 million last month.

Laika’s track record makes the math harder. Its domestic grosses have shrunk with nearly every release since Coraline took in $75 million in 2009: ParaNorman earned $56 million in 2012, The Boxtrolls $50.8 million in 2014, and Kubo and the Two Strings $48 million in 2016, according to Cartoon Brew, before Missing Link bottomed out at $16.6 million. International ticket sales and home entertainment help, but theaters typically keep around half of every ticket sold, and production budgets don’t include marketing costs. By the common industry rule of thumb that a film needs to gross about 2.5 times its production budget to break even in theaters, it’s quite possible that only Coraline, buoyed by a blockbuster 2024 rerelease, has cleared that bar. Laika is privately held and doesn’t report its finances.

How Nike money built a stop-motion studio

So how does Laika keep going? Not through side work: The studio spun off its commercial division in 2014 to focus exclusively on features, unlike peers such as Aardman Animations that still take on advertising work. Travis Knight has also directed outside the studio, including 2018’s Bumblebee and this year’s Masters of the Universe, which struggled at the box office. The real fuel keeping Laika’s puppets moving, one frame at a time, is his father: Nike co-founder Phil Knight.

Phil Knight took a stake in Laika’s predecessor, Will Vinton Studios, in 1998, around the time Travis joined the company as an animator. Knight became majority owner in 2002, and founder Will Vinton was ousted the following year, a bitter episode revisited in the documentary Claydream. The studio was renamed Laika in 2005. It brought on The Nightmare Before Christmas director Henry Selick, who directed Coraline, based on Neil Gaiman’s novella.

Laika’s first four films were distributed by Focus Features before United Artists Releasing put out Missing Link. Fathom Entertainment, a smaller distributor best known for event cinema and rereleases, is releasing Wildwood domestically, including Laika’s 2024 Coraline anniversary run, which became Fathom’s highest-grossing release in the U.S.

A small distributor and big creative leeway

That arrangement may serve the film creatively. Rated PG-13, a first for Laika, Wildwood is no breezy family outing; the MPA cites strong fantasy violence, bloody images, and suicide-related material, among other content. By contrast, Netflix and Skydance Animation’s Ray Gunn and DreamWorks’ Forgotten Island had to make trims this year to land PG ratings.

The Knight family’s wealth, roughly $26 billion by Forbes’ real-time estimate, is what lets Laika take creative and business risks like these. It’s probably also why Laika remains in Hillsboro, Oregon, in the same Portland suburbs as Nike’s Beaverton-area headquarters and more than 800 miles from Hollywood. Travis Knight, for his part, has also served on Nike’s board since 2015.

The anti-Skydance

By definition, Phil Knight bankrolling his son’s studio could fall under the heading of nepotism, a loaded word but a common practice in Hollywood. The most prominent current example is Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison, one of the world’s richest people, who personally guaranteed $40.4 billion to back his son David Ellison’s pursuit of Warner Bros. Discovery. That deal closed Oct. 6, folding Paramount and Warner Bros. into a single company now called Skydance.

Arrangements like that usually draw skepticism, but Laika tends to get a pass, perhaps because the Nike money is funding something that might not otherwise exist. Stop-motion is a painstaking and increasingly rare art form. Travis Knight began developing Wildwood, an adaptation of Colin Meloy and Carson Ellis’ novel, shortly after Coraline, and has described its road to the screen as a 16-year journey, with everything onscreen built and moved by hand. Economically, the medium rarely makes sense. Stable backing gives Laika the rare privilege of creating with less financial pressure.

By comparison, Skydance is now a media colossus whose future rests on franchise blockbusters and technology. Laika is telling original stories while keeping a distinctly human form of filmmaking alive. As studios race to adopt generative AI, Wildwood’s handmade tactility plays like a statement in defense of human artistry.

What’s next for Laika

The company is branching out. It hired former Netflix executive Matt Levin in 2023 to run a live-action division, and it is developing its first series, an adaptation of Emily St. John Mandel’s novel Exit Party, with Emma Stone and Dave McCary’s Fruit Tree. Its animation slate includes an adaptation of Susanna Clarke’s Piranesi, which Travis Knight is set to direct, and The Night Gardener, based on an original idea from Ozark creator Bill Dubuque.

Box office aside, all five of Laika’s previous features earned Oscar nominations for Best Animated Feature. With some of the year’s best reviews, Wildwood could finally win the studio its first.

XRP Ledger Reviews Two Token-Authorization Fixes Ahead of Any Validator Vote

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Two open XRP Ledger proposals address authorization checks for liquidity-pool tokens and existing trust-line balances. The changes remain under review and are not active network rules.

XRP Ledger developers are reviewing targeted changes to the way issued-token permissions are checked in payment and liquidity-pool operations. The current public code record shows two relevant open proposals, both introduced on October 27.

The work is associated with the planned fixCleanup3_5_0 amendment. It should be described as protocol development, rather than a completed upgrade or evidence that ordinary XRP transfers now require issuer approval.

Pool tokens can carry exposure to two assets

The first open proposal, number 8648, would require an account acquiring a liquidity-provider token to satisfy authorization requirements for both assets in the corresponding automated market maker.

The reasoning follows the economic position. A pool token represents a claim connected to the pool’s assets. Checking only the direct deposit route may leave other ways to acquire the same exposure, including a transfer or exchange offer.

The proposal seeks consistency across those routes. That is relevant to an issuer using permission controls because a restriction needs to hold across the ways an account can obtain exposure, not simply at the most obvious entry point.

An existing balance should not create an exemption

The second open proposal, number 8650, addresses a payment-engine check that previously applied when an unauthorized trust line had a zero balance. The proposed behavior would prevent an existing unauthorized balance from being increased through further receipts.

The code discussion distinguishes receiving an issuer’s tokens from returning an obligation in the other direction. That distinction matters: preventing a new unauthorized holding need not prevent a legitimate redemption or remediation path.

Two broader proposals linked in the discussion are closed. Reading the current open changes is therefore more reliable than treating an earlier description of four proposed fixes as the final package.

Code review comes before activation

The XRP Ledger’s amendment documentation explains that changes affecting transaction processing must first be included in software and then receive sustained validator support. The stated threshold is more than 80% support for two weeks.

An open pull request has not completed that process. Review can alter the design, a release can include or defer it, and validators can make their own activation decisions. None of those stages should be collapsed into a claim that a rule is already enforced on mainnet.

For application developers, this means testing proposed behavior separately from the assumptions used in production. An anticipated error response is not necessarily the response a live transaction will receive today.

The impact is on issued assets and integration logic

These proposals concern permissioned issued-token relationships and claims on liquidity pools. They do not turn the native XRP asset into an issuer-controlled token. Keeping those categories clear avoids a misleading impression of a network-wide identity check on all holders.

The most useful preparation for affected integrations is to map the paths through which a customer receives a restricted asset, then identify how the application displays authorization failures and redemption options. A technically correct rejection can still confuse users if the interface presents it as an unexplained transaction error.

The development record does not by itself establish that an outside attacker exploited the paths under review. It establishes a proposed tightening of protocol behavior.

The next meaningful milestones are the merged implementation, its inclusion in a release and actual amendment status. Until then, the story is about improving consistency in permission controls, with the final scope still subject to review.

With daylight saving time ending, bedtime routines are back in focus. But before trying a red-light sleep device, understand one crucial distinction: light that interferes less with melatonin isn’t necessarily light that makes you fall asleep.

That’s the caution from Funke Afolabi-Brown, MD, FAASM, a physician board-certified in sleep medicine, pediatrics, and pediatric pulmonology. She told Men’s Journal why the appeal of red light currently runs ahead of the evidence for a specific bedside gadget.

Sparing Melatonin Isn’t the Same as Boosting It

“What the research clearly supports is that red light doesn’t suppress melatonin the way blue or white light does,” Afolabi-Brown says. “Whether it actively increases melatonin is less settled.”

That distinction matters for Helight Sleep, which the manufacturer says uses 630-nanometer red light for 14 minutes, followed by a 14-minute fade to darkness.

“There’s no app, no screen, and nothing to adjust or check,” she says. However, its wavelength and timer alone don’t establish that it treats insomnia or shortens the time needed to fall asleep.

CDC-backed research adds another wrinkle: at sufficiently high levels, red light can increase alertness without suppressing melatonin. Brightness matters alongside color.

The Athlete Study Has an Important Catch

A 2012 study involving 20 female basketball players reported improvements in self-reported sleep quality and serum melatonin after two weeks of treatment.

But that experiment used 30-minute sessions of whole-body red-light irradiation. It did not test Helight’s bedside protocol. Results from one device and exposure pattern cannot automatically establish another product’s effectiveness.

“We still need a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of Helight’s finished protocol,” Afolabi-Brown says.

“I’d describe it as a promising, low-risk part of a good wind-down routine, not a treatment for a sleep disorder.”

For travelers and people with inconsistent schedules, Afolabi-Brown emphasizes familiar evening cues alongside the basics.

“The strongest signals for shifting your clock are bright light in the morning, darkness at night and consistent wake times.”

She also advises evaluation for loud snoring, gasping, persistent insomnia, or exhaustion despite sufficient sleep.

The Men’s Journal takeaway: Treat red light as an optional wind-down tool. Start with consistent sleep habits, and don’t let a soothing glow delay getting persistent symptoms checked.

Polymarket has published a more detailed account of the third-party compromise that disrupted its international platform on October 29. The update shifts the focus from whether trading had reopened to how the attack worked and how customers would be compensated.

The company’s explanation is also a reminder that an onchain application can depend on offchain website components with access to active user sessions.

The refund figure includes more than drained funds

In its October 30 postmortem, Polymarket said it was returning $3.9 million: $2.7 million associated with the attack and another $1.2 million for pause-related losses. It reported that 95% of affected wallets had been refunded in full as of that afternoon.

The company said a compromised performance-monitoring script used some active sessions to sell positions and move funds. Its response included removing the script, clearing caches, resetting sessions and temporarily stopping trading and relayer activity.

The 95% figure is a timestamped company report. It should not be presented as a live refund completion rate or as proof that every disputed customer amount has been resolved.

Website code can become part of the financial boundary

A third-party script runs because a website chooses to load it. Depending on its permissions and execution environment, that script may interact with parts of the page and the browser session that users associate with the trusted application.

Mozilla’s documentation on subresource integrity describes one defense: a browser can compare a downloaded resource against an expected cryptographic hash and refuse content that does not match. It helps separate an approved file from a changed file served through the same address.

That mechanism is useful but limited. It does not decide whether the approved code was safe, and an application still needs a sound process for reviewing updates and controlling which resources are permitted to run.

A trading pause can create a second category of loss

In a market tied to a time-sensitive outcome, stopping transactions can affect users even if their wallet was never directly drained. A position might settle during the outage, or its market value might change before trading resumes.

Compensation for those effects requires a defined reference point. An operator needs to determine the relevant timestamp, position size and valuation method, then apply the same approach consistently. Otherwise, a refund headline can conceal disagreement about which losses are included.

This is particularly important when forced sales interact with thin liquidity. The difference between a displayed quotation and an executable price can become material precisely when the platform is under stress.

The durable test is the replacement control system

Removing the malicious file closes the immediate route described in the postmortem. The longer-term question is how future code changes are verified, what privileges external scripts retain and how quickly unusual activity is detected.

There is a sensible hierarchy here. Preventing an unnecessary script from reaching a sensitive page is different from detecting bad behavior after it has already begun. Both can be useful, but they should not be treated as interchangeable protections.

Users and counterparties will also benefit from clear reconciliation of completed refunds and any remaining disputes. An incident can be operationally resolved while accounting work continues.

Polymarket’s update provides concrete information about those separate tasks. The next assessment should focus on verified compensation outcomes and the effectiveness of revised controls, without assuming that blockchain settlement alone protects a user from every component of the application used to reach it.

Trying to pin down Kevin Warsh as a hawk or a dove has been tricky with his statements and actions puzzling Wall Street.

But Robin Brooks, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution, said asking whether Warsh is a hawk or a dove is the wrong question. Instead, he views all the mixed messages from the Fed chair through a different lens, arguing the hot inflation readings can’t rationalize Warsh’s statements.

“We’re not in hawkish or dovish land. What’s happening is yield curve management, which itself is a function of how precarious the U.S. debt picture is,” Brooks wrote in a Substack post on Thursday.

That’s because the surge in Treasury yields this year threatens to worsen the already-grim outlook for U.S. debt, which is now above $40 trillion in gross terms with publicly held debt at 100% of GDP.

In fiscal year 2026, which ended in September, the federal government’s debt-interest costs topped $1 trillion, exceeding defense spending.

“The bottom line is that inflation and other macro data are secondary,” Brooks added. “The twists and turns in Fed policy are about what’s going on with long-term yields.”

He suspects the Fed and Treasury Department are in a de facto accord to keep borrowing costs stable. Brooks pointed to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s surprise announcement to buy back more long-term bonds, briefly lowering yields.

Bessent also intervened in the currency market to prop up the yen without Japan having to sell its holdings of U.S. debt, which would have lifted yields.

The actual effectiveness of these attempts at managing bond yields is a different story. Expectations for the Iran war to re-escalate have sent oil prices and yields higher. Signs that the U.S. economy is running hot have also raised inflation and rate forecasts.

But the U.S. also caught some breaks too. Fears that the AI boom, a major driver of GDP growth, will go bust have taken pressure off yields. France’s political turmoil and fiscal dysfunction have sparked worries that it will trigger a debt crisis that spreads to other parts of the eurozone.

Investors have since embraced Treasury debt again as a safe have asset, and the 10-year yield has dipped back below 5% again.

“Warsh is neither hawk nor dove,” Brooks declared. “Instead—like Bessent—he’s just trying to keep long-term yields manageable.”

Such management risks putting the U.S. on the path toward so-called fiscal dominance, where high debt and deficits override the Fed’s ability to independently manage monetary policy and fight inflation.

In fact, the Congressional Budget Office was asked to assess scenarios where yields are 150 basis points and 50 basis points higher than the watchdog’s baseline of 4.3%.

For now, nominal growth remains robust and was 5.6% in the third quarter, helped by the AI boom and high inflation.

“However, both forces are likely to prove more temporary than permanent unless AI adoption ultimately delivers substantial productivity gains,” BofA said in a note on Oct. 19. “Should that occur, concerns around fiscal sustainability would ease materially. 2026 was meant to be the Philippines’ year—yet the country remains stuck in an ‘recurring cycle of underachievement’

When the Philippines took over from Malaysia as chair of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN), expectations ran high that the country could use its one-year term to attract investment and catch up with the more successful neighbors driving the region’s growth story. 

With two months left in 2026, those hopes have been dashed. The country has been Southeast Asia’s worst-hit by high energy prices since the U.S.-Iran war began in February. 

“For decades, the Philippines has been described as a country of enormous promise. It has a young population, a large domestic market, a strategic location, and a highly capable workforce,” Ruben Carlo Asuncion, chief economist of the Union Bank of the Philippines, says. “Yet it has often struggled to convert these advantages into sustained gains in investment, productivity, and competitiveness.”

He notes that the start of 2026 brought expectations for strong growth supported by easing inflation, lower interest rates, resilient household spending, and infrastructure investment. “Instead, the economy faced a series of shocks that slowed momentum and reignited inflation pressures,” he says.

What was meant to be a breakout year, highlighted by the Philippines’ turn as ASEAN chair, has instead become a case study in how the country keeps falling short: its structural advantages are outweighed by corruption, institutional dysfunction, and energy insecurity. 

Some of the Philippines’ woes—particularly energy prices—are out of the country’s control, and may ease in 2027. In fact, the World Bank expects a rebound to 5.2% in 2027, on higher public spending and easing inflation.

Even so, Asuncion sees larger challenges ahead. “The more important question is whether the country can finally break free from its recurring cycle of underachievement.”

Middle-income status a ‘milestone, not a destination’

Peter L. U, dean of economics at the University of Asia and the Pacific in Manila, says the university’s early forecasts put GDP growth above 5% this year, similar to the World Bank’s estimates.

However, high oil prices and cuts in public spending—enacted in the wake of a billion-dollar flood control infrastructure scandal last year—have since dragged this forecast down to a meager 3.7%. 

The one bright spot is the World Bank’s reclassification of the Philippines as an upper-middle-income economy this year. Yet Asuncion calls it a “milestone, not a destination.”

“Many countries reach middle-income status. Far fewer successfully make the transition to becoming high-income economies…the challenge for the Philippines is no longer how to achieve growth, but how to sustain and improve the quality of that growth,” Asuncion says.

The Philippines’ sluggishness is a contrast to Vietnam, the other Southeast Asian country to get upper-middle-income status this year. Vietnam is expected to grow 7.8% in 2026, according to forecasts from the Asian Development Bank. Exports are also booming, reaching about $375 billion in the first eight months of the year. The Philippines, by comparison, just exported $64 billion. 

U puts the milestone in more domestic terms.

“It’s like a child who turned 13, yet is still the same person with the same maturity they had yesterday. That child will need to continue working hard to continue learning more, acquiring more skills and virtues to become an adult,” he says. 

One of the biggest obstacles to climbing the income ladder is that investors still struggle to find projects worth backing. Steep electricity costs—some of the highest in Southeast Asia—as well as bureaucracy and corruption, also raise the cost of doing business. 

Exposing decades-old problems

The biggest drag on the Philippine economy, however, may be entirely out of its control. Continued tensions in the Middle East will keep energy prices elevated for the foreseeable future, driving inflation in the Philippines.

“Oil prices had already begun to come down but spiked again on renewed clashes around a month ago,” U says. Inflation is back at its April peak of 7.2%.

The Philippines is still absorbing sharply higher prices of fuel and fertilizer, along with a peso trading at record lows against the dollar. Those pressures feed into prices across the board, says Michael Ricafort, chief economist at the Rizal Commercial Banking Corporation.

Ricafort cautions that inflation could pick up further through second-round and pass-through effects, including higher transport costs, businesses pushing for higher prices, rising inflation expectations, and wages. The increase in Metro Manila’s daily minimum wage from 695 to 755 Philippine pesos ($10.91 to $11.85) in September could also lead to pay hikes in other parts of the country, he adds.

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, the country’s central bank, has also been hiking interest rates to rein in inflation—moves that will dampen the consumption spending that has traditionally driven the economy. On Oct. 22, the BSP raised its benchmark rate by 50 basis points to 5.5%. And more hikes could be coming: Nomura now expects the Philippine central bank to raise rates by another 75 basis points to 6.25% by February.

Yet Asuncion argues that these external events exposed the Philippines’ existing weaknesses. “Economies inevitably face external shocks. What differentiates successful economies from struggling ones is the strength of their institutions and their ability to respond effectively,” he says. “The energy shock highlighted the country’s dependence on imported fuel, while the flood control controversy reinforced concerns about governance, accountability, and the efficient use of public resources.”

U makes a similar point: the Gulf conflict and last year’s flood-control scandal mirror challenges long familiar to the Philippines.

“The Middle East conflict and the resultant oil price spikes have been recurrent going back to the 1970s. Similarly, corruption scandals in the Philippines seem to also be recurrent,” U says.

Turning the tides

With just a few months left in the year, attention is back on the Philippines to see whether it can turn things around, starting with the ASEAN Summit in November and into 2027. 

“The ASEAN Chairship has opened important economic opportunities, although many of the benefits will take time to materialise and build on initiatives already underway,” Victor Andres Manhit, president of the Manila-based consultancy group Stratbase, says. He adds that key developments include the signing of the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement expected during the summit.

While the ASEAN Chairship may have provided a platform and opened doors to raise the Philippines’ profile on the investment stage and position it as a key player in the region, “whether the Philippines walks through them depends largely on governance and execution at home,” Asuncion says.

The Philippines does have some structural advantages, despite the grim short-term outlook. “The country’s geostrategic location, abundant natural resources, large and young population, and investment opportunities remain important strengths,” Manhit says. 

Ricafort also sees opportunities in what has been touted as one of the Philippines’ greatest strengths: a young population of more than 115 million, with an average age below 25. “The Philippines has been in a demographic sweet spot since 2015, with the majority of the population already of working age,” he says.

Yet this window is also narrowing fast. U warns that declining birth rates and slipping educational quality, as well as the rise of AI, could affect sectors that rely on young workers, like the business process outsourcing industry. 

And if there’s one insight from the past year, it’s that institutions are still the the foundation of a well-functioning economy. “The lesson from 2026 is straightforward: there is no shortcut around good governance,” Asuncion says.

“That is ultimately the difference between an economy that is perpetually promising and one that finally fulfills its promise.”

From Bug Report to Production: How I Fix Bugs with AI

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Hi! I’m Tim, QA Engineer at inDrive. Some time ago, when I was updating tests for new endpoints, one of them behaved strangely. The service rejected a request, but on the next attempt it said the action had already been completed. In fact, nothing had happened.

The problem was with an idempotency key: the service reserved it before validating the request. If validation failed, the key remained reserved anyway. I prepared a fix, went through code review, and shipped the change. After the release, duplicate-key errors on the affected endpoints dropped by about 30%.

Previously, my work on a bug usually ended with a detailed report and retesting someone else's fix. Now, with Claude, I can go through the whole process by myself: understand unfamiliar code, check a hypothesis against logs, prepare a patch and tests, submit the review change, and take it through to release. Today, I independently ship an average of three to five small fixes and improvements a week.

I distinguish three levels of this approach based on my experience at inDrive, which I'll describe below. It's important to note that this is not a mandatory career path for every QA engineer, nor does it mean handing responsibility over to an agent. In this approach, AI helps investigate systems faster, but the engineer still checks the facts, assesses the risks, and takes responsibility for the result.

Level 1. A Bug Report That Lets a Developer Start Fixing Right Away

A typical bug report answers the question, “What is broken?” But the developer still has to find the cause, understand the related parts of the code, and choose a solution. To save developers time, I started adding the results of that investigation to my bug reports.

First, I ask an AI agent to trace the flow of data and find a possible cause. Then I check every conclusion in the repository: whether that piece of code actually runs in the scenario, how similar cases are handled elsewhere, and what other parts of the system the patch might affect. If a hypothesis isn't confirmed, I don't present it as a fact.

As a result, each ticket includes six sections:

  1. The problem and the conditions that trigger it;
  2. A specific proposed fix;
  3. Acceptance criteria and a regression test;
  4. Risks and solutions that look plausible at first but are actually wrong;
  5. Impact on users;
  6. References to files, lines of code, and related tickets.

This kind of report doesn't guarantee that the proposed solution is correct. But it gives the developer a verified starting point instead of a task that still needs to be investigated from scratch.

Example: Passenger Requests That Never Reached the Driver

The inDrive order service has separate ride options for an assistive device, a service animal, and a wheelchair-accessible car. When the service built its response, all three values were mistakenly read from the same field: assistiveDevice.

As a result, the driver might not see a request for an accessible car if the passenger hadn't selected an assistive device. And when the device option was selected, its description appeared under the other options as well. No tests covered this case.

Before the fix:

response.assistiveDevice = describe(order.assistiveDevice) response.serviceAnimals = describe(order.assistiveDevice) response.wheelchairCar = describe(order.assistiveDevice)

After the fix:

response.assistiveDevice = describe(order.assistiveDevice) response.serviceAnimals = describe(order.serviceAnimals) response.wheelchairCar = describe(order.wheelchairCar)

The cause was two copy-and-paste mistakes. In the ticket, I pointed to the exact location and specified the necessary changes and checks. The first available developer quickly made the fix, and the ticket was closed a couple of days later.

What matters here is not only the size of the patch but also the consequences of the bug: because of two incorrect field references, passengers who needed special ride arrangements might not get the service they required. That’s frustrating, and it had to be fixed fast.

What to Try in Your Team

Take a bug you can already reproduce:

  1. Ask the agent to trace the flow of data from the incoming request to the system's response.
  2. Ask it to separate conclusions confirmed by the code from hypotheses, and to provide references to specific files and lines.
  3. Check those findings yourself.
  4. Then add the smallest possible fix, potential side effects, and a test that should stop failing after the patch to the ticket.

In another investigation, the agent helped find an endless recursive call in an error handler: for an unknown error, the function called itself with the same arguments. This could cause a stack overflow and crash the process. I explicitly noted in the ticket that the defect had been found by reading the code, not reproduced on a running instance. It's essential to preserve that distinction.

Level 2. Prioritize Bugs Using Production Data

Even when you've found the cause, a question remains: how serious is the problem? How many requests or inDrive users does it affect? Can you confirm it not just from the code but also from how the system behaves?

For this, I analyze logs and metrics. The agent helps identify which events the relevant code writes, build queries, collect results across clusters, and group errors by cause. Where access to logging and metrics tools is available, MCP connectors are a convenient option.

But the results still need to be checked: the wrong filter or denominator can easily turn a convincing number into a false conclusion.

Example: Nearly 1.8 Million False Server Errors in One Week

While analyzing logs from the inDrive order service, I found a large number of 500 errors occurring during calls to the bidding service. To understand the causes, I used AI to build log queries and collect one week of statistics across 13 clusters.

In total, I found about 1.86 million such errors. Preliminary analysis showed that roughly 96.6% of them—about 1.79 million—were caused by clients cancelling requests.

The cause was in how errors were handled between services.

When a client closed the connection during a call to the bidding service, that service returned gRPC Canceled. The order service converted it into a generic 500 server error and wrote it to the logs.

Yet other parts of the service already handled these situations correctly: if the client closed the connection, the server did not try to send a response or log an error.

I checked whether these cancellations were caused by slow responses from the bidding service. They weren't: calls completed in milliseconds. For example, the median duration of an unsuccessful bid retrieval call was 7.5 ms, compared with 5.6 ms for a successful one.

In other words, slow responses were not the problem. The client had already stopped waiting, while the server continued to count the cancelled request as its own failure.

As a result, monitoring showed errors where the server had not actually failed. Including related scenarios, client cancellations generated several million ERROR-level log entries in a week. This noise made it harder to distinguish real failures from expected system behavior.

Based on the investigation, I proposed changing the error handling:

  1. If the client cancels a request during a call to the bidding service, don't return a 500 error or write an ERROR log entry.
  2. If the cancellation originates in the bidding service itself while the client is still waiting for a response, keep treating it as a server error.
  3. Check how the change affects event consumers, and notify the owners of dashboards and alerts about the expected drop in error counts.

According to a preliminary estimate, this change could eliminate about 96% of the 500 errors in question. That's the expected effect calculated from logs, not the result of a deployed fix. The findings also need to be reviewed with the bidding team.

This example shows why QA engineers should analyze production data. A high number of errors in monitoring doesn't necessarily mean that users experienced the same number of failures. Before assigning priority and proposing a fix, you need to understand what exactly the system measures and how it classifies errors.

Another Example: Errors When Changing a Ride's Status

While analyzing another scenario, I found that about 3% of requests to the ride-status transition endpoint failed over the course of a day. The logs showed that 90% of those errors occurred when the driver requested a status the ride was already in. And 55% of the errors involved a transition to “completed” for a ride that was already completed.

Timing data and traces pointed to repeated taps, stale screen state, and bursts of requests from an older ride screen. The next day, I repeated the analysis and got the same 90%.

We split the work into two tasks:

  1. Return success if the ride is already in the requested status.
  2. Add logging where we still lacked data—on the passenger side.

How to Verify an Agent's Findings

Before putting metrics in a ticket, I check the time period the data covers, which clusters are included, what counts as an error, and what denominator is used to calculate each percentage. Then I check whether a spike can be explained by one instance or a rare scenario and, where possible, repeat the analysis over a different period.

It's also important to distinguish three things:

  1. An observation from the logs;
  2. A hypothesis about the cause;
  3. The expected effect of a fix.

They shouldn't be presented together as one proven fact.

Level 3. Prepare a Fix, Pass Code Review, and Ship It

When the cause is clear, the risks are limited, and the change is small enough, a QA engineer can prepare the patch by himself instead of handing the task to a developer. Although AI helps write code and tests, this in no way means that a patch can be shipped without the usual engineering checks.

At inDrive, I started with simple changes reviewed by developers. Over time, I began taking on more substantial tasks. Now my process includes code analysis, testing, code review, the standard release process, and verifying the impact after release.

Example: An Idempotency Key Was Reserved Too Early

Let's return to the bug from the beginning of the article. Some endpoints reserved the request key before validating its contents. If validation failed or subsequent processing ended unsuccessfully, the key stayed reserved. The user's next attempt received a response saying an operation had been repeated, even though it hadn't happened in the first place.

In simplified form, the original sequence looked like this:

reserve(key)          // the key is reserved before validation validate(request) process(request)

After the fix:

validate(request) reserve(key)          // only a valid request reserves the key try:     process(request) onFailure:     release(key)      // the request can be retried after failure

For an operation that really has already completed, the conflict response remains. But the response now also includes an explicit reason that can be tracked in monitoring.

After the release, I checked the metrics. Duplicate-key errors on these endpoints were down by about 30%; the decrease held every day after the release and across every cluster with traffic. Median response time, the 99th percentile, and other errors showed no noticeable change. The additional load on the shared cache—about 0.01%—had been assessed before the merge and considered acceptable.

That result matters more than the fact that AI helped write a few lines of code: the fix reached production, and we could verify its expected effect.

A More Complex Example: A Bug in a Shared Authentication Library

One service was crashing at startup with an unclear error. At first, it looked like a broken response parser. By tracing execution with AI, I followed the issue beyond our service into the shared authentication library. When the auth service returned a plain-text error page, the client tried to parse it as JSON and lost the actual response status code.

While investigating the source of that error further, I found a more serious problem: a background job that was supposed to refresh keys regularly ran only once and then stopped. After the next key rotation, services using an older version of the library could keep using stale keys, causing token validation to fail.

We reproduced the scenario. The fix was reviewed by the owners of the shared library, and then a new version was released. This case shows why it's important to investigate a root cause all the way through, including beyond the service where the error first appeared.

What I Don't Delegate to AI

The agent is good at reading unfamiliar code, tracing connections between services, and drafting log queries, patches, and tests. But it can also confidently explain a cause that doesn't actually exist.

That's why I keep four decisions for myself:

  1. What has been confirmed by evidence;
  2. How broad the change should be;
  3. Which tests are needed;
  4. Whether the patch is ready to ship and what to check after release.

I also follow specific rules:

  • In each ticket, I state what was reproduced and what was found only by reading code.
  • If a conclusion turns out to be wrong, I add an explicit correction to the ticket history rather than silently replacing the text.
  • Before merging, I measure the load on shared systems.
  • I run automated tests in a test environment and separately note flaky tests and tests that were already failing.
  • I don't turn a small patch into a large refactor.
  • I send changes to shared code to the owners of dependent components.

These aren't special rules for AI. They're ordinary engineering discipline, and they become essential when a QA engineer at inDrive starts changing production code.

Where to Start

  1. You don't need a big task for your first experiment. Choose a bug you filed this week and try only Level 1: use an agent to trace the implementation flow, verify the likely cause, and add code references, a proposed fix, risks, and acceptance criteria to the bug report.
  2. Once you can verify the findings, add Level 2: take one metric from logs or monitoring and use it to confirm the scope of the problem. Don't put a percentage in the ticket until you've checked exactly what it measures.
  3. Move to Level 3 with a small, straightforward change—for example, removing a feature toggle that has been fully rolled out and is no longer needed. Prepare the patch and tests, pass code review by the code owners, and follow the usual release process. After release, make sure the system behaves as expected.

For me, the biggest change in QA work isn't that an agent can write code. I no longer have to stop at reporting a bug: I can find the cause, confirm it with data, prepare a fix, and—when the risk is acceptable—take it all the way to production. Responsibility for quality still stays with the engineer.

A new California law lets children of family vloggers make their parents take down all footage of them

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A new California law will protect the children of family vloggers once they turn 18.

Senate Bill No. 1247 gives adults whose parents, legal guardians, or family members used them in social media content while they were minors the right to demand removal of said content.

Once served a notice of removal, parents/guardians/etc have 10 days to take down violative content or edit it to remove the requester’s appearances. If those 10 days pass and the content is still up, vloggers will face penalties of $3,000 per day moving forward, plus further potential damages, injunctive relief, and attorney’s fees.

Now, to be clear, this law won’t let someone take their mom to court for posting Facebook pictures of them in an embarrassing Halloween costume (though we do think parents should more carefully consider what they post about their kid online, especially in the age of AI).

S.B. 1247 has a couple requirements that tune it to specifically target professional family vloggers.

  • First and most pertinent, vloggers have to have made money off the content involving their kid. If someone posted pictures or videos but made zero revenue, including any ad revenue, their content doesn’t come under the scope of this law. Conversely, vloggers who earned ad revenue, took brand deals, accepted tips through things like Super Chat, or sold merch based on their social presence are all now open to removal requests.
  • Second, occasional appearances in a parent’s content–even if they did make money off it–don’t count. A minor must have appeared in at least 30% of the vlogger’s content to qualify for removal request.
  • Third, this law explicitly applies to a minor’s own parents, legal guardians, or family members. It would not protect a kid who, for example, went to school with a family vlogger’s child, went to their birthday party, and ended up in a vlog posted to YouTube.

We’re guessing it could potentially apply if a parent signed a financial agreement with a family vlogger to have their kid appear in the family’s content on a regular basis, but that circumstance isn’t accounted for in S.B. 1247’s current form.

The law will also lay a mandate on social media platforms: Once it kicks in, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and all other sites “shall provide a clear and conspicuous mechanism by which a child influencer can request a vlogger” remove content featuring them, according to the bill’s text. If a platform doesn’t have a built-in mechanism, it has to be responsible for receiving removal requests, and is required to forward those requests to vloggers within three days of receipt.

Vloggers who receive removal requests and comply with them will not face any consequences. It’s only if they refuse removal or if removal takes longer than 10 days that they open themselves up to further legal action.

This law kicks in in January 2027–and while it’s only a California law, we do expect other states might enact similar protections. YouTube just turned 20 years old, so the vlogging industry has now been around long enough that thousands of kids whose parents once featured them in videos are turning 18 and gaining control over their own lives. S.B. 1247 makes sure that control is retroactive, too.

Strategy’s latest results show how far its financial profile has moved beyond the size of its Bitcoin holdings. The company reported 848,517 BTC as of October 25, alongside a quarter in which digital-asset valuation gains dominated operating income.

The October 29 release also emphasized dollar liquidity and the obligations attached to its financing securities. Together, those figures offer a more useful picture than a single profit number.

Bitcoin valuation drove the earnings headline

Strategy’s third-quarter announcement reported $20.91 billion of gains on digital assets and approximately the same amount of operating income. Net income attributable to common stockholders was $19.12 billion, with diluted earnings of $44.75 per share.

The company also reported $5.07 billion of USD assets and described those assets as covering 3.4 years of dividend and interest obligations on its stated measure. These are company-reported figures with different measurement purposes.

The holdings snapshot is dated October 25. It should not be silently treated as a live wallet balance or substituted for the September 30 financial reporting date.

An accounting gain is different from spendable cash

When the market value of an asset rises, the resulting accounting gain can increase reported income even if the asset has not been sold. That does not make the gain meaningless: it reflects a change in economic value. It does mean the income statement and cash position answer different questions.

Dividend payments, interest and operating bills ultimately require resources that can be delivered when due. A strong quarter for a treasury asset may therefore coexist with a need to manage funding carefully.

The reverse also applies. A subsequent valuation decline can weigh on earnings without establishing that an immediate cash payment of the same size is required.

Comparisons across quarters should preserve that distinction. Stronger reported profit may reflect a larger asset position, a greater price increase, or both, without saying much about the underlying software operation. A useful review therefore separates changes in the quantity of Bitcoin, changes in its valuation and cash flows generated by the rest of the business.

The financing stack matters to common shareholders

Strategy’s business has to be assessed across the securities used to finance it. The SEC’s investor education material explains the general difference between common and preferred stock, including preferred shareholders’ usual priority over common shareholders for dividends and liquidation proceeds.

That ordering helps explain why gross Bitcoin value cannot be read directly as value available to common stockholders. Each layer can have its own payment rights, conversion provisions and sensitivity to market conditions.

New financing may increase the asset base while also changing the claims against it. The relevant comparison is the resulting position for each class of investor, including dilution and ongoing costs.

Liquidity coverage deserves a stress test

A coverage period is a useful starting point, but its assumptions deserve attention. It depends on which obligations are included, how liquid the supporting assets are and whether future commitments change. It is not a promise that every possible financing need has already been funded.

For investors, a practical reading of the quarter separates three questions: how much Bitcoin the company owns, how its securities distribute gains and risks, and how it meets cash commitments through different market conditions.

The latest figures make the scale of the treasury clear. They also reinforce why evaluating Strategy requires attention to cash and capital structure alongside Bitcoin conviction. A rising asset price can improve several measures at once, while still leaving those measures conceptually distinct.
