Off Hours: How Landscape Design Star Cleve West Survives His Day Job

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

What does a top garden designer do to relax? He gardens, of course—but not exactly where you’d expect. Cleve West, winner of seven Chelsea Flower Show RHS Gold Medals, spends his free time tending to a plot that couldn’t be more different from the conceptual spaces he creates for clients. Not especially private and the opposite of chic, his patch is in a West London community garden (in the UK it is called an allotment) and has been his personal project for a decade.

Cleve’s 2011 book, Our Plot, part memoir, part manual, part state-of-the-world discourse, takes us through the highs and lows of allotment life. Meanwhile, we’ve been invited for lunch:

Photography by Andrew Montgomery.

Above: “The oven is like a hearth bringing people together,” says Cleve. “It has made me love the plot even more.”

Cleve West is the Gary Cooper of the garden design world. Modest, sincere, assured, he is admired by all. Did I mention that he can cook? Besides growing vegetables, fruit, and flowers, Cleve does a surprising amount of entertaining in the allotment that he shares with his partner, Christine Eatwell.

Above: After much experimentation, Cleve followed Kiko Denzer’s instructions in the book Build Your Own Earth Oven.

They use a variety of garden-side cooking contraptions: a grill, a gas-fired oven, and a hand-built pizza oven. “Many hours are spent not growing vegetables,” admits Cleve, shown here with his grill, picked up in Normandy, and the impressive pizza oven, which has a brick base, mud and sawdust dome, and bottles placed in the middle to stop the heat from going down.

Above: “Allotments are not always that attractive from a Chelsea judging point of view,” says Cleve. His plot, which is on the large side at over 2,700 square feet, is designed around a series of raised beds.

“Some people think that the word ‘design’ is a little too pretentious and would rather their garden ‘evolve’ over the years,” says Cleve. “But how you arrange your plot, whether you draw it on paper or not, is design.” His intriguing wild tangle—I mean design—contains a rotation of vegetables including squash, beans, leaks, artichoke, onions, and gladioli by the shed.

Above: Corn and cabbage under netting (to keep out pigeons), and, in the foreground, gauzy purple verbena bonariensis.

Cleve’s client-based designs are characterized by sculptural forms and so, too, is his allotment. Wigwams made from coppiced hazel are finished off with heavy duty chains instead of insubstantial twine. They support beans and gourds, and last for about a season.

Above: This shed, with its old corrugated iron roof and reclaimed door, is armed with a lock, which is perhaps its main point. There is no formal security in allotments so essential items are kept under lock and key.

Recycle, improvise, and modify. This is the heart of the British community garden ethos. “If I had control over all the allotments, I would ban pre-fabricated sheds,” says Cleve. “I’d urge people to make their own in the name of local distinctiveness and individuality.”

Above: Cleve, shown here, prepares bhajis, Indian vegetable fritters, from a recipe handed down from his grandmother who was born in Calcutta. Both she and Cleve’s mother were in the movies, which may explain some of Cleve’s star quality.

Cooking at the plot intensifies the enjoyment of fresh food. Basic ingredients—seasoning, spices, oil, and vinegar—are kept in one of the sheds, but key elements, such as pizza dough, are made at home and carried in. True, cooking and entertaining without electricity and hot water involves extra work, but chopping and frying onions is far preferable out of doors than in.

Above: Grilling eggplant and green tomatoes for a chutney.

Feeling inspired? Here’s Cleve’s recipe for a simple relish made on a barbecue or makeshift grill. He promises that allotment cooking “doesn’t get much easier.” Pair with naan or pita bread for a quick lunch.

Blackened Eggplant Chutney

Serves 2-4

Ingredients:

  • 1 medium eggplant
  • A handful of green tomatoes
  • Some chopped fresh chili (up to half a teaspoonful)
  • 1 clove garlic
  • Small bunch of cilantro
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Good squeeze of lemon juice

Instructions:

Put the eggplant and green tomatoes on the barbecue or open fire and char them until they are black and “all squidgy” inside. Then cut along the middle and scoop out the contents of both eggplants and tomatoes. Mash, add the rest of the ingredients, mix, and eat!

Above: Bonfires are considered anti-social in summer, but in autumn and winter they are common practice as a way to burn garden waste. Cleve suggests using these fires as a feast opportunity: roast chestnuts and freshly-dug potatoes amid the embers. Nothing will ever taste so good.

The RFEF has spread the 24 Copa del Rey first-round ties postponed after the Urda rail crash across 29 October to 13 November. Valencia, Betis and Celta will now play their ties during the November international break.

A national tragedy has stopped Spanish football. The Royal Spanish Football Federation called off every Copa del Rey first-round tie scheduled for the evening of 28 October. All other matches in competitions it organises were postponed too. The decision was taken out of respect for the victims of the rail crash in Toledo province.

A Renfe high-speed train and an Iryo high-speed train collided near Urda station. The crash killed 68 people and injured 80, Público reported. An investigating judge at the court in Orgaz has ordered the first inquiries into what caused the collision.

The shutdown reached beyond the cup. The federation also postponed the nine first-round matches of the futsal Copa de España. The futsal draw planned for 16:00 on 29 October was cancelled.

Three top-flight clubs will play during the international break

Valencia, Betis, Alavés, Celta and Levante were the top-flight clubs due in cup action that evening. Each of them must now squeeze a rearranged tie into its schedule. That extra game has to fit into an already crowded run of league and European fixtures. The timing differs sharply from club to club.

Alavés go first. Regional side Anaitasuna host them at 19:00 on Tuesday 3 November. Levante travel to CD Tarancón two days later, on Thursday 5 November.

Three clubs face a longer wait. Celta close the round away to Salamanca, with kick-off at 21:00 on Friday 13 November. Valencia and Betis are in action earlier that same week. A tie that was meant to be settled in October will now be played while national teams are away.

Mallorca face cup and league games three days apart

Mallorca’s tie at CD Alcoyano kicks off at 21:00 on the same Thursday evening, after Levante’s game at Tarancón. COPE’s list of the rearranged ties records Mallorca as a Segunda División side, not a top-flight one.

Kalshi and Polymarket US filed federal suits against Missouri and Louisiana on the same day the CFTC sued Utah. The cases push the preemption fight into two states where licensed sportsbooks and casino partners stand to lose business to untaxed competitors.

Two more states now face prediction market operators in federal court. Kalshi’s case targets Missouri, while Polymarket US went after Louisiana. The CFTC filed its own suit against Utah that same day.

Kalshi is challenging a cease-and-desist demand from Missouri. The state told it to pull its sports event contracts or get a state sports betting license. Polymarket US filed as QCX LLC. It named Louisiana’s attorney general and the Louisiana Gaming Control Board as defendants.

Both companies make the same core argument. They say federal commodities law, the Commodity Exchange Act, overrides state gambling enforcement.

Missouri treats event contracts as unlicensed sports wagering

Missouri’s position is direct. The demand to Kalshi came from the office of Attorney General Catherine Hanaway, and Kalshi was not its only target.

The office says that offering sports event contracts in Missouri counts as unlicensed sports wagering. It also holds that federal law does not override state law here. In its view, sports event contracts are not swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act. Companies “cannot repackage sports bets as event contracts” to get around Missouri law, Hanaway said.

The stakes for the licensed market are concrete. Sports wagering became legal in Missouri through Amendment 2. Oversight of the licensed books sits with the Missouri Gaming Commission.

The commission’s July 2026 revenue detail report shows the scale of that business. A prediction market selling sports contracts without a license would pay none of that tax. It would compete for the same customers as the licensed books.

Louisiana’s casino-first licensing faces a federal challenge

Louisiana’s regulated market works differently. Its licenses go first to in-state casinos. Untaxed sports contracts there would compete with those casinos and their sportsbook partners.

The Louisiana Gaming Control Board had already acted on the issue. In December 2025, it sent an advisory on prediction markets and event-based contracts. The advisory went to all sports wagering licensees and platform provider permittees.

That approach targets the licensed side of the market. Polymarket US’s suit now tests whether the state can reach the prediction market operators directly.

The federal regulator has taken a firm public line. The CFTC said Congress made it the only federal regulator responsible for overseeing prediction markets. Its Utah suit seeks to stop the state from using its own laws to shut down CFTC-registered contract markets. It followed civil enforcement actions that Utah brought in state court.

Companies cannot repackage sports bets as ‘event contracts’ to evade Missouri law.

Federal appeals courts are already split on whether state gambling law can reach these contracts. The question is moving toward the Supreme Court. Each new filing adds pressure on states and operators alike.

Missouri’s dispute reaches well beyond Kalshi. Its cease-and-desist letters remain in play for Polymarket, Robinhood, Crypto.com, Novig and Underdog. The outcome could decide whether sports contracts are shut out of Missouri and Louisiana.

Team Spirit are out of PGL Masters Bucharest 2026 before the playoffs. Natus Vincere, who knocked them out, now face a Vitality side using stand-in jL.

Team Spirit came into the deciding match as the HLTV.org world number one. The last 2-2 decider of the Swiss stage, against Natus Vincere, ended their event.

The match swung sharply. NAVI dominated the opening map on Cache. Spirit pulled level on Dust2, but it was NAVI who closed the series out on Anubis. HLTV reported that it was Spirit’s first defeat by NAVI since February 2025.

NAVI recovered from two straight losses to sink Spirit

NAVI’s run to the playoffs was uneven. They opened with 2-0 wins over G2 and FURIA, and the second map against G2 went to three overtimes. Then the form dipped.

BetBoom beat them 2-0, on Dust II 10-13 and Nuke 9-13. Vitality followed, with NAVI losing Inferno 2-13 and Dust II 9-13. That second straight defeat dropped NAVI into the 2-2 pool, where one more loss would have ended their event.

Spirit took a similar route to the decider. They lost 1-2 to MOUZ on 27 October. Spirit won Nuke 13-2 but then lost Dust2 8-13 and Mirage 14-16.

Elimination at 2-3 has a clear financial cost. Teams finishing ninth to eleventh each receive US$31,250. Each losing quarter-finalist takes US$50,000. The winners’ share is split equally between players and organisation.

NAVI were not the only late qualifiers. Nemiga beat The MongolZ 2-0 and FURIA beat Liquid 2-0 on the final group-stage day. Those three teams took the last three playoff places.

Vitality face NAVI with jL standing in

The rematch comes quickly, against the Vitality side that beat NAVI in the group stage. The other ties are Nemiga v FUT, Aurora v MOUZ and BetBoom v FURIA.

Vitality are not at full strength. The organisation announced in August that Justinas ‘jL’ Lekavicius would stand in at BLAST Open Porto and PGL Masters Bucharest.

The stakes extend beyond this event. NAVI’s tournament page calls Bucharest the penultimate major tournament before the PGL Major Singapore 2026. A deep run here would give any of the eight teams momentum heading into that event.

Juventus lost 2-0 at Genoa on Serie A Matchday 9, a third league defeat in nine games that undid the good work of the win at Lecce. Napoli, the next visitors to Turin, now have the same points total.

The defeat came on 28 October 2026, with both Genoa goals arriving before half-time. Luciano Spalletti apologised to the supporters afterwards. The result came three days after a 2-0 win at Lecce on Matchday 8. That win, built on first-half goals from Weston McKennie and Douglas Luiz, now looks like a false dawn.

Genoa needed little time to break through, and Ehizibue was the man to do it. His goal ended a 504-minute run without a Genoa goal against Juventus in Serie A, going back to an Albert Gudmundsson strike on 15 December 2023, Sportmediaset reported.

Baldanzi then exposed the Juventus defence again. Scoring and setting up a goal in one Serie A match was new for Baldanzi.

Juventus created little and could not respond after the break

Juventus’s best chance of the first half came just before the interval. Kalulu shot off target after a pass from McKennie. Spalletti sent on Edon Zhegrova and Nick Woltemade for the second half. The changes did not help, and Genoa held firm. The win moved Daniele De Rossi’s side up to 10 points.

The coach did not hide from the performance. His team were beaten in every area of the pitch and must improve physically and in their fighting spirit, Spalletti told the club’s website. In his press conference he pointed to a lack of physical impact and to lost duels.

He also noted that the level shown at Lecce had not been repeated. There had been no training session between the two matches. That short turnaround is part of his explanation.

Morning all.

Let’s start with the news that Arsenal are set to appeal Max Dowman’s red card against Fleetwood on Tuesday evening. I think that’s probably the right thing to do, because had that gone to a VAR review, I think they’d have really struggled to make a compelling case that this was violent conduct.

Was it a small retaliation? Yes. Did it meet the threshold for violent conduct? Not for me. As laid out in the rules of the game, it is defined thus: Violent conduct is when a player uses or attempts to use excessive force or brutality against an opponent when not challenging for the ball, or against a team-mate, team official, match official, spectator or any other person, regardless of whether contact is made.

So, on that basis I think Arsenal are right to appeal. They might cite other incidents to make their case. Perhaps the fact Cole Palmer received just a yellow card in Chelsea’s last game against Sp*rs for what was a much more violent action against Mateus Fernandes. Imagine if we even used one of our own players, demonstrating how Bruno Guimaraes was allowed to forearm smash Jorginho in the side of the head, but didn’t get sent off because he didn’t use his arm like a weapon (or something).

Mikel Arteta is a man who, just last week, ‘joked’ he had a collection of images to show other managers outside their techincal area 36 times in ten minutes. If you don’t think he can’t dig up a few more examples of players getting away with violent conduct, you haven’t been paying attention. Whether it’s going to be sufficient to have the red card overturned is another thing, but at the very least we should see a reduction in what is, as it stands, a three match ban. I think the red was very, very, very harsh, but to miss three games because of that is absurd and unfair, so I think we’re right to appeal.

Once again though, there’s a conversation to be had about the punishments for red cards, and how it’s kind of ridiculous that every act of violent conduct = a three game ban. Some acts are more violent than others, some really do endanger the safety of an opponent, and yet they’re all treated the same. It’s never made sense to me, and I don’t really understand how there isn’t a disciplinary panel that assesses each sending off and applies the ban after the fact based on the seriousness of the incident.

Despite his frustration in the moment, I’m sure the manager’s view of Dowman’s behaviour might well have softened on watching the replays. In the heat of the moment part of it, I’m sure, is that we currently have Christos Tzolis and Bukayo Saka sidelined while Noni Madueke missed out on the midweek game to protect an ankle knock. So, we’re very light in the wide areas right now, and while the root cause of that might lie more at the door of the Sporting Director, he would have wanted Dowman available this weekend for the trip to Anfield.

I still think Arteta’s reaction was poor, but football is a game of opinions. I’ve seen some suggest if Max is gonna play with the big boys, he’s got to be treated as an equal. Perhaps that’s fair enough, but I’ve never seen Arteta react like that to a senior player being sent off, and we’ve had plenty of red cards during his tenure. You could argue that his age is why it’s different, that we all need to learn lessons when we’re younger in ways that differ from when we’re more grown up.

For me though, as talented as he is and as excited as we are about what he might become, it’s too easy to lose sight of the fact he is just 16, and that does make it different. It was so interesting that last week, after Bukayo Saka got injured against Bayern, there were myriad conversations about how Arteta was culpable to some extent because of how much he asked of the player since his breakthrough. And in the same breath, many of the same people were saying ‘It’s time for Dowman!’. I find it a little hard to square that circle. Do you actually care about the welfare of a young player or not?

I think Arteta has generally been rightly protective of Max. He knows that exposure at this age is something he and his staff have to consider for all kinds of reasons, and his playing time this season has been limited. In this instance though, regardless of the fact it’s just a few sharp words on the sideline, he’s made this a bigger issue than it should have been, with Dowman firmly in the spotlight, and I’ll be curious to see how he addresses it in his pre-Liverpool press conference tomorrow.

The Times of London is gearing up to test ‘macro payments’

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Times of London has spent years behind a hard paywall and with roughly 680,000 loyal digital subscribers, it has little reason to regret it. Now it wants the readers who’d pay for a single guide or moment but never the whole paper. Just don’t call it micropayments. Director of subscriptions Abs Ahmed has another name for it.

He called it “macro payments,” a term he said was coined in New York while he was meeting with News Corp business units. Micropayments charge readers a little for each article. Macro payments sell something specific, such as one of the Times’ annual guides, at the moment a reader wants it. Ahmed described the work as “creating and identifying moments of value and moments of need, and monetizing that.” Where the Times can hold its own, he wants it to “effectively be able to charge a premium.”
