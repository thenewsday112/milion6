Red Light for Better Sleep? A Doctor Explains the Catch Behind the Promise

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

With daylight saving time ending, bedtime routines are back in focus. But before trying a red-light sleep device, understand one crucial distinction: light that interferes less with melatonin isn’t necessarily light that makes you fall asleep.

That’s the caution from Funke Afolabi-Brown, MD, FAASM, a physician board-certified in sleep medicine, pediatrics, and pediatric pulmonology. She told Men’s Journal why the appeal of red light currently runs ahead of the evidence for a specific bedside gadget.

Sparing Melatonin Isn’t the Same as Boosting It

“What the research clearly supports is that red light doesn’t suppress melatonin the way blue or white light does,” Afolabi-Brown says. “Whether it actively increases melatonin is less settled.”

That distinction matters for Helight Sleep, which the manufacturer says uses 630-nanometer red light for 14 minutes, followed by a 14-minute fade to darkness.

Source: Courtesy Image

“There’s no app, no screen, and nothing to adjust or check,” she says. However, its wavelength and timer alone don’t establish that it treats insomnia or shortens the time needed to fall asleep.

CDC-backed research adds another wrinkle: at sufficiently high levels, red light can increase alertness without suppressing melatonin. Brightness matters alongside color.

Related: Common Habit After Dark Linked to 30% Higher Depression Risk, Study Finds

The Athlete Study Has an Important Catch

A 2012 study involving 20 female basketball players reported improvements in self-reported sleep quality and serum melatonin after two weeks of treatment.

But that experiment used 30-minute sessions of whole-body red-light irradiation. It did not test Helight’s bedside protocol. Results from one device and exposure pattern cannot automatically establish another product’s effectiveness.

“We still need a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of Helight’s finished protocol,” Afolabi-Brown says.

“I’d describe it as a promising, low-risk part of a good wind-down routine, not a treatment for a sleep disorder.”

Related: How to Fall Asleep Faster and Stay Asleep, According to Sleep Experts

Build the Routine Before Buying the Gadget

For travelers and people with inconsistent schedules, Afolabi-Brown emphasizes familiar evening cues alongside the basics.

“The strongest signals for shifting your clock are bright light in the morning, darkness at night and consistent wake times.”

She also advises evaluation for loud snoring, gasping, persistent insomnia, or exhaustion despite sufficient sleep.

The Men’s Journal takeaway: Treat red light as an optional wind-down tool. Start with consistent sleep habits, and don’t let a soothing glow delay getting persistent symptoms checked.

A Decade Ago, a $6.3 Billion Hack Would Have Sunk Binance & BTC, Bitwise CIO Says

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Bitwise Chief Investment Officer (CIO) Matt Hougan says the Binance hack would have sunk the exchange a decade ago. The $6.3 billion theft, he argues, leaves the long-term direction of Bitcoin (BTC) unchanged.

Precedent, however, backs him on the long term but not the short term. US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) did shed $643 million the day after the theft, but the price was already dropping before the hackers hit

Did Earlier Hacks Break Bitcoin?

Mt. Gox, once the dominant Bitcoin exchange, filed for bankruptcy in February 2014 after as many as 950,000 BTC vanished. Bitcoin fell 30% that month, Bloomberg reported.

However, creditors began receiving repayments in 2024, when NBC valued the missing coins above $58 billion.

Bitfinex lost about 120,000 BTC in August 2016. Bitcoin fell 13% over two days, though part of that slide predated the announcement.

Bybit lost roughly $1.5 billion in February 2025, and its reserves fell from $10.8 billion to $6.5 billion within days.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin’s monthly performance fell to negative 13.6%, on-chain firm Glassnode says, amid a broader correction.

Four days later, daily Bitcoin ETF outflows hit a record of roughly $1 billion.

Is the Binance Hack Different From Mt. Gox?

In contrast, Binance says it has already covered $6 billion of the loss.

It restored withdrawals in phases from November 1, starting with Bitcoin.

Binance said partners froze about $3 billion, roughly half the haul, a sign Hougan reads as industry maturity.

Hougan added that five years ago, institutions had no ETFs to turn to for greater security.

The FBI has attributed the attack to North Korea, as it did with Bybit. Bitget Chief Executive Officer (CEO) Gracy Chen said North Korea was very likely behind a $350 million breach in September.

Hougan called it a traditional cybersecurity hack, not a flaw in Bitcoin’s blockchain. North Korea has been linked to Bybit, Bitget and Binance since 2025, so exchanges could remain the weak point.

Coming ETF flows will show whether Binance matches Bybit’s near-$3 billion week or fades faster.

Stack Overflow: 'Reports of Our Death are Greatly Exaggerated'

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Stack Overflow's senior VP of community says predictions of their demise "make for compelling headlines, but they confuse a change in how people access knowledge with the disappearance of the knowledge — and the company — behind it. AI has changed developer behavior. In our recently released 2026 Developer Survey, 64% of respondents said they visit Stack Overflow less frequently for simple questions. But developers also report asking more advanced questions and visiting the site to verify AI-generated answers. Stack Overflow remains a place where people can challenge assumptions, compare approaches, and validate solutions. But it's also important to understand that our public Q&A site is only one part of Stack Overflow, the company. The knowledge created on the Q&A site helps power the AI ecosystem itself. Stack Overflow's human-validated content is used to train and improve models, strengthen retrieval systems, and make AI assistants and other tools more reliable. Developers and technologists may encounter an answer through a different interface — such as their IDE — but the underlying knowledge may still have begun the same way it did in the past: with a developer sharing what they learned on Stack Overflow. We're also building for what comes next. We recently launched the beta of Stack Overflow for Agents, an API-first knowledge exchange where agents can share knowledge and verify solutions. We also brought back Developer Story, giving developers a verifiable way to showcase their expertise, accomplishments, and contributions beyond a traditional résumé. And we have much more planned and in development to give people compelling new reasons to visit the site beyond Q&A. Several years ago, we pivoted aggressively toward the enterprise. In September, we launched the next generation of Stack Internal, our governed AI context layer for companies. It captures and curates organizational knowledge, adds provenance and trust signals, and brings in subject-matter experts when validation is needed. The goal is straightforward: to make sure employees and AI agents are working from accurate, current, permission-aware information — not confidently acting on whatever context happens to be nearby. That is the thread connecting everything we do: making human knowledge useful and trustworthy, whether it is being consumed by another developer, an enterprise team, or an AI agent. Stack Overflow is not standing still, and it is not disappearing. We are evolving from a website people visit for answers into knowledge infrastructure that supports developers, companies, and AI systems wherever they work. Reports of our death are, indeed, exaggerated.
