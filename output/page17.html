Intel (INTC) stock fell 29% on Friday after guiding below estimates, erasing about $11 billion from the U.S. government’s stake. The hit lands on a holding President Donald Trump has publicly credited with making America billions.

Intel closed Friday at $65.76, according to TradingView. However, at an average cost of $20.47 per share, the government’s stake still shows a gain near $20 billion.

Why Did Intel Stock Fall After Beating Estimates?

CNBC reports it was Intel’s worst session since 1974, after a gain of about 150% this year. Still, the stock is up 74% year to date.

Intel topped profit estimates for the quarter, yet sales landed slightly light and the fourth-quarter outlook disappointed.

Intel has lost a lot of ground this month. Source: Trading View

Weak personal computer (PC) demand and high memory chip prices dragged results. Intel expects the PC slump to last into next year.

Supply problems held back the server business. An October explosion at an Israeli chip plant hit the data center segment.

However, data center and artificial intelligence (AI) sales reached $7.3 billion, up nearly 80% from a year earlier.

Is the Government’s Intel Stake Still Paying Off?

Trump posted an AI-generated Intel trading image showing a climb from $20 to $95. Friday’s close sits roughly $29 below the $95 in that image.

An AI image posted by Trump showing Intel’s gains. Source: Truth Social

New Street upgraded Intel to buy. However, Bank of America, Roth Capital Partners, and Seaport Research Partners downgraded it to neutral.

KeyBanc analyst John Vinh calls the sell-off excessive and expects server capacity to arrive in the second half of 2027.

Vinh says the market should value Intel on 2030 numbers, when he expects the foundry (contract chipmaking) unit to contribute.

Seaport Research Partners analyst Jay Goldberg, in contrast, expects flat trading until Intel fixes its capacity problems. He says Intel overbuilt PC chips during the downturn and underbuilt server chips for rising demand.

Neither analyst treats government ownership as a reason to buy by itself.

Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan expects industry server processor units to grow 30% in both 2026 and 2027. He said more supply would push that figure higher.

Toyota's EV Sales More Than Doubled In a Year, While Its Gas Car Sales Dropped 20% In China

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Toyota's EV sales have jumped 127% through September, reports Electrek: Including Lexus, Toyota sold 309,766 battery electric vehicles (BEVs) globally through the first nine months of 2026, up 127.4% from the same period last year. Except for January (+87.5%), Toyota's EV sales have more than doubled every month this year compared with 2025... While its EVs are catching on, Toyota Motor's global sales fell for the third straight month in September. Toyota sold 860,107 vehicles in September, down 2.2% from the previous year. The company said that although overall demand remained steady, sales in China fell, leading to lower overall sales than last year. Through September, Toyota sold 7.51 million vehicles globally, down 3.4%. In China, Toyota's sales plunged nearly 20%, marking its eighth consecutive month with lower sales. Toyota said the overall market in China "remained weak due in part to rising gasoline prices, while the market for gasoline vehicles and hybrid vehicles was particularly affected," resulting in lower sales. In the first nine months of 2026, Toyota sold just over 1.05 million vehicles in China, 18.9% fewer than in the same period last year... After the US, China is Toyota's second-largest market.
