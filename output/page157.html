The data retention challenge for online exam platforms

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The shift from exam halls to online assessment platforms has quietly created a significant data management challenge.

With traditional, paper-based exams, the lifecycle is relatively straightforward. Papers are collected, stored according to institutional policies and destroyed when they are no longer needed.

Online exams differ because they leave a much larger digital footprint. Alongside answers and results, institutions also collect identity information, video and audio recordings, screen captures, room scans and other proctoring data to maintain integrity and identify suspicious behaviour.

The temptation is to retain all of it. Storage is relatively affordable, deleting information can feel irreversible and institutions may reason that the data could prove useful if results are challenged later.

However, “keep everything, just in case” is not a sustainable data strategy. In fact, it creates long-term compliance issues and exposes institutions to major security risks.

More surveillance doesn’t automatically mean more security

Online exam integrity matters. Institutions need assurance that the person taking an assessment is the right candidate and that rules are being followed.

There is, however, a danger of equating more monitoring with better security.

Every additional piece of data collected creates another asset that needs to be stored, protected and deleted. Video recordings are particularly contentious because they contain candidates’ faces, voices and other personally identifiable information.

That creates an important question for institutions and assessment providers: not simply can we collect this information, but do we need to?

Federal laws such as the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act and the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act require institutions to keep data collection, storage and sharing to a minimum and obtain explicit consent.

Other international privacy laws, including the Privacy Act 1988 in Australia, Personal Data Protection Act in Singapore and Protection of Personal Information Act in South Africa, for example, also require institutions to follow similar principles.

A truly secure exam strategy therefore needs to explain why proctoring data exists and clearly define when it’s no longer needed.

The forgotten half of exam security

Proctoring technology used by online exam platforms naturally receives significant scrutiny, and rightly so, as candidates are required to hand over their personal data and consent to some form of monitoring. By extension, how that data is managed deserves just as much attention.

Suppose an institution has clearly defined academic appeal and investigation windows. Once those periods expire, what happens to the associated exam data?

If the answer is effectively “nothing,” we have a problem.

Securely storing data indefinitely is not the same thing as governing it properly. Institutions should instead design online assessment processes around the entire information lifecycle: collection, access, storage, retention and permanent deletion.

That means becoming data purge-ready. A data purge-ready institution knows what information it holds, why it holds it and exactly when different categories of information should be deleted. Once relevant appeal or investigation periods have ended, proctoring data can be securely removed while essential records are retained in line with applicable requirements.

From here, deletion stops being an occasional IT clean-up exercise and becomes an integral part of the assessment process itself.

Data hoarding has a price

Good data governance isn’t only about reducing regulatory risk. There is a practical case for restraint too.

A single online exam may not create an intimidating amount of information. Multiply recordings and monitoring data across thousands of candidates, multiple assessments and several years, however and the storage footprint can quickly grow.

That means higher infrastructure and backup costs, more information for IT and security teams to manage and a larger pool of sensitive data to protect. The consequences of a breach can also become greater.

Nowhere is this vulnerability clearer than in education and professional training. As institutions across the world face an unrelenting wave of cyber attacks, establishing clearer data retention policies and focusing on proportionate proctoring is fast becoming a defining characteristic of secure institutions.

For online exam users, the lesson is clear. Data minimisation should be viewed as more than a compliance exercise. It is an architectural and operational decision that can reduce complexity, control costs and make online exams easier to scale.

Governance will separate the leaders from the laggards

Online exams are likely to remain an important part of education, professional certification and workplace training. That makes questions about their data practices increasingly difficult to ignore.

Institutions and assessment providers should be able to answer some deceptively simple questions: What information are we collecting? Why do we need it? Who can access it? How long will we retain it? And crucially, can we demonstrate an auditable process showing that it has been permanently deleted when that period ends?

The next phase of online assessment therefore cannot be defined solely by better monitoring technology. It needs better information governance alongside it. Trust does not come from collecting the maximum amount of data possible. It comes from demonstrating control over data that genuinely needs to exist.

For secure online exams, that means knowing when to press delete may ultimately be every bit as important as knowing when to press record.

New Perinatal Guidance for Women with ADHD

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

During pregnancy and the post-partum period, hormonal fluctuations and mounting demands on executive function intensify ADHD symptoms. Poor memory, dysregulated emotions, and disrupted sleep become elevated (and exhausting) during an already-stressful time.

Paradoxically, the vast majority of pregnant women with ADHD stop treating the condition with medication on the advice of their healthcare providers. Patients have been historically told to cease using stimulant and non-stimulant medication during pregnancy due to a lack of safety data and fear of potential risks to the fetus. But new research has produced generally reassuring reproductive safety data — and pushed more providers to weigh risks on both sides of the treatment equation.

How might untreated ADHD impact a woman’s mental health and her ability to manage the extraordinary demands of caring for an infant? Could ceasing treatment pose a bigger risk to the fetus than continuing it? What are the dangers associated with abruptly stopping treatment without monitoring, support, or a plan? These questions deserve to be asked.

Not every woman should continue medication, but neither should every woman stop. This decision should account for symptom severity, individual history, co-occurring conditions, gestational risks, and the consequences of untreated ADHD. These factors should be discussed with a medical team over multiple visits.

Use these recommendations — adapted in part from guidelines published in the American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology — to begin conversations with your medical team about your health and your baby’s health.

Preconception

If you take a stimulant, talk with your prescriber about whether a supervised trial without medication is appropriate. Discontinuation may be reasonable for some women with mild ADHD who function well with non-pharmacological supports.

Pay attention to changes in:

  • Work and household functioning
  • Driving and other safety-related activities
  • Sleep, nutrition, and self-care
  • Mood and emotional regulation
  • Relationships and overall quality of life

[Read: ADHD Meds Should Not Be “Panic Stopped” in Pregnancy]

It is reasonable to continue stimulant treatment if it is required for daily functioning. Discuss these options with your doctor:

  • Continuing your current medication
  • Using the lowest effective dose
  • Taking medication intermittently
  • Switching to a non-stimulant, if appropriate

Pregnancy

Most studies have shown no adverse effects among infants exposed to maternal ADHD medications. Amphetamines may increase the risk of preeclampsia and other adverse gestational outcomes, but the absolute risk appears low. Meanwhile, studies show that discontinuing medication during pregnancy may worsen ADHD symptoms, mental health, and daily functioning.

Weigh All Risks

Medication decisions should consider the risks associated with a fetus’ medication exposure and those associated with untreated ADHD. Some women choose to limit or stop medication during pregnancy and optimize treatment after delivery. If stimulant use is a concern, ask about lowering the dose, using medication intermittently, or considering another treatment.

Relevant factors include:

  • Trimester of pregnancy
  • ADHD symptom severity and functional impairment
  • Co-occurring physical and mental health conditions
  • Gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, and other vascular or placental risks

Attend Prenatal Appointments

Regular care allows your clinician to monitor fetal growth as well as your blood pressure, weight, and other health measures that may be affected by stimulant use.

Establish Support

Work with your care team to anticipate changes in symptoms, sleep, and mood in the postpartum period — a sensitive time when you may need greater support. Your proactive plan might include:

  • Therapy or ADHD coaching
  • Prenatal and postpartum doula support
  • Midwifery or obstetric follow-up
  • Pre-scheduled postpartum and psychiatric appointments
  • A plan for meals, chores, transportation, and overnight care
  • Clear responsibilities for partners and family members
  • A list of warning signs and whom to contact for help

Postpartum

Research has found that:

  • Methylphenidate exposure through milk is very low and generally undetected in infants.
  • Amphetamines transfer into milk at somewhat higher levels, though overall infant risk appears low.
  • Studies on non-stimulants and lactation are few.

Ask your provider:

  • If you take an amphetamine derivative and have milder symptoms, could intermittent use or coordinating medication and feeding schedules reduce infant exposure? Depending on your needs and preferences, should combination feeding or formula be considered?
  • If your pre-pregnancy dose no longer provides adequate symptom control after delivery, ask about the risks and benefits of adjusting treatment to support mood, functioning, and safety for both you and your baby.

Postpartum Support

Inattention, emotional dysregulation, and executive dysfunction intensify in the postpartum period. Women with ADHD face a substantially greater risk of postpartum mood and anxiety symptoms.

  • Be candid with your providers about changes in mood, anxiety, sleep, functioning, or your ability to care for yourself and your baby.
  • Consider therapy, ADHD coaching, postpartum support groups, midwifery care, and/or a postpartum doula. A doula can provide infant care education, feeding support, help with household routines, and opportunities for rest. A midwife may help with clinical postpartum care, assess recovery, support feeding, and identify concerns requiring additional treatment.
  • Reduce executive-function demands by:
    • Automating grocery and household deliveries
    • Using shared calendars and written checklists
    • Limiting commitments

Kylian Mbappé is facing the biggest commercial crisis of his career in China. Three Chinese companies announced on Tuesday that they are ending their agreements with the Real Madrid forward, and the fallout has already reached LaLiga’s television coverage in one of football’s most valuable markets.

The trigger was a short clip linked to the French online show The Bridge. In it, Mbappé is asked which meme of himself he likes most, and he refers to a video of “a guy … I think he was a Chinese dictator”, before an edited picture of his face on the body of Mao Zedong appears, as reported by the Associated Press. The footage has not been independently verified and, according to the BBC, the show has since taken the original upload off its YouTube channel.

Dongpeng, Mengniu and Milkground walk away

Beverage firm Dongpeng, the dairy group Mengniu and Milkground, which makes cheese, all issued statements on Tuesday in very similar language. None of them spelled out exactly what Mbappé had said, but each referred to hurt national feelings. Dongpeng’s statement, carried by the state-run Global Times, ended with the line: “Effective immediately, we are terminating all cooperation with Kylian Mbappé.”

Mengniu said it “firmly opposes and has zero tolerance for any words or actions that hurt the feelings of the Chinese people”. Social platform RedNote, whose own content partnership with the player ran out at the end of 2024, said it would take down related promotional material. The Global Times added that Dior, On and Hublot had removed Mbappé content from their Weibo pages, and that new users are currently unable to follow his accounts on RedNote or Weibo.

The row has even reached China’s foreign ministry. Asked about the controversy, a spokesperson said that “words and actions that hurt the feelings of the Chinese people will naturally be met with joint opposition from all sectors of Chinese society”, according to AP.

LaLiga coverage caught in the crossfire

The part of the story with the widest reach for Spanish football concerns broadcasting. According to the Global Times, Shanghai outlet The Paper reported that China Media Group, the parent organisation of state broadcaster CCTV, has suspended broadcasts of matches involving LaLiga and Mbappé. CCTV’s news apps have also removed content about the striker. Neither LaLiga nor Real Madrid had commented publicly at the time of writing, and there was no indication of how long any suspension might last.

Mbappé himself has not responded either, and The Bridge has stayed silent. The timing is awkward for player and club alike. Only on Sunday he was on the scoresheet as Madrid won the Clásico 2-1 at Camp Nou, a result that came with its own fitness worries over Fede Valverde.

A nickname that keeps coming back

The “dictator” tag has followed Mbappé for some time, partly because of the influence he is said to hold at club and international level, and he has spoken about it before. In a France Football interview in September he said the memes “sometimes reveals a lack of political and human awareness among people”, as quoted by the BBC.

On the pitch, the forward has been in fine form, with goals and assists in recent weeks including the 4-1 Champions League win over RB Leipzig. Off it, the pressure is now on him to address a backlash that is still growing, with Madrid due at Racing Santander on Sunday.

The National Council on Problem Gambling has put an outside turnaround specialist in charge while it looks for a permanent leader. Charles H. Jeffers II has taken over as interim executive director with immediate effect, the Washington-based nonprofit said in a press release on Monday.

The appointment comes one month after the council announced on September 26 that executive director Heather Maurer was resigning. Maurer stayed on through October 16 to help with the handover, and the board said at the time that a national search for her successor had already started.

Permanent search pushed to January

That search now has a firmer timetable. According to board president Derek Longmeier, recruitment of a permanent chief starts in January 2027, with the executive search firm Vetted Solutions leading the process. In the meantime, Jeffers takes charge of strategic operations.

“As he navigates the leadership transition, he will be focused on operations aligned with strategy and transparency, and culture reflecting NCPG values,” Longmeier said in the statement.

Jeffers founded The Jeffers Group, a consultancy that places interim executives and advises boards on governance, strategy and organizational culture. NCPG credits him with more than 30 years of nonprofit leadership and consulting work. His earlier posts include a stint as the American Occupational Therapy Association’s interim executive director and a spell as CEO of the Bermuda Tourism Authority. He has an MPA from Georgia State University.

A busy year for the problem gambling council

The leadership change lands at a delicate moment for the group. Founded in 1972, NCPG describes itself as the only national nonprofit focused on reducing gambling harm, and it remains neutral on whether gambling should be legal. It also runs the National Problem Gambling Helpline at 1-800-MY-RESET.

The council has spent much of 2026 working out how to deal with products that sit outside traditional gambling regulation. In June it explained a new Financial Services and Trading membership subcategory, aimed at areas such as financial trading platforms and prediction markets. In that letter, it said questions about legality, licensing and litigation were for regulators, lawmakers and the courts, not for NCPG.

The group has also asked prediction market operators to promote its helpline and filed comments with the CFTC on event contracts, as the federal regulator sets out where its authority ends and state power begins. At state level, rapid betting growth has stretched treatment services, with North Carolina’s addiction support under pressure as one recent example.

NCPG did not say whether Jeffers will be a candidate for the permanent role. For now, the board has turned to a specialist in interim leadership to bridge the gap until that search gets underway.

Massimiliano Allegri will not be on the Napoli bench when his side visit the Allianz Stadium on Sunday night. Serie A’s sporting judge has suspended the former Juventus coach for two matches following his dismissal against Roma, although Napoli have confirmed they will challenge the decision.

The ban means one of the most talked about reunions of the autumn could take place without its main character in the dugout, unless the appeal goes Napoli’s way before the weekend.

Why Allegri was banned

Allegri was sent off late in Saturday’s 3-3 draw with Roma at the Maradona, reacting furiously after Leonardo Balerdi struck a late equaliser for the visitors. As Football Italia reported, he removed his jacket and walked away before returning and having to be held back by his own staff.

The judge’s ruling, published on Monday and detailed by Sky Sport Italia, goes further than the touchline incident. Allegri was punished for an insulting remark aimed at the match officials in the 90th minute, a seriously disrespectful comment directed at them in the dressing room area afterwards, and aggressive behaviour towards an executive of the opposing club. The first two were noted by the fourth official and the referee, the last by staff working for the federal prosecutor.

On top of the suspension, the 59-year-old has been fined €10,000.

Monza first, then Juventus

The two games Allegri is set to miss are both away from home. Napoli travel to Monza in the midweek round on Wednesday 28 October, before heading to Turin to face Juventus on Sunday 1 November, with both matches kicking off at 20:45 local time.

If the ban stands, his first game back on the bench would be the home fixture against Lazio on Sunday 8 November.

Napoli fight the decision

Napoli moved quickly. In a short club statement on Monday afternoon, the Partenopei said that, having requested access to the official documents, they would lodge an appeal against the two match suspension handed to their coach.

Sky Sport Italia notes that a successful appeal would open the door for Allegri to take his place on the bench in Turin. Italian reports suggest the case should be heard before the end of the week, so the picture ought to be clear before Sunday.

What it means for Juventus

For Juventus supporters, any meeting with Allegri carries extra weight. He led the club across two spells, the second of which ended in May 2024, and he remains one of the most successful coaches in its history.

Luciano Spalletti’s side head into the week in a better mood after ending a 35 day wait for a league win with the 2-0 victory at Lecce, which lifted them to seventh on 14 points. Douglas Luiz was among the scorers that night, as covered in our report on his first goal for the club.

The Pokémon Company has confirmed that Pokémon UNITE will end service on Tuesday 23 March 2027 at 00:00 UTC, and the title’s last official competition, the Aeos Crown 2027 in Tokyo, is set to wrap up just two days earlier.

The closure applies to both the Nintendo Switch and mobile versions of the strategic team battle game, which first launched on Switch on 21 July 2021. Aeos gem sales were halted at 05:00 UTC today. New UNITE Club memberships and automatic renewals were stopped at the same time, according to the official end of service notice.

Aeos Crown 2027 still goes ahead

The shutdown does not affect the Tokyo event, which the publisher says will take place as previously announced. Back in July, it confirmed that UNITE would be left out of the 2027 Pokémon World Championships and handed a standalone tournament instead.

Registration for the regional qualifiers opened on the same morning as the closure news. Under the qualifier schedule on Pokemon.com, the first online qualifiers start on 5 December. Teams in Europe, North America, Brazil, Latin America and Oceania can sign up until that date.

Up to 32 teams will make the offline finals. North America and Japan hold four invitation slots each, while Europe, Brazil and Latin America North get three apiece. Japan’s quota includes ZETA DIVISION, who won the WCS 2026 title. Two Last Chance Qualifiers, for APAC West and APAC East, offer one extra place each.

Format and broadcast plans

The finals run on 20 and 21 March 2027. Day one splits the field into eight round-robin groups, and the top two sides in each group progress to a single-elimination bracket on day two. Every match uses the “All-Star Draft” ruleset.

There will be no general admission seating in Tokyo. Instead, the organisers plan to stream all matches live on official channels in several languages, with full regulations hosted on each regional registration site.

What players keep after the servers close

A farewell update arrives on 12 November. It adds special Unite coins, earned through log-in bonuses and match wins, which can be traded for items released earlier in the game’s life, such as Unite licences and Holowear.

The same patch introduces a “library” mode that keeps certain records and cosmetics viewable after 23 March. Players must complete a one-off data download before the shutdown to use it, and the publisher warns that library data generally cannot be moved between devices.

For the competitive community, the Tokyo finals now close out more than five years of official UNITE play. It is another shift for organised Pokémon events, following the North American International Championships’ move to Chicago for 2027.
