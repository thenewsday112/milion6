Landucci: ‘Napoli had a bad second half, Juventus deserved win, but new injury problems’

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Max Allegri’s assistant Marco Landucci reveals new Napoli injury problems, but ‘we had a bad second half and Juventus deserved to win, there’s not much else to say.’

The coach completed his two-match touchline ban by sitting in the stands of a stadium he knows so well thanks to 10 years at Juve in two different spells.

Assistant Marco Landucci was on the bench for this evening’s big match, but there were some late decisions forced upon them, with Rasmus Hojlund and Vanja Milinkovic-Savic laid low by a virus.

Stanislav Lobotka, Benoit Badiashile, Leonardo Spinazzola, David Neres, Alisson Santos, Costantino Favasuli and Alessandro Buongiorno were already out of action.

It was an even match with few big chances until two moments of individual magic, as Kerim Alajbegovic and Jeremie Boga hit stunners from outside the box, along with Luca Marianucci’s second yellow card.

Landucci acknowledged Napoli errors

TURIN, ITALY – NOVEMBER 01: Federico Gatti of Juventus is challenged by Scott McTominay of SSC Napoli during the Serie A match between Juventus and SSC Napoli at Allianz Stadium on November 01, 2026 in Turin, Italy. (Photo by Valerio Pennicino/Getty Images)

“Quite simply, we had a bad second half and Juve deserved to win. When you lose, you have to just stay quiet and accept it,” Landucci told DAZN Italia.

“We had some absences, but this isn’t an excuse, we should’ve done better after the break. I thought it was a good first half, Lucca was denied by a great Vicario save, but that wasn’t enough.”

Napoli are now stranded in eighth place on 14 points alongside Bologna, already nine adrift of Como in the Champions League spots.

They are eight points worse off than at this stage last season, when after 10 rounds they were alone in first place.

“We are not worried, but we are very disappointed, because we cared about this match. The truth is that’s how the game went, we need to shut up and prepare for Wednesday. We have many injuries, as many teams do with this fixture list, it is somewhat inevitable and you have to keep going.”

TURIN, ITALY – NOVEMBER 01: Jeremie Boga of Juventus celebrates scoring his team’s second goal with team mates during the Serie A match between Juventus and SSC Napoli at Allianz Stadium on November 01, 2026 in Turin, Italy. (Photo by Valerio Pennicino/Getty Images)

It was noted that on the two goals, the Napoli defenders backed off and allowed the Juventus players to come forward, so was that a tactical choice from the bench?

“Absolutely not,” replied Landucci. “Those were great finishes, but we should not have allowed them to get into that space. We back-tracked too much, this is the difference between conceding and not conceding, and it’s not the first time. If you push up and defend well, you’re not going to concede that.

“We all could’ve done better in the second half. It seems a clear and logical analysis to me. Juventus did better than us and deserved to win.”

TURIN, ITALY – NOVEMBER 01: Kevin De Bruyne of SSC Napoli is challenged by Weston McKennie of Juventus during the Serie A match between Juventus and SSC Napoli at Allianz Stadium on November 01, 2026 in Turin, Italy. (Photo by Valerio Pennicino/Getty Images)

Napoli looked as if they had more injury problems, because Kevin De Bruyne and Giovanni Di Lorenzo were substituted at half-time, then Matias Olivera looked distraught when hobbling off the field just after the Marianucci red card.

“Clearly, they could not continue. De Bruyne had a problem with his ankle, Giovanni Di Lorenzo I’m not sure, but he could not continue either. Even with that, we need to do better. It was a balanced encounter decided by these great goals, but we didn’t help our own situation.”

Has Landucci spoken to Allegri yet, seeing as the coach was in the stands?

“Yes, we spoke in the dressing room, but there’s not a lot to say. Obviously, he wasn’t happy, would you have been happy? He just said we should’ve done better in the second half, we paid a heavy price for errors, that’s it.”

Frustrated Justin Jefferson didn't speak to reporters after Sunday's loss

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →
'

When a team is winning, it's hard for any offensive players who aren't getting the ball as often as they should to be upset. When winning becomes losing, the frustrations can quickly surface.

That happened for the Vikings on Sunday against the Lions. Receiver Justin Jefferson, who made a spectacular touchdown catch in the first half, didn't see the ball as often as he should have during proverbial nut-cutting time.

After the Vikings' final drive — which curiously ended on a fourth-and-five punt with 3:01 to play at a time when the Detroit rushing game was clicking — Jefferson made a rare display of frustration. After the 19-15 loss, Jefferson declined to speak to reporters, per Kevin Seifert of ESPN.

Which means that Jefferson, who has a habit of always saying all the right things, doubted that he would have been able to do that.

"Obviously he's frustrated, and that's understandable," quarterback Kyler Murray told reporters, via Seifert. "He is who he is, and he has every right to the way he feels when he doesn't get the ball. That's on us to find a way to get him the ball."

"We had some opportunities for [Jefferson], and there were some chances there," coach Kevin O'Connell told reporters. "But we've got to find a way to get him the ball, especially on potential chances for him on some of those sequences where we're looking to kick off some momentum. He's a great guy to do it behind, and we'll continue to find ways to get him the football. But it was a division game. These guys want to win. I don't fault the frustration or the competitiveness in any way, shape or form."

Whatever the cause, there's clearly a problem. And Murray, who missed time early in the season due to a concussion, should be up to speed now. On Sunday, he wasn't good enough.

And here's the basic reality. At some point, either en route to the playoffs or after the Vikings get there, Murray will have to make a big play in a big spot. The jury is out on whether he will.

The Vikings don't have many options. They could try to make a trade, but who's out there? They wanted to keep Daniel Jones after the 2024 season; maybe, at this point, the Colts would welcome the chance to move on.

If the Raiders had lost to the Jets, Kirk Cousins may have been available for a reunion. With Las Vegas now at 4-4, why would they move him?

The other option would be to ride with Carson Wentz, who has shown that he can and will get the ball to Jefferson.

However it goes, Sunday's game exposed the biggest problem with the Vikings. Against teams that can't be held to 15 points or less, the Vikings may not be able to muster enough points to beat them.

Ferrari Challenge Stradale Is Reportedly Returning After 20-Plus Years

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Done Only Once

For over 30 years, Ferrari has been letting its customers race its cars in one-make championships. It started in 1993 with the 348, first around Europe, then with a North American series, and later in other parts of the world.

The cars have changed a lot since then. The 348 Challenge became the F355 Challenge, and its successors have become the go-to for the racing series. But in 2003, Ferrari thought it would be a good idea to sell road-going versions of the race cars to even more customers who’d like the experience without having to enter the championship series.

The result was the 360 Challenge Stradale, effectively a road-legal version of the actual race car. At the time, it was matched up against the 911 GT3 RS of the 996.2 generation, and it made for very interesting comparisons. But while Porsche continued building stripped-out, lightweight road-legal racing 911s, Ferrari did not.

Bring a Trailer

The Challenge Stradale is Coming Back

But after more than 20 years, Ferrari is bringing back the 296 Challenge Stradale. Like many of 360 Challenge Stradale, it’s taking about four years to get it done, but it’s reportedly coming before the end of the year. That’s according to The Supercar Blog, and that’s not the only bit of information that the publication managed to obtain.

Previously, it was said that the 296 Challenge Stradale would debut sometime in November. Now, a date appears to have been locked in, and it’s slated to premiere during Finali Mondiali at the Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya. If so, it’s a fitting way to end the 2026 Ferrari Challenge season, which will be held from November 17 to 23. Ferrari will pull the covers off the car on the 21st, and prices are said to start at €320,000 or around $358,000.

Derek Cornelissen/Instagram

What We Know So Far

The first Challenge Stradale model in over 20 years was first spied back in February. The camouflaged prototype was seen driving around the streets of Maranello, sporting a unique aero package that also includes a whopper of a rear wing among other things.

It’s said that the car won’t have the standard 296’s plug-in hybrid system. If true, that means it’ll be exactly like the 296 Challenge that has been running around the world’s circuits for about three years now. For power, we can expect around 690 hp, which is far less than the hybrid model; the removal of the electrified bits does mean it’s over 300 lbs lighter at the very least. Of course, the Stradale will have some creature comforts, but we’re expecting it to weigh closer to 3,000 lbs. For comparison, the hybrid coupe tips the scales at over 3,500 lbs.

Of course, Ferrari can eke out more horses out of its twin-turbo V6 to make up the difference for losing the electric boost. It’s been rumored that it’ll have about 720 hp, which actually makes it more powerful than the race car. Of course, the race car is a far lighter vehicle, but that’s not to say the Stradale will be a blunt instrument. Mind you, Ferrari may have a couple more track-focused cars coming soon, and 2027 might just be an exciting year for the Prancing Horse.
