Manchester United could be forced to strengthen their attacking options in January after another injury setback involving Benjamin Sesko.

With Michael Carrick needing additional firepower to maintain competition for places up front, an experienced Premier League striker has reportedly emerged as a surprise target.

Leeds United forward Dominic Calvert-Lewin is among the names being considered by United’s recruitment team. However, attempting to sign a key player from one of their biggest rivals could prove extremely difficult.

Manchester United Consider Dominic Calvert-Lewin

According to The Athletic, Manchester United are exploring the January market for centre-forwards, with Calvert-Lewin one of the players under consideration.

The 29-year-old England international has reportedly been on United’s radar for some time, and recruitment staff are now assessing whether a potential transfer would be feasible when the window opens.

The interest comes amid concerns over Benjamin Sesko’s availability. The Slovenian striker is expected to miss an extended period with a shin injury, leaving Carrick with fewer recognised centre-forward options.

Calvert-Lewin has rebuilt his career since joining Leeds on a free transfer from Everton in 2025. His performances at Elland Road have attracted renewed attention, with the striker scoring 20 goals in 49 appearances for the club across all competitions.

He has also returned to England international contention following his improved form, making him an appealing option for United as they consider their attacking requirements.

However, there are significant obstacles. Leeds are reportedly keen to reward Calvert-Lewin with a new contract, despite his current agreement running until 2028. The club are also understood to be protective of a player who has become an important part of Daniel Farke’s side.

The rivalry between Leeds and Manchester United presents another major complication. A transfer between the clubs would attract considerable attention, and reports suggest the possibility of an agreement is currently remote.

What This Means for Manchester United

Calvert-Lewin could offer United a different profile in attack, bringing physical presence, aerial ability and considerable Premier League experience. His availability could become particularly important if Sesko remains sidelined into the January window.

United have previously considered alternative centre-forward options, but the latest developments could encourage the recruitment department to widen its search. Calvert-Lewin’s experience in English football would potentially make him an attractive short-term solution.

Nevertheless, Leeds are unlikely to make negotiations straightforward. They have little reason to weaken their squad midway through the campaign, particularly by allowing an important player to join their historic rivals.

United must also consider whether pursuing Calvert-Lewin is realistic when other striker targets may be easier to acquire.

For now, the reported interest represents an option under consideration rather than an imminent transfer. Sesko’s injury has increased the urgency surrounding United’s search for attacking reinforcements, but persuading Leeds to sell their striker could prove a significant challenge.

Real Madrid defender Trent Alexander-Arnold has emerged as a potential option, with former Manchester United chief scout Mick Brown suggesting the England international could be tempted by a move to west London if Chelsea decide to make an approach.

Chelsea Monitoring Trent Alexander-Arnold

According to Football Insider, Chelsea are keeping tabs on Alexander-Arnold as questions continue over his role at Real Madrid. The 28-year-old has struggled to establish himself as a regular starter under Jose Mourinho, with competition for places limiting his opportunities.

“It appears Mourinho doesn’t rate him particularly in that right-back position, and he’s not the only one, because he’s been out in the cold for England as well.”

Despite those concerns, Brown believes Alexander-Arnold remains an attractive player because of his technical ability and reputation. Chelsea, meanwhile, have reportedly been assessing potential replacements for Gusto as they consider changes to their defensive options.

Declutter before investing in storage solutions.

Prioritize accessibility.

Convenience is a core component of organizing a garage. Storage placement should reflect how frequently an item is used, says Sanford. “Homeowners shouldn’t have to move multiple bins or climb a ladder every time they need something,” she explains. “The more convenient a system is, the more likely everyone in the household will maintain it.”

Here are some tips for organizing in a way that takes accessibility into account:

  • Plan around how often you use items and who needs to reach them. Store sports equipment used often within easy reach on racks, hooks, or open shelving. However, place seasonal decorations up high and out of the way. 
  • Organize around your car, not your walls. “One thing homeowners often overlook when organizing their garage is the space their vehicle actually occupies,” says Sanford. “Before installing shelving or storage systems, park your cars in the garage and open every door, including the trunk.” This gives you a realistic understanding of where storage can go without interfering with how you use the space.
  • Consider how you move through the garage. Think about where you unload groceries, access sports equipment, or enter your home, says Sanford. “These everyday routines should influence where storage systems are placed. The goal is to create a garage that functions just as well when you’re using it as it does when everything is neatly put away.”
  • Label everything. Labelling is the key to keeping everything organized over time.

She recommends using appropriate moisture-resistant storage containers and keeping belongings elevated off the floor when possible. “Clear, durable bins with secure-fitting lids also help protect belongings from dust and debris, while allowing homeowners to identify contents quickly.” 

Sanford suggests avoiding cardboard boxes for long-term garage storage, especially if pests are a concern. “Cardboard can absorb moisture and provide hiding places for pests, so durable plastic containers with secure lids are often a better alternative.”

Rock Spire Collapses at Popular Turkish Tourist Site, Killing Three

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Part of a rock spire known as a fairy chimney collapsed yesterday near the entrance to one of Turkey's most popular tourist destinations, killing three people and injuring five others.

The incident occurred around 3:15 pm local time in the town of Goreme, in central Turkey’s Cappadocia region yesterday. Rocks broke away and fell onto people near the parking area. The site is part of a UNESCO World Heritage landscape, famous for its cave dwellings and cone-shaped rock formations. Nevsehir Governor Huseyin Kok said that heavy rainfall triggered a landslide that caused the rocks to detach from the formation, according to Reuters.

Rescue teams initially recovered two bodies from the rubble before discovering a third victim overnight, bringing the death toll to three. The deceased include two Turkish citizens and one foreign national. Five others were injured in the collapse. Some of them were later discharged from hospital, according to AFP.

Sudden collapse

Eyewitnesses described a loud noise followed by a large dust cloud. The area was closed to the public, and search operations concluded overnight under floodlights.

Turkish Justice Minister Akin Gurlek said an investigation by the chief prosecutor’s office, along with expert assessments, would begin. The collapse occurred on Turkey’s Republic Day, a public holiday when visitor numbers were high.

Cappadocia, in central Anatolia, is renowned for its distinctive fairy chimneys. These tall, thin spires of soft volcanic rock topped by harder caps were shaped over millions of years by wind and water erosion. The Goreme Open-Air Museum and surrounding sites were put on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985 for their blend of natural landforms and human history, including Byzantine rock-cut churches and underground settlements. The region’s soft rock is geologically fragile and prone to weathering and occasional rockfalls, particularly after heavy rain.

Turkish authorities have previously carried out restoration and monitoring work to address erosion and climate-related risks, and smaller non-fatal collapses were recorded in the area in 2024 and 2025. The site remains one of Turkey’s most visited destinations, drawing tourists for hot-air balloon rides, hiking, and exploration of its unique geology and heritage.

JASON AVELAR AND GARRETT BEAL SHARE THE STORY OF PRESCOTT JUNIOR HIGH’S LEGACY OF PHYSICAL EDUCATION EXCELLENCE

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump announced the re-establishment and restoration of the Presidential Fitness Test on July 31, 2025, when he signed an Executive Order revitalizing the test along with the President’s Council on Sports, Fitness, and Nutrition. Since then, the Trump administration also announced the Presidential Fitness School Innovation Challenge, a competition which invites “schools, districts, and youth-serving organizations to design and execute creative, repeatable models that inspire student participation, teamwork, and enthusiasm for the Presidential Fitness Test (PFT).”

One school that could serve as an example for others to follow and may be a top contender should they enter that challenge would be Prescott Junior High School in Modesto, California. The school overall has a great reputation, but it is best known for their P.E. program. Current teachers Jason Avelar and Garrett Beal are two of the teachers for this program, but its legacy as a fitness-focused juggernaut goes back nearly a half-century. 

Beal, who also served as a police officer for a period of his life, played sports as a kid but credited a high school wrestling coach for introducing him to the weight room.

“It was the first sport where I saw that being strong helped,” he said. “I fell in love even more with fitness than wrestling.”

Avelar was bitten by the iron bug while playing football in his high school days but also found he had natural leadership skills as a linebacker. His coaches confirmed this and could have played a role in him becoming a gym teacher.

 “I had a few coaches and teachers pull me aside and said, ‘Hey, I think you’d be a great teacher.’”

Prescott Junior High

THE ROOTS OF PRESCOTT RUN DEEP IN THE COMMUNITY

 Both Beal and Alevar are reminded often about the history they are a part of. Modeled closely after the La Sierra High School Physical Education Program (the famous 1960s President’s Council on Physical Fitness model) Prescott’s program has challenged junior high students for decades using a high-standard and military-inspired structure.

In 1973, then Prescott P.E. teacher Mark Wilson saw a demonstration of a high-intensity, physical fitness-based curriculum. Wilson was inspired by the concept and used his own version on his students, challenging them to make fitness a priority at an early age.

There is a color and points based system to help gauge the students’ progress and motivate them to work harder. All students start with zero points and white trunks, and there are 20 different fitness tests. Each test offers one, two, or four points for their performances. Once a student achieves 60 points, they earn red trunks. However, the scoring goes back to zero because they are at a new level with harder challenges. Once they work back up to 60 points, they then earn blue trunks.

“The tests at that point get very challenging,” Avelar said. There are also gold and purple trunks for those students that do very well in the program. Prescott even has a P.E. Hall of Fame, where students can be recognized. Avelar acknowledged one student who even cleared the purple trunk scoring early, meaning they had to create a new level just for her.

“We decided on custom trunks. Once you pass purple, you get to pick your own.”

The bars may be seen as super high for some, but Avelar and Beal share that either students rise to the occasion and reach it, or they get closer than they may have had there not been that high bar in the first place. 

The results speak for themselves. One student ran two miles in 12 minutes, 27 seconds. Another performed over 6,000 continuous sit-ups, and multiple students have reportedly performed over 1,000 unbroken pushups. The Prescott program has had a generational impact in Modesto.

Beal said, “I wear my Prescott shirt anywhere around town, and people stop to talk to me about when they went there.” He even met one man at a book club, and he recognized the last name on their Hall of Fame wall.

“He told me, ‘Yeah, my grandma.”

Prescott Junior High

THE PASSION FOR FITNESS STAYS WITH THEM

Other teachers at Prescott were also students there, and both Beal and Avelar confirmed that they strive to be the best examples possible by making their wellness a priority. They also said that even though the high school P.E. programs may not have as intense of programs, the students who went to Prescott continue their fitness journeys after they move on. Others may take the school for granted but realize what they were a part of later on.

Avelar said, “As they leave, they realize ‘Oh crap, this is a super special place.’”

Both teachers take great pride in not only what they are a part of but having added their own chapters to the Prescott story. They are reminded of how important their work is every day, and they definitely don’t take the positions they are in for granted.

Avelar stated. “The fulfillment I get from showing up to school every day and seeing kids reach their goals, push themselves, do things they’ve never done before, and seeing the excitement on their faces is why I do it.”

Beal added, “This is such a special place to be,”

There is no official word on how many schools will enter the Presidential Fitness School Innovation Challenge, but both Avelar and Beal were pleased that the Presidential Fitness Test has been reinstated, and they hope that it can serve as a catalyst for other P.E. programs to do more to help their students embrace fitness, pushing themselves, and taking on great challenges. As they have seen first hand, the impact goes beyond the school walls and can last for many years to come.

To learn more about the PFT or the School Innovation Challenge, go to fitness.gov. 

You can follow the Prescott program on Instagram @prescott_standard and Beal and Alevar on LinkedIn.

Why Is BNB Falling Harder Than Bitcoin?

Because it is the coin most tied to Binance itself, and futures traders are now betting on that link. From the 20:00 UTC price on Thursday, BNB is still down 5.6%, more than Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, XRP or Dogecoin.

Large-cap coins since the Binance hack. Source: Binance

The hack itself didn’t touch BNB Chain. TRM Labs’ breakdown lists ten blockchains the attacker drained Binance wallets on, from Ethereum to Polygon, and BNB Chain isn’t among them. What traders are pricing is the exchange: BNB is Binance’s token, so a hit to Binance reads as a hit to BNB.

“The Binance exploit has intensified market-wide selling, with BNB facing the greatest exposure,” Zeus Research analyst Dominick John said. Rick Maeda of Laevitas saw it the other way, calling the drop “quite modest for possibly the largest exchange theft ever.”

The futures market sides with the bears. On OKX:

  • Funding turned negative. The BNB perpetual’s funding rate flipped from +0.01% before the hack to −0.0266% at midnight and −0.0434% at 08:00 UTC, so shorts are now paying longs. Bitcoin’s funding stayed positive through the same hours.
  • Open interest jumped 20%, from $54.8 million to $66 million, as new positions opened after the drop.
  • Most accounts are long, at 2.79 longs for every short, up from 2.66 before the hack.

Put together, that reads as many small accounts buying the dip and fewer, larger traders shorting it. If BNB bounces, those shorts are fuel for a squeeze. If it breaks lower, the crowded longs are.

What Did BNB Crypto Do After Binance’s Last Security Scares?

It fell further at first, then recovered within a month both times. Binance has been here twice before:

  • May 7, 2019: hackers stole 7,000 BTC from a Binance hot wallet. BNB slid 17.5% from its pre-hack close to a low of $18.30 three days later. A month on, it was 39.2% higher.
  • October 6, 2022: an attacker minted 2 million BNB through a bug in the BNB Chain bridge, worth about $570 million. BNB bottomed at $256.80 a week later, 12.7% below its pre-hack close, and was up 20.5% after 30 days.

This time BNB is 8.7% under its pre-hack price at its worst so far. If it follows either path, there could be another leg down before any recovery.

The comparison traders keep reaching for is FTT, the FTX token that lost almost all its value when the exchange collapsed in November 2022. The difference is the balance sheet. FTX had spent customer money and couldn’t pay it back. Binance says it will cover all losses from this hack, and Tether, Circle and others froze about $2.7 billion of the stolen funds within hours.

Traders haven’t fully relaxed, though. Binance hasn’t said how it will fund the payout, and withdrawals stay shut until the reopening it expects within 48 hours of its Friday morning update.

Sector Snapshot: Travel Tech Funding Heads Toward Multiyear Low Despite AI Push

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Travel demand appears to be holding up, but at the same time, investor appetite for travel tech startups has cooled. Even a wave of startups promising to improve travel with AI hasn’t managed to reverse the funding slowdown, a review of Crunchbase data shows. Whether uncertainty over how AI will reshape travel planning and booking is contributing to that pullback remains an open question.

Startups in the travel tech space have raised $2.2 billion this year through the third quarter, according to Crunchbase data. The largest funding recipients span a wide range of categories including hotel management software, travel booking platforms, alternative accommodations and corporate events. The mix also includes electric aviation, travel finance and insurance, and consumer hospitality businesses, reflecting a broad definition of travel tech.

The broad trend: Investors are making far fewer bets on travel startups than immediately before and after the COVID-19 pandemic, Crunchbase data shows. This year could be the lowest since at least 2018 when it comes to dollars raised and deal count.

AI is a recurring theme among the largest funding recipients, with many using it to perform functions such as automating hospitality operations, personalizing bookings and streamlining travel planning.

The numbers: In the first three quarters of 2026, startups in the travel tech space have raised $2.2 billion globally across just 302 funding rounds, Crunchbase data shows. That’s down fairly significantly so far from the $3.7 billion raised in 2025. Deal volume is on track to fall for a third consecutive year — pointing to a market where investors are putting more money into fewer companies.

Funding and deal volume in recent years remain far below past levels. In 2021, for example, funding in the sector topped $12 billion. That’s a bit surprising considering that so much travel was halted in the midst of the COVID-19 pandemic. But even prior to the pandemic, funding and deal totals were much higher — totaling $9.4 billion across 1,277 deals in 2018 and $10.4 billion across 1,190 transactions in 2019.

Even travel industry researcher Skift noted the funding downturn in a recent newsletter, saying: “Though it’s too early to say whether this is a long-term market movement, it’s a clear sign of the times.”

Notable deals

Interestingly, several of the year’s largest deals involved startups headquartered outside of the United States.

The year’s largest funding recipient through the third quarter was Mews, which raised a $300 million Series D in January led by EQT Growth, with participation from Atomico, HarbourVest Partners, Kinnevik, Battery Ventures and Tiger Global Management. The Amsterdam-based hospitality software company helps hotels manage reservations, payments and operations, and says it plans to expand its use of AI agents to automate workflows. The financing valued the company at $2.5 billion.

Chinese electric aircraft developer Aerofugia raised about $144 million in February in a financing led by CITIC Securities, joined by New Alliance Capital, Vertex Ventures China and other investors. Chengdu-based Aerofugia, also backed by automaker Geely, develops electric passenger aircraft that take off and land vertically, often called flying taxis.

Montreal-based Stay22 secured a $122 million minority growth investment from Summit Partners in February. The startup uses AI to help publishers, creators and event organizers turn their audiences’ interest in travel into bookings and affiliate revenue.

Home-swapping platform Kindred announced an $85 million Series C led by Index Ventures in February. The San Francisco-based startup operates a members-only network where travelers exchange stays in one another’s homes. Its announcement also included a $40 million Series B co-led by New Enterprise Associates and Figma CEO Dylan Field, bringing the combined funding disclosed that day to $125 million.

Another large deal went to Paris-based Naboo, which announced a $70 million Series B in February led by Lightspeed Venture Partners with participation from Notion Capital, Isai and Ternel. The company uses AI to help businesses source venues, organize corporate events and manage related purchasing and payments.

Exits

Several travel startups have found buyers in 2026, including companies developing AI tools for planning and booking trips. Amadeus acquired corporate travel AI assistant SkyLink in February, while Expedia acquired AI trip-planning startup Layla in July. Both deals illustrate a trend of established travel companies buying startups to expand their AI capabilities.

Expedia also completed its acquisition of venture-backed Tiqets, a booking platform for museums, attractions and experiences, in February.

On the IPO side, a potentially prominent prospective exit fell through. Travel experiences platform Klook withdrew its planned U.S. offering in October after having filed last November.

Still, the downturn in startup funding and dearth of IPOs in the sector hasn’t affected one of the bigger now-public players in the space. Corporate travel booking and expense management platform Navan has seen its stock rise nearly 60% this year. Formerly called TripActions, it began trading on Nasdaq under NAVN in October 2025. Its stock closed at $27 on Oct. 28, up about 58% this year from its Dec. 31 closing price of $17.08. It was also 8% above its $25 IPO price, meaning much of this year’s gain recovered from losses following the listing.

“Neighbor down the road had a squirrel dog,” said Larry White as he led a cast of dogs and hunters to the woods. “Got its legs cut off by a mowing machine. Had to hunt that dog from a wheelbarrow.

“One day a friend asked to borrow the dog, so the owner explained that you had to push the wheelbarrow in whatever direction the dog pointed his nose. If he barked, the track was hot, so you pushed faster. When he looked up and barked, he had the squirrel treed and you got a shot. The friend came back late that night with no squirrels. His clothes were torn to shreds and his skin was scratched up and looked like a road map. ‘What in the world happened to you?’ the hound’s owner asked.

“‘What’s it look like?’ the friend snapped. ‘Your fool squirrel dog hit a deer and we ran that thing all day long!'”

Larry White was judging a cast in the 1981 World Champion Squirrel Hunt. His tale got a laugh, but it also emphasized the importance of handler/dog teamwork in any form of hunting.

Outdoor Life

A good squirrel dog works for the hunter by locating squirrels through scent on the ground and on trees as well as by seeing and following bushytails moving through the treetops. The dog’s manner of barking signals its degree of progress to the hunter. A good hunter works for the dog by staying keenly and constantly aware of what the barking means and by following almost within sight so he can get to the tree quickly and shoot the squirrel before it escapes.

Good competition squirrel dogs are more decisive, accurate and consistent than average squirrel dogs. They do the job with less wasted time and motion.

Squirrel-treeing talent always has been wherever you found it, but these days some unusual dogs are showing up in the woods. Chuck Trehern’s purebred Doberman pinscher learned to bark up a tree by watching the family coonhounds. Ronnie Liles’ 6-year-old Australian shepherd, Smokey, was never taught: he just started treeing squirrels. T.C. Jones won the 1980 Championship with a registered liver and white English pointer! Trophies similar to bowling trophies are awarded. The statuettes portray a dog barking treed.

Most squirrel dogs are not pedigreed, however. Two Illinois dogs were described as terrier and feist crosses. Randy Pannell, who trains 15 to 20 squirrel dogs every year, came over from Blue Springs, Mississippi, with a redtick coonhound/mountain cur cross. Fola Hart brought a Norwegian elkhound/feist cross from Virginia. And the 1981 winner was Orville Hart’s “Whiskey,” a 4-year-old Norwegian elkhound/feist from Leesburg, Ohio.

Feists, those wiry little mongrels that are intelligent enough to learn any job on the farm, are becoming rare because most people prefer to keep purebred dogs. As a result, many squirrel hunters are making their own mongrels by crossing two different purebreds. There’s a constant search for the best dog, and that’s one reason why Dick Sheets thought up the World Champion Squirrel Hunt.

Sheets reasoned that squirrel-dog competition would not only preserve and enlarge the sport, but that the dogs would improve through breeding. Perhaps breeds of purebred squirrel dogs of consistent ability would emerge from such an effort. The Yockanookany Coon Hunters Association of Ethel, Mississippi, agreed with Sheets and sponsored the first championship in 1980.

Yockanookany is Choctaw for “crawls on belly like snake” and also applies to the way hunters must cross a spongy swamp. The 100-mile-long Yockanookany Swamp is just two miles from the club grounds.

By 1981, competition had already influenced squirrel-dog breeding. L.E. Goutierez came over from Louisiana with two pups out of the 1980 second-place winner. The pups’ dam was Spot, his own small Chihuahua/feist cross.

Goutierez arrived a day early, pitched his tent and drove his dogs down the old Natchez Trace to the nearby woods where they could become accustomed to hunting new territory. The original Natchez Trace, which was once an extensive trading trail between the Indian tribes, runs right past the Yockanookany club grounds. The entire area is well forested with both hill-country and creek-bottom squirrel habitat.

Little Spot ran through the woods with her nose slightly down, searching for scent on the ground, on logs and on fallen limbs. Every tree got a sniff as she trotted past. But the squirrels weren’t moving. When Spot finally barked, L.E. didn’t become excited. “She scatter-barked,” he said. “It’s a cold track.”

Scatter barking is barking here and there instead of barking enthusiastically in one place such as on a hot trail or at a tree.

By the end of the championship hunt, Goutierez had learned one more thing about breeding squirrel dogs. “Have to get more size in my dogs,” he said. “My dogs can’t compete with the longer-legged, wider-running dogs.”

Outdoor Life

Four dogs were cast together in the first running of the championship in 1980 and many handlers thought that that was three too many. By nature, squirrel dogs do best alone. They’re highly intelligent, very independent and more than a little jealous of their relationships with their human partners. In 1981, a compromise was struck: only two dogs to a cast—male and female, if possible—and in weight categories of under and over 35 pounds.

The only reported fight occurred when the Doberman’s handler decided to be gentlemanly and let his competitor’s dog smell the dead squirrel, too. Getting to smell the squirrel is an encouraging reward of sorts and the Dobe was not ready to share that with another dog.

An impartial judge accompanies each cast, but it is up to the handler to “call” his own dog—that is, notify the judge when satisfied his dog has treed. As in coonhound competition, 100 points are earned by the first dog at the tree, 75 by the second. Unlike coonhound scoring, no points are awarded for striking (finding) a trail. This is because sometimes the trail is short, sometimes there is none and too many squirrel dogs are silent trailers. Also unlike coonhound scoring, tree points are not subtracted if no squirrel is found in the tree because squirrels all too readily travel from tree to tree.

If the squirrel is not found, the tree points are circled and do not count on the final score. Judges are allowed considerable latitude, however. If the squirrel is seen several trees away from the one the dog is treeing on and the judge is satisfied that the squirrel could have gotten there through the treetops, he can give the dog credit for that squirrel.

Six or eight squirrels an hour is not impossible in well-populated timber when the bushytails are really active. Ronnie Liles’ Australian shepherd treed 700 for him and his friends during the past two years. “The trick,” said Liles, “is to catch ’em stirrin’. You might not see a squirrel today. But you can come back through the same place tomorrow and see 10.”

Ronnie believes that the old hunters’ barometer, “If the cows are lying down, so are the deer,” also applies to squirrels. And as he says, the dogs are helpless if they can’t see, smell or possibly hear the bushytails. The squirrel that hasn’t been on the ground and is lounging on a limb or in a vine tangle probably won’t be discovered by a dog unless falling air currents allow the dog to “wind” it. This would happen mostly during the cool of late afternoon rather than during early morning when the warming sunlight causes air to rise.

Liles prefers hunting little cat squirrels (grays) in the big, swampy, bottom timbers. Shaking a vine spooks grays out of hiding faster and easier than it does fox squirrels. Other hunters choose the hills where more fox squirrels live because they don’t like the way grays often chase off through the treetops when a dog barks.

Randy Pannell minimizes that problem by staying fairly close to his dog and then getting to the tree fast. In competition, however, that’s not always possible. The second dog has five minutes in which to join the first dog at the tree. During that time, handlers and the judge stay clear.

Both Pannell and Fola Hart demonstrated the wisdom of walking apart from other hunters. At 79, Fola appeared to be walking far behind because of age. But he was the first to hear his dog bark treed while other members of the cast were busy talking.

The perceptive rapport between hunter and squirrel dog is nothing less than amazing. During a Friday afternoon cast, Orville Hart was reluctant to say that Whiskey had treed. “Them two dogs are looking at us,” he observed. “If they believed they had the squirrel, they’d be looking up the tree!”

When a squirrel was shot in Ronnie Liles’ cast, Smokey grabbed it and ran off with the second dog in hot pursuit. As Smokey raced past his master’s leg, Ronnie grinned. “That dog expects me to catch the other dog as it goes by. Then Smokey will bring me the squirrel. He won’t let the other dog have it. But if another dog gets to the squirrel first, Smokey won’t bother it.” Retrieving is an added nicety, but it isn’t rewarded with extra points during the Yockanookany competition.

Some dogs won’t bark if their masters happen to be present when the squirrel is treed, strongly suggesting that they have enough reasoning power to recognize that, in such a case, the boss doesn’t have to be called.

One Mississippi hunter recalled a boyhood dog that would always dance around the opposite side of the tree, yanking and shaking every vine or sapling it could find. Invariably, this commotion chased the squirrel around to the gunner’s side. Since such a dog is quite rare, hunters often go in pairs, taking turns circling the tree and shaking the bushes after the dog has treed.

What’s it take to train a dog that shows interest in squirrels? Mostly, lots of time in the squirrel woods. But trainer Randy Pannell speeds things up with a slingshot and steel pellets. “Just hit the tree near the squirrel, and it will bail out or take off,” Pannell said. “The dog gets a sight chase through the treetops or maybe a short ground chase. This works best with gray squirrels, especially the young ones. And there’s no problem with game laws while training dogs because you’re not killing or hunting the squirrels.”

Never study treetops when the dog is in sight and isn’t treeing. A perceptive dog believes you see the squirrel and will start treeing. And never bring the dog running by shooting at something other than a squirrel. Fooling a squirrel dog ruins it fast.

The Yockanookany fox squirrels are flat out the most colorful in America. Some are rusty orange, as expected, but others are decorated with white noses, feet and tail tips and, occasionally, black scalps. Others are solid black. Two taken during the 1981 Championship were black with white tips. Southern grays are smaller than Northern grays, but the fox squirrels are bigger. Some, according to reliable reports, grow half again as big as their Northern counterparts and range—according to very unreliable exaggerations—up to the size of house cats, groundhogs and raccoons.

Yankee squirrel hunters are treated so many different ways during the Yockanookany event that they have to like some. They travel from as far away as Michigan to enter the event. “We welcome Yankees; it’s only the Damn Yanks we don’t like,” said one young Southern belle with a wink.

The Top 10 Best Horror Games To Play This Halloween

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Humans are drawn to horror in all forms, real life and fiction. Perhaps it’s the desire to get as close to death as possible without actually dying; perhaps it’s the morality of life and knowing death comes for all of us; perhaps all our heads are just a little miswired. Whatever the reason, we flock to movies where supernatural entities and puppets on tricycles upend otherwise lovely lives, TV shows where murderous cults kill and stump local detectives, and books where the words on the page seem to follow you to bed. Games, however, go a step further, letting you enter the horror and control it (or become its victim), and you could argue that, as a result, they’re the most immersive form of fiction. 

We at Game Informer love a healthy dose of horror from time to time, and we often review games like this. Some terrify us. Others… don’t. And the best ones stick with us for years and years after the credits. Below is a list of 10 of the best horror games we’ve played recently. This isn’t an all-time list, so don’t fret if your personal favorite isn’t here. Instead, this is a collection of 10 horror games ordered by release date that we think are great for spooky season and beyond. Let us know in the comments what you would add to the list!

Village In The Shade

PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, Switch, PC

The cozy corner of the horror genre seems to have exploded overnight, and now tons of new slice-of-life horror games are in various stages of development. That said, the team behind newcomer The Village in the Shade is no stranger to the form; they made the Yomawari series, a cute-and-terrifying collection of creepy virtual tales. The Village in the Shade takes things back to the homestead, giving you (a child who emerged from a hole in the ground and was immediately given the responsibility of running an entire farm by yourself) the freedom to plant, sow, reap, ranch, and more. Just follow the important rules: don't go out at night, don't share secrets, and don't leave the village among others. You wouldn't want to break the rules, right? You could, theoretically, go about your idyllic rural living. But as you unravel the threads of what this village is, who you are, and why you're here, it's hard not to get drawn into the strange, mysterious, delightfully disturbing truths hiding in its shadows. Just don't forget to water your turnips before you go sneaking out again.

Tenebris Somnia

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC

Fans of survival horror and old-school FMV adventure games will find plenty to love (and fear) in Tenebris Somnia. When a young woman visits her ex-boyfriend’s apartment and discovers he's trapped in a hellish dream world, it kicks off a well-executed survival-horror trek of inventive puzzles and resource management to overcome dangerous monsters. High-production, live-action cutscenes help tell the story of a deteriorating relationship and the toll an indie horror film took on its creators, and they play an important role in gameplay. Tenebris Somnia may not look like much due to its classic pixelated presentation, but its scares and story captivate. – Our Review

Resident Evil Requiem

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC

Resident Evil Requiem is not just one of the best Resident Evil games, but it’s also one of the best horror games ever made. It features a unique dual-protagonist structure. Grace Ashcroft is timid, new to the whole “shooting zombies in the head while dealing with Umbrella viruses” thing, and is generally just trying to do her job. Leon S. Kennedy, however, is a veteran of all that and more. Requiem splits its runtime between the two characters, letting you experience both sides of Capcom’s marquee survival-horror style. Grace’s sections leave your hands clammy and your blood pressure high as you desperately try to survive. Leon’s sections leave your hands shaking with adrenaline and your blood pumping with excitement as he mows down zombies (sometimes literally, courtesy of those pesky chainsaws). The result is a Resident Evil game that blends two typical RE experiences – terrifying and tough or exhilarating over-the-top action – into one cohesive package. It’s perfect for someone who wants to feel scared and then soon after receive the catharsis of being the one in control, like being the tragic victim who dies in Act One of the horror movie and the last one standing who lives to tell the tale. – Our Review

Reanimal

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC

Little Nightmares developer Tarsier Studios has a knack for delivering creepy side-scrolling adventures, but Reanimal eschews any whimsy to become its scariest game yet. While mechanically it is similar to Little Nightmares and still puts small children in unimaginable peril, Reanimal is capital-H horror thanks largely to its terrifying threats and disturbing animal-themed imagery (this probably isn’t the game for animal lovers; some messed-up stuff happens to these critters). Escaping the game’s island setting is thrilling and chilling whether you’re playing alone or with a friend in co-op. Months after release, we’re still thinking about Reanimal’s memorable final sequence, which is worth the price of admission on its own. – Our Review

Silent Hill 2 (2024 Remake)

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC

2001’s Silent Hill 2 is considered one of the best horror video games of all time for good reason. At release, it was a graphical powerhouse, using the PlayStation 2's horsepower to create a setting that begged to be explored, even though every bone in your body said you shouldn’t. The remake from developer Bloober Team took on a big task in modernizing the game without losing the original’s soul, and it did a great job. Among all the recent Silent Hill releases (Silent Hill f, Silent Hill: Townfall), the remake is our favorite. The story of James Sunderland and his troubling relationship with his departed wife is complex, emotional, and – important for the purposes of this list – unsettling and scary. – Our Review

Alan Wake 2

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC

Alan Wake 2 is an excellent pick for some spooky madness, in the vein of David Lynch’s seminal Twin Peaks mystery-surrealist series. Don’t worry too much about the “2” part of the game’s title. You just need to know that the titular Alan Wake character is a not-great writer who acts like he’s really great, his wife is missing, and he got trapped in the ambiguous Dark Place. He’s only one half of the Alan Wake 2 equation, with his segments set in a dream-like version of New York City. Playing as him feels the most Twin Peaks-y. The other half is Saga, a police officer who arrives in the Pacific Northwest town of Bright Falls to investigate a murder that might be linked to a cult. You can expect more traditional survival-horror gameplay from her. But this is still a Remedy Entertainment game, and without spoiling anything, it goes places. And along the way, you can expect plenty of scares. Turn the lights down low (or off), light a candle, and sit back and enjoy one of Remedy’s best games. – Our Review 

Paranormasight: The Seven Mysteries of Honjo

Switch, PC, iOS, Android

Square Enix has gone two-for-two with its Paranormasight series, an anthology of stories about the strange and occult. And while the more recent Mermaid's Curse is a brilliant tale all its own, the first entry – The Seven Mysteries of Honjo – brings significantly more scares. A group of people in the city of Honjo find themselves caught in a spectral battle royale, using powerful objects and conditions to curse one another, with the allure of a prize at the end. Its magic isn't just in the flowchart timeline, where you can hop between different perspectives and protagonists to see how events play out from every angle, but also in the gorgeous stereoscopic environments and narrative puzzles. Adventure games are a classic genre, and Paranormasight will remind you of what the genre is capable of the moment you slowly spin your camera around, your eyes peeking out from behind your fingers. 

Signalis

PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, PC

Survival horror can contain multitudes, and as the years have worn on, the genre has grown to encompass a wide range of action-combat mechanics. Signalis feels like a re-centering in that respect. It's an isometric survival-horror indie project from developer rose-engine, filled with clever puzzles, scarce resources, limited inventory slots, and looming, oppressive dread. As the Replika unit Elster, you delve deep into a remote complex filled with horrors and monsters, searching for something, or someone, waiting down below. While love for classic horror games and other hallmarks of genre fiction will certainly draw you in, the stylish presentation and devastatingly human core will keep you forging ahead to the finale. Once you've breached the surface of Signalis' retro-future super-structures, you won't be able to turn back.

Immortality

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC, iOS, Android

In horror movies, audiences often groan at a protagonist for putting themselves in seemingly grave danger just to learn more about a cursed amulet or bloodthirsty killer. But in Immortality, you are the protagonist, and despite a lingering sense that you shouldn't be digging into the archives of these abandoned films, each eerie new scene makes you want to dive deeper. It's a game about Marisa Marcel, an actress who almost starred in three movies in the late 20th century, but has since disappeared. None of the movies were ever released due to mysterious circumstances, but you have access to the archived, unedited live-action footage, including behind-the-scenes conversations. By scrubbing through footage, you can select people or items in each scene and jump to random footage from any other movie that includes the same person or item. The approach to puzzle-solving is unique and keeps you appropriately disoriented. The mystery at the center is an unsettling treat to unravel, and scary in new and unexpected ways. Immortality is one of a kind and an easy recommendation for horror fans who want to try something a little different.

Dead By Daylight

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, PC, iOS, Android

In the decade since launch, Dead by Daylight has continued to succeed, thanks to a dedicated (and newcomer-friendly) fanbase and its towering roster of original and licensed personas from across pop culture horror. The asymmetrical multiplayer title has become an anthology of horror’s biggest faces, including Freddy Krueger, Ghostface, Pyramid Head, and even the Xenomorph. Whether you play as a Survivor, trying to escape all manner of nightmare locations through stealth and cunning, or as a Killer, whose experience feels more like a hunting club, you’ll understand why Dead by Daylight has become a recurring destination for fans of the gruesome and bizarre. 

Read about the making of Dead by Daylight and how it sunk its teeth into millions of players in our six-page exclusive feature in Issue 368 (p. 42), available in our magazine archive to anyone with a free Game Informer account.
