How they work

There are three different types of CBD: isolates, broad-spectrum, and full-spectrum. Full spectrum CBD products contain THC which can trigger an entourage effect in some people.

Safety and side effects

CBD will not get you “high”, unlike it’s cousin THC. Experts generally recognize CBD as safe, though there are some side effects that may occur, like fatigue, diarrhea, appetite changes, and weight changes.

There is some research that suggests CBD may interact with the liver, potentially interfering with the metabolism of other medications. If you take any medications or supplements, talk with a healthcare professional before taking any CBD or THC products.

Research notes that THC can induce symptoms of psychosis in people who do not already have the condition, and THC at high doses can increase anxiety. Other research also cautions against THC use if you have cardiovascular or respiratory conditions.

Will the new hemp ban affect access to CBD THC gummies?

Stricter legislation is coming in, and it may affect access to CBD and THC products. The federal government is changing how they define “hemp” at the federal level. The new definition seeks to ban hemp-derived THC, the cannabis compound that causes a “high.”

The current law states that hemp is any part of the cannabis sativa plant that contains up to 0.3% of THC by dry weight. The change will restrict any hemp-derived products to a total of 0.4 milligrams (mg) of THC per container. This is much stricter than the current law and will impact access to products with a higher THC content.

The new law also bans synthetic cannabinoids, affecting CBD manufacturing. Cannabinoids, like CBD or THC, occur naturally in the hemp plant. However, chemical synthesis is often necessary to make CBD available on a commercial level, and this is where we may see further restrictions.

States with established recreational and medical cannabis regulations may be less affected by the ban. Meanwhile, those without existing regulations, such as Texas and Kentucky, may see a bigger impact.

Full-spectrum and broad-spectrum CBD products are most likely to be affected as they contain trace amounts of THC.

The ban is currently due to take effect by mid-December. It’s unclear exactly how these changes will affect the industry, but until then, people can still purchase CBD THC gummies and other CBD products, like oils and topicals.

The October 29 announcement is therefore more than a listing headline. It is also a roadmap with elements at different stages, which need to be kept separate when assessing what customers can actually use.

The initial asset connection is clearly defined

The Sui Foundation announcement says Mercado Bitcoin will integrate SUI, USDC and USDsui, supporting purchases, sales and transfers on Sui. It also says USDsui will be eligible collateral in the exchange’s lending product.

Distribution and settlement solve different problems

Making an asset available through a familiar platform can reduce the number of steps a customer must navigate. It may connect a local payment method, an exchange account and an onchain transfer inside one service relationship.

Settlement infrastructure then determines how the asset moves after it reaches the blockchain. A fast transfer does not by itself remove account checks, conversion costs or the conditions attached to receiving funds at the other end.

This distinction is useful when comparing payment propositions. The total customer experience includes the first conversion, the blockchain movement and the final use of the money. Improving one stage can be meaningful without making every stage immediate or free.

Collateral eligibility is a separate product decision

A token becoming acceptable as collateral does not mean a lender values it without limits. The amount that can be borrowed depends on the lender’s rules, the asset’s valuation and the conditions under which a position can be liquidated.

A customer also needs to distinguish the stablecoin’s own backing and redemption terms from the credit terms of the loan. A token designed to track a currency can still be used inside a lending arrangement with variable costs and liquidation risk.

Those are general features of collateralized finance, not evidence that this particular integration has encountered a problem. They explain why a product launch is most informative when it includes the actual borrowing and custody terms alongside the list of supported assets.

Tokenized assets need legal rights as well as a network

Bringing an investment instrument onto a blockchain changes how records and transfers can be handled. It does not automatically determine the holder’s claim against the underlying issuer, who may buy it or how cash flows reach the investor.

That makes distribution permissions, asset documentation and servicing arrangements central to the next stage of the partnership. The technically available token and the legally accessible investment may have different boundaries.

Sui’s official publication archive places the announcement alongside its broader financial infrastructure work, but the commercial test remains specific: functioning products, clear eligibility and observable use.

The partnership gives Sui a route into an established customer relationship in Brazil. Gen Z is ditching the family SUV for the humble sedan U.S. carmakers gave up on. Now, Ford and GM are reconsidering

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Every generation of Americans seems to have a problem with the family car that came before it. Station wagons gave way to minivans, which eventually surrendered American driveways to SUVs. Now it may be Gen Z’s turn to turn up its nose at the family SUV.

Instead, it’s turning to the classic sedan.

A September analysis by insurance-comparison site Compare.com of more than 58 million auto insurance rate quotes found that eight of Gen Z’s 10 most commonly driven models were sedans, led by the Honda Civic, Honda Accord and Toyota Camry. The other two were full-size pickups, the Ford F-Series and Chevrolet Silverado. No SUV made the list.

The younger the generation, the deeper its apparent love affair with sedans, while SUVs steadily slide down the rankings. Six of millennials’ top 10 models were sedans, compared with five for Gen X and just three for baby boomers, whose top 10 included five SUVs.

And it’s not just one study. A February report from automotive research firm Escalent, which surveyed more than 1,000 teenagers ages 14 to 19, found that 51% envisioned driving a sedan in the future, compared with 31% who pictured themselves in an SUV and 14% who chose a truck.

A YouGov study published in May 2025 found a similar pattern among young adults already behind the wheel. Among Americans ages 18 to 29, 37% said their primary vehicle was a sedan, compared with 30% who drove an SUV or crossover.

Why Gen Z is choosing sedans

So what does Gen Z see in the humble sedan?

For one thing, the price tag. According to Kelley Blue Book data from Cox Automotive, a new compact car sold for an average of $27,977 in September, compared with $37,614 for a compact SUV and $50,630 for a midsize SUV. That gap matters for a generation that, as Fortune has reported, says it is struggling to get ahead financially despite starting to build wealth earlier than older generations did.

Younger buyers are also showing signs of keeping their car purchases modest. As Fortune previously reported, 55% of Gen Z respondents in a 2023 Cars.com survey had bought a used car. More recent LendingTree data found that 51.5% of Gen Z borrowers with active auto loans originally borrowed less than $25,000, compared with 36.2% of millennials and 31.4% of Gen Xers. For a generation watching its budget, an affordable sedan may make more financial sense than a larger SUV.

There’s also the question of how much space younger Americans actually need. A Pew Research Center analysis of federal survey data found that Americans ages 20 to 39 had or planned to have an average of 1.8 children in 2023, down from 2.3 in 2012.

SUVs aren’t going anywhere

For all Gen Z’s apparent enthusiasm for sedans, SUVs still dominate American driveways. A November 2025 YouGov analysis found that 41% of U.S. car owners primarily drove an SUV or crossover, compared with 30% who drove sedans. And among SUV owners planning to buy another vehicle within a year, 66% intended to stick with an SUV.

Family needs could also change the equation. A 2024 Mazda survey of 1,776 U.S. parents found that 96% said parenthood had influenced their vehicle choices, with 48% reporting a stronger preference for more seating and 46% for additional cargo space.

“We think this is more of a generational shift, akin to the shift from station wagons to minivans in the ’80s and from minivans to SUVs in the ’90s and 2000s,” K.C. Boyce, vice president of automotive and mobility at Escalent, told Fortune.

Boyce acknowledged that some Gen Z drivers may gravitate toward SUVs as they start families, but he believes the generation’s interest in sedans is strong enough to boost sedan sales among its members.

Sean Tucker, managing editor at Cox Automotive’s Kelley Blue Book, isn’t so sure.

“Affordability is likely the driving force,” Tucker told Fortune. He suspects Gen Z would buy SUVs if more affordable options were available, describing the popularity of taller vehicles as a “height war” in which drivers increasingly want to see over the cars ahead of them.

Detroit’s change of heart

For Detroit, the stakes go well beyond a generational preference.

After years of favoring more profitable SUVs and trucks, Ford ended production of its last U.S.-market sedan, the Fusion, in 2020. GM’s Chevrolet ended production of its last mass-market sedan, the Malibu, in 2024.

“The sedan market is very vibrant,” Ford CEO Jim Farley told Automotive News at the Detroit Auto Show in January. “It’s not that there isn’t a market there. It’s just we couldn’t find a way to compete and be profitable.”

Now Detroit is having second thoughts. Ford and GM are exploring a possible return to sedans as consumers increasingly look for more affordable vehicles, The Wall Street Journal reported in February. GM President Mark Reuss said at a company town hall that he “would kill to have a hybrid-electric sedan.” And Ford told dealers in February that it plans to launch five all-new models priced under $40,000 by 2030 across its lineup of “cars, trucks, SUVs, vans,” according to Automotive News.

Boyce warned that American automakers need to rethink their product strategies now or risk losing the next generation of buyers to Japanese and Korean competitors that still offer sedans.

Tucker, meanwhile, sees a potential solution in electric vehicles. Their flexible platforms can reduce the cost of developing different models, he said, pointing to Ford’s Universal Electric Vehicle Platform, which will underpin the Ford Fathom, a midsize electric pickup with a starting price just under $30,000 that’s slated for pre-orders in early 2027. Ford “could easily develop it into a sedan if the market showed it wanted one,” he said.

The question remains: Will Gen Z still want sedans once kids and bigger paychecks enter the picture?

The announcement leaves an important operational detail at account level. It does not publish one universal withdrawal deadline, so users should not assume a date circulating elsewhere applies to their circumstances.

The restriction covers the whole platform

KuCoin’s official notice attributes the decision to applicable legal and regulatory requirements. It says affected customers will receive notifications containing closure instructions and relevant deadlines. Customers who have not received a notification are told that no action is required on the basis of this notice.

The statement does not identify one particular enforcement order as the sole trigger. It should therefore not be expanded into a claim that a named government compelled the exit unless further documentation establishes that connection.

The published decision is nevertheless broad: the withdrawal from the two markets is not limited to a particular token listing or an individual trading product.

Closing exposure is different from transferring a balance

An account can contain more than assets that are immediately available to send elsewhere. A trader may have open orders, borrowed funds or positions that need to be closed before the remaining balance can be withdrawn. The exact procedure depends on the product and the account’s state.

That is why account-specific instructions matter. A simple transfer deadline would not explain how to deal with every possible obligation. It also would not identify which assets or networks remain available during a wind-down.

Customers should use the official account channel to establish those details. The exchange’s terms of use provide the wider contractual context, but a general terms page is not a substitute for the notification governing a particular closure.

Global brands still operate through regional boundaries

A blockchain transaction can cross borders without changing its basic technical format. An exchange’s relationship with a customer is different. Access depends on the legal entities, services and jurisdictional restrictions under which that relationship operates.

This creates a practical difference between ownership of a token and access to one venue that supports it. Losing access to a platform does not itself change the token’s network rules, but it can change how a customer trades, converts or withdraws that holding.

For businesses integrating exchange services, jurisdiction checks cannot be a one-time onboarding exercise. A later restriction can affect treasury operations, recurring transfers and the availability of a previously convenient source of liquidity.

Clear notices matter during an exchange exit

An orderly closure should make the remaining steps understandable: which positions must be settled, which transfers can still be requested and how exceptions are handled. Ambiguity can turn a manageable deadline into a rushed operational decision.

It can also create an opening for impersonators. KuCoin’s announcement specifically directs users to official channels. A message offering to accelerate an account exit should not be trusted merely because it references the real withdrawal from Russia and Belarus.

The relevant next evidence is the implementation of the closure process. The notice establishes the policy; account communications establish the individual timetable. Until those are read together, it would be misleading to describe every affected user as already unable to retrieve funds or to promise that all accounts will follow the same path.

For market observers, the story is another example of access risk at the service layer. The underlying assets can remain transferable while a particular commercial gateway changes the countries it serves.

HTX Ends UK Activity as FCA Secures Court Undertakings on Crypto Promotions

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The FCA says HTX is no longer active in the UK following a settlement over financial promotions. The agreement does not constitute an admission of wrongdoing.

The Financial Conduct Authority says HTX has ceased UK activity after a settlement in litigation over cryptoasset promotions. The October 29 announcement describes restrictions on attracting new UK users and accepting deposits, with existing customers directed to the exchange’s official arrangements for residual assets.

The outcome shows why an exchange’s global availability and its ability to serve a particular jurisdiction need to be assessed separately.

The settlement changes the customer relationship

In its announcement, the FCA said it had secured High Court undertakings that HTX would not knowingly promote cryptoasset services unlawfully to UK consumers. New UK customers cannot register, and deposits are no longer available.

The regulator said HTX would not target, solicit or actively seek UK consumers. It also made clear that the settlement does not constitute an admission of liability or wrongdoing by Huobi Global or another person or entity.

That qualification matters. A negotiated restriction and an admission of the regulator’s allegations are different legal outcomes, even when the commercial result is a withdrawal from a market.

The proceedings have a longer history

The FCA’s published proceedings page records a sequence of court documents, beginning with the October 2025 claim and extending through the October 2026 orders. It provides the procedural background behind the latest announcement.

This is relevant because enforcement against an overseas platform is rarely a single action. Identifying defendants, serving proceedings and obtaining effective restrictions can involve several stages.

For other crypto businesses, the operational implication is that jurisdictional controls need to be reflected in more than the wording of a disclaimer. Marketing channels, onboarding systems and service access all form part of the practical customer relationship.

A platform also needs consistent information across those channels. A registration screen that rejects an applicant while a promotion still invites that same customer to join creates avoidable confusion. The practical task is to align the messages, account permissions and support responses so that the legal restriction has an intelligible effect for the people encountering it.

Permission checks need to match the service

The FCA’s warning page for HTX gives the regulator’s view of the firm’s permission status and the limitations on UK consumer protections. A recognizable exchange brand should not be treated as evidence that every service it offers carries the same safeguards in every country.

Even where a business has some form of registration, the scope matters. A permission for one activity does not necessarily cover another, and a registration entry is not a general guarantee of asset recovery.

For consumers, the most useful check is therefore the exact legal entity, the activity being offered and the relevant regulator’s current record, rather than a broad claim that a group is regulated somewhere.

Residual balances are a separate operational issue

Closing a market to new business and handling existing customer assets are distinct tasks. Customers need accurate information about the arrangements applicable to their own accounts, including any service limitations or deadlines.

The FCA directs UK users to HTX’s published information. That is more reliable than assuming that a general headline creates an identical withdrawal procedure for every account. The announcement itself does not provide a universal deadline or promise of instant processing.

The wider significance is a clearer boundary around the UK business. For the exchange, the next measure of an orderly exit is how consistently it implements the restrictions and communicates with affected users. For the industry, the case illustrates that operating across borders does not make local promotion rules irrelevant.

SEC Charges BG Wealth and DSJ Exchange Over Alleged $130M Crypto Scheme

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The SEC alleges BG Wealth and DSJ Exchange raised more than $130 million using false filings and trading claims. The civil complaint is an allegation, not a final judgment.

The US Securities and Exchange Commission has brought a civil case against BG Wealth Sharing and DSJ Exchange, alleging that the businesses used official-looking filings to help sell an investment program built around purported crypto trading signals.

An October 30 litigation release details the complaint filed on October 29. The case puts a familiar distinction at its center: appearing in a government database does not mean a regulator has approved an investment.

What the SEC alleges

According to the SEC’s litigation release, the defendants raised more than $130 million from tens of thousands of retail investors. The regulator alleges that false Form ADV and Form D filings were presented as evidence of legitimacy, alongside promises of extraordinary, risk-free trading returns.

The SEC says neither entity was registered with it. It is seeking remedies including injunctions, disgorgement and civil penalties. The disputed filings have been removed from the Commission’s website, according to the release.

These are allegations in civil proceedings. The announcement does not establish a final court finding, a criminal conviction or a confirmed recovery amount for investors.

A filing is not an endorsement

The SEC’s guidance on false registration claims warns that the appearance of an entity or document in its systems can be used to mislead the public. The important question is what the particular filing actually means, rather than whether a screenshot bears a familiar logo.

A notice submitted by a business can contain information supplied by that business. It is not automatically a regulator’s independent assessment of solvency, trading performance or the safety of customer assets.

That distinction becomes especially important when a promoter uses a real database link to support a much broader claim about licensing or approval.

The identity behind the document matters as much as the document itself. A filing for one entity does not validate another business with a similar name. Nor does a registration claim explain who holds customer funds. Comparing the contracting entity, payment recipient and service provider can expose gaps that a polished presentation leaves out.

Trading signals can hide the basic investment questions

Sophisticated terminology can make a proposition sound more precise without making its economics clearer. Investors still need to understand who controls the money, how returns are generated, what losses are possible and how withdrawals are funded.

A displayed account balance is also different from an independently verified asset. If a platform controls both the dashboard and the explanation of returns, apparent consistency on a screen is weak evidence that the underlying activity occurred.

The SEC separately maintains an alert on misuse of exempt reporting adviser filings. Its relevance is practical: a technically authentic filing can still be used to create a misleading overall impression.

The case now turns on evidence and remedies

The legal process will determine how the allegations are resolved and what remedies, if any, are ordered. Even when a regulator seeks repayment, the amount ultimately available can depend on locating assets, establishing claims and completing the relevant proceedings.

For readers following the case, verified court developments will be more useful than claims circulating through former investor groups. A complaint, an asset restraint and a distribution to victims are separate milestones.

The wider lesson is specific rather than technological. Crypto trading language, automated signals and public filings do not remove the need to examine the legal entity and the investment mechanism. The SEC’s case challenges whether those signals of legitimacy were used to conceal the absence of the protections investors believed they had.

America’s EV Adoption Problem Isn’t Going Away

America’s EV market has made progress, but convincing traditional car buyers to switch remains difficult. As we’ve discussed before, electrification depends on more than adding vehicles to dealership lots. Charging access, affordability, and consumer confidence also shape demand.

A study from HERE Technologies and SBD Automotive puts that challenge into perspective. Its extensive report found that just 11% of surveyed US owners of gasoline and diesel vehicles consider an EV as their most likely next purchase or lease. The result suggests that growing infrastructure and wider model availability have yet to translate into strong buying intent.

Existing EV Owners See Things Differently

The study surveyed 4,058 drivers across eight markets and found a difference between current EV owners and people who have not made the switch. In the US, 85% of EV owners rate charging coverage as good or very good, up from 72% in 2025. That is the highest satisfaction figure among the surveyed markets.

Only 42% of non-EV owners give charging coverage a positive rating. The 43-point gap suggests that direct experience may make drivers more comfortable with the network than they were beforehand.

And that’s despite the massive infrastructure progress we’ve seen this year. US public charging points grew by more than 14% between 2025 and 2026, while average charger power increased from 65kW to 83kW, a 28% rise.

In terms of costs, well, that’s a different problem. Just 34% of US respondents believe EVs are cheaper to own than gasoline-powered vehicles. Another 29% say costs are about the same, while 28% consider EVs more expensive.

There is a brighter picture among people who already own electric cars: 77% of US EV owners say they are likely to buy another one. The report also pointed out that the end of purchase incentives in late 2025 contributed to dampened EV sales into 2026.

Next Technology Authorizes Sale or Pledge of 5,833 Bitcoin After Risk Review

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Next Technology has authorized selling or pledging its reported 5,833 BTC. The decision creates flexibility; it does not confirm that a transaction has occurred.

Next Technology Holding has given management permission to reduce or finance its Bitcoin position after a board review of custody and market risks. The company reported approximately 5,833 BTC when announcing the decision on October 30.

For the wider market, the important distinction is between authorization and execution. A board can create the option to sell an asset without deciding when, at what price or through which counterparty a transaction will happen.

The board has approved several routes

The company’s announcement, distributed through GlobeNewswire, permits transactions in one or more tranches. The stated options include exchange sales, negotiated block trades and pledging Bitcoin to obtain financing.

Next Technology cited the recent Binance security incident and broader technological concerns in explaining the review. It specifically said none of its Bitcoin was held on or through Binance.

The release does not establish a completed disposal or a signed financing agreement. It also gives no assurance about the timing, price, amount or completion of a future transaction. Those qualifications belong at the center of any interpretation of the announcement.

A sale and a pledge have different consequences

Selling Bitcoin exchanges price exposure for cash. Once a sale settles, subsequent gains or losses on the disposed coins no longer accrue to the company. The trade-off depends on the price received and how the proceeds are used.

A pledge instead uses the coins as collateral. That can provide liquidity while retaining some economic exposure, but the borrower also takes on contractual obligations. Interest, collateral requirements, maturity and a lender’s enforcement rights can become as important as the asset’s market price.

Without those terms, it is impossible to treat a potential borrowing as equivalent to a sale, or to determine which route would leave shareholders with greater flexibility.

The sequence could matter too. A company might use financing to meet a near-term need and later reduce that borrowing through asset sales. Alternatively, it could sell only enough to fund a particular commitment. Neither sequence is announced here, but both illustrate why the full authorized balance should not be mistaken for a fixed disposal schedule.

The equity story extends beyond coin count

A corporate Bitcoin holding is only one part of an investment in the company that owns it. As Investor.gov explains, shares represent ownership in a business. They do not give each shareholder direct control of particular treasury assets.

The value reaching ordinary shareholders depends on liabilities, operating expenses, financing terms and the number of shares outstanding. A headline multiplying a reported coin balance by a market price therefore cannot settle the investment case.

Investors would need to know what restrictions already apply to the assets, whether proceeds are earmarked for specific commitments and how any financing changes the company’s overall capital structure.

Execution disclosures will carry more weight

The next meaningful development would be a disclosure identifying a completed sale, an executed pledge or a decision to retain the position. Price, quantity and resulting cash or debt balances would turn the board’s general authority into something that can be assessed.

The company’s reference to quantum computing is a statement of its risk considerations, not evidence that Bitcoin’s cryptography has been broken. A corporate risk review and a demonstrated technical exploit require different standards of proof.

Until transaction details emerge, treating all 5,833 BTC as immediate market supply would go beyond the announcement. The present news is that management has more options, with the consequences still dependent on what it actually does.

The First Billboard: All That Was ‘New, Bright & Interesting on the Boards’

On Nov. 1, 1894, Billboard published its first issue, one that’s vastly different from today’s magazine, as well as Billboard’s expanded platforms. The premiere issue wasn’t even focused on the music industry; Billboard’s eventual trademark concentration on music, and, of course, charts, would develop over decades.

The First Issue

Before it became a weekly publication, and a 24/7 presence online, Billboard launched as Billboard Advertising. Its original mission? One that helps explain its name: “A monthly résumé of all that is new, bright and interesting on the boards.” (Circus promoter Jared Bell is widely credited as a pioneer of 19th century marketing through billboards, beginning in the 1830s.)

Upon its premiere, the eight-page magazine (priced at 10 cents an issue, or, 99 cents per year), was “devoted to the interests of advertisers, poster printers, bill posters, advertising agents & secretaries of fairs.”

The magazine’s first cover subject was R.C. Campbell, then-president of the Associated Bill Posters’ Association. “No more fitting tribute can be paid to Mr. Campbell than to state that he is a man of infinite resource, progressive ideas and tireless industry,” Billboard noted in the inaugural issue.

“In selecting his photograph for the first number of this magazine, the Editor was actuated by the fact that he is the acknowledged leader, the first and foremost and most eminent man in the field which we aim to cover.”

The Charts

The magazine’s first national music chart, the 10-position National List of Best Selling Retail Records, appeared in the July 27, 1940, issue. Previously, Billboard had highlighted the national Sheet Music Best Sellers, Records Most Popular on Music Machines (compiled from national reports from phonograph operators), and Songs With the Most Radio Plugs on a handful of New York radio stations. The National List of Best Selling Retail Records, however, was the first to poll retailers nationwide on record sales.

In more recent years, Billboard’s charts offerings have expanded to include surveys covering social and streaming activity, with streams added to the Hot 100’s weekly tabulation. Billboard has also launched the weekly Billboard Artist 100, expanded touring tallies, weekly surveys ranking songwriters and producers, the Billboard Global 200 and Billboard Global Excl. U.S. lists and more.

Southwest says a widebody order has “no credibility.” Six months before it ended free bags, it said no work was underway. Denials from Dallas expire fast.

A Denial With A Short Shelf Life

On its October 22nd, 2026 earnings call, CEO, Bob Jordan, shot down reports of a widebody order as having “no credibility.” The reports came from a Reuters exclusive two weeks earlier, citing three unnamed sources who said Southwest was looking at Boeing 787s for a long-haul push, possibly leased or second-hand, with a launch window of roughly three years. Operating aircraft with more than 175 seats would send the airline back to its pilots, and the pilot contract likely opens next year. Another 737 variant would be “significantly easier” than a widebody. Long-haul flying would be upside “if we decide to pursue that.” My eyerolls are likely audible at this point.

We Have Heard This Before

I know how this goes because I have watched the free bag story (and every other unique characteristic that Southwest would “never” change) play out. In July 2024, Jordan told investors that bags flying free was the number one reason customers choose Southwest, behind only fare and schedule. On September 17, 2024, a Southwest spokesperson told The Points Guy there was “no work currently underway” to change the policy, and Jordan, pressed on the question, would only say never say never, as CBS reported.

Six months later, on March 11, 2025, Southwest ended the policy. The airline set fees of $35 for the first checked bag and $45 for the second on flights booked on or after May 28, 2025. Jordan’s explanation was that bags flying free was not part of the airline’s DNA, and that open seating was not either. The bags went first, and assigned seating arrived on January 27, 2026, with eight boarding groups, four fare tiers and extra-legroom rows on every flight. Basic fares board last. Open seating, which was the other half of the cattle-call charm, was gone. This has all been covered over the last 18 months but it’s important preamble when considering the next logical step for the airline, and the claims it is denying. What remains uniquely Southwest is one fleet type, and a partially point-to-point map and a reputation for friendliness, and the fleet may be next. Jordan said Southwest is not building toward any one thing, but the addition of 11 lounges, a premium credit card, and first class tell a different story.

It’s a sea of sameness (to steal another industry trope.) Delta and United sell the same choices at similar prices, and Southwest sells them with a smaller map and limited international reach. Every concession after the bag fee was an admission that the discount model was not enough on its own, even though I personally believe that it was.

Why Global Is The Only Growth Left

The numbers explain why management keeps moving the goalposts. In the third quarter, revenue per available seat mile jumped a record 21.6%, but capacity fell 0.4% and unit costs excluding fuel rose 3.8%. Capacity for 2026 is up only about 1.2%. That mix tells me growth came from price and fees, not from flying more, but in order to keep up that frothy pace it will have to find new markets to enter.

Domestic markets are mature, and Southwest’s biggest rivals already own the best international partners. Southwest has partnerships with Icelandair, China Airlines, EVA Air and Turkish Airlines, and Jordan said international loyalty partnerships and partner redemption launch next spring (I can’t wait to see what the redemptions look like.) Partners only go so far, though. Will some EVA Air customers book connecting flights on Southwest for its customers, maybe, probably, but that’s not going to move the needle.

Chicago Midway can technically accommodate a 787, as can Dallas Love, and at LAX and Baltimore that’s a very easy fit. But all of those markets have real competition from major carriers. Other Southwest hubs like Fort Lauderdale face serious challenges with nearby Miami, Houston Hobby against United at Intercontinental, Denver for both international carriers and United.

But Southwest wanted to assimilate the carrier to every other carrier so it has to compete with every other carrier too. And in that regard, I can’t see a lounge program as aggressive as 11 locations working without it. If the fleet was exclusively Airbus, the international case is easier with longer legs and a JetBlue-like model (though that’s not working out well for them at the moment and they already have premium cards, lounges, and international partners.)

Unless Southwest sees unprecedented growth from what it’s already doing – possible, but not probable – this is the last frontier. So why deny it? Doesn’t that indicate a lack of forward thinking?

Conclusion

I will take the other side of the denial. My prediction is that Southwest announces a widebody order or lease within eighteen months of the pilot contract opening, and that Jordan or his successor will explain it as a natural evolution of the brand. The pattern is set. Every sacred cow gets slain once the numbers demand it, and the statement of the day is always true until the day it is not. Travelers should treat Southwest’s promises the way they treat any airline’s, as information about the current quarter and not a commitment about the next one.

TL;DR: Binance has set out a phased withdrawal restart and says it has replaced 95% of affected assets. Availability still depends on the individual asset and network.

Binance’s recovery effort has moved from a promise to replace missing assets toward the harder operational test of letting customers move funds again. Its November 1 update set a 06:00–09:00 UTC window for the first stage of withdrawal restoration, while stressing that reopening would happen progressively.

The distinction matters. A timetable for restoring services is useful information, but it should not be read as confirmation that every listed withdrawal route is available to every customer.

What the latest recovery update establishes

In its fourth recovery announcement, Binance said approximately 95% of the impacted amount had been replenished, compared with 90% in the previous update. It attributed the outstanding portion to less-liquid tokens that take longer to obtain at scale.

Customers need to check the specific asset-network combination in the withdrawal interface rather than infer availability from a general reopening notice. The company also warned that initial processing could take longer than usual.

Those statements describe the exchange’s recovery progress. They do not provide an independently audited assessment of every remaining operational or financial exposure.

Backing and access answer different questions

Binance’s proof-of-reserves explanation describes a system for showing asset backing and allowing customers to check that their balances were included in a liabilities snapshot. Such verification can help assess a particular set of balances at a particular time.

Withdrawal availability asks a different question: can the platform process a customer’s chosen transfer now? An exchange can report adequate backing while temporarily restricting access during security work. Conversely, a functioning withdrawal button alone does not describe the full condition of a balance sheet.

The useful assessment therefore combines the financial evidence with observable service performance. Neither should be treated as a complete substitute for the other.

The timing of evidence matters as well. A reserve snapshot taken before an incident cannot establish what happened afterward. A later snapshot is more relevant to replenishment, while a record of completed transfers addresses access. Reporting those dates together would make it easier to distinguish a lasting improvement from figures describing different stages of the recovery.

Why the remaining assets can take longer

Replacing a thinly traded token creates a different execution problem from acquiring a widely traded asset. A large purchase can consume available offers and move the price, while an over-the-counter transaction still requires a willing counterparty and completed settlement.

That makes the last portion of a replenishment exercise potentially slower than the first. The percentage completed is helpful, but it does not measure the difficulty of the work still outstanding.

For customers holding those assets, the practical milestone is restoration of the relevant transfer route. A broad recovery percentage should not obscure differences between tokens, networks or processing queues.

The next evidence will be operational

The most informative follow-up will be a clear record of which services have reopened, how pending transfers are handled and when remaining restrictions are removed. A post-incident review would also help distinguish the initial entry point from any weaknesses in approval and monitoring systems.

Market confidence is unlikely to be rebuilt through a single headline. Reliable withdrawals, understandable status reporting and evidence that controls have been repaired all address different parts of the problem.

For now, the update marks a concrete step in the recovery plan. It remains a staged restoration, with the condition of each withdrawal route more useful than any assumption that a general reopening announcement means normal service everywhere.

Can Emirates Make Denver Work Without A Smaller Jet?

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Emirates named Denver as a candidate for its next US route. Without a small widebody to test the market, every new city is an all-in bet, and Denver is high stakes.

Emirates Puts Denver On The List

At Routes World in Frankfurt, Emirates President Tim Clark named Denver International Airport, Detroit and Philadelphia as the next candidates for nonstop Dubai flights in the United States. It would be the first new US station since Miami returned in June 2021; Emirates currently serves 12 US airports. Clark would not say which aircraft would fly the route though that largely depends on the size of the market and distance. Of the three possible destinations, the distances are 7,758 miles to Denver, 7,083 to Detroit and 6,928 to Philadelphia.

Emirates Denver service has been rumored for a while. Former DEN CEO, Phil Washington, said in spring 2025 that the airport’s next new route would “likely be Dubai with Emirates,” while Emirates said it had no official plans. Emirates and United have been codeshare partners since 2023, a tie-up Live And Let’s Fly detailed when the partnership was first announced.

An Equipment Challenge

Emirates’ unique fleet gives it an incredible advantage in large markets, but makes smaller market expansion a challenge. According to Emirates’ own fleet figures from late 2025, 235 of its 269 aircraft, or 87%, were A380s and 777-300ERs. Those big birds carry roughly 350 to 500 seats depending on the configuration. Emirates had just 13 A350-900s at that point, and it has no 787 in service even though 35 are on order (though many have speculated they will be converted to 777-X orders assuming it eventually passes certification.)

That informs the decision on every new expansion city. Most airlines test a new market with a smaller jet, run it at three or four weekly flights and grow it if demand holds. A perfect example of that was British Airways growth at Pittsburgh. It started with subsidized service 4x weekly on a Boeing 787-8, but based on its success moved to 5x, then 6x, and now daily service and fluctuates between the 787-8 and 787-9 with some frequencies operating a 787-10 or very occasionally a 777-200. Emirates cannot do that. Its smallest widebody, the A350-900, is only now reaching the US network, entering service to Orlando on November 1 with 298 seats. A 777-300ER to Denver means filling roughly 350 or more seats, on a route 7,758 miles long, departing an airport at about 5,400 feet of elevation. High elevation (hot and high) hurts takeoff performance, and it makes a full-length route even harder especially at a frequency of more than 5x weekly.

The ideal test would be a 787-8 if it had the legs, a smaller airplane at the right size to prove the market and to link Denver to Dubai without staking a big fleet commitment on it. But Emirates has no 787s flying (it may never operate the type depending on the order book swaps I mentioned before), and the reality is that with a 20% reserve range variance, the altitude, an especially in the summer, it can’t make the trip even if they had it.

That means a Denver bet has to be a larger aircraft than ideal if it makes the cut. The other mentioned possible destinations come with their own challenges. Philadelphia has more international service but also a much larger market. It faces flights on Qatar for connecting global service along with a heavy American Airlines slate, British Airways, Lufthansa, and Aer Lingus for Europe/Middle East, and connections to Asia or Africa. Detroit has an even smaller market with more competition from Air France, Royal Jordanian, Turkish, and a heavy Delta tilt.

Does Denver Have The Demand

Denver does not have nonstops to the Middle East or Africa, and Narita is its only Asian destination, flown by United on a 787. DEN served 34 international destinations in 19 countries in 2025. The question is how many people in Denver need to get to Dubai or beyond, and cannot do it more easily another way.

Travelers who can already connect through Frankfurt or Munich on Lufthansa, through Istanbul on Turkish, or through Toronto on Air Canada, and favor Star Alliance, have little reason to switch. Many of the rest are captive to United, either because of status or because of corporate contracts, and United already flies its own long-haul routes to Europe and Tokyo. Emirates is left with a smaller pool of travelers who are not tied to a hub carrier and want to go to India, the Gulf, Africa or the Indian Ocean, along with the premium leisure market that wants Emirates’ product.

What Emirates brings from the other end is a global network. Denver would feed Dubai’s connections to South Asia and Africa, and Emirates has the reach to sell Denver as a gateway to places United cannot. But that seems like a tough challenge. I’m a fan of Denver and have visited several times personally, I grew up in nearby Omaha, my brother lived there for some time – Denver has plenty to offer to the world. But I am trying to imagine the pitch to a family in Singapore, or a upwardly mobile couple in Mumbai. Can Emirates convince locals in the UAE to choose Denver over Switzerland for those who want to hit the slopes? I’m not so sure.

Conclusion

My prediction is that Denver stays a candidate, but not yet a route, until Emirates has a smaller widebody aircraft in meaningful numbers. Then again, some markets where Emirates has been successful would shock readers of this site. Manchester, England had three and sometimes four daily A380s and is not a particularly premium market with even more competition. If Emirates does move forward with Denver, I expect the airline to start with a seasonal schedule, probably timed to the winter ski market, and to treat the first year as a test. Emirates built its network on massive airframes and highly premium configurations, which is great for flooding a market where you can be successful and challenging to experiment. Then again, financial performance for Emirates is less public and pressured than publicly traded companies so maybe it doesn’t matter anyway.

The 10 best places to visit in November 2026

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

November is peak shoulder season: The weather can be unpredictable (but you can get really lucky), crowds are small nearly everywhere and prices are often the lowest they'll be all year for many destinations. It is an ideal time to travel in many ways, if you're willing to risk less-than-ideal weather in some places.

Thanksgiving also takes place in the U.S., and if you are not obligated to visit family, you can enjoy a wonderful four-day vacation without missing any school or work. And for those who love Christmas markets and lights, several spots start their displays at the end of the month.

Here are 10 great destinations to consider for your November getaway.

Marrakech and the Atlas Mountains, Morocco

MICHAEL HANSON/GETTY IMAGES

This stunning desert and mountain region enjoys cooler weather in November that's ideal for hiking in the Atlas Mountains. Meanwhile, the nearby city of Marrakech is as vibrant as ever, with more people coming out in the less-than-scorching temps that linger all summer. Wander the souk, stroll the gorgeous gardens, and visit architectural highlights without breaking a sweat.

Nov. 18 is Morocco's Independence Day, and this year's Marrakech Coffee & Tea Festival will run Nov. 5-7, with more than 400 exhibitors and various workshops and classes. This year's Marrakech International Film Festival runs from Nov. 20–28.

Use points to stay at the Park Hyatt Marrakech, which opened in 2025 (rates from $594 or 25,000 World of Hyatt points per night), or check out the new Riad Beij, in the heart of the Medina (rates from $168 per night). To stay in the Atlas Mountains, you can use Virgin Red points at the luxe Virgin Limited Edition Kasbah Tamadot (rates from $577 or 140,000 Virgin Red points per night).

TPG tip: Marrakech has increased seasonal direct flight access, including a direct flight from Newark on United Airlines, which launched in 2024, and from Atlanta on Delta Air Lines, which debuted at the end of 2025.

Oaxaca, Mexico

JAN SOCHOR/GETTY IMAGES

Oaxaca's celebration of Dia de los Muertos, or Day of the Dead, is legendary, especially in Oaxaca City, where festivities and commemorations take place every year from Oct. 31 to Nov. 2. Expect elaborate costumes, parades, street festivals, cemetery and altar visits, and plenty of music, food and drinks.

After the city festivities, plan to stay at one of the region's design-forward hotels. Boutique hotel 7 Casa Laurel opened in April this year, and Casa Yati opened in Ventanilla on the beach last summer. La Valise Mazunte is a member of Small Luxury Hotels of the World with just six suites and a villa (rates from $400 or 130,000 Hilton Honors points per night).

TPG tip: You can fly directly to Puerto Escondido on United Airlines' Houston-to-Puerto Escondido route, which debuted in 2025. American Airlines launched its direct flights to Puerto Escondido from Dallas-Fort Worth in December 2025.

Bangkok

PRASIT PHOTO/GETTY IMAGES

November is the very beginning of the dry season in Thailand, so it's a good time to beat the winter crowds and score good airfare and hotel pricing. Nov. 24 is Loy Krathong, the beautiful floating lantern festival that lights up the Bangkok waterways on the night of the full moon in the 12th month of the Thai lunar calendar. Various events happen along the Chao Phraya River, in public parks and at cultural landmarks.

The city, which has several new hotels this year, with something for almost every loyalist. Voco Bangkok Surawong opened in January 2026 (rates from 19,000 IHG One Rewards and $137 per night). Canopy by Hilton Bangkok Sukhumvit opened in August 2026 (rates from 45,000 Hilton Honors points and $170 per night). The long-delayed Fairmont Bangkok Sukhumvit finally opened, also in August, with LEED Gold certification, a jasmine-inspired spa, seven food and beverage outlets and 474 rooms (rates from 12,345 ALL Accor points and $278 per night).

TPG tip: Last year, United Airlines launched a new daily flight to Bangkok via Hong Kong from Los Angeles and San Francisco, making it the only domestic airline to offer a daily one-stop flight to Bangkok from the U.S.

The Amazon, Brazil

FG TRADE/GETTY IMAGES

Looking for an adventure? November is the last month of the dry season to visit the Amazon, so there's still time to squeeze in an adventure before the end of the year. If colorful birds, jaguars, river dolphins and caiman sightings aren't enough for you, the first-ever Global Citizen music festival in the Amazon, in Belem, is Nov. 1. Global Citizen Festival Amazonia brings Brazilian music stars Anitta, Seu Jorge and Gaby Amarantos and Coldplay's Chris Martin together with Indigenous leaders to amplify climate action campaigns.

TPG tip: Avianca flies to Belem via Bogota, Colombia, from New York's John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), and LATAM flies there through Sao Paulo/Guarulhos International Airport (GRU). Book a room at Ibis Styles Belem Batista Campos ($67 per night) or Grand Mercure Belem do Para ($474 per night). Both are members of Accor Live Limitless.

St. Barts

PER BREIEHAGEN/GETTY IMAGES

If you've dreamed of a vacation in St. Barts but thought you couldn't afford it, November may be the best time to visit the gorgeous Caribbean island. You'll enjoy warm weather, beat the winter crowds and enjoy better prices on hotels and flights. And while the French island is known for always having excellent food, November brings even more culinary star power to the isle.

The Saint-Barth Gourmet Festival is Nov. 9-15 and features five chefs from Michelin-starred restaurants, including chef Amaury Bouhours of Restaurant Le Meurice Alain Ducasse and Thibault Sombardier of Les Parisiens.

TPG tip: Getting to St. Barts can be a bit of a journey if you don't have a private jet (ahem), but Cape Air flies twice daily from well-connected St. Thomas.

Western Australia

CHAMELEONSEYE/GETTY IMAGES

Looking to the other hemisphere is always a good idea, and in Australia, they're experiencing the other shoulder season: spring. In Western Australia, this means gorgeous wildflowers and some excellent festivals worth traveling for.

Foodies should plan to attend the world-renowned Pair'd Margaret River Region food and wine festival (Nov. 19-22), which brings world-class wine, food and music to the Margaret River region. This year's lineup includes chefs Rodney Scott, Matt Abe, and Mindy Woods, as well as wine writer Erin Larkin.

TPG tip: Hyde Perth opened in March of this year (rates from $239 per night) as the first Hyde hotel in Australia, a brand from Ennismore (also behind the Hoxton, Mama Shelter, Delano, and 25Hours brands) that also has locations in London, Ibiza, Dubai, and Miami.

Palm Springs, California

MATTHEW MICAH WRIGHT/GETTY IMAGES

Some November trips require compromising on the weather, but not in the desert. Palm Springs in November is ideal: With a daytime high of 79 degrees Fahrenheit and a low of 52 degrees Fahrenheit at night, you'll still avoid its peak tourist months, which begin in January. It's also ideal weather to hike in nearby Joshua Tree National Park.

Though most Pride events occur in June, the Palm Springs Pride Festival is a fall event. This year, it runs from Nov. 6–8, with the big parade on the final day. Expect live music, comedy, local vendors and artists, and plenty of food and drink stands in several areas across the downtown area. The Sunset Music and Comedy Festival runs concurrently each night at the Agua Caliente Casino.

There are several new and improved attractions in the desert: The Palm Springs Air Museum recently completed a $2.5 million renovation, and reopened in September, featuring a turbine-inspired façade, new exhibits, and the Ronald M. Auen Learning Center, and DSRT Surf, a manmade surf lagoon that creates the perfect wave, every time, opened this summer in Palm Desert.

TPG tip: The iconic La Quinta Resort & Club celebrates its centennial in 2026, with special events and guest experiences all year long. It's now a part of Hilton's Curio Collection, so you can use and earn rewards points there (rates from 85,000 Hilton Honors points and $329 per night)

Florence, Italy

GUIDO COZZI ATLANTIDE/PHOTOTRAVEL/GETTY IMAGES

Florence in summer can be a hot, crowded mess, but come November, there are fewer crowds and mild weather (although rain is likely). It's also truffle-hunting season in Tuscany.

Film lovers should mark Oct. 31-Nov. 8 for the Festival dei Popoli, Europe's oldest international documentary film festival. And Nov. 29 brings runners from around the world to the Estra Firenze Marathon. Finally, most Christmas markets open around the city in the last week of November.

TPG tip: The city recently welcomed Borgo Pignano Florence, set on a 5-acre estate with 11 historic buildings and 32 rooms and is part of Relais & Chateaux. La Resérve Firenze opened Oct. 15 inside a restored 15th-century palazzo. If you want to use points, W Florence (rates from $507 or 70,000 Marriott Bonvoy points per night) opened last year.

Las Vegas

CLIVE MASON/GETTY IMAGES

Rev your engines for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, when Formula 1 racing comes to Sin City from Nov. 19-21.

Outside the hugely popular event, you can find some hotel and flight deals this November. And there's still plenty to do in November beyond F1. At the Sphere, you can catch the 4D version of "The Wizard of Oz" daily throughout the month, or see Metallica Nov. 4-7, the Eagles Nov. 13-14 and 27-28.

Christmas also starts early in Vegas on Nov. 5, when the 3-acre Cactus Garden at Ethel M Chocolate Factory opens its annual lights show, and Nov. 14 at the Bellagio Conservatory, where they pull out all the stops to create a lit-up floral winter wonderland.

Foodies should also be excited: Taylor Swift favorite and NYC hotspot The Corner Store is opening its second location at the Cosmopolitan of Las Vegas on November 9.

TPG tip: United restored its nonstop service between Cleveland and Las Vegas in September, with five flights a week and budget airline Breeze Airways added a daily route between Las Vegas and John Wayne Airport in Orange County.

Miches, Dominican Republic

ZEMI MICHES PUNTA CANA ALL-INCLUSIVE RESORT, CURIO COLLECTION BY HILTON

November is the tail end of hurricane season so the possibilities of one hitting are slim—and there are great deals to be had, and usually, excellent weather. Miches — located on the northeast coast of the Dominican Republic — has been increasing its number of high-end all-inclusive resorts, and it's typically much less crowded than Punta Cana, which is near the airport.

Hilton loyalists can turn Hilton Honors points into an all-inclusive stay at Zemi Miches Punta Cana All-Inclusive Resort, Hilton's first Curio Collection all-inclusive resort in the country. Hyatt has also planted its all-inclusive flag in Miches with two openings: Dreams Playa Esmeralda Resort & Spa and Secrets Playa Esmeralda Resort & Spa. At Dreams, families and kids are welcome to enjoy the beautiful beaches, pools, and many restaurants and bars, while Secrets is adults-only.

TPG tip: No matter where you stay, let the hotel arrange your transportation from Punta Cana International Airport for ease and affordability. The transportation area at this airport can be overwhelming, and after a long flight, who doesn't want everything made easy?

Facing a Trump backlash, New York Republicans blame blue state incumbents

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

ALBANY, New York — New York Republicans have a closing argument for voters unhappy with the cost of living: Blame the incumbent Democrats who run Albany.

President Donald Trump’s deep unpopularity in his native state — made worse by high gas prices and a Canadian tariff war — has created a nightmare environment for the GOP in this deep blue state. Yet Republican candidates, strategists and former officeholders believe their party can leverage New Yorkers’ vexation with high taxes, housing costs and skyrocketing utility bills to offset the midterm agony their party is poised to suffer on Tuesday.

Gubernatorial nominee Bruce Blakeman’s final TV ad warns voters that Democratic Gov. Kathy Hochul’s reelection will result in a tax hike at the behest of her political ally, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani. On the trail, he blasts the sharp increase in energy costs and pledges to reverse the state’s 12-year-old ban on natural gas drilling along the Pennsylvania border. And he’s pledging to bolster public safety by repealing a package of criminal justice law changes.

Republicans hope those attacks will offset Democrats’ efforts to make the election a referendum on Trump. GOP candidates running in down-ballot House and state legislative contests have used Hochul as a foil — a reflection of her middling poll numbers for much of the year.

Rep. Mike Lawler, running in a district Trump lost by one point two years ago, has gleefully tied Democrats to Hochul.

“If Democrats in New York complain about affordability, we have the highest state taxes in the country, income and property taxes. We have chronically poor educational performance. We have a housing market that’s stultified,” said former Rep. John Faso, a Republican who ran for governor in 2006. “Since the Democrats have been running New York state at the statewide level for many years — it’s hard to blame all of that on Donald Trump.”

Hochul’s campaign insists New York voters will positively affirm her five years in office.


“From the beginning, this race has been about records: Governor Hochul has put money back in New Yorkers’ pockets, invested $4 billion in public safety, and fought back against Trump’s attacks – all Bruce Blakeman has done is raise taxes, stick his neck out for ICE, and defend everything Trump does,” Hochul campaign spokesperson Ryan Radulovacki said in a statement. "New Yorkers don’t want that and they never have.”


The rhetoric reflects a broader bet about voters’ collective mood in an intensely disruptive political era and is a test of whether increasingly nationalized elections can still hinge on local concerns. Skyrocketing gas and grocery costs, along with the perception among younger people that their generation will not be as financially secure as their parents, has fueled insurgent primary candidates’ successful bids against their establishment foes.

New York Republicans are making this anti-incumbent case eight years after Democrats won the state Senate, giving them control of both legislative chambers and the governor’s office. GOP officials contend that Democratic control in Albany has made the Empire State less safe and more expensive. They hope the argument will appeal to swing voters, including the growing number of people who aren’t enrolled in a party, and disaffected Democrats concerned with the party’s leftward lurch — especially after Mamdani’s election.

The stakes for Republicans extend beyond Blakeman’s longshot bid for governor. The GOP is trying to retain control of the closely divided House, and New York is home to several swing districts.

Republicans are fretting about the political fate of Lawler, who occupies one of those swing seats. They are also nervously watching the outcome of a race for a deep red House seat being vacated by Rep. Elise Stefanik — an election that was never expected to be competitive.

Polls have shown their entire statewide ticket, which is led by Blakeman and includes state attorney general and comptroller candidates, down by double digits against the incumbent Democrats. One survey also showed the limits of both candidates’ affordability pitches: 35% of likely voters said neither would make life more affordable for average New Yorkers, compared with 30% who chose Hochul and 27% who chose Blakeman.

Voters across the country are enraged by the cost of living, and Hochul’s allies expect they will take it out on Trump.

“When you have a level of anger at the government or life, sometimes that spills out on people who aren’t to blame,” said Democratic former New York City Council Speaker Christine Quinn. “But in New York as it relates to Gov. Royal Caribbean Out-Earned Hilton’s Whole 2025 In A Quarter

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Royal Caribbean earned $1.7 billion in one quarter, more than Hilton made in all of 2025. Cruising now out-earns hotels and car rentals by a wide margin.

Royal Caribbean Just Posted Another Record

Royal Caribbean earned $1.7 billion in Q3 2026, or $6.45 per share, up from $5.74 a year ago. Revenue climbed 9% to $5.6 billion, and the company raised its full-year adjusted earnings guidance to between $17.85 and $17.95 per share. RCCL Management said bookings are up in the mid-teens since the last call, and 2027 sailings are up in the high teens, booked at record rates.

Net yields (revenue per passenger cruise day) rose only 0.9%, so most of the growth came from more capacity rather than higher spend per guest. That capacity is spread across three brands many travelers don’t realize share a parent. Royal Caribbean International is the big-ship family product, Celebrity Cruises is the premium line, and Silversea is the luxury line. A traveler who would never book Royal might happily book Celebrity, and plenty of Silversea guests have no idea Royal Caribbean owns it. Each serves a distinct market, and all three feed RCCL’s results.

How Cruising Stacks Up Against Everyone Else

Comparisons are fun, but until I looked at the numbers, I wasn’t aware of just how impressively cruise stacks up against even asset-light industries like modern hotel chains. The four big cruise companies with public data available earned about $8.6 billion in net income on roughly $60.8 billion in revenue. That includes Carnival at $2.8 billion, Royal Caribbean at $4.3 billion, Viking at $1.1 billion and Norwegian at $423 million (a GAAP figure; its adjusted net income was Norwegian at $423 million.05 billion). These four operate on a net margin of about 14%. MSC Cruises, the other giant, belongs to the privately held MSC Group and does not publish comparable results.

Hotels, comparatively, are less profitable for the most part. Marriott earned $2.6 billion, Hilton earned $1.5 billion, and Hyatt lost $52 million. IHG earned $759 million on $5.2 billion of revenue. The four combined for about $4.8 billion, or a little over half of what the four cruise companies made.

Car rental is worse. Avis Budget lost $995 million, including a $518 million impairment on its electric vehicle fleet, and Hertz lost about $747 million, according to the wider-than-expected losses. Together that is a $1.7 billion loss on about $20 billion of revenue. Enterprise Holdings (Enterprise, National, and Alamo) is a private company and does not report results publicly.

Airlines are the only group that earns more than cruising in nominal dollars, and only because of their sheer scale. IATA put 2025 global airline net profit at $45 billion on $1.065 trillion of revenue, a 4.2% margin, and cut its 2026 forecast to $23 billion at 2.0%. Simply, four cruise companies earned about 19% of what every airline in the world made combined, on less than 6% of the revenue.

Why The Margins Look So Different

Hotel companies like Marriott and Hilton are asset-light, so their revenue includes massive amounts of reimbursed costs and franchise fees, and most of the physical buildings are owned by other entities. A fair comparison would use margin on fees, and I would not suggest these numbers are apples-to-apples. Cruise lines own the ships, the fuel bill and the debt, with Royal Caribbean carrying about $22.4 billion of it, so their profit has to cover a much heavier balance sheet even after accelerated (and substantial) depreciation.

Passenger captivity on a ship is unrivaled throughout the travel industry. Travelers pay for the room, the meals, the drinks, the excursions and the Wi-Fi in one transaction, all on a property the cruise line controls. Royal Caribbean says close to half of this year’s guests are repeat customers and about 40% belong to its loyalty program. A hotel chain would love to know exactly how much each guest spends in its properties but they don’t; cruise lines know it down to the cent.

Royal Caribbean’s $1.7 billion, Carnival’s $1.9 billion and Norwegian’s $491 million add up to about $4.1 billion in net income in a single quarter. That roughly matches what Marriott, Hilton and Hyatt earned together in all of 2025. Hilton’s own third quarter net income was $536 million, about a third of Royal Caribbean’s. That’s absolutely incredible, especially when

Two caveats belong here. My sample includes only the hotel companies and cruise lines that publish numbers I could confirm, so MSC and plenty of private hotel owners are missing. The fiscal years also do not line up perfectly, because Carnival’s year ends in November. But it’s also worth noting that Hilton has about 18x the keys that Royal does, owns less of the product itself (and carries fewer costs), and still falls woefully behind Royal.

Why This Explains The Sandals Deal

When I wrote about Royal Caribbean’s $3 billion Sandals deal in September, part of the claim was that Royal knows the customer base well (it does) and that it knows the market well (it does) but understanding the power of capturing the total spend for a client trip is the magic. With this much cash coming off the ships, buying 50% of Sandals, expected to close in early 2027, gives Royal Caribbean a stake in the land-based all-inclusive business. The same logic is why I argued that every big hotel brand wants your all-inclusive money. The cruise industry is not trying to beat hotels at hotels; rather it’s buying the segment of the hotel business that already works like a cruise.

Conclusion

My agency sells both cruises and hotel rooms, and the difference in how the two operators treat the traveler’s wallet is all the difference. Cruise companies have figured out how to capture the whole vacation budget on a floating property they control, and the earnings show it. I expect the gap with hotels to widen next year as 2027 bookings convert at record rates, and I expect more hotel companies to copy the model through all-inclusive resorts and bundled pricing. But I was still shocked that Hilton, which owns very few hotels, and is mostly a marketing arm for a far larger footprint, is dwarfed by a cruise line in a single quarter.
