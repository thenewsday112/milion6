Here’s What Most Hunters Get Wrong About Whitetails, According to a Top Deer Expert

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Jeff Sturgis has the unique experience of living through dozens of deer seasons all at once. As the founder of Whitetail Habitat Solutions, he works with clients around the country to improve their deer hunting and private land habitat. 

But before ever stepping foot on a property, Sturgis does in-depth interviews with these hunters to understand what they’d like to improve and what problems they’ve been having. Through this process, trends start to emerge. 

Sturgis calls these problems “huntisms.” But they could also be described as misconceptions, misunderstandings, or straight up bad information. Whatever you want to call them, there are certain things that hunters get wrong when it comes to hunting mature bucks and managing habitat for those mature bucks.

So in this episode of the Outdoor Life Podcast, Sturgis will reference his decades of experience working on private land habitat as well as his time spent hunting on public ground to outline the most common and detrimental deer hunting mistakes. Heed his advice for a better season next year.

Huntism #1: You Can’t Kill ‘Em from the Couch

This common mentality of having to pound the woods and hunt all the time has saved a lot of big deer over the decades, Sturgis says. 

“In some cases I can see this being partially true,” Sturgis says. “Maybe if you’re hunting public land, you’ve got ten spots and you’ve got the time to hunt freely.” 

But if you’re hunting small parcels where you can’t afford to spook deer, then it’s usually smarter to stay home when hunting conditions are poor in order to not burn out your stands.

“There’s something to be said for minimizing your hunting, taking care of other priorities in life and expecting a higher rate of potential success because you’ve not over-pressured your stand locations.” 

“It’s not just your stand locations [that you don’t want to burn out, it’s also your family, your career, your friends — it extends to an entire way to look at things. When I go out in the woods I want there to be a reasonable expectation that I’m going to shoot a deer for this reason.”

Sturgis says that if you have a “rutcation” planned where you know you’re going to hunt for a solid seven to nine days in a row, then it’s critical to reduce your impact. Instead of charging into a core area regardless of conditions, hunt spots where your scent cone won’t spook deer (because it’s blowing over a lake or off a cliff). And when conditions aren’t ideal, hunt transition areas where you could catch a big buck walking by, but you won’t spook deer near bedding areas or primary food sources. 

Huntism #2: More Food Plots Means More Big Bucks

Sturgis rarely hunts over food plots during bow season. Photo by Whitetail Habitat Solutions

Sturgis works with many clients who have the financial resources to grow and maintain sprawling food plots on their properties. Many of these clients believe that growing the maximum amount of food on the property will translate to holding the most amount of mature bucks in the fall. 

But that’s not how it plays out most of the time. Growing too much summertime food — like big clover or bean plots — will increase the number of does and fawns on the property. During the summer and early fall, mature bucks don’t like to live on crowded food sources and bedding areas, Sturgis says.

“When you add summer food to good cover, you get a lot of does and fawns,” Sturgis says. “And those does and fawns that are here today are here to stay.”

These deer take up space and resources. Mature bucks will live on the fringes of these high-density deer areas but not establish a core zone there. 

“Yeah, some bucks will come through during the rut. But bucks, the older they get, they don’t want that commotion and stress of a bunch of does and fawns. That’s why they live somewhere else.”   

Sturgis says it’s better to focus on a comprehensive management strategy with proper deer cover, screening cover, sanctuaries, water holes, fall food, and yes, maybe some summer food plots, than it is to simply plant clover plots throughout the entire property.

“If you want to raise the population, add summer food. If you want to lower the population, get rid of summer food,” Sturgis says. “When I go to a client and see 20 acres of beans on their 200-acre property and we drive around and see over 100 does and fawns and only a couple bucks, they have a severe problem. I’d rather have those does and fawns be somewhere else in the summertime and shift over to the property starting in September and October.”

Huntism #3: The Single Best Way to Kill a Big Buck Is to Wait on a Food Plot

Jen Sturgis with a stud Midwest whitetail. Photo by Whitetail Habitat Solutions

Don’t read this as an attack on food plots. Sturgis has written countless articles on the benefits of food plots, he’s got an online food plot class, he’s authored a book on the subject, and Whitetail Habitat Solutions sells food plot seed. So yes, food plots can be a critical element in consistently harvesting mature bucks.

But that doesn’t mean you should always hunt over them. 

“This year I shot four bucks in four states with my bow … and not one of them was shot on a food plot,” Sturgis says. “Then I go to clients and they’re spending 90 percent of their hunts on food plots — and I’m not saying I never hunt on food plots — but the point is that the vast majority of my bucks are shot not in food plots.”

Sturgis tries to convince his clients to hunt away from food plots. Focusing instead on waterholes or funnels in the woods that might lead to food. This helps hunters spook fewer deer and see target bucks during daylight hours. 

“Food plots have the greatest level of potential reward and the greatest level of risk,” Sturgis says. “If you spook out those food plots, you’ve just destroyed your property.”

Huntism #4: Other Hunters Will Ruin Your Public Land Spot

Even though Sturgis has access to prime whitetail properties in the Midwest, he also hunts public land in Pennsylvania every year. In 22 seasons of hunting the same public area, Sturgis has shot 18 bucks. 

It’s pretty common for him to bump into other hunters on these hunts, but he doesn’t sweat it. He just moves somewhere else. The key is being flexible and using tactics that match the conditions. For example, if it’s snowy and windy, Sturgis might still hunt through an area. If the woods are blanketed in crunchy leaves, he’ll opt to sit for the day. 

One way that Sturgis makes sure that he avoids other hunters is by walking in at twilight (not in the dark). 

“You don’t have to be to your spot before light,” Sturgis says. “When I’m walking into a spot like that, I don’t know what tree I’m going to be sitting under or in… So we’re walking into an area in daylight and still hunting [our way in].”

It’s also important to remember that other hunters will bump deer long distances, not just a few hundred yards.

“Public land deer will get pushed a long way,” Sturgis says. “Especially in open woods. Where we hunt we can get 200-yard shots in the woods in some places. So when people push deer out there, they’re moving three quarters of a mile. So don’t think about just one little thicket. Think about how deer are moving in the big picture [across the landscape].”

For more than a decade, I’ve driven trucks, and always with a fiberglass hardshell topper. The cap transformed the bed of my pickup into a gear shed for all of my outdoor equipment and a tiny cabin for me to crash in on climbing trips and before alpine starts. But, as my hard shell weighed north of 150 pounds, I never—and I mean that literally—took it off.

That’s all fine and dandy for the dirtbag climber and runner, jaunting around New Mexico and Colorado without a care in the world in her twenties. But at this stage of life, as a mom of three kids with a house to maintain, a farm to care for, and loads of bikes and strollers to haul to and fro on the weekends, my truck needs to be a little more versatile.

Enter: the Flated Cap56.

The topper weighs less than 60 pounds, but keeps truck beds dry (Photo: Abigail Wise)

As its name suggests, Flated shells are inflatable. I blew it up using the plug-in pump reserved for my SUP in just a few minutes. Installation required no further tools. At under 60 pounds, installation is not only simple, but easy to do by myself. I just inflate the cap and lift it onto my truck. Then, I attach it with a handful of tie-downs. In total, installation takes about 30 minutes, but I bet I’ll get faster with practice.

The shell features screen windows (Photo: Abigail Wise)

Flated doesn’t skimp on features, either. It’s fully waterproof with a rain skirt built in to repel water from the truck bed. There are two tinted windows, two screens on the side windows, and one rear window. The part of the topper that connects to the truck cab also features a pass-through window, so you can easily access the truck bed from the backseat. Sturdy zippers and Velcro secure window shades while you drive, and a couple of interior mesh pockets on the cab end help sort smaller gear and gadgets. I have yet to camp with the Flated Cap56, but when I do, that’s where I’ll stash my phone and headlamp for safekeeping.

At $1,600, it’s not cheap, but it does cost several thousand dollars less than a traditional hardshell.

Heavy-duty zippers and Velcro keep everything in place (Photo: Abigail Wise)

I live at high elevation and full sun in northern New Mexico, so time will tell how the new topper stands up against the elements, but so far, I’ve driven it for about a month without any signs of wear.

Last weekend, I had it on for transporting bike gear and keeping it safe from the rain, but I pulled it off the next day to haul home some new trees from the local nursery. This, in a nutshell, is the beauty of the Flated Cap56: it’s versatile and goes with the flow of life, no matter how chaotic my days may get.

At first, it appears that two turtles with yellow spots are calmly sitting side by side. Suddenly, one of them turns, slowly—and lunges out with its head, its open beak clearly trying to take an aggressive chomp out of its neighbor. Moments later, the turtle tries again. Now, both reptiles are going head-to-head, though each is retracted into the protection of its shell. 

The two combatants are male eastern box turtles (Terrapene carolina carolina) competing for dominance. Eastern box turtles are a subspecies of the common box turtle (Terrapene carolina), with bright orange and yellow coloring on its dark shell. They live throughout the eastern and midwestern United States. 

“I wouldn’t say that they’re normally aggressive, but male box turtles can definitely become territorial, especially since males’ home ranges (the area they frequently occupy) often overlap,” Eamon Caffrey, the turtle research coordinator at Mass Audubon who captured the video, tells Popular Science. “We do occasionally see this sort of brawling behavior between two males when out in the field; you just have to get lucky enough to actually witness it. This experience was a first for me, and I was thrilled to capture it on camera.” 

Over the summer, another Mass Audubon staff member saw two male box turtles opting for a pushing contest instead of biting. At one point, one of the two flipped the other contestant onto its back and pushed him down a small hill. Box turtle battles can also feature spinning each other around.  

“From my understanding, fights between male box turtles are much more opportunistic than anything else,” Caffrey tells Popular Science. “Often, when two males come in contact, they will display a variety of aggressive behaviors that can include head butting, shell ramming, biting, pushing, and shoving – all in attempts to assert dominance. These disputes are often territorial but can be over mates as well.”

What’s more, the turtle with the darker shell is a well-known individual over 30 years old. Caffrey says he’s seen him various times this year, and since capturing the video the celebrity reptile has been spending time in an area where the Mass Audubon team frequently spot two to three adult females. However, there’s been no trace of his opponent. 

“Does that mean he ‘won’ the battle?” Caffrey muses. “Who knows.”

West Ham Have Not Fallen Below Their Levels – This Is Their Level

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

West Ham captain Jarrod Bowen has apologised following the Hammers’ 3-1 defeat against West Bromwich Albion, admitting the team have fallen well below their usual standards over the past fortnight. However, while I appreciate Jarrod’s honesty, I fundamentally disagree with his assessment of what has gone wrong.

My argument is that West Ham’s performances haven’t suddenly deteriorated over the last two weeks. If anything, the standards have remained remarkably consistent, and the defeats against Southampton and West Brom simply represent the results that many of our earlier performances deserved.

Nuno Espírito Santo’s team have slipped to eighth in the Championship, with their hopes of an immediate Premier League return beginning to look rather less straightforward. But rather than examining two disappointing weeks in isolation, I think we need to look at the warning signs that have been there for considerably longer.

Bowen’s West Ham Assessment Doesn’t Tell The Whole Story

Speaking to the official West Ham website following Saturday’s defeat, Bowen admitted the Hammers had been below their best but had previously found ways to win.

He also acknowledged that the team had fallen below their levels in recent weeks and apologised to supporters.

I appreciate Bowen taking responsibility, particularly after he was one of the few players to emerge from the West Brom defeat with a shred of credit. However, I completely disagree with the suggestion that this represents a sudden collapse in standards.

I think West Ham have simply started getting the results their performances deserve.

Take the 1-0 victory away at Swansea City. It was an excellent result against the league leaders, but West Ham were incredibly poor at the start of that match and hadn’t registered a shot on target after half an hour.

The 6-0 victory against Lincoln City looks fantastic on paper. However, Lincoln were reduced to nine men, and even when they had ten players on the pitch, I thought they were the better team.

It wasn’t until the second half that West Ham really began flexing their muscles against an exhausted side playing with two fewer men.

Queens Park Rangers outplayed us for large periods of our encounter, while everybody remembers quite how dreadful we were during the first half against Millwall.

These weren’t isolated incidents. They were the norm.

West Ham’s League Position Has Flattered Their Performances

To an extent, West Ham have got away with it.

Until a week ago, you could reasonably argue that our league position had been inflated by two 6-0 victories. Those scorelines certainly helped create the impression of a team considerably more dominant than the performances themselves suggested.

The truth is that we’ve already lost three times at home this season.

We might have beaten Wolverhampton Wanderers 4-2, but I thought there were large elements of that match where we were fortunate. Charlton Athletic didn’t find it particularly difficult to beat us either.

Airidas Golambeckis was one of the few who came out of the game without big criticism

In terms of matches played eleven against eleven, I would only really consider the victories against Wrexham and Derby County to have been dominant performances.

That is hardly the form or consistency of a team expecting to secure automatic promotion.

And it is why I find Bowen’s suggestion that West Ham have recently fallen below their usual levels so difficult to accept.

For that to be true, the previous standards would presumably have been considerably higher.

They haven’t been.

The problems aren’t particularly new. The lack of confidence, cohesion and defensive organisation has been evident for some time.

The difference is that opponents are now making us pay.

Bowen’s Most Damning Admission Was About West Ham’s Talking

There was another part of Bowen’s interview which I found even more concerning.

Discussing West Ham’s habit of starting matches slowly, the captain acknowledged that the problem had been discussed repeatedly within the squad.

As he put it:

“We speak about it all the time”

He went on to explain that individual players must take responsibility for putting those discussions into practice on the pitch.

And this is where I think the situation becomes particularly worrying.

Because if the players have repeatedly discussed the problem, presumably identified what needs changing and agreed on a plan of action, why does the same thing keep happening?

In many respects, this is the most damning indictment of all.

The players have talked about it. They’ve discussed how to improve. They’ve presumably received instructions from the coaching staff.

And it hasn’t worked.

Which leaves us with two possibilities.

Either the plan itself is wrong, in which case serious questions must be asked of Nuno Espírito Santo and his coaching staff, or the plan is perfectly reasonable and the players are incapable of executing it.

Neither possibility is particularly reassuring.

West Ham Are Running Out Of Answers

Bowen concluded his interview by making another revealing admission:

“I think talk is very, very cheap.”

On that particular point, I couldn’t agree more.

But where does that leave West Ham?

The talking isn’t working. The tactics aren’t working. The plans aren’t working. And increasingly, the performances on the pitch aren’t working either.

There is only so much discussing, analysing and apologising that can be done before somebody has to produce an actual solution.

West Ham now face consecutive away fixtures against Cardiff City and Blackburn Rovers before the international break, and Bowen has made it clear that the players need to respond with victories.

I certainly wouldn’t question the captain’s commitment. He scored an excellent goal against West Brom and was one of the few players who genuinely looked capable of making something happen.

But his assessment that West Ham have only fallen below their levels during the past fortnight misses what I consider to be the bigger problem.

We’ve been playing like this for much of the season.

The results have simply stopped disguising it.

And if Nuno and his players have already talked through the problems, agreed on solutions and still cannot put them into practice, then I am not entirely sure what there is left to say.

Either the manager’s plan is wrong or the players cannot carry it out.

Whichever it is, West Ham are in a bit of trouble.

The Trump administration has sought to eliminate what it alleges is partisan bias within the Justice Department, but the vast majority of political donations from individuals identifying themselves as DOJ employees are still going to Democratic-aligned committees, according to a Fox News Digital analysis of federal campaign finance records.

Between January 2025 and August of this year, just over 75% of campaign contributions from individuals identifying themselves as Department of Justice employees went to Democratic committees, the analysis of federal campaign finance records found.

Fox News Digital analyzed roughly $300,000 in political contributions recorded by the Federal Election Commission between January 2025 and August 2026 from donors who listed their employer as "Department of Justice," "DOJ" or a variation of those names. Contributions were categorized by the political affiliation of the recipient candidate or committee, while independent-expenditure committees were classified according to the candidates or parties they supported. When exporting data from the Federal Election Commission's dashboard, an option to include donations from "unique individuals only" was selected to filter out duplicative records.

As the Trump administration has progressed, the partisan slant of DOJ donations has become more pronounced. Through 2025, about 70% of donations from those identifying themselves as DOJ staff went to Democratic committees, a figure that rose to just under 90% in 2026, according to Fox News Digital's findings.

The size of Democrats’ victory Tuesday could help determine their future

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

For Democrats, the size of their victory matters.

Progressives and moderates have been sparring all cycle over who will get to claim credit if they win on Tuesday. A new POLITICO Poll shows that the bigger the win, the more Democrats appear inclined to see it as a mandate to run more progressive candidates.

A smaller-than-expected victory next week would leave the party — and voters — torn over which direction to go.

The results reveal that the midterm election will have major implications over the next stage of the Democratic Party war over their identity — and could give progressives fresh ammunition ahead of a critical 2028 presidential race. Party insiders are closely watching contests where that infighting has been on display: From Abdul El-Sayed’s battle for Michigan Senate and Troy Jackson’s for Maine Senate, to Randy Villegas’ House race in California and Manny Rutinel’s in Colorado.

More than a dozen Democratic Party operatives and strategists said in interviews that both factions are already preparing to see a blue wave as validation of its own approach. Top super PACs and campaign firms are finishing strategy memos and finalizing lineups for a slate of post-election conferences where they intend to claim their way is the party’s future.

“You know what they say: Victory has a thousand mothers, right?” said Oliver Libby, the Democratic author behind the book “Strong Floor, No Ceiling,” which became a rallying cry for House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries this cycle.

The survey, conducted by PF Global, found Democrats are nearly evenly split over what a smaller-than-expected victory next week would mean for future elections: 37% said it would signal a need to run more progressive candidates in the next election, while 35% said it would show the party should run more moderates. Another 28% said they didn’t know.

Those divisions largely fall along ideological lines. Among self-identified progressives, 52% said a smaller-than-expected victory would show that Democrats should run more progressive candidates. Meanwhile, a 45% plurality of self-identified moderates said that the same result would be evidence that the party should nominate more moderates, while just 28% percent said to run more progressives.

But if Democrats sweep key races — presented in the survey as a “large victory” — both progressives and moderates move toward running more progressive candidates in the next election. A majority of self-identified progressives — 61% — said it would show that the party should run more progressive candidates, while 35% of self-identified moderates agreed (compared with the 28% who said this for a smaller margin of victory).

A majority of Democratic midterm voters, including majorities of both self-identified progressives and moderates, also said a large victory would mean the party has a winning strategy for the 2028 presidential election, according to the poll.

"If Democrats had half a brain cell, they would look for the things that those two movements have in common,” said Chuck Rocha, a veteran Democratic strategist who has advised prominent candidates in both camps. “Whoever can combine these two things is who's going to end up being somebody who is going to be a stalwart in ‘28."

Progressives are eager for Tuesday’s results to bolster their argument that the party should move further to the left and that their candidates can win in competitive general elections. They’ve spent years feeling like they were being discounted by the party’s establishment wing, which was convinced that only more moderate candidates could cut into Trump’s coalition.

Progressives have cast a slate of battleground House and Senate races where their candidates felled more moderate primary rivals as major proof points — none more so than El-Sayed in purple Michigan’s marquee Senate race. A victory there, they say, could help springboard some of their biggest stars, like Reps. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-N.Y.) and Ro Khanna (D-Calif.), for presidential bids. Both representatives and a slate of other progressive surrogates up through Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) have hit the trail to help.

“Our North Star in 2026 is to prove the concept that bold, shake up the system economic fighters can win anywhere, including purple and red areas, as a prelude to the presidential election in 2028,” said Adam Green, co-founder of the Progressive Change Campaign Committee. “We think we will have plenty to work with after the election. For us, Abdul is Exhibit A.”

PCCC is drafting a memo touting what it predicts as “two common themes in election winners: Outsiders and economic fighters who challenge corporate power,” according to a draft viewed by POLITICO.

Moderates, meanwhile, are poised to point to results as evidence that building broad coalitions can help Democrats compete in Republican strongholds.

“The interpretation game is already under way,” said Matt Bennett, co-founder of the centrist think-tank Third Way, which has been conducting advertising analysis and polling the electorate, with plans to release its findings after Election Day. “[Progressives] are going to cherry pick the couple of races that they win, and they are going to say, therefore … everybody should go to the left.”

A memo from The Bench — a Democratic organization that supports candidates with real-world experience, such as teachers, nurses or veterans — touts recruits like James Talarico in Texas and Adam Hamilton in Kansas as evidence of the strength of its approach working. Public polling shows Talarico and Hamilton in a competitive position in once-solid safe red states.

“While much of the Democratic establishment was busy studying how to talk to working people, our answer was simpler: Run them,” the memo released this week reads.

“I saw Democrats, a lot of people in the 2020 primary, adopt positions that were just electoral poison,” said Democratic strategist Lis Smith, an architect of Pete Buttigieg's 2020 presidential campaign who is a senior adviser to The Bench and works with The Bench’s sister organization, Majority Democrats. “You had Democrats say decriminalize border crossing, defund the police, and things like that. And we just can't go back there as a party. We're not going to be a majority party if we go down that road again.”

A new memo obtained by POLITICO from Blue Dog PAC, a moderate Democratic group chaired by Rep. Adam Gray (D-Calif.), already makes the case that there was a “Blue Dog overperformance in 2024 and the Trump era.” It points to over a dozen House races the group is involved in that were initially rated as “Solid Republican” or “Likely Republican" by the Cook Political Report, and are finishing the cycle in the “Lean Republican,” “Toss Up” and “Lean Democratic” columns.

But some Democrats say that all this focus on who won the progressive-moderate fight is missing the point entirely.

“Actually doing something if we do win majorities, that's more important than a bunch of memos saying we're the reason we won,” said Bill Neidhardt, who worked on Bernie Sanders’ 2020 presidential campaign and is now advising Will Lawrence, who’s running for Michigan’s 7th District, and working to boost El-Sayed.

“Whether one super PAC says to the other super PAC, ‘We’re the better super PAC’ — I just think if that’s what victory looks like to you in 2026, you’re going to set the Democrats up to lose in 2028,” he added.

Dezeen's favourite furniture and homeware from October

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The first piece of furniture by architecture office Studio Hagen Hall and a pleated cabinet by Didi Ng Wing Yin feature in this roundup of design pieces spotted by Dezeen's Jane Englefield over the last month.

SHH-001 table by Studio Hagen Hall

Louis Hagen Hall of London-based Studio Hagen Hall has made his first foray into furniture with this sculptural coffee table, delivered as two legs and a weighty glass tabletop that require no mechanical fixings.

Hagen Hall originally designed the asymmetric piece for Pine Heath, a modernist Hampstead renovation with a corner sofa that required a bespoke coffee table to offset the negative space left by the seating.

Cherry veneer legs are paired with recyclable brown smoked glass to create the made-to-order piece, informed by the design philosophy of modernist American architect John Lautner.

Exo 01 vase by Flora Lechner

Austrian, Netherlands-based designer Flora Lechner originally caught the attention of London's Max Radford Gallery with her alternative aluminium chandeliers that look like ornate robots

Lechner has now translated her distinctive style into an unusual vase, composed of aluminium clips held together by stainless steel bolts. Stems can be slotted into the clips, which are supported by a base that can be filled with water.

"Exo 01 approaches the vase more as a support structure than as a container," explained Lechner. "Inspired by braces used to hold and align parts of the body, the work applies this principle to flowers."

Low-relief table by Anne Holtrop

Anne Holtrop is known for his rough-hewn furniture pieces, which conjure images of volcanic craters and mountainous landscapes.

For his inaugrual exhibition with Carpenter's Workshop Gallery in London, the Dutch architect and designer is showing off his Low-relief collection of tables made from cast glass.

Their unrefined legs were informed by stone reliefs Holtrop visited in Aswan, Egypt.

Pleats Cabinet No 1 by Didi Ng Wing Yin

Helsinki designer Didi Ng Wing Yin is known for his distinctively pleated wooden furniture, which he makes from locally sourced Finnish timber.

Last week, the designer debuted his first cabinet at Paris's Spazio Nobile Gallery. Made from a mixture of fir and pine, finished with tung oil, the cabinet features a delicately pleated door made using Ng's combined splitting, brushing and carving technique.

"I wanted to explore the dynamic contrast between form and texture," Ng told Dezeen.

"The chunky pedestal base is built from straightforward architectural beams that retain their natural knots and raw characteristics, providing a heavy, structural anchor for the light, organic silhouette of the pleated cabinet door."

OBJ-05 Chair by Manu Bañó

One of many design events in October was PAD London, a collectible fair that takes place annually in Mayfair's Berkley Square.

Among this year's exhibitors was Mexico-based Manu Bañó, who showed a duo of striking patinated brass chairs formed from stacked, sculptural slabs of metal.

The chairs are a continuation of Bañó's existing brass and stainless steel furniture, and were presented as part of the Objects with Narratives exhibition.

Ballon furniture by Seungjin Yang

South Korean designer Seungjin Yang has been making playful furniture shaped like balloons for years, using colourful epoxy resin.

At PAD London, Yang showed his most recent balloon-like creations in the form of a sofa, two armchairs and two stools.

The designer coats real balloons in resin, painstakingly building their strength. Each piece is complete after around two weeks of delicate testing.

Don't be misled by the intriguingly delicate appearance of Yang's finished pieces, which are solid enough to be sat on.
