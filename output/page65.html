Democrat Haley Stevens endorses GOP Senate nominee Mike Rogers at Detroit church

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Rep. Haley Stevens, D-Mich., endorsed Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers on Sunday amid Democratic infighting that has roiled the Michigan Senate race.

The endorsement came on the heels of Stevens' appearance alongside Rogers at Detroit’s Perfecting Church, where Fox News confirmed she attended a service. Stevens lost in the primary to fellow Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, and has been critical of his unwillingness to condemn the overt antisemitism of some of his supporters. 

"Hey Michigan, as you may have seen, our Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate, Abdul [El-Sayed] said that I was no longer welcome in his campaign and that I should stay away and he's right. And that's why I'm endorsing Mike Rogers for the United States Senate today," Stevens said in a video posted on X.

Stevens said she remains "a very proud Democrat" who loves Michigan but could not support El-Sayed, citing his backing from political streamer Hasan Piker.

"I'm honored to have Haley Stevens support today. I'm honored to have earned that vote," Rogers told reporters gathered outside the church. "This is more a statement about standing together against hate and the future of this state and this country than it is anything else," he added.

The appearance came after Stevens was disinvited from a planned church event with El-Sayed, the man who narrowly defeated her in Michigan’s August Democratic primary.

The reversal left Stevens at the same church as Rogers, El-Sayed’s Republican opponent.

Stevens has pressed El-Sayed to condemn an outside group’s ad linking U.S. support for Israel to higher grocery prices, which she called antisemitic.

El-Sayed said the ad was not produced by his campaign and that he disagreed with its contents.

Rep. Debbie Dingell, D-Mich., who helped organize the planned appearance, said Stevens was asked to skip it after the dispute became a distraction and upset the church’s bishop.

The dispute has put a spotlight on Democratic divisions in a close Senate race that could help determine control of the chamber.

Why is there only one Premier League game on Sunday November 1?

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →
Andoni Iraola and Mikel Arteta will meet at Anfield on Sunday afternoon (Picture: Getty Images)

It is a very quiet Sunday in terms of Premier League football, with just one match on the fixture list for November 1.

That game is a big one as Liverpool host Arsenal at 4.30pm, with the Gunners needing just a point to move to the top of the Premier League table.

The Reds can climb one place to fourth with a win at Anfield, but will remain in fifth with anything less.

It could be a cracker, but it is all we are getting as there were eight games on Saturday and there is one more on Monday night as Newcastle host Everton.

It is very unusual for there to be such a paucity of Premier League action on a Sunday, with Sky Sports really struggling to claim this one as Super.

This is just the second time since May 2024 that a Sunday during the Premier League season has featured just one match.

So why are we restricted to a solo contest as November gets underway?

Chelsea beat Manchester United in one of the eight games on Saturday (Picture: Getty Images)

There were initially two games scheduled for Sunday November 1, with Aston Villa vs Fulham originally set for 2pm, ahead of Liverpool vs Arsenal.

However, the Champions League schedule came out after that decision was made by the Premier League in August.

That European schedule set Barcelona vs Aston Villa for Tuesday November 3, which means the Villains could not play on Sunday as it would be too close to their glamorous trip to Spain. The general rule is that teams should not play two games in 60 hours.

If the Champions League game had been set for Wednesday then no change would have been needed, but Tuesday meant Villa’s clash with Fulham had to be switched to Saturday.

They played that game at 8pm on Saturday, with Nicolas Jackson opening the scoring for the home team at Villa Park, before Zion Suzuki let in a nightmare own goal.

Zion Suzuki dropped the ball in his own net at Villa Park (Picture: Shutterstock)

Liverpool and Arsenal are both in Champions League action next week, but both their fixtures are played on Wednesday.

The Reds go to Turkey to play Fenerbahce in a 5.45pm kick-off, while Arsenal travel to face Sparta Prague.

Manchester United host Roma on Tuesday night, which is plenty of time after they were beaten at Chelsea on Saturday lunchtime.

Inside top Trump bulldog’s plan to save House GOP majority and defy history

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Rep. Jim Jordan, R-Ohio, was jumping into the car with his wife on the way to the airport when he took a call from a Fox News Digital reporter on Thursday morning.

It’s a microcosm of what the House Judiciary Committee chairman’s recent weeks and months have been: a seemingly endless series of planes, trains and automobiles across the U.S. campaigning for fellow Republican lawmakers.

House Republicans’ senior leadership is crisscrossing the country raising money and support for their members to win Tuesday’s midterm elections. Jordan, though he’s not part of the trio, has much the same schedule.

"Sometimes they’re fundraisers, sometimes they’re rallies, sometimes they're both," Jordan said. "We're doing everything we can just to help our team win, because… if these Democrats get in there, my real concern, I gotta be honest, is what they're gonna try to do to the structure of our government."

MAPPED: TRUMP’S NONSTOP CAMPAIGN BLITZ SWEEPS KEY STATES

A source familiar with Jordan’s travel schedule said he’ll have gone to 115 districts across 34 states during this election cycle. That includes 19 consecutive days on the road in the lead-up to Nov. 3 in states such as Washington, Oregon, Texas, Arkansas, Florida, Illinois and South Carolina.

And despite the diverse areas, Jordan said his message is mostly the same: Republicans cut taxes, secured the border and Democrats will make it all worse.

"We understand things cost a little too much, and we're working on that. But remember, most of that's because of the Biden administration and their record inflation. We're bringing that inflation rate down, but you know, we still understand things, things cost too much, and we're gonna keep working on that," Jordan said.

TRUMP VOTERS REVEAL THEIR BIGGEST FEAR HEADING INTO MIDTERMS — AND IT'S NOT RISING PRICES

"But the big issue we talk about is, if these radicals that now control the Democrat Party, if they get control of the government, the crazy things they're gonna do are just frightening."

And because he’s not a member of leadership, he’s not afforded the same charter flights and police motorcades that come with that job.

While he’s had some private flights, most of Jordan’s travel has been comprised of commercial airlines and long drives between destinations — including sitting in traffic.

He said his favorite travel tunes include the greatest hits of late country star Glen Campbell.

But Jordan said he also uses much of that time texting or calling colleagues, asking how their races are going, and talking to staff about what issues to raise at his next event.

He’s also raised a significant amount of cash for their campaigns, donating $1.97 million to Republicans across the country and $2.65 million to the House GOP campaign arm.

It comes as House Republicans fight an uphill battle to retain their majority, as multiple political forecasts show Democrats poised to take over the chamber.

Jordan’s volume of travel is notable for someone who is not in senior House leadership, but it’s not a surprise, given his years-long high profile.

"His celebrity status is very helpful," one House Republican previously told Fox News Digital. "When Jim Jordan comes to town for you, you put 300 butts in seats."

Another lawmaker shared a similar figure. Rep. Tim Moore, R-N.C., told Fox News Digital that Jordan reached out to him about doing an event in his district that wound up a success.

DEMOCRATS SEE NEW CHANCES TO FLIP RED STATES — BUT A $400M GOP SPENDING ADVANTAGE COULD DERAIL THEIR PLANS

"We had a fundraiser in Gastonia and it was very well attended.  A huge crowd, nearly 300 people there — raised a lot of money, a lot of excitement, a lot of enthusiasm, and so I appreciated him coming down to do that," Moore said. His district also saw a visit from House Majority Leader Steve Scalise, R-La., which Moore said also raised a significant sum.

Jordan has appeared alongside conservatives in deep red districts and moderates in battlegrounds — walking out to Survivor’s "Eye of the Tiger" for many of those appearances — painting a picture of party unity that’s a far cry from his roots as an anti-establishment rebel leading the House Freedom Caucus.

"It’s kind of a Jordan 2.0," the first House Republican said. "He’s just a great citizen of the conference."

A second House Republican told Fox News Digital that a wide range of members see Jordan as a "mentor." They said Jordan’s travels are a mix of Republicans asking him to come out and him extending those offers himself.

"Jim doesn't care if you're moderate or even if you have to lean left to keep your seat, you have to represent your people," the second House Republican said.

Asked whether he’s changed or the party has, Jordan said it was "the overall picture."

"Any differences that exist in the Republican Party are pretty small compared to the differences between Republicans and the left, the hard left, the radical left, which now controls the Democrat Party," Jordan said.

It has also inspired questions about what he wants out of it.

House Republicans and members of the media are widely expecting that Jordan is eyeing a bid for senior leadership after failing to clinch a position in the past.

If Republicans lose the majority, he’s expected to run for minority leader, where he’d be Trump’s top defender in Congress.

But Jordan himself has publicly dismissed those notions.

"I’m focused on… Tuesday," he told Fox News Digital. "The way you stop the radicals from making these structural changes to our constitutional form of government, these radical policy positions that they have, the way you stop them is at the ballot box, and that happens on Tuesday."

And then the conversation ended, because Jordan had to catch a flight to campaign with Republican candidates in Florida that night.
