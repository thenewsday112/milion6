XRP Ledger Reviews Two Token-Authorization Fixes Ahead of Any Validator Vote

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Two open XRP Ledger proposals address authorization checks for liquidity-pool tokens and existing trust-line balances. The changes remain under review and are not active network rules.

XRP Ledger developers are reviewing targeted changes to the way issued-token permissions are checked in payment and liquidity-pool operations. The current public code record shows two relevant open proposals, both introduced on October 27.

The work is associated with the planned fixCleanup3_5_0 amendment. It should be described as protocol development, rather than a completed upgrade or evidence that ordinary XRP transfers now require issuer approval.

Pool tokens can carry exposure to two assets

The first open proposal, number 8648, would require an account acquiring a liquidity-provider token to satisfy authorization requirements for both assets in the corresponding automated market maker.

The reasoning follows the economic position. A pool token represents a claim connected to the pool’s assets. Checking only the direct deposit route may leave other ways to acquire the same exposure, including a transfer or exchange offer.

The proposal seeks consistency across those routes. That is relevant to an issuer using permission controls because a restriction needs to hold across the ways an account can obtain exposure, not simply at the most obvious entry point.

An existing balance should not create an exemption

The second open proposal, number 8650, addresses a payment-engine check that previously applied when an unauthorized trust line had a zero balance. The proposed behavior would prevent an existing unauthorized balance from being increased through further receipts.

The code discussion distinguishes receiving an issuer’s tokens from returning an obligation in the other direction. That distinction matters: preventing a new unauthorized holding need not prevent a legitimate redemption or remediation path.

Two broader proposals linked in the discussion are closed. Reading the current open changes is therefore more reliable than treating an earlier description of four proposed fixes as the final package.

Code review comes before activation

The XRP Ledger’s amendment documentation explains that changes affecting transaction processing must first be included in software and then receive sustained validator support. The stated threshold is more than 80% support for two weeks.

An open pull request has not completed that process. Review can alter the design, a release can include or defer it, and validators can make their own activation decisions. None of those stages should be collapsed into a claim that a rule is already enforced on mainnet.

For application developers, this means testing proposed behavior separately from the assumptions used in production. An anticipated error response is not necessarily the response a live transaction will receive today.

The impact is on issued assets and integration logic

These proposals concern permissioned issued-token relationships and claims on liquidity pools. They do not turn the native XRP asset into an issuer-controlled token. Keeping those categories clear avoids a misleading impression of a network-wide identity check on all holders.

The most useful preparation for affected integrations is to map the paths through which a customer receives a restricted asset, then identify how the application displays authorization failures and redemption options. A technically correct rejection can still confuse users if the interface presents it as an unexplained transaction error.

The development record does not by itself establish that an outside attacker exploited the paths under review. It establishes a proposed tightening of protocol behavior.

The next meaningful milestones are the merged implementation, its inclusion in a release and actual amendment status. Until then, the story is about improving consistency in permission controls, with the final scope still subject to review.

3 bright spots hiding beneath the Suns’ ugly start

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

I feel as if much of my focus lately has been doom and gloom as the Phoenix Suns have stumbled out of the gate to a 1–3 record, doing so in a manner that has been particularly frustrating. If there’s one thing I appreciate when watching any sport, and one of the primary reasons last year’s team resonated with the fan base as a whole, it’s defense. I value the ability to stop the opposition from executing its game plan.

Defense travels. Defense wins championships. Defense changes the outcomes of games. And thus far, the Suns’ defense has more holes than Swiss cheese. Yes, I spent about as much time putting together that analogy as the Suns have spent playing defense through their first four games.

As I sit here looking out my window at the literal gloom hanging over the Valley, I can’t remember a time in my life when we’ve experienced rain this consistently. And rather than allowing the weather to dictate my mood, I’ve decided to do what the motto of this very site encourages us to do. I’m going to look at the bright side. That’s right. Here are three things I’ve appreciated about the Phoenix Suns through the early portion of this season.

1. Three-Point Shooting

One of my biggest fears about this team entering the season was how they would perform from beyond the arc. When you ship off Grayson Allen and Royce O’Neale, who are currently shooting a combined 41% from deep with the Charlotte Hornets, there’s a legitimate concern that your team might end up sucking from three-point range.

When you look at it holistically, the Suns rank 17th in the NBA, shooting 34.8% from deep. They’re certainly maintaining their three-point volume, however, ranking seventh in the league with 40.3 attempts per game. But like so many things early in this season, those numbers are being dragged down by one particularly ugly game.

In the season opener against the Portland Trail Blazers, Phoenix shot 6-of-31 from beyond the arc, a miserable 19.4%. Since that game? The Suns are shooting 38.8%, connecting on 50-of-129 attempts. That 38.8% would rank fifth in the NBA. Damn you, Portland game. Damn you! It feels like we’re going to spend the next 15 games waiting for that statistical disaster to work its way out of the numbers.

Even when you include the Portland game, the Suns are doing something that shouldn’t surprise anybody. They’re getting quality three-point shooting from their bench. Phoenix’s reserves are shooting 38.8% from beyond the arc, the sixth-best mark in the league. Remove that Portland game from the equation, and the Suns possess the best shooting bench in the NBA at 45.9%.

Luke Kennard is shooting 53.8% from deep this season, Ryan Dunn is at 40%, Dillon Brooks is connecting at 42.9%, and Jordan Goodwin is shooting 39.1%. And if Collin Gillespie hadn’t gone 0-of-8 in that Portland game, he’d be sitting at 42.9% himself.

All of this bodes well for Phoenix moving forward. The Suns have demonstrated that they possess the ability to knock down shots from beyond the arc, and as the season progresses, those numbers should begin to better reflect what we’ve seen since opening night.

2. Miles Bridges

Yeah, Miles Bridges.

Don’t get me wrong, his defense has left plenty to be desired as he continues trying to understand where he needs to be positioned and how to properly support those around him. His missed assignments have been atrocious, and there’s no world in which I’m going to sit here and consistently defend his defensive performance throughout the early portion of this season. But holistically, I’ve liked what I’ve seen from him thus far.

He’s third on the team in scoring at 16.3 points per game, leads the Suns in blocks, and is their top rebounder. Yes, that 22.2% shooting from beyond the arc sucks, and it certainly doesn’t help the argument I made earlier about the team’s three-point shooting. My assumption, however, is that those numbers will begin to normalize with time.

What I’ve appreciated most about Miles Bridges is that he reminds me a little bit of Shawn Marion, somebody you don’t necessarily have to draw up offensive plays for. His offense is generated through effort, whether that’s putbacks, hustle points, or simply finding opportunities within the flow of the game. Every roster needs somebody like that. Somebody who can generate those junkyard points without demanding the basketball, yet still provide meaningful offensive production.

Think about it. Dillon Brooks is averaging 16.5 points per night, but he has to be incredibly ball dominant for those points to materialize. Those are proactive points, whereas much of what Bridges provides comes reactively, taking advantage of opportunities that develop around him. And through that lens, I can see the vision of what Miles Bridges is designed to be within the context of this Suns lineup.

The shooting absolutely needs to improve, as does the defense, but there’s something valuable about having a player who can contribute offensively without requiring the entire offense to revolve around him.

3. The Bench Unit

Without a doubt, the bench has been the bright spot for the Phoenix Suns. The connectivity that this group possesses is something the starting unit should envy, and a big part of that comes from the continuity the organization talked about throughout the offseason. Much of that continuity has manifested itself within the second unit, where the only new addition to the rotation has been Luke Kennard, who has stepped into the Grayson Allen role pretty seamlessly.

The Phoenix Suns’ bench ranks 10th in the league with a +2.2 net rating, third with a 72.5% assist percentage, and fourth with a 1.94 assist-to-turnover ratio. And that’s where, as a Suns fan, you begin to realize that this team might not be as far away from being successful as it feels after watching those first four games.

The starting unit certainly needs to pick up the slack, and those players need to learn how to play together. But with 40% of that group being new to the lineup, it’s going to take some time for that chemistry to develop. Meanwhile, the backbone of this team has been the bench and the way it operates.

So even as I sit here pounding the table for Collin Gillespie to become a starter, perhaps the stability he provides to the second unit is something that needs to be considered as part of that equation. Because if the starting unit can figure things out, the bench can continue delivering punch after punch after punch when it enters the game, beating the opposition into submission.

Of course, there are multiple issues with that starting unit, and we don’t know whether time will ultimately solve them. But you know it, and I know it, because we’ve seen it with our own eyes while watching these games. When the second unit checks in, you immediately feel a sense of responsibility entering the game.

The Suns have legitimate problems, particularly defensively, and those aren’t going to disappear because I decided to write something positive. But the shooting is encouraging, Bridges is finding ways to contribute, and the bench has established an identity worth building upon. There’s work to be done, plenty of it. But even with the clouds hanging over the Valley, there’s still a little sunshine breaking through.

Nets get first win of season with strong defensive effort, beating Pacers, 98-90

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →
INDIANAPOLIS, IN - NOVEMBER 1: Julius Randle #30 of the Brooklyn Nets moves the ball against the Indiana Pacers on November 1, 2026 at Gainbridge Fieldhouse in Indianapolis, Indiana. The Brooklyn Nets and Indiana Pacers both played in their ABA-inspired uniforms tonight. Their offensive performances looked like they were straight out of the 60s and 70s, but so was Brooklyn’s defense, and it got them their first win of the season.

So, if you ask us, old is gold.

The Nets were led by their two high-scoring vets, Michael Porter Jr. (23) and Julius Randle (20) as well two of the team’s younger set, Day’Ron Sharpe who finished with his first double double of the season (15 and 13) and the fast-developing Nolan Traore (16 points, four 3-pointers and six assists.)

Chaney Johnson started beside Nolan Traoré at the top of the key for a second straight game this evening, but the rotation was more fluid than what we’ve seen so far this year. The Nets went nine men deep in the first period. Terance Mann, who has only gotten burn during garbage time so far this year, even logged some first quarter minutes as the backup point guard with Mikel Brown Jr. sitting.

But with both teams looking to run early, and this being the first of a back-to-back for Brooklyn, those extra bodies were needed. There weren’t a lot of statistical fast break points, but both teams moved up the floor with a sense of urgency off misses in the first. Michael Porter Jr., who had a quiet night vs Detroit, was aggressive there and in the halfcourt, pitching in seven points while shooting 2-5 from deep in the opening frame. He ended up as our leading scorer by the end of the game with 23 points on 7-16 shooting from the field and 3-10 from deep. He also pulled down nine rebounds and dished three assists.

But outside of what MPJ provided early on, makes from beyond the arc came at a premium. When Indiana smoked Brooklyn last week, the triple was their primary weapon, as they shot 23-41 from downtown. But today, the Nets kept the safety on, limiting the Pacers to a 2-15 start from deep.

Nobody shot well from the other parts of the floor early either. In the first half, Brooklyn posted .385/.250 splits while Indiana put up .324/.200 marks. Both teams also combined to miss 10 free throws between the first two periods.

“We took good shots and they were not falling,” Fernández said. “A lot of times, the anxiety hits, but the team stayed together and won the first quarter, stayed together and won the second quarter, stayed together and won the third quarter, and I think that’s what matters the most.”

Indeed, even as eager participants in the rock fight, the Nets led after one. Despite their 0-5 record going into tonight, they’ve now won the first quarter in four straight games, and are 4-2 in the period on the season. They maintained that advantage heading into the break too, up by a 43-40 score. Porter Jr led the team offensively at that point with 13 points on 5-10 shooting, followed by Mo Wagner, who added 10 after a perfect 4-4 start.

“Just taking what the game gives me,” Wagner said at halftime. “I think we’re playing really hard. We really need one.”

Get one, they did. After another string of misses, Porter Jr. began the scoring for both teams in the second half with a four point play…

Then, Traoré hit his first triple of the game off a back-tapped offensive board from Day’Ron Sharpe. That gave Brooklyn their first double digit lead of the ball game. Day’Ron finished tonight with 15 points and 13 rebounds. Fernández called his effort tonight “unbelievable” postgame.

“I mean, he he was big on the boards,” Fernández said. “He did a little bit of everything. He made his three. He set screens. Very happy for him. We don’t win this game without him, and the reality is how much he cares about winning and impacting winning. And this game, 5-7, 13 rebounds, four assists, two turnovers? I think that line is impressive. So, all his energy and willingness to do the right things, it paid off.”

“Day’Ron’s a beast, for real,” Traoré added. “Like, he’s so strong, and when he’s playing like that he’s unstoppable. Me and him in the pick-and-roll is a really great thing for us, and if I keep finding him like that, he’s gonna be great like that every time.”

However, that make from MPJ seemed to open the floodgates for everyone. Sharpe buried his own three from the corner a few minutes later. He’s now shooting 3-7 from deep on the season. But the Pacers also nailed three triples in the third after making just four in the entire first half. That kept Indiana in striking distance, and with Porter Jr. picking up his fourth foul with 4:39 to go, Brooklyn’s lead, which already felt like something out of a dream, looked at risk of fading. It might’ve too if not for Traoré and Randle, who continued to hit shots with MPJ on the bench.

But while the Nets went into the fourth up nine, the Pacers wouldn’t go away. Triples from Andrew Nembhard and Larry Nance Jr. made it a three point game with 9:29 to go.

And, Randle and Traoré stiff armed Indiana again, closing out the game in the process as well.

After two made triples from Quenton Johnson, Julius Randle swung back with an isolation three from the top of the key before driving and assisting on two for Traoré during back-to-back possessions. That reestablished a 10-point lead for Brooklyn with about two and a half to play.

Traoré finished with 16 points and six assists while shooting 6-14 from the field and 4-8 from deep with just one turnover. Randle added 20 points, six assists, and five rebounds while shooting 5-of-15 from the field and 4-of-9 from deep. He was also a +17 for the game. Over the past four games, the 6’4” 20-year-old is averaging 14.3 points on 46/39/72 splits along with four assists and a steal in 26 minutes a game.

“Great spacing,” Traoré said. “I know they want to double Julius, so we take advantage of this and make shots. I’m happy and proud of my teammates.”

“I thought Nolan had great moments, especially in that second half,” Fernández added. “You know, he shot the three well, but those were very good threes. I think that all of them are like Julius spraying the ball, and I think that’s huge.”

They didn’t have the strongest competition with Tyrese Haliburton, Obi Toppin, Ivica Zubac and T.J. McConnell out, but the Nets defense was stout all night, and it stayed that way down the stretch. After those makes from Traoré, the Nets forced Indiana to miss four of their next five shots. It was tough, disciplined, and a crafty effort, as Noah Clowney sold an offensive “push off” foul on Nembhard to get a final stop 52 seconds to go.

Already up seven, that gave the Nets a technical free throw, as Nembhard got hit with a technical with after arguing against the call, and eventually, their first win of the season.

Final: Brooklyn Nets 98, Indiana Pacers 90

Milestone Watch

  • With 15 points and 13 rebounds in tonight’s win vs. Indiana, Day’Ron Sharpe recorded his fourth career game with 13+ PTS and 13+ REB, two of which have now come against the Pacers (16 PTS, 13 REB on 1/5/25 at home). It was also his 20th career double double, and his first since February 26th of last year.

Next Up

For the first time this season, the Nets will play the defending champs on Monday night at Madison Square Garden. New York’s title defense hasn’t gotten off to a great start with the team 2-4. The Nets have their unprotected first rounder this year, so a win here helps increase the value there. Also, it’s just good to win against the Knicks. The Nets haven’t done that since January 2023, a rivalry record of 14 straight.

“We need to get a win,” Traoré said of the matchup. “I think it’s been a long time since we’ve won against the Knicks, so we got to do it.”

This one tips off at 7:30 p.m. EST.
