5 momentous plays from Week 8: Lamar Jackson's shocking injury and Jim Harbaugh's passivity

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Lamar Jackson was back on Sunday. For one play.

His return was marred by a shocking injury one play into Sunday's game, and his injury figures to have massive long-term ramifications for the Ravens.

That was clearly the most momentous play of Sunday's early and afternoon action, although we also saw a struggling kicker miss again, threatening his career with the only franchise he's known.

We also witnessed the Cowboys melt down in real time and the Buccaneers somehow win a game without completing a forward pass.

The Chargers also have gone this season without a win, and it's easy to see why, given their passive approach.

Here are the five most momentous plays from Sunday's action:

1. Lamar Jackson's shocking thumb injury

Momentous? It could derail the Ravens' entire season.

On Jackson's first snap of Sunday's big showdown against Buffalo, he suffered a thumb injury after being hit by the Bills' Greg Rousseau.

Lamar Jackson heads to the locker room after his first pass attempt with an apparent hand injury. pic.twitter.com/JVUQYjJV8H

— NFL on CBS 🏈 (@NFLonCBS) November 1, 2026

Jackson was ruled out shortly thereafter and will be placed on injured reserve. Backup Tyler Huntley later was also knocked out of the game, replaced by undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano in his NFL debut. The Ravens hung around until the third quarter, but the Bills won going away, 36-10.

Fagnano was thrown into a tough spot, throwing a pick and losing a fumble. The Ravens' season is now very much in question, in spite of a 4-4 record. 

There was no mystery with this play. No second-guessing strategy. Just terrible luck in Jackson's return for the first time in nearly a month. His stay lasted exactly one play.

2. Bucs win going away … without completing a pass

Let's call the Buccaneers' third-down play on their opening possession of the second half their most momentous snap, just for laughs. Why? Because it was the final forward pass the Bucs attempted all game. 

The Bucs led, 9-0, at that point. Baker Mayfield's third-down pass in the deluge that was Tampa on Sunday fell incomplete. The Bucs would run 28 more plays from that point on. None of them would be thrown passes.

It was the first time since 1974 that an NFL team completed zero passes and won a game. The Bucs actually finished with negative pass yards thanks to a 2-yard sack on Mayfield.

Baker Mayfield completed zero passes, and the Tampa Bay Buccaneers still won. (Photo by Mike Stobe/Getty Images) Mike Stobe via Getty Images

Mayfield finished the game 0-for-3 passing in the rainy conditions. He also had three fumbles, but he recovered them all. 

He still had a better game than Falcons QB Michael Penix Jr., who attempted 24 passes, completed eight to his team and four to the Bucs. Penix's four INTs were a career high and were as many as he'd thrown in his previous 14 games combined. 

The Bucs appeared to figure out the wet conditions better than the Falcons. After Mayfield's final throw, they ran the ball 28 straight times — and 56 times total for 324 yards on the ground. Credit offensive coordinator Zac Robinson for understanding what it would take to win Sunday.

3. Minshew magic leads to stunning comeback in Dallas

The Cardinals overcame a 14-point deficit and survived a missed extra-point attempt at the end of regulation to shock the Cowboys in overtime. 

After Jacoby Brissett was benched, the Cardinals turned to magic man Gardner Minshew. The wizard delivered — and two key plays late in regulation helped complete the miracle comeback. 

The Cowboys took a 30-24 lead with just over a minute left, and the Cardinals were staring at a third-and-5 play from Dallas' 40-yard line with 27 seconds left. That's when Minshew hit Michael Wilson for a 27-yard pass to the 13-yard line.

The Cardinals were out of timeouts and had to spike it with 11 seconds left. Minshew went back to Wilson for the TD strike — and what appeared to be the game-winner on a terrific catch.

Tyler Allgeier, Wilson and Minshew made that final drive happen. It wasn't their fault that kicker Chad Ryland missed the extra point, sending the game to overtime.

In OT, it was more Allgeier and Wilson, despite Wilson reportedly suffering two broken ribs, which may have occurred on the game-tying TD.

Ryland made amends with the overtime field goal to win it. But they wouldn't have won without the clutch drive and Wilson's two great catches. 

4. Chargers' passive approach hurt them early

The Rams and Chargers started out slowly offensively in Sunday's matchup, but the Chargers gained offensive inertia first. The problem was they slowed themselves down.

The Chargers ended up kicking three field goals. Those kicks came on fouth-and-2, fourth-and-3 and fourth-and-3. Chargers head coach Jim Harbaugh was happy enough to take a 9-3 lead and not attempt to go for any of those first downs, despite all three coming from the Rams' 28-yard line or closer to the end zone.

You could argue the fourth-and-2 situation was the key play because it previewed the level of aggression with which the winless Chargers would play: shockingly low. 

Chargers coach Jim Harbaugh, offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel and Justin Herbert, left to right, tried to beat the Los Angeles Rams with field goals. (Photo by Harry How/Getty Images) Harry How via Getty Images

If Harbaugh wasn't going to go for it on that one, from the Rams' 16-yard line, he apparently wasn't ever going to go for it from the 28- or the 23-yard line. The funny irony was that all three third downs that preceded the field goals were handoffs, which often indicates an offensive coordinator willing to go for it on fourth. Nope!

The biggest fakeout came right before halftime. The Chargers were facing a fourth-and-3 after Omarion Hampton was stuffed for no gain on third. They let the clock tick down to the two-minute stoppage, brought the offense back out there and tried to get the Rams to jump. No one was buying it. The Rams knew how conservative they were, and the plot was easily foiled.

The Rams took the ball down the field in the final 90 seconds of the half, scored the go-ahead touchdown and never looked back.

5. Another missed field goal hurts Chiefs

Up three points in the fourth quarter in Denver, facing a fourth-and-8 in Broncos territory, Chiefs head coach Andy Reid did Sunday what he historically has done with a narrow lead in similar situations: He called on his kicker.

Reid's faith in Harrison Butker, however, backfired. Butker missed a 54-yard field-goal try with 2:36 remaining and the Chiefs leading, 24-21. It was Butker's sixth missed field-goal try of the season. Considering the Chiefs signed Justin Tucker to their practice squad last week, it's fair to wonder whether Butker’s miss was the final one in his Chiefs career.

Harrison Butker goes wide right!@Broncos take over with good field position on CBS & Paramount+ pic.twitter.com/rzelhOMh3n

— NFL on CBS 🏈 (@NFLonCBS) November 2, 2026

That was his 16th miss total, including extra-point attempts, since the start of last season.

The Broncos took over on their own 44-yard line, and — boom! — two plays later, they were in the end zone with a four-point lead.

Some might argue missing a three-point kick is immaterial in such a situation, but the Chiefs would have been kicking off, likely with better field position.

The Chiefs also had a shot to win the game late, down all the way to the Broncos’ 1-yard line, before a false start and a sack ended their hopes. Still, it's hard not to point to the critical field-goal miss — what it meant for this game and for Butker's career — as one of the crucial turning points.

Chicago Bears Trade Deadline Rumors on Khalil Mack and Tyson Bagent

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
INGLEWOOD, CA - OCTOBER 29: Tyson Bagent #17 of the Chicago Bears throws a pass during an NFL football game against the Los Angeles Chargers at SoFi Stadium on October 29, 2023 in Inglewood, California. (Photo by Kevin Sabitus/Getty Images) | Getty Images

On the Sunday Night Football pregame show, Mike Florio speculated that the Baltimore Ravens, who lost their two top quarterbacks today, could be in the market for a quarterback ahead of the NFL’s trade deadline, and he mentioned the Chicago Bears’ Tyson Bagent.

Perhaps it’s just logical dot-connecting by Florio, since Baltimore’s offensive coordinator, Declan Doyle, spent 2025 with the Bears, but it would make sense for them to check with Chicago about Bagent’s availability.

The Ravens are 4-4 and still in contention in a competitive AFC North, and with QB1 Lamar Jackson heading to injured reserve with thumb surgery, and QB2 Tyler Huntley dealing with turf toe, they’re left with rookie undrafted free agent Joe Fagnano. In their loss today, Fagnano completed 10 of 21 passes for 91 yards, 1 interception, and a passer rating of 40.0. He was also sacked 3 times, rushed for 3 yards, and lost a fumble.

Sports Illustrated’s Jason La Canfora (yeah, I know, JLC’s track record with Bears scoops has been bad)is reporting that the Ravens have already reached out to the Bears.

The Ravens top pursuit is Bears back-up quarterback Tyson Bagent, according to multiple high-ranking NFL personnel execs. They have reached out to Chicago, but are in a desperate position, DeCosta already got fleeced trading a second-round pick for a concussed and suspect offensive lineman. Multiple league sources told me through the summer that the Bears were rebuffing all overtures for Badgent (who makes $4M this year and $4M next year), who they believe can be a valuable back-up in Ben Johnson’s system.

“Chicago doesn’t have to trade him,” one NFL GM told me. “They like him … Wonder how hard they squeeze DeCosta?”

There were offseason rumors that the Bears received calls on Bagent, but the desperation of two injured quarterbacks and a deadline may be what they need to get an offer they can’t refuse.

The other Bears-related rumor that popped up on Sunday was a familiar one.

NFL Insider Ian Rapoport talked about Chicago’s big trade for Jeffery Simmons this morning on NFL Game Day, then added, “The Bears may not actually be done. I’m told they are still calling around. Still looking on the edges of their defense. Wouldn’t be surprised if a Khalil Mack trade was in the offer here. A possible reunion for Mack and the Bears.”

Mack’s current team, the Los Angeles Chargers, just lost to the Los Angeles Rams, dropping to 0-7, so if they aren’t ready to deal the 35-year-old Mack, they never will be.

Mack played a season-low 35 snaps in the 24 to 9 loss to the Rams, making 2 tackles. In 7 games, all starts, Mack has 21 tackles, 5 tackles for loss, and 3 sacks.

Mack was asked about the trade rumors today, and he said (via FOX Sports’ Eric Williams): “You’ve got to be where your feet are, man, ultimately. That can be the most difficult thing when you’re dealing with a lot of outside noise and stuff. Just trying to be where your feet are at all times. That’s kind of been my thing in years past.”

The Chicago Bears play tomorrow on Monday Night Football against the Seattle Seahawks, and the NFL’s trade deadline is November 10.
