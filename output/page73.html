november exhibitions from DESIGNBOOM RADAR

 

November’s designboom exhibition radar begins with an unlikely reunion in Paris, where two Alexander Calder mobiles return to public view after decades. In Zurich, 125 familiar lamps become a walk-through tunnel, while a new gallery in Hong Kong transfers the grain of wood into cast aluminum. Elsewhere, a house lost to Mexico City’s architectural history returns through an imagined botanical setting, and a new museum in Western Sydney opens with exhibitions on flight and water. 

 

Some of the exhibitions highlighted in earlier radars and listings on our dedicated events guide remain on view, giving designboom readers more time to encounter them around the globe.

 

 

mariko mori: all that shines

 

Visitors can enter Mariko Mori‘s Wave UFO, an interactive work that incorporates brainwave feedback, as part of All That Shines at Tokyo’s Mori Art Museum. Developed between 1999 and 2002, the large, futuristic installation connects neurological activity with visual experience. It appears in a retrospective of approximately 40 works spanning more than three decades of the Japanese artist’s practice.

 

The exhibition begins with Mori’s early performance-based photography and science-fiction-inspired imagery, including her experiments with cyborg identities in the 1990s. Later works turn toward Buddhist concepts of consciousness and questions about the cosmos, using sculpture and technology to approach subjects that resist direct representation. Together, the installations and moving-image works trace Mori’s changing ideas about the relationship between human perception and technological experience.

 

name: Mariko Mori: All That Shines
artist: Mariko Mori
museum: Mori Art Museum
location: Tokyo, Japan
dates: October 31st, 2026 — March 28th, 2027

Mariko Mori, Wave UFO, 1999-2002. installation view, Mariko Mori, Wave UFO, Kunsthaus Bregenz, Austria, 2003. image © Richard Learoyd | (header image: James Turrell’s Shangri La (Over The Hump), courtesy Powerhouse)

 

 

hella jongerius: whispering things

 

More than 400 objects trace the experimental practice of Dutch designer Hella Jongerius at the Museum für Kunst und Gewerbe Hamburg. Whispering Things, organized by the Vitra Design Museum, brings together furniture, textiles, ceramics, and early prototypes to examine how she has approached the making of objects over three decades.

 

The exhibition moves from commercially produced designs for companies such as Vitra and IKEA to more sculptural investigations of material and color. Sketches, collages, and films reveal the processes behind the finished work, including experiments that resist the predictability of industrial production. Throughout her career, Jongerius has returned to the relationship between craftsmanship and manufacturing, considering how objects can retain the irregularities and physical traces of their making.

 

name: Hella Jongerius: Whispering Things
designer: Hella Jongerius
museum: Museum für Kunst und Gewerbe Hamburg
location: Hamburg, Germany
dates: October 23rd, 2026 — June 13th, 2027

Hella Jongerius in ihrem Studio, Vorbereitungen für den Archiv- Umzug (detail), 2024, © Vitra

 

 

charlotte perriand: free to rethink the world

 

A reconstruction of Charlotte Perriand‘s 1929 domestic prototype, Équipement intérieur d’une habitation, appears at Barcelona’s Fundació Joan Miró in Free to Rethink the World. Produced by Cassina in collaboration with Perriand’s daughter, architect Pernette Perriand-Barsac, the installation revisits a project developed with Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret during Perriand’s early career.

 

The exhibition follows four decades of work through ten sections, extending from her Parisian interiors to her encounters with Japanese craftsmanship. Photographs and archival documents reveal her interest in creating spaces around the changing needs of their occupants. The presentation also revisits her friendship with architect Josep Lluís Sert, whose building for the Fundació Joan Miró opened in 1975, with Perriand attending its inauguration the following year.

 

name: Charlotte Perriand: Free to Rethink the World
designer, architect: Charlotte Perriand
museum: Fundació Joan Miró
location: Barcelona, Spain
dates: October 9th, 2026 — February 28th, 2027

Charlotte Perriand. Free to Rethink the World, curated by Martina Millà. photo by Alice Brazzit

 

 

no ordinary light: akari at 75

 

A monumental paper lantern measuring roughly two meters across takes its place at the Noguchi Museum in New York for No Ordinary Light: Akari at 75. The exhibition traces the development of Isamu Noguchi‘s Akari light sculptures, first conceived in Gifu, Japan, in 1951. Over the following decades, Noguchi developed more than 200 variations of the familiar illuminated forms.

 

Among the works identified for the presentation is the large-scale 200D, created around 1985, alongside archival sketchbooks and examples of Noguchi’s sculpture in other materials. The exhibition revisits Akari’s appearance at the home of Charles and Ray Eames in 1951 and its later presentation at the 1986 Venice Biennale, following the movement of these paper forms between sculpture and the domestic interior.

 

name: No Ordinary Light: Akari at 75
artist, designer: Isamu Noguchi
museum: The Noguchi Museum
location: New York, USA
dates: November 4th, 2026 — September 5th, 2027

Isamu Noguchi with Akari light sculptures in Japan, c. 1950s. The Noguchi Museum Archives, 07022. image © INFGM, ARS

 

 

sabine marcelis: light and color

 

At the Museum für Gestaltung Zürich, 125 circular IKEA lamps form an eleven-meter-long tunnel in Sabine Marcelis: Light and Color. A mirrored wall with a color gradient extends the installation visually, turning a familiar household object into an illuminated passage. The Dutch designer’s first museum exhibition brings together recent projects and new works conceived for the museum’s Toni-Areal location.

 

Another room contains eight Mathmos lava lamps, each nearly three meters tall, multiplied through surrounding mirrors. In Sunbeam Captured, developed with Boris Acket, mechanical waves move across a thin layer of water as a shifting light source follows the path of the sun. Material studies accompany the installations, revealing how Sabine Marcelis works with transparency and reflection.

 

name: Sabine Marcelis—Light and Color
designer: Sabine Marcelis
museum: Museum für Gestaltung Zürich
location: Zurich, Switzerland
dates: October 30th, 2026 — April 4th, 2027

Sabine Marcelis, image ©Jeroen Verrecht

 

 

misha kahn: all intensive purposes

 

A sprawling cast-metal dining table, complete with a sculpted torn napkin and scraps of food, appears among the new works in Misha Kahn‘s All Intensive Purposes at Friedman Benda in New York. Its glass-and-stone surface introduces a surprising degree of order to an object assembled from seemingly accidental gestures. Elsewhere, an onyx table adopts the appearance of a far more roughly constructed piece.

 

The exhibition marks ten years of collaboration between Kahn and the gallery, following his interest in the unpredictable paths of making. New upholstered furniture incorporates exaggerated forms and graphic surfaces, while multilayered plywood becomes the basis for sculptural objects. The title deliberately alters the familiar phrase ‘all intents and purposes,’ placing the emphasis on experimentation itself.

 

name: Misha Kahn: All Intensive Purposes
designer: Misha Kahn
gallery: Friedman Benda
location: New York, USA
dates: October 29th, 2026 — December 12th, 2026

Misha Kahn, All Intensive Purposes. image courtesy Friedman Benda

 

calder: françois premier

 

Two major Alexander Calder mobiles return to public view at Hauser & Wirth Paris in Calder: François Premier, an exhibition devoted to the artist’s years in France during the early 1950s. The presentation brings together Les Granettes (1953), a large standing mobile exhibited for the first time in 72 years, and The Putney Mobile (1954), making its public European debut.

 

The show takes its name from a seventeenth-century stone house in Saché, where Calder lived and worked after returning to France in 1953. Ink and gouache paintings from this period accompany the sculptures, tracing a particularly productive chapter in his career. There is a geographical coincidence, too: the Paris gallery occupies a historic building on rue François Premier.

 

name: Calder: François Premier
artist: Alexander Calder
gallery: Hauser & Wirth
location: Paris, France

dates: October 19th, 2026 — December 19th, 2026

Calder with Les Granettes (1953) in front of François Premier, Saché, c. 1965. photo by Paul Almasy, Corbis, VCG via Getty Images. © 2026 Calder Foundation, New York, ADAGP, Paris

 

 

dan flavin: some light

 

Green fluorescent tubes stretch across several meters in Dan Flavin‘s greens crossing greens (to Piet Mondrian who lacked green), one of the works presented in Some Light at the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao. Made in 1966, the installation arranges ordinary industrial fixtures into two illuminated sections, bringing color directly into the surrounding architecture.

 

The exhibition draws on the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum‘s holdings, with works dating from the 1960s through the 1980s and particular attention to Flavin’s early experiments. Beginning in 1963, the American artist used commercially available fluorescent lights to construct corners, barriers, and corridors. Their arrangement changes the appearance of the gallery itself, allowing visitors to experience color through the spaces the light occupies.

 

name: Dan Flavin: Some Light
artist: Dan Flavin
museum: Guggenheim Museum Bilbao
location: Bilbao, Spain
dates: November 11th, 2026 — April 4th, 2027

Dan Flavin, Some Light, Guggenheim Museum Bilbao

 

 

powerhouse parramatta

 

With architecture by Moreau Kusunoki, Powerhouse Parramatta opens in Western Sydney on November 7th with five inaugural exhibitions devoted to art, design, science, and technology. Among them is Task Eternal, an ambitious exploration of aviation and space designed by OPEN Architecture. Its more than 850 objects include drawings of flying machines by Leonardo da Vinci and a NASA Moon rock, alongside a new light installation by James Turrell.

 

Elsewhere, Overflow brings together seven Aboriginal organizations to examine the living systems of Water Country. The Mall, developed with OMA/AMO, investigates the shopping mall as an architectural and cultural form, while Es Devlin presents five immersive installations in The Turning. A fifth exhibition, The Dark, creates an imaginative environment for children. Together, they introduce the museum’s extensive new program.

 

museum: Powerhouse Parramatta

participants: multiple artists, designers, architects, and First Nations organizations
location: Parramatta, Australia
dates: from November 7th, 2026

Es Devlin, The Turning. image courtesy Powerhouse

 

 

tai shani: m.i.a.s.m.a.

 

British artist Tai Shani brings M.I.A.S.M.A. to Jupiter Artland outside Edinburgh, transforming the gallery through sculpture and sound. The exhibition revisits the ancient Greek tragedy Antigone, asking how mourning becomes a form of resistance and whose lives are allowed to be remembered. Its expanded title, Moral Injury in the Apocalyptic Sunsets of Modernity disAssociation, gives some indication of the political and emotional territory Shani is exploring.

 

Developed as an international co-commission, the project was first presented at Kunsthalle Bern earlier this year. Jupiter Artland’s presentation combines imagery with architectural elements to construct an environment shaped by feminist mythology. Visitors can also encounter Shani’s separate installation The Spell or The Dream in the sculpture park’s orchard, where its setting changes with the approaching winter.

 

name: Tai Shani: M.I.A.S.M.A.
artist: Tai Shani
gallery: Jupiter Artland
location: Edinburgh, UK
dates: October 30th, 2026 — April 11th, 2027

Tai Shani, M.I.A.S.M.A., exhibition view, Kunsthalle Bern, 2026. image © David Aebi

 

 

paul r. williams: architect for living

 

Previously unexhibited drawings and photographs offer a closer look at the career of Paul R. Williams in Architect for Living at LACMA in Los Angeles. Working from the 1920s through the early 1970s, Williams designed buildings across Southern California during a period of extraordinary urban expansion. His commissions ranged from private residences to major civic projects.

 

The exhibition explores this breadth through archival material, tracing a practice that moved between different architectural languages and types of clients. Williams became particularly well known for his houses for Hollywood figures, while his larger body of work included public housing and hotels. Presented alongside related exhibitions at the Getty Research Institute and USC Fisher Museum of Art, the LACMA survey places his architecture within the wider development of Los Angeles.

 

name: Paul R. Williams: Architect for Living
architect: Paul R. Williams
museum: Los Angeles County Museum of Art
location: Los Angeles, USA
dates: November 15th, 2026 — May 31st, 2027

Julius Shulman, Paul R. Williams portrait (1204-2), 1952, Getty Research Institute, Julius Shulman archive, image by Julius Shulman

 

 

sophie calle: matrimoni, rotture e funerali inclusi

 

An attempt to capture her mother’s final breath is among the personal investigations revisited in Sophie Calle’s Matrimoni, Rotture e Funerali inclusi at MAXXI in Rome. Bringing together eight projects made between 1978 and 2026, the exhibition follows the French artist’s longstanding interest in the rituals people invent around intimacy and loss.

 

Photographs and written accounts guide visitors through experiences shaped by separation, absence, and the passage of time. Calle’s projects often begin with a self-imposed rule, whether documenting the end of a relationship or recording encounters with people seeing the sea for the first time. The presentation extends beyond the museum with a new permanent installation at Rome’s Non-Catholic Cemetery for Foreigners, adding another setting to her exploration of how personal histories are remembered.

 

name: Sophie Calle: Matrimoni, Rotture e Funerali inclusi
artist: Sophie Calle
museum: MAXXI
location: Rome, Italy
dates: October 16th, 2026 — February 7th, 2027

Sophie Calle, SIAE (Roma, 2026), Autobiografie, Infarto silenzioso, 2017. image © Sophie Calle, courtesy the artist and Perrotin

 

 

álvaro urbano: helen

 

A vanished house in Mexico City becomes the starting point for Álvaro Urbano’s Helen at Museo Jumex. The exhibition revisits Casa Cueva, the home and garden created by architect Juan O’Gorman and inhabited with his wife, artist and botanically minded writer Helen Fowler O’Gorman. Built in the rocky landscape of Pedregal de San Ángel, the house brought architecture into close contact with its surroundings.

 

Urbano draws on Fowler O’Gorman’s 1961 book Mexican Flowering Trees and Plants, imagining the botanical world that once occupied the property. References to Juan O’Gorman’s stone murals and later interventions by artist Helen Escobedo add further layers to the presentation. Through an atmospheric architectural reconstruction, Urbano considers how a building’s history can survive through fragments, images, and the people who lived there.

 

name: Álvaro Urbano: Helen
artist: Álvaro Urbano
museum: Museo Jumex
location: Mexico City, Mexico
dates: October 16th, 2026 — February 7th, 2027

Álvaro Urbano, Helen, installation view, image by Ramiro Chaves

 

 

jeonghwa seo: precise play

 

Wood grain appears on the surface of cast aluminum in Precise Play, an exhibition by South Korean designer Jeonghwa Seo at Carwan Gallery‘s new Hong Kong space. Seo uses sand casting to transfer the texture of wood into metal, producing objects that retain traces of the organic material used in their making. The exhibition inaugurates the gallery’s new location.

 

Bringing together established pieces and newly commissioned works, the presentation moves between sculptural furniture and lighting. Seo also works with resin and natural rush, combining materials with different physical qualities in precisely constructed forms. His objects invite a second look at the relationship between surface and structure, particularly when a metallic piece appears to preserve the grain of timber or a functional object adopts an unexpected sculptural form.

 

name: Jeonghwa Seo: Precise Play
designer: Jeonghwa Seo
gallery: Carwan Gallery
location: Hong Kong, China
dates: November 20th, 2026 – February 20th, 2027

Cylinder Series JS-012, Jeonghwa Seo, image courtesy Carwan Gallery

 

 

yu nishimura: towards a sea

 

A sea glimpsed through a train window or partially hidden behind trees runs through Yu Nishimura’s Towards a Sea at Lafayette Anticipations in Paris. The Japanese painter’s first institutional solo exhibition in France brings together approximately 50 works from the past decade, including new pieces made for the presentation.

 

Working in oil and tempera, Nishimura moves between landscape and portraiture, allowing familiar subjects to become blurred or partially obscured. A bicycle wheel might appear alongside a human face, while the boundary between water and sky remains difficult to locate. The exhibition follows the artist’s interest in how places are recalled through imperfect images. His compositions borrow from the language of photography and cinema, and give ordinary scenes the elusive quality of something remembered.

 

name: Yu Nishimura: Towards a Sea
artist: Yu Nishimura
museum: Lafayette Anticipations
location: Paris, France
dates: October 21st, 2026 — January 3rd, 2027

Yu Nishimura, Towards a Sea at Lafayette Anticipations. image © designboom

 

 

giuseppe penone: the inner flow of life

 

Trees and other natural forms enter the architecture of the National Museum of Qatar in Giuseppe Penone: The Inner Flow of Life. Installed across the museum’s Baraha and Old Palace, the exhibition brings together sculptures and architectural interventions by the Italian artist, whose practice has explored the physical relationship between human bodies and the natural world for more than five decades.

 

The presentation moves between early works and recent pieces, including Rovesciare i propri occhi from 1970 and Vene di bronzo from 2026. Penone uses materials such as wood and bronze to investigate the forces that shape living matter, often bringing the traces of touch or growth into sculpture. A newly installed public artwork, Idee di Pietra, extends the project to Doha Old Port.

 

name: Giuseppe Penone: The Inner Flow of Life
artist: Giuseppe Penone
museum: National Museum of Qatar
location: Doha, Qatar
dates: October 26th, 2026 — February 13th, 2027

Rashid Johnson: A Poem for Deep Thinkers

 

More than 70 works spanning nearly three decades of Rashid Johnson’s practice come together at the Museum of Contemporary Art Chicago in A Poem for Deep Thinkers. The survey brings together photographs, large-scale installations, and films, including his black-soap Cosmic Slops paintings and the expressive faces of the Anxious Men and Broken Men series.

 

Throughout his career, Johnson has drawn on Black intellectual culture and art history to explore questions of identity, anxiety, and freedom. His 2024 film Sanguine turns toward his own family, examining relationships with his father, brother, and son, and incorporating a poem by writer and activist Amiri Baraka.

 

name: Rashid Johnson: A Poem for Deep Thinkers
artist: Rashid Johnson
museum: Museum of Contemporary Art Chicago
location: Chicago, USA
dates: November 7th, 2026 — April 25th, 2027

Rashid Johnson, The Reader, 2008. courtesy of Kenneth Montague, The Wedge Collection, and Monique Meloche Gallery. image © Rashid Johnson

 

 

global antiquity

 

An ancient Indian ivory statuette discovered in Pompeii offers a glimpse into the far-reaching connections explored in Global Antiquity at Fondazione Prada in Milan. Bringing together more than 150 artworks and artifacts, the exhibition examines cultural exchange across Afro-Eurasia between approximately 600 BCE and 1000 CE. The presentation occupies the foundation’s Podium building, with exhibition design by Rem Koolhaas and AMO/OMA.

 

Curated by Salvatore Settis, Anna Anguissola, and Chiara Ballestrazzi, the exhibition considers how objects traveled through systems of trade and political contact long before the modern idea of globalization. Their places of discovery become as revealing as their origins. Together, the artifacts trace connections between distant societies across 1,700 years.

 

name: Global Antiquity: Cultural Interactions in Afroeurasian Space, 600 BCE–1000 CE
curators: Salvatore Settis, Anna Anguissola, Chiara Ballestrazzi
exhibition design: Rem Koolhaas and AMO/OMA
museum: Fondazione Prada
location: Milan, Italy
dates: November 5th, 2026 — March 1st, 2027

Indian ivory statuette from Pompeii, 1st century AD. courtesy Ministero della Cultura – Museo archeologico nazionale di Napoli

Bitcoin Signal Crashes to 4-Year Low Last Seen During FTX Disaster, but Top Analyst Sees a Silver Lining

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin’s Coinbase Premium has plunged to a four-year low, a level last recorded in November 2022 after FTX’s implosion, when BTC was approaching a market bottom. The latest drop comes amid renewed market turmoil following reports of a historic $6.4 billion security breach involving Binance, the largest cryptocurrency theft on record.

However, one prominent crypto analyst believes the extreme bearish sentiment could signal a potential opportunity rather than further trouble for Bitcoin.

BTC’s Coinbase Premium Hits FTX-Era Low; Bullish or Bearish for BTC?

CryptoQuant CEO Ki Young Ju has highlighted a striking Bitcoin market signal after the Coinbase Premium plunged to its lowest level in four years, reviving memories of the November 2022 market bottom following the collapse of Sam Bankman-Fried’s FTX empire. As you may recall, BTC eventually bottomed out around $15,500 to $16,000.

The premium measures the difference between Bitcoin’s dollar price on Coinbase and its USDT-denominated price on Binance. CryptoQuant’s Coinbase Premium Index tracks this gap as a percentage of Bitcoin’s price. The latest reading on the chart stands at approximately -0.2%, equivalent to a price gap of roughly $156 based on Bitcoin’s displayed price of $77,900. 

Coinbase is often viewed as a barometer of U.S. investor demand, particularly among institutional players and dollar-based traders. A sustained negative premium suggests Bitcoin is trading cheaper on Coinbase than on Binance, potentially signaling stronger selling pressure from U.S. market participants or a lack of fresh buying interest. While the indicator does not reveal investor behavior with certainty, it points to relatively weak demand on a key U.S. trading platform.

While the indicator currently reflects weakening buying pressure from U.S. investors, Ju believes sentiment has become so bearish that it could present a contrarian bullish opportunity for Bitcoin, even as renewed turbulence linked to the reported Binance security breach rattles the broader crypto market.

“Market sentiment is bearish enough to be bullish, imo,” CryptoQuant CEO Ki Young Ju wrote on X, sharing the following chart.

Bitcoin Crashes Below $77,000 as Crypto Sell-Off Intensifies

Bitcoin has plunged more than 5% to sub-$77,000 as panic surrounding the reported $6.3 billion Binance security incident weighs on market sentiment. The weakening Coinbase Premium Index suggests U.S. trading activity is offering little support as Bitcoin grapples with mounting market uncertainty.

Notably, Binance has pledged to cover all losses linked to the incident using its own funds. The exchange reportedly set aside more than $6.34 billion to address the fallout, including $5 billion held in the exact token assets affected by the incident.

At press time, Bitcoin was trading at $76,739, according to CoinGecko. Meanwhile, the Crypto Fear and Greed Index dropped to 30, placing market sentiment firmly in the “Fear” zone and marking its lowest reading since Aug. 14.

That said, positive developments in the coming days could help restore confidence and support a rebound. However, traders may remain cautious until Binance withdrawals resume and greater clarity emerges about the incident’s broader implications for the crypto market.

West Ham’s £22m Midfielder Appears Untouchable Despite Poor Performances

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

West Ham have dropped to eighth in the Championship following Saturday’s 3-1 defeat against West Bromwich Albion, and the search for answers is becoming increasingly urgent. The Hammers are already losing ground in their attempt to secure an immediate return to the Premier League, with questions understandably being asked about Nuno Espírito Santo and his players.

There is clearly more than one factor behind West Ham’s problems, but one particular selection decision continues to baffle me. Arne Engels appears to have firmly established himself as a Nuno favourite, irrespective of how he performs. The Belgian midfielder remained on the pitch throughout yesterday’s defeat despite, in my opinion, being one of the worst players on it.

As West Ham’s biggest summer signing, costing £22million, Engels was always going to attract scrutiny. That doesn’t mean I have any particular axe to grind with him, but when a player commands that sort of fee and is repeatedly selected ahead of other options, surely we’re entitled to ask whether his performances justify it?

West Ham’s £22m Midfielder Appears Untouchable

Against West Brom, I thought Engels was truly awful. For large parts of the match, he was the worst player on the pitch.

Arne Engels has struggled in his Championship debut season

His passing was dreadful, he was repeatedly dispossessed by Ousmane Diakité, failed to follow players he was supposed to be marking and generally did an abysmal job with his set-piece deliveries.

In fact, aside from running around a bit, there was barely a facet of his game that he executed particularly well.

In short, Engels endured an absolutely dreadful afternoon, yet somehow managed to remain on the pitch until the final whistle.

While Nuno began withdrawing other players in an effort to fix whatever was broken, Engels appeared impervious to any repercussions.

And this is what I find increasingly difficult to understand.

Why Does Nuno Refuse To Rotate Engels?

A lot of my friends believe this Newcastle United squad explains our erratic start to the season.

We lack quality in the right places, they say, and the manager is being judged for weaknesses in the players he has been given.

There is substance in that argument. But there is also a danger in stopping there.

If the Newcastle United squad is the problem, we still have to ask why this is the squad we have.

Its limitations cannot become an explanation that leaves everyone responsible for assembling and managing it beyond criticism.

After the Carabao Cup exit at Everton, I wanted to test the premise. How good are these players compared with the rest of the Premier League? Are we demanding too much of them, or accepting too little from the people directing them?

I looked at published player and team ratings, squad valuations and the club’s current personnel. My judgement was that eighth is a defensible working estimate of Newcastle’s standing on individual quality, with seventh to eleventh a reasonable range. Those positions are my synthesis, not a published forecast or a statistical confidence interval.

The central conclusion is more useful than the precise number. These measures do not identify an obvious top-four squad. Neither do they establish that Newcastle are getting everything this group can give. Expectations need to reflect the resources available; accountability must then focus on what the club does with them.

The FC 27 team ratings published by FCRadar put Arsenal on 85, Manchester City on 84, Liverpool on 83 and Manchester United on 82. Chelsea and Tottenham are on 81. Newcastle are on 79, alongside Aston Villa, Crystal Palace, Nottingham Forest and Sunderland. Six clubs sit above us; five share the next band. The evidence supports a group of broadly comparable squads, not a precise order within it.

These are computer-game ratings and their limitations matter. They are subjective judgements on a common scale, not scientific measurements of footballing ability. Different websites reproducing the same database do not provide independent corroboration. I would give them weight as one reference point, never treat them as a verdict.

FotMob’s squad values offer a separate, equally imperfect comparison. Newcastle stand eighth at €641 million, behind Brentford at €651 million and narrowly ahead of Palace at €635 million and Villa at €629 million. Tottenham, sixth, are valued at €951 million. Age, contracts and resale prospects influence these figures. They cannot tell us how a team ought to play. They do, however, reinforce the picture of Newcastle among a closely matched chasing group.

The internal balance is also revealing. Published unit ratings put our defence on 81, midfield on 78 and attack on 79. Hall is rated 83; Livramento, Schär, Botman and Thiaw are on 81. Nico González is also on 81, Joelinton on 80, with Barnes and Wissa both on 80. On this assessment, the defensive resources are stronger than those further forward.

That does not make any player inadequate. It does suggest that talk of “the squad” can obscure the real issue. A collection of capable footballers can still lack the balance required to produce a capable team. Recruitment has to answer that problem, and coaching has to work with it.

So my friends have grounds for questioning the squad’s immediate strength. They have not thereby established that the players are the principal cause of our inconsistency. Ratings describe attributes; they do not explain decisions, preparation or performances. An approximate eighth-place squad can be well coached or poorly coached. The ranking cannot distinguish between the two.

Matthias Jaissle must therefore be judged on the collective he builds. Are players clear about their jobs? Does the approach suit the strengths available? When opponents expose a weakness, does he adapt? A manager cannot manufacture elite quality, but he can be expected to make the quality he has count.

That judgement requires a meaningful run of performances. The win at Palace belongs in the assessment alongside the defeat at Everton. Neither should become a convenient exhibit selected to prove a conclusion already reached. Over time, we should see clearer roles, better chances and a side that is harder to play through. Patience has a purpose when it allows improvement to take hold. It becomes harder to defend when improvement cannot be identified.

The recruitment policy deserves the same scrutiny. Sky Sports reported in July that Newcastle were pursuing younger players with development and resale potential, taking inspiration from Borussia Dortmund. The logic is credible. Buying talent before its price becomes prohibitive may offer a route towards stronger competition.

But development is work and its costs arrive before its rewards. Young players need suitable roles, experienced colleagues and a stable environment. Their potential cannot be counted as present quality when setting expectations, then invoked as a reason to suspend judgement when results disappoint.

If the club chooses to exchange some immediate readiness for future potential, that is a strategic decision. Those making it must explain the sporting benefit, the timescale and how progress will be judged. Supporters can understand a trade-off. They should not have to discover it through disappointing results.

Nor should resale value quietly become the measure by which sporting progress is judged. Developing valuable players can help finance a stronger Newcastle. The test is whether the team becomes stronger as the policy matures. Otherwise, we risk becoming better at producing assets without becoming better at competing.

This approach is not without risk. Its success depends on recruitment identifying the right players, development turning potential into performance, and the manager building a team capable of competing while that work takes place. Get even one of those major elements seriously wrong and the consequences could extend well beyond missing out on Europe. We could find ourselves playing our football in the lower divisions again. That is a risk, not a prediction, but any credible long-term plan must protect the club against it. Premier League survival cannot simply be assumed while we wait for the strategy to bear fruit.

That is why responsibility needs to be specific. Players answer for their performances. The manager answers for organisation, selection and improvement. Those directing recruitment answer for the quality and balance available to him. Shared responsibility must never mean responsibility dispersed so widely that nobody has to account for the outcome.

Photo by The Mag

On this evidence, a top six finish would exceed the working baseline; Champions League qualification should not be treated as an automatic entitlement. But accepting that distinction does not oblige us to accept drift. We can recognise the squad’s limitations and still demand convincing evidence that Newcastle are overcoming them.

I have supported this club since 1965. I know that ambition alone does not win matches, and that teams can achieve far more than their individual reputations suggest. What concerns me is the possibility of lowered expectations acquiring the language of a long-term plan, without the discipline that makes a plan credible.

The squad may explain why Newcastle cannot yet compete consistently with the best. It cannot, by itself, explain whether we are moving closer to them. That is the question the manager and those running the club must answer together.

If we are being asked to accept less today in pursuit of more tomorrow, what evidence will distinguish a club building towards success from one learning to live without it?
