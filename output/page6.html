The Washington Post reports: Tens of thousands of South Koreans had their financial data exposed online in recent weeks after at least nine major banks there suffered cyberattacks that suggested a dark future for online security. One man, a 26-year-old living in southern China, was allegedly behind the attacks, according to an investigation by cybersecurity company CrowdStrike. Traces left online and failure to secure his own systems allowed investigators to piece together the man's name, age, location and what he allegedly did. The clues revealed that the devastating assault on some of Asia's most sophisticated financial institutions was the work of an amateur. The attacks were launched with help from a few artificial intelligence chatbots, CrowdStrike found, before asking one to help sell the stolen data online and draft a fake résumé touting cybersecurity skills... The hacker who broke into the South Korean banks had access to Anthropic's Claude, but because the U.S. firm's tools generally refuse to take illegal actions, free Chinese models were also used, including DeepSeek, CrowdStrike found. "The models are interchangeable," said Meyers, the CrowdStrike executive. The Post calls it "part of a wave of AI-enabled hacking that is advancing across the world, triggering alarm inside national governments as well as multinational companies." "Basically any pedestrian, non-hacker can take these frameworks and start leveraging them to go after different targets," said Adam Meyers, senior vice president of counter adversary operations at CrowdStrike. The company has tracked an increase in the speed with which cyberattacks can move once inside a target's networks, as seasoned hackers and rank amateurs alike get a boost from AI tools. "Anyone can be a super-hacker now," Meyers said... Many cyber experts still expect the security reckoning to intensify as AI tools that can find and exploit software bugs become more widely distributed and understood. [Last] Monday, The Post reported that cybersecurity researchers from the start-up Cathedral who set out to test Anthropic's security found that they could access internal messages and meeting transcripts. Cathedral reported its findings to Anthropic, which said it resolved the newly discovered problems... Security experts say it's unlikely that any hacker is forgoing the use of AI tools right now. Even in America, critical infrastructure like energy and water companies need to be "immediately and comprehensively" tested for exploitable bugs, according to Snehal Antani, chief executive of cybersecurity company Horizon3. He tells the Post the government might need to fund that remediation, and even form "a public and private partnership to accelerate incident response."

Bitcoin Core 32.1 Arrives With Privacy Fixes and New Compatibility Checks

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Bitcoin Core 32.1 introduces network, privacy and interface changes, while 31.2 and 30.4 provide maintenance updates. Operators need to check integrations as well as version numbers.

Bitcoin Core’s late-October releases give node operators several upgrade paths, but the details deserve more attention than the version labels. The 32.1 release notes explain that 32.0 was tagged but never fully announced or released because of last-minute issues.

That makes 32.1 the relevant new major-version release to examine. Separate 31.2 and 30.4 updates address maintained earlier branches, with their own change lists and compatibility considerations.

Privacy and relay behavior are part of the update

The 32.1 release notes describe a fix for a possible IP-address exposure in a particular private-broadcast fallback configuration. They also describe changes to outgoing transaction relay limits and additional controls around private-broadcast queues.

These are specific implementation changes, not a claim that all prior Bitcoin transactions exposed their senders or that the new release guarantees anonymity. The circumstances described in the notes matter when assessing who is affected.

Node software manages connections and message handling as well as checking blockchain rules. Improvements to those functions can therefore be useful even when they do not create a new transaction type or alter Bitcoin’s supply.

Applications need to inspect interface changes

The same release changes some defaults and removes previously deprecated fields from certain interfaces unless compatibility options are requested. An application that assumes a field always exists can fail even when the node itself starts and synchronizes correctly.

For an exchange, wallet service or payment processor, an upgrade is consequently an integration task. The important question is whether the software calling the node still interprets responses correctly and handles the outputs it receives.

A version number alone cannot answer that question. The exact interfaces used by the application determine which release-note items need testing, while unrelated changes may have no effect on that deployment.

Earlier branches have separate maintenance releases

Bitcoin Core also publishes release notes for 31.2 and 30.4. Both describe bug fixes and performance improvements, providing a route for operators who need to maintain an existing major-version environment while planning a broader migration.

That does not make every branch interchangeable. Support status, available fixes and application compatibility should be checked together. Remaining on an old branch indefinitely is different from using a maintained branch as part of a deliberate upgrade plan.

The project’s lifecycle information is especially relevant to that distinction. A release once considered stable can eventually move beyond the period in which its maintainers expect to provide further updates.

Operational verification is the practical milestone

A successful upgrade should leave the node on the intended version, connected to the correct network and serving its dependent applications as expected. Merely installing a binary does not establish those outcomes.

Operators also need to preserve the information required to recover their service if an integration issue appears. Where a node manages wallet data, that includes understanding the compatibility of the wallet format before attempting any rollback. The release notes, not assumptions based on a previous upgrade, should guide that assessment.

For ordinary Bitcoin holders who do not operate their own node, these releases are infrastructure news rather than a requirement to move coins. A node-software update is not a token migration.

The significance of 32.1 lies in the accumulated work behind reliable validation, private transaction handling and maintainable interfaces. Those improvements are less visible than a price move, but they affect the systems on which wallets, exchanges and independent verification depend.

Where did Wordle come from?

Originally created by engineer Josh Wardle as a gift for his partner, Wordle rapidly spread to become an international phenomenon, with thousands of people around the globe playing every day. Alternate Wordle versions created by fans also sprang up, including battle royale Squabble, music identification game Heardle, and variations like Dordle and Quordle that make you guess multiple words at once. 

Wordle eventually became so popular that it was purchased by the New York Times, and TikTok creators even livestream themselves playing.

What's the best Wordle starting word?

The best Wordle starting word is the one that speaks to you. But if you prefer to be strategic in your approach, we have a few ideas to help you pick a word that might help you find the solution faster. One tip is to select a word that includes at least two different vowels, plus some common consonants like S, T, R, or N.

What happened to the Wordle archive?

The entire archive of past Wordle puzzles was originally available for anyone to enjoy whenever they felt like it, but it was later taken down, with the website's creator stating it was done at the request of the New York Times. However, the New York Times then rolled out its own Wordle Archive, available only to NYT Games subscribers.

Is Wordle getting harder?

It might feel like Wordle is getting harder, but it actually isn't any more difficult than when it first began. You can turn on Wordle's Hard Mode if you're after more of a challenge, though.

Amid Lamar Jackson's latest injury and Joe Burrow's roller-coaster day, are Steelers quietly pulling away in AFC North?

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

As Jaylen Warren hit a center hole and then zigged to the right, 5 yards still stood between the Pittsburgh Steelers running back and the successful conversion of a third-and-13 in the first quarter.

But Warren's teammates blocked Cleveland Browns pursuers downfield. Warren slipped behind one block and out of the grasp of another defender as he zagged back toward the middle of the field. 

Not until Warren had barreled 51 yards would the Browns down him. By then, the Steelers were well on their way to a field goal that would extend Pittsburgh's lead to 10-0.

"I was running, running out of gas, then finding the next dude to make a cut," Warren said. "I try to make cuts into my blockers so I can get a blocker on my way down so that's basically what it looks like."

5️⃣1️⃣ YARDS 🚂 @Nunless2pic.twitter.com/3d2lNSEUfv

— Pittsburgh Steelers (@steelers) November 1, 2026

The Browns would never catch up.

And on a day when the Steelers' 27-6 statement delivered them sole possession of the AFC North lead, the Steelers also found themselves facing another reality.

Like Warren's run, gaining 51 yards when they needed just more than 13, the Steelers won one game and were arguably helped as much by their other two division rivals.

Pittsburgh's recipe to win seemed as telling as its statement itself.

A Steelers team that won just two of its first four, dropping a game to the same Browns team it’s now beaten, has since ripped off four straight wins.

In the AFC North, they seem to have the right ingredients at the right time.

"You got to stop the run and you got to make sure you run the ball," defensive lineman Cam Heyward said after tallying 1.5 sacks, two tackles for loss, one pass deflection and six tackles. "And that's what it's always been about in the AFC North."

For the second straight week, the Steelers outgained their opponent on the ground by more than 130 yards.

Their 148 to 18 advantage over the New Orleans Saints last week secured a win in Paris. Their 200 rushing yards to the Browns' 34 on Sunday made a divisional opponent one dimensional, paving the way for eight sacks of Deshaun Watson a month after the Steelers notched just two on him.

Credit the Steelers' two-headed running attack of Warren and Rico Dowdle for part of that defensive success. Yes, Heyward gave the running backs flowers for what the defense did.

Why? 

"They do a great job of being a one-two punch," Heyward said. "And so when they're able to sustain drives, that means [the defense] is on the sideline, and we can put our feet up."

Rodgers after 21-point win: 'Should've been a lot more points'

As rhythm eluded the Browns against the Steelers, AFC North counterparts Baltimore and Cincinnati were struggling, too.

The Baltimore Ravens entered Sunday with high hopes of avenging last year's blown game against the Buffalo Bills. Quarterback Lamar Jackson, for the first time in four games, was healthy enough to suit up.

Then, on the Ravens' first offensive snap, Jackson hit his throwing hand against Bills edge rusher Greg Rousseau. A close-up shot from CBS' broadcast footage revealed what appeared to be swelling and an unnatural bend.

Later Sunday afternoon, the Ravens placed Jackson on injured reserve and announced he would undergo thumb surgery.

The Bengals escaped less scathed from a shootout against the Tennessee Titans. Most importantly for their playoff hopes, quarterback Joe Burrow suffered no injuries. But the Bengals still needed overtime to beat a one-win Titans team, Cincinnati's defense ceding a season-high 36 points. Burrow's 472 and six touchdowns from scrimmage (462, five passing) suggested his ceiling is as high as ever. The Bengals' four turnovers, with three Burrow interceptions and one Chase Brown fumble, called into question how reliable they are.

For a team that's already lost in a head-to-head with the Steelers, can the Bengals afford that?

The Ravens' and Bengals' weaknesses on display — Baltimore's more punishing, down to its emergency third quarterback after Tyler Huntley suffered a right toe injury — highlighted the division opportunity in the Browns' matchup against the Steelers.

Pittsburgh rose to the occasion. Cleveland stumbled.

"We certainly earned that," Browns head coach Todd Monken said of the loss. "We didn't start fast, turned the ball over a couple times early, then going three-and-out. Really never got the run game going. Couldn't stop the run early. Went three-and-out twice to start the second half, 10 penalties, didn't play with poise, however many sacks. So, didn't coach well enough, didn't play well enough. 

"That's what's going to happen when you play a good team, a well-coached team, and you don't play your best. You don't give yourself a chance."

The Steelers leaned into their run game, parlaying 33 carries into 200 yards and all three of their touchdowns. 

Warren led the pack with 143 yards and a score. Dowdle added 15 for 56, while tight end Pat Freiermuth scored on a 1-yard keeper.

"He's a bowling ball with teeth, man," outside linebacker T.J. Watt described Warren.

The Steelers appear to be finding their identity in their first year under head coach Mike McCarthy, who's reuniting with longtime Packers colleague and quarterback Aaron Rodgers.

Rodgers still makes a handful of key plays, including a third-quarter pass to tight end Darnell Washington that Washington took 58 yards. But even those plays most often feature heavy yards after the catch, Washington tacking on 44 to the 14 Rodgers sent the ball by air.

Mastering the line of scrimmage to set up his run game, Rodgers set up his teammates for success against the Browns. He felt the performance was "not very complete," particularly with two missed opportunities in the red zone, even as winning at the line of scrimmage powered Pittsburgh to victory.

"Defense played excellent, special teams did fine, but offense should've been a lot more points…" Rodgers said. "We'd like to play our best and still win. We also like to win. So we're 6-2 going into the bye, first in the division, so right where we want to be."

What do playoff predictors say about AFC North teams' chances?

At 4-4, the Ravens' chance at the division seems to be slipping. Healthy or nearly that, they may be the most talented team. But in a competitive division, with Jackson out at least four weeks and "potentially" the whole season per head coach Jesse Minter, Derrick Henry and co. seem unlikely to compensate.

The Ravens already return to prime time on Thursday to face the 5-2 Jacksonville Jaguars. Will Baltimore's fifth loss come then?

The Browns, right now, hold a 32% chance of making the playoffs, according to Next Gen Stats' playoff predictor model. It's a chance halfway through the season that perhaps exceeds some Browns fans’ expectations entering Week 1 (and even moreso coming out of a miserable Week 1) and a benchmark that shouldn't surprise in light of the Browns' quiet rebuild. But it may disappoint fans taken in by Watson's fourth-quarter comebacks amassing more early wins than predicted.

This loss to Pittsburgh wasn't one Cleveland's playoff chances could comfortably afford. The Browns have more wins than every NFC East and NFC South team, but the AFC wild-card bid is still an uphill battle.

Then comes Cincinnati. At 5-2, the Bengals currently have an 86% chance to make the playoffs even as their division hopes are complicated by a 30-27 Week 3 loss to the Steelers. The teams rematch again in just two weeks, in Cincinnati. Pittsburgh will be more rested, coming off the bye. If the Steelers win, their path to the division crown will clarify still further.

"That game in two weeks is going to be really important," Rodgers said. "But yeah, right where we want to be."

Can the Steelers' frenetic defense and old-school offense power them deep into the playoffs? Despite their current hold on the division, Next Gen Stats — which factors strength of remaining opponents into its 10,000 simulations that create the formula — estimates a 75% chance to make the playoffs, strong if not as strong as Cincinnati's.

Pittsburgh was guarded in celebrating that its lopsided win could represent a step forward from four prior one-score wins. The Steelers' identity is starting to crystallize. But nine regular-season games remain.

"We can't take the foot off the gas one second here," Watt said. "Get our bodies back for bye week and come back blazing. …

"We still need to continue to go. Our identity is still being formed. A lot of football left."
