Luka Dončić knows the Lakers have to be ‘way better’ when teams blitz him

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →
Los Angeles, - October 21: Guard Luka Doncic #77 of the Los Angeles Lakers reacts after a foul by forward Yaxel Lendeborg #1 of the Golden State Warriors as guard Brandon Williams #12 of the Golden State Warriors tries to separate them in the first half of a NBA basketball game at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. (Photo by Keith Birmingham/MediaNews Group/Pasadena Star-News via Getty Images) | MediaNews Group via Getty Images

Any team that rosters Luka Dončić is going to have to learn how to adjust to defenses blitzing ball screens.

Few players in the league, if any, are as dangerous in the pick and roll as Luka. The easiest way for opponents to keep him from doing damage is to stop him before it can really start. That typically manifests itself in blitzing Luka with two defenders on ball screens.

When things are clicking, the Lakers can quickly turn that into an advantageous 4-on-3 scenario. Things have not been clicking for them yet this season, though, and they certainly weren’t in the team’s loss to the Warriors.

While Luka had a huge night with 37 points, the rest of the Lakers struggled as they blew a big early lead and dropped their second game of the season. After the contest, Luka spoke about teams doubling him in the pick and roll and how the team needs to adjust.

“We just need to do better with that,” Luka said. “You create an advantage with two on the ball and you got to go score. It’s four on three so we have to be better there. …We have to be aggressive to score. It’s an advantage, four on three, and I think we have to be way better at this.”

At the beginning of that process is the chemistry with Luka and Walker Kessler. But nearly every aspect of the process is a work in progress, including making good on the opportunities they create when they have an advantage.

“I think the biggest thing is just shooting the shots that we get,” Jake LaRavia said postgame on Friday. “A lot of times when you pass up open shot opportunities, it turns into a disadvantage for us and it ends up into either a missed shot or a turnover and we’d much rather take the good shot over that. It’s just something that we’re going to learn more as the season goes on and as we see this more often.”

The irony of LaRavia being the one saying they can’t turn down shots considering how he ended last season. Even on Friday, there were multiple open looks he did not take from three. But, if nothing else, that just means he’s the one who has heard the most about the need to take advantage of those opportunities.

The good news is that it’s only just November and this isn’t a surprise that the team needs to build chemistry. But once they are able to get things to click out of the pick and roll, then it feels like things will really take off to another level.

Marty Erlichman, Barbra Streisand’s Longtime Manager, Dies at 97

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Martin Lee “Marty” Erlichman, Barbra Streisand‘s manager for more than six decades, died on Oct. 31. He was 97.

Erlichman’s death was due to “age-related causes,” a press release issued on Sunday (Nov. 1) states.

As an entertainment manager, Erlichman is credited with discovering Streisand and helping to shape and guide her career since the early 1960s.

Erlichman was born in Brooklyn, New York, on Sept. 13, 1929, and is an alumni of Drake University in Des Moines, Iowa. He’s the recipient of both an Emmy Award and a Peabody Award for his work as executive producer of the 1994 HBO television special Barbra: The Concert.

He first encountered Streisand when she was 19 years old and singing as the opening act for comedian Phil Leeds at Bon Soir nightclub in Greenwich Village. Streisand has recalled that her soon-to-be new manager told her he got chills the moment he heard her vocals; she felt comfortable with him immediately, feeling he “validated all my dreams” while being “like a guy from my old neighborhood. Turns out he grew up near me in Brooklyn. We spoke the same language.”

He helped her secure her first recording contract, with Columbia Records. Streisand is thankful he negotiated giving her creative control on her repertoire and album covers, “an unprecedented contract for a first-timer.”

“Although Marty lived a long and full life, nothing can prepare you for the reality of losing someone so dear,” Streisand said in a statement following Erlichman’s passing. “I truly thought he would live forever. In my eyes, he was simply indestructible — always by my side, through everything. And we never even had a contract. Marty can never be replaced, and I will be forever grateful for his friendship, loyalty, and unwavering support.”

Streisand also said, “A few weeks ago, he was trying to convince me to go on tour, and told me he’d be there every step of the way. I know it’s extremely rare for show business relationships to endure for as long as mine did with Marty. He trusted me, and I trusted him. This is a challenging business, but I always felt protected with Marty by my side.”

A statement from Erlichman’s family reads, “To the world, he was a giant of Hollywood; to us, he was a deeply devoted and loving father, husband, grandfather, and rock. We feel his loss deeply but are endlessly proud of the life he lived and the indelible mark he left on the world.”

Throughout his career in management — with The Clancy Brothers, Tommy Makem and Gary Puckett & The Union Gap among his other music artist clients, and additional clients including comedian/actor Chevy Chase, heavyweight boxing champion George Foreman and record producer/songwriter Bob Crewe — Erlichman also worked as a film producer, with credits including Streisand’s 1974 comedy For Pete’s Sake; psychological thriller Coma (1978), starring Michael Douglas, and romantic thriller Breathless (1983), starring Richard Gere.

Erlichman is survived by his wife of 52 years, Miko Mayama Erlichman, as well as son J.J. Erlichman (who is a tour and production manager) and daughter-in-law Talia, and four grandchildren: Mia, Sarah, Olivia, and Jack. His family plans to hold a private service to honor his life.

Donations may be made in Erlichman’s honor to St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital or the National Kidney Foundation in lieu of flowers.

Larry King and Jerry Seinfeld's Classic Exchange Over Whether NBC Canceled 'Seinfeld' Happened 19 Years Ago Today

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Legendary CNN talk show Larry King Live ran from 1985 to 2010 and was one of the network’s longest-running, most popular shows that featured King interviewing world leaders, professional athletes, and really just about everyone in-between.

Back on November 1 in 2007, King’s guest happened to be Jerry Seinfeld, who was roughly a decade removed from the series finale of Seinfeld, the wildly successful sitcom he famously co-created with Larry David.

Given the immense popularity of the show, the topic was bound to come up in some capacity. So when King asked Seinfeld about it, an equally awkward and funny exchange between the two was gifted to the masses.

It’s the type of clip that, regardless of whether it’s landing on the anniversary of the moment or not, always tends to make the rounds on social media for one reason or another.

King, an icon of the industry, was always very open about being purposely unprepared for the thousands of interviews he conducted over the years.

Unfortunately, at times that free-flowing approach did not prove to be the greatest formula, as he made the mistake of tossing up the idea that Seinfeld may have been canceled by NBC after nine seasons.

“You gave it [Seinfeld] up, right?” King asked. “They [NBC] didn’t cancel you, you canceled them.”

After what felt like a much longer pause than it actually was, a baffled Seinfeld replied, “You’re not aware of this? You think I got canceled, are you under the impression I got canceled?”

From there, the chaos train had already left the station with some unintended laughter well on the way:

“I thought that was pretty well-documented,” Seinfeld continued as he talked over King’s words.

“Is this still CNN? … But, yeah, I went off the air, I was the number one show on television, Larry. Do you know who I am?” Seinfeld asked while attempting not to burst into laughter. “Seventy-five millions viewers! With King offering an apology while desperately trying to get to a commercial break in promoting the release of Seinfeld’s new Bee Movie, the exasperated comedian kept interrupting, finally shouting with a laugh, “Can we get a resume in here for me, that Larry can go over?”

After King sadly passed away at the age of 87 in 2021, Seinfeld showed love to both the legend himself and their amusing exchange that had once again begun circulating on social media:

Google says Asian advertisers spending less off due to Europe's customs changes

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Google has revealed some of its Asian customers – almost certainly Chinese e-tailers – are buying fewer ads.

Speaking on Alphabet’s Q3 earnings call last week, chief business officer Philipp Schindler addressed the slowdown in ad sales growth with the following remark: “In retail, some of our APAC advertisers were impacted by the EU eliminating its de minimis exemption.”

That’s a reference to the European Union’s changes to import duties, which saw the bloc introduce a temporary €3 customs duty for every consignment valued at under €150 imported from outside the EU.

The EU previously operated the de minimis exemption that Schindler referred to, meaning it collected no import duty on almost 5.9 billion low-value items shipped to the continent in 2025 alone. The new regime aims to protect European retailers from cheap imports.

A great many of those items came from Chinese e-tail giants – the likes of Temu, Shein, and Alibaba – who are thought to account for perhaps ten percent of advertising revenue at Google and Meta.

EU policy has therefore contributed to slower growth at Google.

Huawei’s brake pedals reportedly break

Huawei’s automotive efforts appear to have driven it into a cul de sac of controversy.

The Chinese tech giant works with a company called Anhui Jianghuai Automobile Group Co. (JAC) under the Harmony Intelligent Mobility Alliance, which aims to produce intelligent cars.

The flagship model produced by Huawei and JAC is a luxury EV called the Maextro V800, which when tested by Chinese automotive review site Dongchedi produced a rather terrifying fault: the brake pedal bracket broke when drivers exerted maximum force during tests to assess the car's ability to decelerate suddenly at 100 km/h.

The site’s drivers were still able to stop the car, but rather more slowly than is optimal.

Within days of the review site posting its findings, reports of its work started to disappear from the internet, according to China Digital Times, a US-based outlet that says it publishes independent news on Chinese affairs.

Even some opinion pieces that called for further assessment of the car’s safety disappeared, giving rise to theories that Beijing is displeased with negative coverage of its technology champions and automotive industry.

North Korea named as Binance hacker

The USA’s Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) on Saturday named North Korea’s elite cybercrime crew “TraderTraitor” as the source of last week’s attack on cryptocurrency exchange Binance.

Cyber-defense company TRM also reached the same conclusion. Its analysts last week declared they have “high confidence” that “on and off-chain intelligence” suggest TraderTraitor did the deed.

“On the evening of October 29, billions of dollars in crypto began leaving wallets belonging to Binance,” TRM’s analysts wrote. “Within minutes, Binance wallets on the XRP Ledger, Ethereum, Bitcoin, Tron, Solana and four other networks had sent their balances to addresses controlled by a single attacker. A second wave around 23:05 UTC reached Binance wallets on Avalanche and Polygon.”

TRM thinks Trader Traitor started its raid 36 hours before the theft. “The attacker's operator wallet received three small withdrawals from Tornado Cash, a service that obscures where crypto comes from, starting the morning of October 28,” TRM wrote. “Early on October 29, the operator deployed a first piece of code for the attack and tested it on two wallets. Later that day, it rehearsed the final version on a third wallet, then routed the test funds back to itself. Four hours after that rehearsal, the same code was installed on a Binance hot wallet.”

Binance first said the cause of the incident was a single compromised laptop. It has since detected “additional points of breach” but has not offered additional information while it investigates the matter. The crypto exchange continues to reassure users that its platform is safe and that it will make good any losses.

Samsung posts monster profit

Samsung last week delivered the monster profits it promised early in October.

The Korean giant announced record Q3 revenue of ₩195.3 trillion ($146.2 billion ) and operating profit of ₩107 trillion ($80 billion).

Samsung’s DS division, which covers all its chipmaking efforts, earned over 99 percent of that operating profit and $110 billion of the company’s revenue.

The company said its foundry business “experienced a notable revenue and earnings improvement on HBM base-die sales growth” and expects it will notch up “double-digit revenue growth and continued earnings improvement on new mobile products, mass production ramp-up of 4nm NPUs, and expanded memory product sales” in its next quarterly report.

“In 2027, the Business will aim for revenue growth led by 4nm and 2nm advanced nodes based on strong AI and HPC demand. It also expects to strengthen its global supply capabilities with the commencement of operations at its new fab in Taylor, Texas,” the company said.

That last item is welcome news as the reason Samsung and other memory-makers are making so much money is shortages mean they can inflate prices. More factories coming online may help to ease price pressures.

Fast-charging batteries set for takeoff

Dr Rachid Yazami, the scientist who developed the graphite anode that made lithium-ion batteries mainstream, has shown off a new battery design that can achieve 70 percent charge in under four minutes.

In tests of its lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery cell, Dr Yazami’s startup KVI was able to go from 10 percent to 70 percent charge in 3.7 minutes, and to full charge in seven minutes, using “Non-Linear Voltammetry (NLV)” technology that he says “uses a proprietary, stepwise voltage-controlled approach” that “allows the current to respond to the battery’s changing electrochemical characteristics” during charging.

“The challenge is not simply to push more electricity into a battery in less time,” Dr Yazami said. “It is to understand how the battery responds during charging and use that information to deliver energy more effectively.”
