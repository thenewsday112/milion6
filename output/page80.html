Is This the Beginning of the End for Nuno? West Ham Fans Are Losing Patience

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Perhaps it’s not the ideal time to write in the immediate aftermath of a 3-1 home defeat. Yet after back-to-back losses and six goals conceded across those matches, it would be equally wrong to ignore the growing concerns.

The reality is stark. While other clubs around us are building momentum, we’ve slipped from third to eighth in the table. That’s not a minor setback, it’s a worrying trend.

The statistics only reinforce what supporters are witnessing on the pitch.

At half-time, we had managed just one shot on target, producing an xG of 0.58 compared to West Brom’s 1.39. To be fair, West Brom had only registered one shot on target themselves. However, after the break our xG improved to 0.98 thanks to four additional efforts on target. West Brom, meanwhile, generated an xG of 2.06 and demonstrated the clinical edge that we’re currently lacking, scoring three times from just three shots on target.

The most pressing concern is undoubtedly the defence. Hermansen looks completely devoid of confidence, and calls for Areola’s return are becoming louder with every passing week. Meanwhile, Morato’s deployment on the left side increasingly resembles a failed experiment. I had reservations when the signing was made and wrote about those concerns at the time. Unfortunately, those fears now appear to be justified, and there’s no satisfaction in being proven right.

But the issues extend far beyond individual players.

This squad is underperforming, and ultimately responsibility lies with the manager. Nuno is now facing genuine pressure. Automatic promotion was the objective at the start of the season. The uncertainty of the play-offs, or being dragged into the lower half of the table was never part of the plan. Yet the uncomfortable truth is that we are becoming far too easy to play against. Opponents understand our weaknesses and are exploiting them week after week.

If that sounds overly critical, so be it.

Looking back at the season so far, the only performances I can confidently say I thoroughly enjoyed were the victories against Wrexham and perhaps Wolves. The 1-0 away win at Swansea also deserves recognition, and I guess the strong win against Lincolns nine man team. Beyond that, supporters have largely witnessed inconsistent displays, and the current criticism is a direct consequence of what they’ve seen on the pitch. With the quality available to Nuno, this team should be producing more.

Off the pitch, there are still unanswered questions.

I’ve consistently defended Kretinsky, yet we are still waiting for formal confirmation regarding the shareholder situation, while Sullivan remains in the directors’ box. The uncertainty surrounding the club continues to create an unwelcome backdrop.

So, is it too early to suggest that Nuno’s position should be under review?

Not anymore.

That conversation is beginning to gain real traction among supporters. It’s worth remembering that after relegation, Nuno’s departure appeared inevitable. It was only the intervention of Kretinsky that kept him at the club. Even Nuno himself seemed to believe his time was up.

Right now, the club still feels unsettled, both on and off the pitch.

The first step? A convincing win against Cardiff. Because if that doesn’t arrive, the pressure on everyone involved will only intensify.

MILIMETRIK ARCHITECTS CONVERTS RAMI LIBRARY INTO FAMILY FESTIVAL

 

Staged across the historic Rami Library in Istanbul, AileFest 2026 is a temporary spatial intervention designed by Turkish practice Milimetrik Architects. Held from September 26th to October 6th, 2026, the 91,000-sqm installation adapted the site’s historic courtyard and interior halls into an interconnected landscape for family-oriented recreation, education, and cultural programming, hosting approximately 825,000 visitors over an eleven-day period.

all images ©Fevzi Ondu

 

 

MODULAR COURTYARD INSTALLATIONS FOSTER OPEN-ENDED RECREATION

 

The project employs a child-centric, semi-structured spatial syntax designed around ergonomic scales for varying age demographics. Rather than defining rigid play equipment, the studio populated the expansive outdoor courtyard with modular geometric volumes, abstract shelters, and chromatic gathering nodes that allow for open-ended circulation and spontaneous use. Inside the library, the Book Fair departs from standard orthogonal booth layouts, organizing the space with pitched house-shaped pavilions, primary-toned modular display stands, and graphic floor patterns. The intervention establishes continuous circulation between the open-air courtyards and the historic interior galleries, utilizing temporary timber-and-steel fabrications to activate the civic site without altering its heritage fabric.

Patrick Stewart, as Captain Jean-Luc Picard. (CBS)

 

The great British actor Sir Patrick Stewart passed away on Thursday. While Stewart began his career as a stage actor and performed in many Shakespeare plays, he is best known for his portrayal of Captain Jean-Luc Picard, in Star Trek: The Next Generation. Thanks to his impressive performance, Stewart and the character he played became science fiction icons.

Stewart brought extraordinary depth and perception to the role. And the character is an interesting contrast to the more instinctive and aggressive, less cerebral, Captain Kirk in the original Star Trek Series. Perhaps more than any other character, Stewart's Picard epitomized Star Trek's progressive liberal values: Enlightenment rationalism, technological optimism (highlighted by his defense of the rights of the android Data), racial and ethnic equality, and commitment to peace and diplomacy.

In many ways, Picard and TNG promoted these ideas to an even greater extent than the original series. Whereas Captain Kirk was often belligerent and acted on instinct, Picard sought diplomatic solutions to conflict, and tried to avoid doing anything drastic without careful deliberation. In the original series, the Klingons and Romulans are mostly depicted as evil and irreconcilable enemies of the Federation, in TNG the Klingons have become allies and there is hope that there will be peace with the Romulans, as well. In multiple episodes, Picard takes the lead in working to maintain and improve Federation relations with these and other previously hostile alien societies.

There is much to admire in this ideology and Picard's advocacy of it. But, as I have pointed out in a 2016 article (see also this more recent piece), Star Trek's ideology has a dark side, as well: its uncritical embrace of socialism. This is also reflected in the Federation's seeming ideological homogeneity. Picard also exemplifies this negative aspect of Star Trek ideology, with his thinly veiled disdain for private enterprise, and people who seek to make a profit.

As depicted by Stewart, the Picard character also highlights another key tension in Star Trek ideology, this one relating to cultural diversity. Picard is supposed to be French; but other than his noticeable British accent, he seems little different from other human Federation characters. The "Doylist" real-world explanation for this is that Stewart himself was British, and trying to get him to speak with a French accent might have had bad results. But the "Watsonian" in-universe effect of this is to accentuate how the human characters in Star Trek have largely lost their cultural distinctiveness. French, British, and other human cultures have largely blended together, by the 24th century (when the show is set). Star Trek may champion diversity. But, culturally and politically, the human characters are mostly people who look different, but largely think alike on almost all major issues. The few dissenters are generally depicted as either villains or fools.

As  I have previously noted, this ideological homogeneity and uncritical acceptance of Federation ideology gets challenged more in later Star Trek series, beginning with Deep Space 9. DS-9 and later series depict more ideological conflict among humans, and raise serious questions about Federation ideology. But Stewart's Picard remains an iconic depiction of Federation ideals.

As always in assessing actors, we should not assume that the character necessarily reflects the views of the actor. We can and should recognize Picard's shortcomings and those of Federation ideology, without blaming Stewart for them. Indeed, his performance deserves credit for providing a valuable window into these issues.

The more recent Picard series is not as good as TNG. But I would argue the blame for that rests with the producers and their problematic plotlines, rather than Stewart.

In addition to his justly famous Star Trek role, Stewart also starred in the X-Men movie series (which I won't comment on, as I know little about it). His first significant science fiction role was playing Gurney Halleck in the 1984 movie version of Dune.

Patrick Stewart as Gurney Halleck in Dune (1984). (Universal Pictures)

 

The 1984 Dune movie is an incoherent mess, and not as good as the more recent Denis Villeneuve version (see my reviews of the latter here and here). But Stewart did a great job with his role, and really brought Halleck to life.

In sum, Sir Patrick Stewart was a great actor, and well deserves his status as an SF icon. He will be greatly missed.

Shigeru Miyamoto Proclaims November 1 is Pikmin Day, Pikmin 4 Demo Coming To Switch 2 Soon

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

This morning, Nintendo shared some updates related to the Pikmin franchise and has officially claimed November 1 as Pikmin Day, starting today and moving forward presumably forever.

 

Aside from the announcement of Pikmin Day as a general idea, Pikmin creator Shigeru Miyamoto appeared on camera to remind everyone that Pikmin 4 – Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy is coming November 12. A free demo for the game, which will include one standard level and two Dandori Academy levels, will be available on November 2.

The Switch 2 version of the game (you can read Game Informer’s Pikmin 4 review here) will have improved visuals and take advantage of the Switch 2’s mic to let you say things like “Rush” and “Go to work” to Oatchi. The Dandori Academy side of the game is essentially time trials you can practice, and then on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays, you will have one chance to post your best time. You can read more about the Switch 2 version of the game here.

Miyamoto also shared that the mobile game, Pikmin Bloom, is also getting a fairly substantial update soon with secret caves. In Bloom, players will be able to find maps that reveal caves that you can send your Pikmin into to battle and find treasure. You can also find maps for Bloom by playing Pikmin 4. Caves will be coming to Pikmin Bloom later this year.

The Pikmin Day presentation also reminded viewers that every Pikmin game is available on Switch and Switch 2, though no announcements or hints were made implying that Pikmin 1 and 2 would be coming to the Nintendo Switch Online subscription service, which includes GameCube games on Switch 2. Those games can be purchased individually on the eShop.

Finally, Miyamoto brought actress Fuka Koshiba on the presentation (best known for voicing Kiki in Kiki’s Delivery Service) to share her appreciation for the Pikmin franchise (she likes to knit Pikmin hats that she shares on social media) and introduce a Pikmin short film. In the short, a Pikmin interacts with a toad, but I won’t spoil what happens. Miyamoto promised more shorts are on the way and in the future, they will be found in the Nintendo Today app.

Unfortunately, the stream concluded without a single reference to 2017's Hey! Pikmin. I wouldn't expect it to get a port to Switch 2 anytime soon.
