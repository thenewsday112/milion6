An Oregon sheriff’s deputy rescued a missing and hypothermic hiker along the Pacific Crest Trail after following a set of bare footprints in the snow, the Marion County Sheriff’s Office said.

According to the department, the deputy was patrolling near Breitenbush Lake on the PCT (Mile 2039.4) on the afternoon of October 30 when he came across the footprints and realized that while one of them showed the outsole of a shoe, the other appeared to have been made by a bare foot. The officer, who was “concerned for the individual’s safety,” followed the tracks for almost five miles to Forest Service Road 4220, where he discovered a man in a sleeping bag.

“Thanks to the quick actions of our Forest Patrol Deputy, the man was located and immediately transported to Detroit, where medics were able to treat him for exposure to the elements,” the sheriff’s office wrote. The hiker, who had been lost for 4 days, is being treated for hypothermia and frostbite.

﻿﻿

The Salem-based Statesman Journal identified the deputy who made the rescue as Derrick Ramseyer, a 19-year veteran of the department.

The sheriff’s office said that Ramseyer had “likely saved the man’s life, and called the incident “an important reminder to always be prepared when heading into the wilderness.” The office urged hikers to carry an inReach or other emergency communicator and keep a close eye on weather conditions, which can shift rapidly at higher elevations.

The northern stretches of the Pacific Crest Trail are experiencing freezing temperatures with the onset of late fall, especially at higher elevations. On October 30, the Boulder Creek weather station, which is located roughly 13 miles from Breitenbush Lake and 2,000 feet lower, recorded a low of 31 degrees Fahrenheit.

While it’s not entirely clear how the man came to be partially barefoot, hikers losing shoes in snow or deep mud is a surprisingly common occurrence during the shoulder season, and can leave them frostbitten and trapped outside in inclement weather. In April 2023, three separate groups of hikers had to be rescued on Colorado’s Quandary Peak in the span of a single week after losing their shoes in soft snowpack.

College Students are Pushing Colleges to 'De-Flock'

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Inside Higher Ed reports: In September, Emory University removed a dozen license plate-reading cameras from its campus in Atlanta and declined to renew its contract with Flock Safety, the company that sells and operates the automatic license plate readers and other mass-surveillance technology. For the coalition of Emory students and employees who had spent months pushing the university to "de-Flock," this was a victory... "We made it very clear that these cameras were unneeded surveillance considering we already have regular security cameras," [said Tony Torres, a sophomore biology major]... Through campus protests, petitions, meetings and teach-ins during the spring and summer, DeFlock Emory broadcast their concerns about the devices: Opaque data-sharing agreements could leave undocumented students vulnerable, the cameras could be hacked, institutions could use them to retaliate against student protesters and they could make students who fear being surveilled less likely go to class, see their friends or seek medical attention when they need it. Such concerns aren't unique to DeFlock Emory. College students across the country — including at Indiana University Bloomington, the University of Florida and the University of North Georgia — are mobilizing to de-Flock their campuses. And like Emory, other institutions are nixing their contracts. Cornell University announced last week it had not renewed its contract with Flock Safety and would be removing the cameras. Pennsylvania State University officials also announced earlier this month plans to end their contract with Flock. An Emory spokesperson told Inside Higher Edthat eliminating Flock's cameras "retains institutional control over our safety infrastructure." But the article also includes a quote from media/technology studies professor Torin Monahan at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. He's spoken with campus police officers about how they're using Flock's cameras, and learned police departments have upgraded surveillance efforts in recent years. "They have an elaborate arsenal in place of high-tech surveillance equipment that really seems overpowered for what their day-to-day needs are, and also incredibly expensive."

Nuno Has Had Long Enough – West Ham Need a Change

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

I’ve seen enough. For me, Nuno Espírito Santo’s time as West Ham manager should be up.

I’ve had nearly 24 hours to reflect on the dreadful 3-1 defeat against West Bromwich Albion, and without the immediate anger and frustration of the final whistle, my opinion hasn’t changed. If anything, I’m even more convinced that Nuno isn’t the man to take West Ham forward.

This isn’t simply a reaction to one bad result or even two consecutive defeats. It’s the culmination of more than a year of watching a manager who still doesn’t appear to know his best team, his preferred formation or how to get the best out of the attacking players at his disposal.

West Ham Are Getting Worse, Not Better

The football has become stale, the players look unmotivated and, perhaps most worrying of all, I’m struggling to see any genuine improvement.

Jarrod Bowen says words are no longer enough, and I completely agree. But when I look back at our performances throughout the season, I’m not convinced these last two defeats represent some sudden collapse in standards. I think the problems have been there for quite some time.

In my latest video, I explain why I’ve finally lost faith in Nuno, why I don’t believe this is an overreaction and why West Ham need to act before our promotion ambitions suffer any further damage.

I’m also concerned about the empty seats, the growing apathy amongst supporters and the fact that watching West Ham has become such a chore.

I know plenty of supporters will disagree with me, and that’s absolutely fine. We all want West Ham to succeed.

But for me, the time has come for a change.

Nuno has had long enough. West Ham need to move on.
