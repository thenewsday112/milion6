Are American voters actually gloomy about the economy?

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Polling is looking grim for Republicans ahead of the U.S. midterm elections on November 3, due in large part to deep pessimism among the public about the economy.

At first glance, this might seem surprising. With the exception of inflation, many economic measures remain strong, including gross domestic product growth. But consumer sentiment continues to languish.

This has prompted a debate over whether mere “vibes” are in play, or whether these long-used metrics are no longer capturing something fundamental to Americans’ economic well-being.

This debate matters politically because voters are placing an overwhelming priority on the economy as an issue in the midterms. A Pew Research survey in early October 2026 found that 84% of registered voters said the economy was very important to their vote, and the concern is bipartisan. Among the issues in that Pew survey, for example, “the economy” was the least polarizing, with 82% of Republicans and 88% of Democrats ranking it as very important.

Meanwhile, the overall drop in voters’ economic outlook has largely been driven by growing pessimism among Republicans. While Democrats remain much gloomier about the economy than Republicans, the gap is narrowing.

These trends help explain why Democrats are expected to make significant gains in Congress: This election will likely be seen as an indictment of how well the economy is working for ordinary Americans under President Donald Trump and the Republican-controlled Congress, despite strong macroeconomic measures.

So what is going on?

As a scholar of financial markets and global inequality, I believe the relationship between how people see their own finances and their view of the broader economy is also changing on a more fundamental level. And this shift will affect how they judge the next class of lawmakers in power.

Reality vs. “vibes”

When scholars try to understand how people judge the economy—and how they separate their own personal circumstances from the broader economy—one of their key reference points is the University of Michigan’s consumer sentiment index. It asks respondents about their personal finances, their short-term and long-term economic outlook, and whether they think it’s a good time to make major purchases.

This gauge has historically tracked key economic measures of consumer spending and inflation: The state of the broader economy usually shapes the way people think about their own financial well-being.

But in recent years, these have diverged. By many measures, the economy is doing well, but people feel they’re falling behind. And this has only become more pronounced ahead of the midterms.

Despite strong consumer spending, robust business investment, and low unemployment, consumer sentiment keeps slipping and is now 10% below where it was a year ago, according to the Michigan index.

Some commentators point to ongoing inflation as the reason for gloom. It has remained stubbornly above the Federal Reserve’s annual 2% target rate due to drivers like tariffs, energy price hikes stemming from Middle East turmoil, and massive amounts of investment in the artificial intelligence build-out.

But what’s really happening is that consumer sentiment data and inflation data have become untethered. Inflation has actually dropped sharply from its 21st-century peak of 9.1% in June 2022 after the pandemic to 3.7% in September 2026, but consumer sentiment is lower now than it was even then.

What about wages? Here, too, the data also doesn’t fully explain this disconnect. While inflation makes consumer goods pricier, it also lifts pay. Inflation-adjusted wages actually increased between 2022 and the first half of 2026, all while consumer sentiment remained weak. What has changed in recent months is that inflation-adjusted wages are now falling—which could help explain the darkening outlook among voters.

Inequality vs. growth

One possibility is that deeply entrenched inequality explains this divergence.

Compared with other eras of moderate-to-high inflation, the U.S. economy today is much more unequal. Income inequality has increased over the past 20 years, narrowing only during the 2008 and 2020 crises before quickly widening to prior levels.

This trend is important for understanding which consumers are shelling out cash. The Fed has consistently cited strong consumer spending in its economic assessments, but the richest 10% of Americans account for half of that spending today. Meanwhile, spending by the bottom 80% of earners has fallen behind inflation.

In other words, the strong total consumer spending figures reported as official data are being driven by very wealthy Americans and fail to reflect the experience of typical consumers who are the bulk of the electorate.

This trend of increasing inequality has been particularly acute in terms of wealth: The top 0.1% of American households together own five times as much wealth as the bottom 50%. Much of this reflects the uneven gains of the stock market boom and how wealth is taxed.

In short, Americans’ economic malaise might be driven by concerns over income and wealth that conventional measures don’t fully capture.

It’s not just about me

When it comes to consumer surveys like the University of Michigan index, economists generally assume that the answers reflect the personal experience of respondents.

But I agree with other scholars who argue that sometimes these answers may be based less on people’s own circumstances and more on how they view the economy as a whole. This assessment could reflect, for example, concerns like equity, equality of opportunity, and economic justice.

When responses are driven by a concern for collective well-being rather than just personal material self-interest, social scientists refer to such perceptions as “sociotropic.”

This last point isn’t just theoretical. Research suggests voters tend to weight their perceptions of the economy more heavily than their personal financial circumstances when deciding whether to vote for the incumbent party.

Trade is one good example. Americans’ preferences for trade policy are driven less by how a particular measure, like tariffs, would impact them personally and more by how they think it would affect the country as a whole.

If consumer sentiment follows a similar path, that could explain the divergence between generally strong economic trends and weak consumer sentiment. People are thinking not just about their own conditions but those of the U.S. as a whole, and it’s possible that their understanding of “the economy” includes its relationship to inequality.

Understanding the disconnect between the economic data traditionally used to assess the economy’s health and Americans’ perceptions of that economy is important not just to the scholars trying to make sense of it—it could also help set an agenda for the incoming Congress. If economic insecurity punishes the governing party at the polls, the next Congress will be expected to do something about it once sworn in.

Voters generally back the incumbent party when the economy is doing well and the out-party when it’s not. But how they judge the health of the economy is not straightforward, and the past six years suggest that improving headline measures of economic health—without regard to Americans’ lived experience and their concerns about what kind of economy is being built—may not be enough to assuage voters’ concerns.

Erin Kay Lockwood is an assistant professor of political science at the University of California, Irvine.

Lionel Messi holds a frankly ridiculous number of records at Barcelona, and Raphinha has started 2026-27 in a manner that makes some of them look (slightly) less untouchable.

The Brazil international has scored 15 goals in 10 La Liga appearances, with another two in the Champions League, and is thriving at No.9 after Barcelona lost Robert Lewandowski and Ferran Torres and failed to land Julian Alvarez in the summer.

A thigh injury and recent defeats to Real Madrid and PSG have interrupted his momentum, but a brace in Barcelona’s 6-1 demolition of Alaves suggests he’s not going anywhere.

We’ve rounded up seven ridiculous Messi records that Raphinha can try to break after his stonking start to 2026-27.

Longest scoring streak in a league campaign

Messi more than doubled the best-ever scoring streak set in La Liga by Ronaldo Nazario back in 1996-97 (10) when he scored in 21 consecutive outings in the 2012-13 season.

Unbelievably, that run included the eight-time Ballon d’Or winner scoring against all 19 other clubs in La Liga. No one in European football history has ever even gotten close.

Raphinha could’ve been up to six straight matches if he hadn’t gifted Yamal a penalty against Levante. The Brazilian did assist twice, but the goalscoring streak reset.

He’s since managed back-to-back goalscoring appearances against Racing Santander and Sevilla, but failed to find the net in his brief cameo against Real Betis.

A goal in the defeat to Real Madrid followed, and he’s now scored in two successive appearances after his brace against Alaves. Just another 20 to go to beat Messi. Ahem.

Most goals for Barcelona in a Champions League campaign

Here’s an achievable one.

This is a Barcelona-specific record, rather than an outright one. Messi scored well over a hundred Champions League goals and stands second in the all-time rankings, but his best tally of 14 goals back in 2011-12 is only the joint seventh-best individual season.

Raphinha bagged a brace in Barca’s 2026-27 opener against Feyenoord, but has since missed the victory over Galatasaray through injury and drew a blank in the 4-2 defeat to PSG.

That leaves him with two goals from two appearances, with five league phase games remaining before we even get to the knockouts. There’s still plenty of time to make up for lost ground.

Given the expanded format, we’d be amazed if Cristiano Ronaldo’s all-time record of 17 goals for Real Madrid in the 2013-14 season doesn’t get broken in the coming years. In this form, Raphinha could be among the contenders.

Highest WhoScored rating for a league season

Messi averaged above an 8.00 average rating from WhoScored’s statistical algorithm for every season between 2009-10 and 2020-21. Twelve years of outrageousness.

To put that in context, only one player in the whole of La Liga – Lamine Yamal with 8.23 – managed that last season. And Yamal’s score was lower than Messi’s worst of the lot (8.34 in 2013-14).

Maintaining the standards to challenge Messi’s best-ever WhoScored average rating (8.88 in 2011-12) would be evidence of a remarkable individual campaign.

Raphinha, currently, isn’t far off – with 8.50 from the 2026-27 campaign so far. Just maintain, actually slightly improve, on that over the following 27 matches. No biggie.

Most goals in a La Liga season

The big one. Messi scored a mindblowing 50 goals in the 2011-12 La Liga season. That’s also an all-time record across Europe’s five major leagues.

Obviously, it’s a daft ask for Raphinha to get anywhere near that tally – but he’s made a solid start, currently leading Europe’s goalscoring charts with 15 goals in just 10 league appearances so far.

Extrapolate that 1.5 goals per game over the course of 38 appearances… and the Brazilian would be on for 57 La Liga goals this season.

He’s already missed one of Barcelona’s 11 league matches through injury, but even at his current rate across the club’s remaining 27 fixtures, he’d finish with 55 or 56 goals.

Usual caveats apply. Sample size. Pinch of salt. Naturally.

Most La Liga hat-tricks in a season

It makes sense that Messi was frequently scoring bagfuls when he notched 50 goals in 2011-12.

That year he scored eight hat-tricks in La Liga, which remains a record – joint with his old foe Cristiano Ronaldo, who matched his record at Real Madrid in 2014-15.

Playing as a centre-forward, with the likes of Anthony Gordon, Lamine Yamal, Fermin Lopez and Pedri among the supporting cast, he’ll back himself to get a few more this season.

He’s already got two, remarkably scoring back-to-back hat-tricks against Racing Santander and Sevilla. That’s a quarter of the way to Messi’s record after just 11 league games. Just six more to go…

Most La Liga games scored in a La Liga season

Messi’s logic-defying 2011-12 season – when Barca somehow didn’t even win the title – is doing a lot of work for us here.

But it’s actually the following season, when they wrestled their league crown back from Jose Mourinho’s Real Madrid by matching their record 100 points, that Messi set the record for the most games scored in.

The Argentinian scored in 27 of Barca’s 38 games that season.

Raphinha has now scored in eight of his 10 La Liga appearances, finding the net against Elche, Athletic Club, Rayo Vallecano, Valencia, Racing Santander, Sevilla, Real Madrid and Alaves.

That leaves him needing to score in 19 of Barcelona’s remaining 27 league games to match Messi’s record.

Given his form so far, that’s not completely out of the realm of possibility. Provided he stays fit, of course, which is an admittedly big if.

Most goals in a season

Same as the above, but all competitions. Probably even more ridiculous.

As well as his 50 La Liga goals in 2011-12, Messi notched 73 in all competitions. That’s an all-time record in the entire history of European football, seven more than Ferenc Deak and Gerd Muller notched in Hungary and Germany in the 1940s and 1970s, respectively.

So far Raphinha has notched 17 goals in 12 games in all competitions. That’s an average of 1.42 goals per appearance, despite his recent injury setback.

Maintain that rate over the course of about 52 appearances and he’d be in Messi territory. The trouble is that he’ll need to stay fit, keep scoring at this ridiculous rate and hope Barcelona go deep in the Champions League.

Easy. Good luck, Rapha.

Barcelona have made a terrifyingly good start to the 2026-27 season, blowing away the opposition on a weekly basis.

Hansi Flick’s side have scored four or more goals in nine of their opening 11 La Liga matches, although recent defeats to Real Madrid and PSG have taken some of the shine off their extraordinary start.

A 2-1 home defeat to Madrid and a 4-2 loss away to PSG have exposed some vulnerabilities, but Barcelona have otherwise been utterly ruthless. Their latest victims, Alaves, were thrashed 6-1.

It’s very early days, and we’ll see how they fare once the bigger tests roll around again, but this season still has the makings of something special and historic.

We’ve identified five major records that Barcelona could potentially smash if they can keep up this form throughout the season.

La Liga Points Record

After stomaching three years of an all-conquering, era-defining Barcelona side, Jose Mourinho’s Real Madrid knocked them off their perch in 2011-12. And they did so in style.

Los Blancos set the record for the most points in a La Liga season by notching 100 points in a stupendously dominant 2011-12 campaign.

The following year Barcelona reclaimed their crown by matching that tally.

There would be very little wiggle room for dropped points, though, and it might be a big ask after club president Joan Laporta plainly stated that Europe is the top priority this season.

“We have a special enthusiasm to win the Champions League,” Laporta told reporters.

Winning their first nine league games was a pretty good start, but the defeat to Real Madrid means Barcelona can now afford to drop just 11 more points if they’re to match that century.

European Points Record

Real Madrid, Barcelona and Pep Guardiola’s 2017-18 Manchester City centurions hold the joint second-best record for points accrued in a major European league season.

Juventus are marginally ahead of them, having notched 102 points at the height of their domestic dominance in 2013-14.

Antonio Conte’s side – powered by Giorgio Chiellini, Leonardo Bonucci, Paul Pogba, Andrea Pirlo, Arturo Vidal and Carlos Tevez – managed 33 wins, three draws and just two defeats in Serie A that year.

Barcelona’s defeat in the Clasico has eaten into their margin for error, but the record remains well within reach. With 30 points from their first 11 matches, they can afford to drop another nine points and still equal Juventus’ tally.

La Liga goals record

Not only did Mourinho’s Madrid get a hundred points in 2011-12, but they scored a whopping 121 goals.

That might be the best counter-attacking side we’ve ever seen, with Mesut Ozil setting up bagloads of chances for Cristiano Ronaldo, Gonzalo Higuain and Karim Benzema. The three forwards notched a combined 89(!) league goals between them that year.

Barca have certainly made a strong start with 46(!) goals from 11 outings so far. Extrapolate that average over a full campaign and they’re on track for… 159 La Liga goals this season. One Hundred And Fifty-Nine. Outrageous.

European Goals Record

Last season, Vincent Kompany’s Bayern Munich scored an astonishing tally of 184 goals across all competitions; 122 in the league, comfortably a Bundesliga record, 17 in the cup, two in the Super Cup, and 43 in the Champions League.

That’s only the second top goalscoring season for a European club, just ahead of Northern Ireland’s Linfield, who scored 180 goals in 1933-34, but behind Guardiola’s Barcelona in 2011-12.

That Barca side played 64 games in all competitions, ending up with a total of 190 goals. Having Lionel Messi at the height of his goalscoring prowess – 50 goals in La Liga, 73 in all competitions – probably helped on that front.

That works out an average of 3.0 goals per match. This season’s side were averaging a ridiculous 4.4 goals per game after their first 10 outings, and they’ve barely slowed down since. Even the defeats to Madrid and PSG saw them find the net three times.

Best La Liga Goal Difference

Unsurprisingly, Madrid’s goal glut in 2011-12 also accounts for the best-ever goal difference in the Spanish top flight. They conceded 32 goals that year, giving them a +89 goal difference.

Luis Enrique’s then treble-winning Barca side matched that record in 2014-15.

Barcelona’s defensive record this season has been a little sketchy, conceding two goals against Rayo Vallecano, Levante and Racing Santander. The defeats to Real Madrid and PSG have further underlined that Flick’s high-risk approach can leave them exposed against top-quality opposition.

That’s all a bit academic, though. Barcelona’s goal difference currently stands at +34. That’s 20 better than closest challengers Atletico Madrid. Continue scoring at this rate and they won’t have to worry too much about their defence; the goal difference will take care of itself.

Newcastle United have revealed their Remembrance Day commemoration plans.

An official club release ahead of Monday night’s match.

The home game against Everton is the closest one at St James’ Park to Remembrance Day.

The club announcing: ‘Newcastle United will pay tribute to service personnel ahead of the Monday evening fixture against Everton, as part of the club’s Remembrance Day commemorations. As the closest home fixture to Remembrance Day, the occasion gives players, staff and fans a chance to unite in paying respects to those who have lost their lives in military conflict.’

The statement going on to say: ‘An on-pitch tribute will take place at St. James’ Park, with players also set to wear poppy shirts during the match, which will later be auctioned to raise funds for the Royal British Legion.’

The details of what will happen at St James’ Park tomorrow night:

‘Prior to kick-off, both teams will be joined on the pitch by local service personnel representing three branches of the Armed Forces:

Terry Brown (Bugler)
Ronnie Collins (British Army)
Mel Simm (Royal Navy)
John Bond (RAF)
Members of the Armed Forces will lay wreaths in the centre circle before joining players, staff, match officials and fans for a minute’s silence, during which The Last Post will be played. Fans are encouraged to take their seats early to participate in the period of silence.

A pre-match tribute has also been coordinated by fan group Wor Flags. Supporters in the East Stand are invited to raise cards to depict a display of remembrance throughout the silence.’

‘Commemorative shirts

Newcastle United players will wear shirts displaying poppies for both the Everton home match and away fixture against Fulham on Saturday 7th November. The shirts will be signed by players and made available for auction by MatchWornShirt, with proceeds going to the Royal British Legion. Auctions begin at kick-off and bids can be placed – here.

PTSD matchday space

The club is providing a private 24-person executive box offering local veterans living with PTSD a safe, comfortable space to enjoy the match alongside a guest, with options to view the game from inside the box or seated directly outside.

Matchday mascot

A child from a local veteran family has been selected to lead the team out as the official matchday mascot for the Remembrance fixture.’

Other events surrounding Remembrance Day and additional info from the club:

Wor Heroes’ veterans’ gathering

The club will host its second annual Wor Heroes invitational veterans’ gathering at St. James’ Park on Tuesday 3rd November. The event welcomes former service members for an evening of community, connection and reflection ahead of Remembrance Day.

Veterans wishing to register interest in attending should email supporter.services@nufc.co.uk (registrations close at 6pm on 2 November)

NUAFSC v Sunderland

Newcastle United Armed Forces Supporters Club face Sunderland in an annual charity derby match, with Newcastle United providing the official team kit.

Date & Time: Friday 6th November | 7:30pm kick-off
Location: The Trustmark Group Stadium, Hebburn Town FC
War Memorial Garden at St. James’ Park

Supporters wishing to pay their personal respects are invited to visit the War Memorial Garden, located behind the Milburn Stand at St. James’ Park. Bearing the inscription “Lest We Forget”, the memorial honours all 23 former Newcastle United players who lost their lives serving their country during the First and Second World Wars.

Official poppy pin badges

Magpies fans can show their support for both the club and the Royal British Legion with official Newcastle United poppy pin badges, available in official club stores at St. James’ Park and Metrocentre. Priced at £6 each, all profits benefit the work of the Royal British Legion.
