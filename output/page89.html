G20 foreign ministers in Atlanta meeting head to an ending without consensus on key issues

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

ATLANTA (AP) — Top diplomats from the Group of 20 largest world economies are wrapping up two days of talks Saturday without reaching consensus on several critical global security issues after divisions, notably over Russia’s war with Ukraine, overshadowed the start of the Atlanta meeting.

As a result, the group will not release a joint communique but will rather rely on a chairman’s statement summarizing the talks, which Secretary of State Marco Rubio will release at the conclusion, according to diplomats who attended.

Nonetheless, Rubio said the first day of the session was “very productive,” and he was hosting talks with his counterparts from Australia, India and Japan on Saturday before leading the final G20 event. That discussion on supply chains will examine freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red, Black and South China seas.

“Together, I think that we are mapping a vibrant, dynamic, and bold economic future, one that will pioneer new horizons for the entire world,” Rubio said at a reception for participants late Friday.

The traditional group photo was canceled

Rubio had opened the talks Friday with an appeal for unity, but then canceled the traditional group photo of the foreign ministers. European diplomats objected to the inclusion of Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov and threatened to boycott or walk out of the snapshot.

Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski, who was representing his country for the first time as a guest at a G20 meeting, said he was not surprised by the failure to reach consensus.

“We have wars going on, and we have participants in those wars around the table, so it’s very difficult to agree on a common line,” he told reporters.

Other participants in the meetings, who spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss the private talks, said all had agreed to try to overcome their differences despite the long odds and to work to make next month’s summit of G20 leaders hosted by President Donald Trump in Florida a success.

These diplomats said a chairman’s statement had long been the plan because of the deep differences among the attendees, including over the Russia-Ukraine war, the U.S.-Israeli war against Iran, U.S. tariffs and Chinese moves in the South China Sea. All have contributed to spikes in the global cost of fuel as well as uncertainty about trade.

They described the opening session as cordial but said Russia’s position on Ukraine was unyielding — as was its opposition to Poland’s membership in the G20. They said the disagreements also included the Trump administration’s decision not to invite G20 member South Africa over the objections of others.

Russia’s presence at G20 gathering roils Europeans

Before the meeting began, some G20 countries had expressed concern that the U.S. invited Russia during its heightened attacks on Ukraine, especially after Trump pushed ties with Ukraine to new lows by waiving fresh sanctions on Moscow and temporarily limiting logistical and intelligence assistance.

The State Department, however, announced Friday that it had approved a proposed $2.1 billion sale of Patriot air defense interceptors for Ukraine.

Lavrov said Moscow would not allow a situation in which Ukraine poses a “military threat to legitimate security interests of the Russian Federation.”

Lavrov has traveled only infrequently to NATO member states since Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022 and resulting severe sanctions were imposed on Moscow. It is unclear whether Russian President Vladimir Putin will accept Trump’s invitation to attend the summit at Trump’s Miami golf club in December.

The Iran war hangs over the G20 event

Also hanging over the G20 meeting was the prospect of new U.S. military intervention in Iran. That has put the region, including G20 member Saudi Arabia, on edge. Rubio met on Friday with his Saudi counterpart, Prince Faisal bin Farhan, as well as Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, whose country has been a key mediator.

The Associated Press has reported that U.S. officials have told several Middle East countries and regional allies to be prepared for potential renewed military strikes against Iran after U.S. elections Tuesday and the possibility that Iran will retaliate.

New York City Marathon 2026: How to watch live coverage, track runners, see the route map, and more

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

On Sunday, November 1, the Big Apple will be taken over by running enthusiasts.

The TCS New York City Marathon is back for the 50th anniversary of its expanded course. Here’s everything you need to know, from the history of the race to road closures, as well as how to tune in and cheer from the comfort of your couch.

A brief history of the NYC Marathon

One hundred and twenty-seven runners began the first New York City Marathon on September 13, 1970. Fifty-five finished the race located in Manhattan’s Central Park, which was put on by the New York Road Runners (NYRR). It cost just a dollar to enter and $1,000 to execute.

Beginning in 1976, the course expanded to include all five New York City boroughs. This change was made to celebrate America’s 200th birthday and was so popular that it stuck.

The race continued even in times of adversity, showing the strength and resilience of the city. For instance, runners gave their all just two months after the September 11 attacks in 2001. During the Covid-19 pandemic, the marathon went virtual.

In 2024, it became the largest marathon in history with 55,646 finishers, according to NYRR. The New York City Marathon broke its own record the following year with more than 59,000 finishers.

This year, there are nearly 60,000 hopefuls ready to take their mark.

The 411 on the map and course

The race begins at Fort Wadsworth in Staten Island. Runners head immediately to the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge.

From there, they head to Brooklyn. Miles 13 to 15 are spent in Queens, crossing the Pulaski Bridge.

Next up, the first of two legs are in Manhattan. A quick detour to the Bronx follows before returning to Manhattan and making a big finish in Central Park.

You can check out the marathon’s full map and course on NYRR’s website.

There’s also an interactive version here.

What about road closures?

Because of this expansive course, there will be many road closures, parking restrictions, and bus diversions.

For a full list of these disruptions click here and get the latest details from the city’s events coordination office.

One of the most significant closures is that of the  Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge. This begins with the upper level at 11 p.m. ET Saturday evening. The lower level will follow suit the following morning at 7 a.m. ET.

Additionally, the Bay Street and Lily Pond Avenue exits on Staten Island will be closed at 3 a.m. ET Sunday but reopen by 4 p.m. ET.

Also, do not rely on the Central Park transverse roads at 65th, 79th, and 96th streets, because they too will be closed.

How can I track a runner’s progress?

The official app from New York Road Runners lets you follow your favorite runners. You can also check leaderboards, learn about race results, and watch live coverage. Find it here.

Which celebrities are running the race?

Many celebrities from film, television, and Broadway will be pounding the pavement.

Academy Award winner Jennifer Connelly will be among the runners hoping to go all the way. Connelly is an old pro, having run the race in 2024 and finishing with a 3:45:47 timestamp. She is running to support Fred’s Team, a charity that supports the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center.

Media personality and journalist T.J. Holmes is also no stranger to the marathon. This will be his fourth race.

Queer Eye alum Antoni Porowski will give it his all to support City Harvest, while Reading Rainbow’s new host, Mychal Threets, is running to support the CMAK Sandy Hook Memorial Foundation.

Broadway star Jordan Litz ran the 2025 marathon and then did two shows as Fiyero in Wicked. This year, he is flying in from London’s West End to do the run. Thankfully, no post-race performances are necessary. Another alum of the Great White Way, Patina Miller, is also back for more.

How can I watch or stream the race live?

If you don’t want to lace up your shoes and get in on the action, you can cheer on others from the comfort of your couch.

If you are located in the New York area, WABC-TV, Channel 7 will cover the race beginning bright and early at 7 a.m. ET. You can also utilize ABC7NY.com for a live view of the finish line from 10:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. ET.

For those outside of the area, you can turn to ESPN2 from 8:00 a.m. to 11:30 a.m. ET for live coverage.

In New York, you can also listen to a race-day broadcast on iHeartRadio’s 710 WOR AM and 104.3HD2, beginning at 7:00 a.m. ET.

Or check it out on the iHeartRadio app starting at 8:00 a.m. ET.

Review: Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai (DXB – B Concourse)

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

I returned to the Emirates First Class Lounge in Dubai’s Concourse B during my latest trip through Dubai. Below are my updated photos and impressions, followed by my original 2019 review for your comparison.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Review (DXB – Concourse B)

With 30 minutes until boarding, I stopped by this lounge after a longer visit at the Emirates First Class Lounge in Concourse A. This Concourse B lounge is virtually unchanged since my last visit and by now, feels dated…not that I would touch it because it still serves its purpose well. I did notice one important change, though.

Hours + Access + Location

The Emirates First Class Lounge in Concourse B is located airside in Terminal 3 at Dubai International Airport, beyond security and passport control. Take the elevator or escalator to Level 4. The lounge is open 24/7.

Access is available to:

  • Emirates First Class passengers
  • Emirates Skywards Platinum members traveling on an eligible flight, with one adult guest and two children under 17 traveling on the same flight
  • Qantas Platinum and Platinum One members traveling on an eligible Qantas or Emirates flight
  • Passengers with Emirates Business Class Lounge access: upgrading to the First Class Lounge costs $150 per person for Skywards members or $175 for nonmembers, plus 5% UAE VAT. The totals are $157.50 and $183.75, respectively.
  • Other eligible paying passengers: First Class Lounge access costs $250 for Skywards members or $300 for nonmembers, plus 5% VAT. The totals are $262.50 and $315, respectively.

Paid access is subject to availability and covers one visit for up to four hours before scheduled departure. Business Class Special fares do not automatically include lounge access. Check Emirates’ current eligibility and pricing before your trip.

Seating

As you enter the lounge, you’ll note the high ceilings and the sooting sound of water fountains.  

It’s massive, though does not feel as large as the Concourse A Lounge.

Dining + Drinks

I had just had breakfast and wasn’t hungry, but one advantage to this lounge over the Concourse A lounge is that it has better barista coffee at the bar (unless I missed it in the other lounge). I enjoyed an excellent flat white.

There’s also a Costa Coffee in the lounge.

Had I been here longer, I’m sure I would have enjoyed a nice a la carte meal in the dining room.

In addition, there was a separate stand-alone bar in the smoking area with a limited section and drinks and Arabic coffee.

There was also another coffee nook with a small selection of breakfast items (it was 6:30 am). Let me commend Emirates here because you’ll notice if you scroll down to my prior review that the coffee machines were automatic prior to the pandemic and those simply cannot compare to a proper manual coffee machine. The coffee has been upgraded!

Smoking

This lounge has a large smoking section that is not separately ventilated from the rest of the lounge, though thankfully the cigarette smell was not too bad.

Showers + Restrooms

Restrooms and showers are available – I did not go inside the restroom, but there are pictures of both the restrooms and shower suites below in my prior review.

Spa

The spa is located one level above the lounge, with treatments available for purchase plus 15-minute complimentary treatments for first class passengers, though there’s a desk in this lounge from which you can reserve your treatment.

Quiet Room

A quite area with recliner chairs is available for longer layovers (there’s also an airport-operated transit hotel located one level up as well as a full gym, but that’s not included for first class passengers).

Children’s Play Area

A large children’s play room offered space for kids and even had a Pacman-style arcade video game.

CONCLUSION

While this does not feel as massive as the Concourse A lounge, I actually prefer this lounge for the superior coffee. Yes, it does look a bit dated by now, but it is quite functional and is a great place to wine and dine before your flight. I look forward to returning.

Original Emirates First Class Lounge Concourse B Review — 2019

The review below was originally published on June 4, 2019. Photos, menus, and observations in this section reflect that visit, including references to renovations and temporarily closed facilities.

The Emirates First Class Lounge in Dubai’s Concourse B is well-suited for the huge crowds that pass through it each day, but lacks the intimacy and luxury that typically characterizes a first class lounge.

I say it is huge–and it was–but not nearly as big as the Concourse A lounge, which I’ve reviewed before here. The lounge is undergoing a remodel of sorts, which is desperately needed. On the one hand, the lounge is just fine the way it in that is still functional and clean. But it is bereft of character and simply dated in appearance.

We walked down the long hall from check-in, though security and passport control, upstairs, down another concourse, and finally up another escalator to the lounge..it was quite a hike from curbside to lounge and Emirates offers no escort (or gulf cart).

The lounge is open 24/7, but was virtually empty when we arrived at 4:30 AM. I used the “calm before the storm” to capture some photos of the lounge.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Concourse B Seating

Seating is spread out around the lounge and includes chairs, couches, and desks.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Concourse B Spa

The spa is currently under renovation (or perhaps just closed). There was still an attended “Timeless Spa” desk in which spa treatments could be scheduled. First Class passengers were then directed to the business class lounge for treatment. I took advantage of a complimentary 15-minute back massage shortly after arrival and had no wait for a treatment. It’s a small gesture, but an appreciated one.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Concourse B Dining

Actually, the plan was to eat first…both John and I were hungry. But the restaurant does not open until 5:15 AM so we ended up having to wait. A buffet is available 24/7 and included mostly breakfast selections.

Shortly after the restaurant opened, we sat down for breakfast…dinner actually. I wrote about it here. Here’s the a la carte menu:

As you can see, if you’re not in the mood in the for breakfast there are lunch/dinner items available. The nice leather-bound, multi-page menus have been replaced by oversized menu cards.

I enjoyed some berries and juice from the buffet followed by a filet mignon with French Fries and a salad. The food was excellent, although the coffee was quite disappointing. Best to stick to Arabic coffee and avoid the machine-dispensed.

Around the lounge there are a couple of additional food stations, one offering Mediterranean food and the other offering Japanese food. There’s also a full bar is self-serve and was open during Ramadan.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Concourse B Shoe Shine

Complimentary shoe-shine is available. There’s a station in the middle of the lounge, though the shoe shiner approached us in the restaurant and asked if he could take our shoes. In their place, he offered us slippers.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Children’s Room

The children’s room, with video games, was roped off.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Concourse B Restrooms + Showers

The restrooms reminded me a bit of the Concorde Room in London Heathrow…that’s not a good thing. I’d call the showers and restrooms still functional, but past their prime.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Concourse B Smoking

Rather than a separate room for smokers, an area directly adjacent to the dining room has been designated for smoking. While there are no walls, barriers, or other partitions, I did not smell any smoke (and there were plenty of smokers there). Perhaps the super-high ceilings were helpful in that regard. It’s fairly rare to see smoking so integrated into a lounge.

Emirates First Class Lounge Dubai Concourse B Service

The staff in the lounge was all Filipino and provided friendly and attentive service. Some of the passengers did not treat them well, especially in the restaurant. They barked orders and were simply rude. As I remind myself, I remind you: let’s treat everyone with respect.

CONCLUSION

You cannot really compare an Emirates First Class Lounge to ones like Air France or Lufthansa. Emirates offers first class on such a wider scale that it has a larger and less-personal first class ground experience by necessity. I did appreciate the tasty lunch and spa treatment during my three hours in the lounges. That said, now that I have reviewed both lounges next time I will just stay a bit longer in my hotel.

Inside Tall Timber: You Can Only Reach This Hand-Built Colorado Estate by Train or Helicopter

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There are only two ways to reach the 200-acre Colorado property known as Tall Timber: by train or helicopter. But back when octogenarian matriarch Judy Beggrow was just a girl, there was a third way—by horse.

Tucked inside the 1.8-million-acre San Juan National Forest near Durango—and surrounded by protected public land with no public road access—the estate is now on the market for $25 million.

Judy, her son, Johnroy, and his wife, Dionne, have listed it with Max Cawal.

Its centerpiece is the three-story timber lodge Judy and her late husband, Denny, built by hand.

It went up in a single summer and opened as a resort in 1972. Expanded in the mid-1990s, the lodge has 21 bedrooms, 22 full bathrooms, and 10 half-baths, laid out more like a boutique mountain hotel than a family home.

A three-story great room anchored by a river-rock fireplace opens onto decks with views of forest, river, and surrounding peaks. The dining and gathering spaces are served by a commercial-grade kitchen built for feeding a crowd.

Guests arrive to Tall Timber by train or helicopter. (Woods Photography) The property comes with exclusive river frontage throughout its 200 acres. (Woods Photography) At the center of the property is a timber lodge that was hand built by Judy and her husband in the 1960s. (Woods Photography) A private deck leading to the master suite (Woods Photography)

Cabins and staff quarters add 18 bedrooms, 15 full baths, and two half-baths, bringing the property total to 39 bedrooms, 40 full baths, and 13 half baths.

Then there are the details you won’t find on most estates. The property has an old-fashioned soda shop and ice cream parlor, a vintage-style barbershop, a resort-style pool and hot tub, and a three-story greenhouse where the family grows 35,000 flowers.

It has extensive frontage on the Animas River, and one of the longest zip-line courses in the country runs through its trees.

The sale also includes use of a nearby 10,000-square-foot private hangar with a one-bedroom apartment. By helicopter, local ski hub Purgatory Resort is about five minutes away and Telluride about 20 minutes away.

But the real story of this property is set deep in the canyon itself. Over the past century, it has been a mining-era outpost, a family dude ranch and hunting destination, a five-star resort that shared top honors with the Ritz-Carlton, and a family-run zip line.

Judy has been here for all of it.

"My father had a reputation in the canyon," says Judy, who spent a day in July walking me through the land her family has worked for 80 years.

Her father, Ross McCausland, was an electrical engineer who helped develop the automatic transmission during World War II and met his wife, Ethel, on a train from Chicago to Denver.

In 1946, the couple opened Ah! Wilderness Guest Ranch, an 80-acre dude ranch a mile south of what is now Tall Timber. Ross later described it as “a place where children and adults could play together.”

The family ate venison, kept a garden and dairy cows, and raised pigs. In the lean early years, they boarded the train with sandwiches and homemade cream puffs to sell—a venture that yielded what was sometimes their only income. Ross led hunting trips that included a train ride into the canyon, a week of hunting, a personal chef, and lodging, all for $175.

Back then, the Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, which today is a tourist line carrying some 200,000 riders a year, only ran about once a week, mostly hauling freight out of Silverton.

“The train really didn’t like passengers back then,” Judy says. So her father built his own custom motorcars to run on the tracks, seven in all.

As a girl, Judy chased the train on horseback. She also watched scenes from “Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid” being filmed on the line in the late 1960s.

The family has a number of collectibles that will convey with the sale, including Oscar Schaaf’s 1930s Ford truck (Woods Photography) An authentic soda fountain bar, bought by the family from a now-closed Durango drugstore where Marilyn Monroe is rumored to have sat after long days filming the 1949 movie, “A Ticket to Tomahawk" (Woods Photography) The dining room inside the main lodge, where the family hosted guests during their years as a five-star resort (Woods Photography) One of 39 bedrooms on the property, which can be found in the main lodge and in private cabins throughout the 200 acre parcel (Woods Photography)

The ranch was built with help from a neighbor, Oscar Schaaf, who ran a one-man sawmill. His older sister was Olga Little, Colorado’s first female jack packer, who once roped 18 snow-stranded miners together and led them on a 16-hour trek to safety. A nearby mountain bears her name.

When Judy and Denny bought the property in the late 1960s, the land had no power, so the young couple dug the holes and set the poles to bring electricity from the nearby Tacoma hydroelectric plant. Next came water. Judy remembered playing as a child in a freshwater stream that ran down from the mountain, so she and Denny hiked up the peak behind today’s lodge and laid pipe to bring spring water down to the homesite.

Tall Timber opened to guests in 1972 and went on to earn five-star and five-diamond ratings from the Mobil Travel Guide and AAA. Judy did all the cooking, sharpening her skills in food and wine classes at Epcot, where they kept a second home for the off season. With 10 guest cabins, the resort hosted up to 30 guests at a time, served by a peak staff of about 40. 

The resort was no stranger to celebrities. Christie Brinkley once stayed 10 days—something she wrote about in a tell-all piece in People magazine in April 2025. On Tuesdays, a helicopter carried guests to picnics above 11,000 feet at the nearby alpine lakes known as the Jewel Lakes, where chefs waited in tall white hats.

Humans weren’t the only guests, either. Welcome baskets warned visitors to close their windows at night, and one evening a mother bear turned up on a guest’s couch, eating an apple. She later taught her cubs to pop off window screens and raid the rooms for fruit.

The family also became the first private party in Colorado to successfully rewild a juvenile deer, found abandoned beside its dead mother. During hunting season Philippe wore a red scarf so no one would mistake him for game, and tennis balls on his antlers kept him gentle with the dogs.

In the mid-2000s, the family added a zip line through the trees. When visitors showed more interest in “playing in the trees” than in staying overnight, it became the property’s main business, and the last resort guests left in 2008. This past summer, at the height of its final season, the zip line drew 60 to 80 riders a day.

Now, the family is ready to move on.

Judy is preparing to move to Florida. And with Johnroy and Dionne’s only daughter in college, the couple wants to spend summers traveling rather than running the business. They’d love to see a buyer revive Denny’s vision of a boutique five-star destination, but they’re realistic.

“If Kevin Costner buys it, pick a celebrity,” Johnroy says. “In the end, it will be the same thing: someone enjoying it with their families.”

Dionne goes briefly quiet when asked for her favorite memory of living here.

“Waking up one Christmas morning to a proposal,” she says, glancing over at Johnroy.

It’s hard to picture this place in winter, with the canyon so green in summer and Judy’s flower arrangements filling baskets on the porch. But like everything here, winter is steeped in stories: snowmobiling, Christmas trees as tall as the great room can hold, a canyon blanketed in white beyond the windows while a fire roars in the three-story chimney.

Whoever buys the place will inherit the seclusion of a wild Colorado canyon and a property built to host a crowd.

Much of the hard work is already done, from the power lines and spring water the Beggrows brought in by hand to the commercial kitchen and staff quarters that once kept a five-star resort running year-round.

The property could serve as a private family compound, an executive retreat or, subject to approvals, a return to hospitality.

During the summer, the river, pool, and zip line take center stage. In winter, the slopes at Purgatory await. The buyer will also inherit the stretch of track that is the only way in other than by air. For years, the Beggrows rode the train or ran their own homemade cars on the rails.

When asked what getting in and out was like in those early years, Judy answers without a hint of fear.

‘Act III’ of mass protests can’t run through central Paris on Halloween, France’s top court rules

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

PARIS (CN) — The Conseil d’État, France’s top court, ruled Saturday morning that “Act III” of the mass demonstrations could not pass through the city center, just hours before its planned kickoff at Place de la Bastille.

“There is a very high risk that… further major disturbances to public order will occur on [Saturday] on the fringes of the authorised processions, in a tense social and protest-related context, and at a time when numerous calls for violent actions on that day are being circulated on social media,” the court decision read. “The prohibition of this route and the proposal of an alternative … appears necessary and is neither inappropriate nor disproportionate.”

The new route, from Porte de Bagnolet to Porte de Charenton, runs along the outskirts of the capital. Saturday afternoon, buses heading toward the area were rerouted and nearby streets were closed to cars and bikes.

A protester holds a French flag at Republic Square during a demonstration against rising living costs, joining students’ grievances, in Paris, Saturday, Oct. 17, 2026. (AP Photo/Thibault Camus)

The ongoing protests are fueling debate over demonstrators’ fundamental rights and the state’s responsibility to maintain order. In Paris, the route of the demonstrations has become a political and philosophical flashpoint.

Protesting is a fundamental right under the French Constitution. Unions must declare their intended route in advance so authorities can prepare, but do not need permission to demonstrate because protests fall under freedom of expression. Authorities can impose restrictions only when there is a serious safety threat.

On Oct. 24, “Act II” of the demonstrations was pushed to the outskirts of Paris, where hundreds of thousands marched in the rain. This week, the interunion alliance declared that “Act III” would follow a traditional demonstration route from Place de la Bastille to Place de la Nation, used for similar marches for decades.

Paris police prefect Patrice Faure then announced that demonstrations along the route would be banned “for a while.” The prefecture said “Act III” would be allowed only along the city limits, citing Halloween as a public order risk because of potential masks, fake weapons and social media calls to emulate the American horror movie “The Purge.”

The interunion alliance, calling the decision an infringement on democratic rights, appealed to the Paris Administrative Court on Friday evening and won.

“The judge in chambers begins by recalling the scope of the freedom to demonstrate, which extends to the right to choose the time, place, and modalities of the gathering,” Chirinne Ardakani, a lawyer representing the interunion alliance, live-posted from the hearings. “The judge does not see how the imposed alternative route would avoid ‘clashes’ … alleged by a ‘vague and unsubstantiated’ note.’”

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez announced the prefecture would immediately appeal to the Conseil d’État, France’s equivalent of the Supreme Court. Hearings began at 10 p.m. Friday and continued into early Saturday.

The court ultimately ruled in Nuñez’s favor, citing a heightened risk of public disorder, the Oct. 17 riots, threats to businesses along the Bastille-Nation route and demands on police resources. It concluded that the alternative route preserved the right to protest.

Roughly 70,000 officers were deployed nationwide Saturday.
