These Were The Most Popular Baby Names Of 2026, According To BabyCenter

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →
d3sign/Moment/Getty Images

Each year, BabyCenter compiles all the data from their users and publishes a list of the year’s most popular baby names. The official rankings from the Social Security Administration, based on actual issued birth certificates, usually don’t come in until months into the new year, so this is our first peek at which names might have been given to the most babies born in 2026. But they also spot names that have risen quickly in popularity, helping identify what’s coming next in baby-naming trends.

The most popular baby names of 2026 are a predictable pair: Olivia for girls and Noah for boys. Olivia was also the most popular girl name of 2025, according to the SSA, and Noah was #2 on the boys’ chart.

Here’s how the rest of the top 10 shook out, according to BabyCenter’s data:

  1. Olivia / Noah
  2. Eliana / Liam
  3. Amelia / Oliver
  4. Charlotte / Elijah
  5. Sophia / Mateo
  6. Isabella / Lucas
  7. Emma / Elias
  8. Aurora / Levi
  9. Evelyn / Ezra
  10. Mia / Leo

Many of these names have fallen somewhere in the top 10 for multiple years running, but Evelyn in particular rejoins the chart after falling off in 2024. And five completely new monikers broke into the top 100 this year. Making their debuts are Magnolia, Milo, Dean, Archer, and Stetson.

Baby Name Trends We’ll See More Of In 2027

Speaking of Magnolia, long baby names are generally gaining in popularity, according to BabyCenter’s report. In fact, 29% of the top girl names and 31% of the top boy names had seven or more letters (21% had four or less, by comparison). Perhaps parents are opting for longer names that sound good on an adult’s resume, with cute nickname options to use ‘til then.

If you’re big into initials, you’ll like to know that E is currently the most popular first initial for girls (15% of BabyCenter’s top 100 girl names begin with it), while J ranks highest for boys at 14%.

Western-inspired baby names have been trending for a couple of years now, and that doesn’t seem to be slowing down. BabyCenter noted big jumps in popularity for Stetson, Wilder, Callahan, Sadie, and Sutton, to name a few. Names that include an X got a lot of use this year too, according to BabyCenter’s Steph Coffield. Prepare to see even more baby Jaxsons, Maximillians, Ximenas, Xaidens, X-cetera (sorry).

And thanks of course to the eponymous movie, the name Odysseus rose an astronomical 1,456 spots on BabyCenter’s popularity charts. While it won’t be everyone’s first choice for a baby boy in the new year, it does seem like more parents are leaning into Greek mythology for name inspo. Monikers like Atlas, Ares, Evander, Daphne, and Gaia are all scooching their way up the rankings as we speak.

While it’s hard to say exactly what 2027’s baby name trends will bring (you never know when a pop culture moment like Odysseus will change the charts), we can’t wait to hear more interesting names being called across the playground.

Only 32% of Americans have high confidence that news orgs will report midterm election results accurately, survey finds

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

About six in 10 Americans said they have at least “some” confidence that news organizations will report the upcoming midterm election results accurately, according to a new national survey. But only 32% said they had “quite a bit” or “a great deal” of confidence that journalists would do the job.

In the survey from Medill Local News Initiative and the Alliance for Trust in Media, 37% said they had either “not too much confidence” or “no confidence at all” that news outlets would accurately report the election results.

The survey was completed by a nationally representative sample of 1,057 U.S. adults either online or by telephone earlier this month. The findings echo other national surveys, including a Gallup survey that showed trust in media to report “fully, accurately, and fairly” at 33% — an increase from an all-time low last year.

The Medill/Alliance survey found about half of Americans said they had “not too much confidence” or “no confidence at all” in news outlets to investigate voter fraud claims thoroughly. U.S. President Donald Trump has cast doubt on elections on 70% of days since returning to office — starting with false claims about voter fraud on the first day of his second term.

In another bleak result from the same poll, slightly more survey respondents said journalists manipulate the news for political or commercial purposes than report it accurately. Across the survey, Democrats reported being more confident in (and trusting of) news organizations than Republicans. Nearly 80% of Republicans said journalists try to manipulate the news compared to 38% of independents and 24% of Democrats.

No matter how well you’ve prepared or how careful you are with your gear, at some point, whether by cactus spine or sheer volume of trail miles, you’re going to tear a piece of clothing. Or, if you’re me, you’re going to flop down on your sleeping pad, which is on top of your toiletry bag, which holds a pair of impossibly vertical tweezers, and punch an irreparable hole in your mat on the first night of a 225-mile backpacking trip.

You can’t fix every gear problem in the field—a melted down jacket is going to need professional attention or replacement—but with a few simple supplies and some know-how you can handle more of them than you think. Here are four field repairs beyond taping your down jacket you should know, along with a minimal repair kit to get you to the next road crossing.

The bottom package of your hiking shoes split from the rest of your shoe.

The glue line that attaches the bottom package—the midsole and outsole—to the shoe upper is the most common structural failure in footwear. While you can manage a few days with a blown-out upper or worn-down outsole tread, the bottom of your shoe flapping in the breeze is a problem.

If you’re close enough to town or have a sense of humor, you can wear your Crocs as hiking shoes. For the rest of us, use a combination of lacing, zip ties, and duct tape to keep your kicks together long enough to get back to civilization. The most secure way to reattach the bottom of the shoe to the top is with an X pattern, similar to how you’d wrap a sprained ankle. If you have a spare set of laces or shock cord, begin at the front of the shoe and criss-cross over the bottom and top in a series of figure-eights until you reach the top of the laces. From here, you can cover the lacing with duct tape in the same pattern. If you brought zip ties, you can punch through the outsole/midsole (be sure to punch far enough in that it doesn’t wear through the side) and the edge of the upper where it won’t rub or cause blisters. Secure it tightly, then finish with the duct tape in a figure-8 pattern.

A tent pole snapped in half or seriously bent.

I typically don’t carry single-use items in my pack aside from the core components of my kit, but a tent pole splint is an exception. Tent poles carry high amounts of tension from taut pitches and are under even more strain in high winds, and the tape-a-stake-to-the-pole simply does not work. A tent pole splint (or sleeve) is a tube made from the same material as the pole, and simply slides over the bent or broken section to create an unbroken line.

Before you do anything else, straighten the bent pole as much as possible or line up the two broken sections as close as they can get. Once the sleeve is centered over the break, secure it with plenty of duct tape. The pole splints are typically not much wider than the pole itself, so it should hold in place fairly well until you can get a replacement pole section.

You got a tear in your tent fly.

I’ve slapped plenty of patches onto my down jacket and sleeping pads, but tearing your fly is trickier because you’re relying on the material for waterproofing. I ripped a 10-inch hole in my Big Agnes Copper Spur tent a few years ago, and my attempt at a duct-tape repair held up for about six minutes before condensation separated it again. This is where the Tenacious Tape comes in handy, with its flexible nylon, vinyl, or ripstop material and aggressive adhesive backing. It sticks better than duct tape and can fend off a decent amount of moisture for a surprisingly long time.

Be sure to clean the area around the tear as thoroughly as possible (alcohol wipes come in handy here) and dry it thoroughly before applying the tape. GearAid also makes an incredibly waterproof Aquaseal adhesive compound that can act as a seam sealer or smooth on over raincoat tears.

Your stove ignitor isn’t working.

I must be cursed with backpacking stoves, because I feel like my ignitor stops working after my first few trips. I try to remember a lighter for this very reason, but if you didn’t and the finicky ignitor stopped sparking, you can try repositioning the electrode tip. This works if you can hear the igniter clicking but not lighting the gas—the delicate metal electrode wire likely bent away from the port.

Use your multitool pliers here and position the tip five millimeters (just under 1/4 inch) from the center ring, which should be enough for the spark to jump. The wire on the popular MSR PocketRocket tends to slip out of its guide tube, and if you know what to look for, you can slide it back into the alignment sleeve to restore contact. This video demonstrates replacing the ignitor on a similar stove. (Alternatively, just remember your lighter.)

If you’re going to spare three ounces for a repair kit, what should you bring?

Some items, like load-bearing pack straps or buckles, will need to be tied together until you get to town, but there are a few things that are handy to take into the backcountry.

The short answer is tape, tape, tape, tape. I wrap a healthy dose of duct tape (bonus points for weird patterns) around my trekking poles or a prescription bottle to cobble together many repairs. I also bring a few inches of Tenacious Tape, designed to patch waterproof fabrics like rain jackets and tent flies, and a NoSo Patch, which works wonders for keeping the feathers inside a torn jacket or sleeping bag.

For thru-hikes or longer trips where I’m going to be way in the backcountry, I bring a tiny sewing kit, a lightweight knife with grippy pliers and a screwdriver end, a few zip ties for holding a backpack together, a tent pole splint, and several safety pins. Finally, your inflatable sleeping pad likely came with its own tiny repair kit, including an alcohol wipe and a matching repair patch with adhesive optimized for the pad material. This whole kit weighs three ounces and can help prevent some serious headaches.

Your partner is your person, your safe place, your rock — so when they betray you unexpectedly, it can feel like the ground split open beneath you, and there’s no more safety net. Maybe you’re not sure what to do next. Maybe you’re thinking of ending things, or you want to try and work on it first. Whatever happened and wherever you are, there are certain steps couples take after a betrayal that relationship experts say predict whether or not they’ll move forward together or fall apart.

For starters, a betrayal doesn’t just mean someone cheated. Any breach of trust can feel like betrayal, says Blythe Landry, M.Ed., licensed clinical social worker, author of Trauma Intelligence, and founder of Grief to Purpose. Quitting a job without letting your partner know, buying a car or adopting a pet without discussing it first, or parenting in a way your partner is opposed to behind their back — these are all betrayals. So too are having affairs and endangering your kids, like drinking and driving with them in the car, Landry explains.

Those first six months after a betrayal are when the pain is most acute, Landry says. And following any type of betrayal, each partner is going to face their own unique hurdles.

“Oftentimes, the betrayed partner has difficulty determining if things are safe emotionally, as well as being uncertain of what they can believe about their relationship,” Suzanne Koch Eckenrode, licensed marriage and family therapist at Carlsbad Beach Recovery. “The person responsible for breaking the trust may be experiencing feelings of guilt, shame, or defensiveness. One of the biggest obstacles to healing is the fact that both parties will heal at a different rate. It can be very challenging to begin the process of healing when one feels ready to proceed while the other does not feel ready.”

So, how do you begin to move forward at all? Here’s what the experts advise.

What Couples Who Survive A Betrayal Do In The First Six Months

1. They focus on consistency.

Instead of trying to immediately forgive and move on, couples who stick together put their energy toward being consistent with one another, says Koch Eckenrode. This is what rebuilds trust in the long run.

“The couple establishes clear boundaries, communicates in an open manner when they feel uncomfortable or angry, and takes responsibility without blaming the other person,” she says. “Typically, rebuilding a relationship is not achieved by a single grand action but through many actions, to create enough positive interactions so the two people begin to trust each other again gradually.”

2. They both take accountability for their roles in the relationship.

In most cases, the “perpetrator” has not suddenly turned evil and betrayed their partner. These things don’t happen in a vacuum.

“There is often a history of communication differences and things that are going on that lead one party to do something,” Landry says. “Now, that does not make the other person responsible for their choice to betray. But for example, if someone is constantly criticized and they are afraid to tell the truth because [of it], they may be much more likely to do something that they don’t tell their partner about. So one very positive sign is if both parties can look at their accountability.”

One partner may feel chronically unheard, rejected, or disrespected, which ultimately leads to a betrayal of some kind, Landry says. While it doesn’t excuse them, it does explain the behavior — and it reveals what needs fixing to move forward in your relationship together.

3. The betrayer genuinely strives to understand why they did it.

When a normally good, moral person betrays their partner, typically something inside of them is not OK. Either because they don’t want to or don’t know how to process those feelings, they look for something external to help. Maybe it’s an affair partner because their spouse no longer seems sexually interested in them. Maybe it’s drinking too much because it takes the edge off the bad feelings. But their commitment to understanding their own behavior plays a major role in healing and rebuilding trust.

And, importantly, the other partner is patient while they suss out what led them to stray.

“It’s always easy to see when it’s not you,” Landry says. “That’s what I would say to anyone going to couples counseling or even going to a pastor or whoever you go to for help. When it’s inside of you, it’s so evasive sometimes.”

4. They both commit to working on the relationship, even if it seems like only one partner is the problem.

Thinking back to her own clients, Landry says the couples in which both partners were genuinely invested in fixing things — those were the ones who repaired their relationships. That meant both showing up for counseling, both taking accountability, and both being vulnerable throughout the process.

5. They communicate, communicate, communicate until that becomes the norm.

Let’s say one partner travels often for work, and has also used that time to gamble away large sums of money in secret before. Short of quitting that job and never traveling again, how can the couple handle this scenario in a way that rebuilds trust?

“Discuss your travels beforehand and establish reasonable times for checking in with one another, then immediately inform each other of any changes from the plan,” says Koch Eckenrode. “The person doing the travel will be expected to follow through and do as agreed upon without having to constantly be reminded. The purpose here is not to provide some sort of long-term surveillance system; it’s to create an environment where each person feels safe enough that they are transparent about their actions naturally because it has become a part of who they are as opposed to being something they need to do.”

If you had good communication before the betrayal, that’s usually the biggest indicator that you two will successfully get through this as a couple, Landry says.

6. They hold each other in high regard, even when it’s hard.

This has more to do with your personalities than the relationship itself, Landry notes, but the ability to see each other in a positive light will get you far. When the betrayed party has the capacity to look at their partner and say, “This is a good person who did a bad thing,” that’s a really good sign.

Of course, there are those people who don’t deserve this level of grace. “There’s a difference between giving someone another chance and holding them in high regard and letting someone abuse you. Make sure that you’re not so attached to someone that they’ve done something repeatedly to betray you and you’re holding onto it in the name of compassion, and really you’re harming yourself,” she says.

7. They get outside support.

Again, it’s impossible to see yourself and your behaviors clearly all the time, Landry says. This is where the support of a therapist, family member, religious leader, or other non-biased third party can be helpful.

“Before you go to a professional, make sure to ask them questions like, ‘What have you done before? Have you worked with other couples in this situation? Do you work on yourself?’ I have never had a pre-client ask me if I go to therapy, but I really wish they would because you really shouldn’t be helping people unless you're helping yourself,” Landry says.

If you do opt to lean on a pastor or church elder for guidance, make sure they are not going to encourage you to stay in an unhealthy relationship, she adds. “The cool thing about therapy is that the therapist will have to see each of you individually prior to doing couples counseling. And they are doing that to make sure the relationship is safe.”

Senate Democrats’ top super PAC spends in South Carolina for the first time

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Senate Democrats’ top super political action committee is pushing into South Carolina — just five days before the election.

Senate Majority PAC is putting $750,000 into digital ads to boost Democrat Annie Andrews, who they see as in an increasingly competitive position against GOP Sen. Darline Graham. The first spot could air as soon as Friday, and is set to attack Graham over rising costs and position Andrews as a fighter against corruption, according to plans shared first with POLITICO.

The Chuck Schumer-aligned group’s investment comes as public polling shows a tightening race in the closing stretch. Republicans say they’re confident in Graham’s ability to hold the seat, in part because the GOP-aligned Senate Leadership Fund recently pledged to spend at least $5 million in the race. President Donald Trump has already held two rallies in the state to boost his hand-picked senator.

“South Carolina is tightening, and Republicans know it — you don’t send $5 million and the president to a state if you're not nervous,” SMP spokesperson Lauren French said in a statement.

“Darline Graham is an empty suit who was handed a Senate seat and has no idea what to do with it,” French added. “Annie Andrews will lower costs on gas, groceries, insurance, and housing. In a race this close, a late infusion of cash can make the difference, and that’s why we’re in.”

Representatives for Andrews and Graham did not immediately respond to requests for comment on the investment.

Even with the extensive outside support, Graham’s campaign has been weighed down by voter fatigue after several contentious primaries, questions about her political inexperience and an increasingly difficult national environment for Republicans.

The South Carolina spending marks Democrats’ newest late-stage push into deep-red Senate races as they look to leverage Americans’ frustrations with the cost of living and Trump’s agenda to keep expanding the map. If Andrews wins next week, it would mark the first time South Carolina elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1998.

Strong showings by former Gov. Roy Cooper in North Carolina and Sen. Jon Ossoff in Georgia have allowed SMP to draw down its spending in those erstwhile battlegrounds while pumping millions of dollars into traditionally red territories like Texas, Kansas and Mississippi.
