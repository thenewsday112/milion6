Most marketers say AI is improving things, but few of them can prove it.

According to a new Metric Sherpa study produced with Sprinklr, 92% of organizations with customer-facing AI in regular use said it met or exceeded expectations. Only 27% said they could show proof of that.

Similarly, 67% of the people who said AI made work faster also said it reduced costs. However, most of those had no baseline to base it on: Only 32% of that group said they tracked costs before launch.

Measurement needs to start before the AI does

This is likely because most teams know what they want AI to improve, but few define the metric, record the starting point, or set a clear threshold for success. So when the results arrive, even if there’s a success story, there’s no way to prove it.

The study found that while 99% could name an intended outcome for their AI use, only 26% defined success metrics, and just 10% also recorded a baseline.

Without the last two, it’s impossible to prove AI’s ROI.

If the goal is lower acquisition cost, the team needs a cost baseline before the AI starts making media or campaign decisions. If the goal is better customer service, containment alone won’t tell you much. The team needs data on resolution, repeat contacts, escalations, and handoffs.

These measurements must be built into the workflow before launch.

Don’t let the platform define success

If they aren’t, teams are forced to rely on whatever numbers the platform gives them.

A paid media platform can optimize conversion. A service platform can optimize containment. A content system can optimize engagement. All three can show improvement inside their own dashboards while the business outcome stays flat.

Eighty-five percent of those surveyed reported improvement in at least one metric category they hadn’t tracked before launch. The researchers couldn’t tell whether those teams added measurement later or relied on another source, but the disconnect is hard to ignore.

Marketing ops needs to connect platform activity to CRM, finance, customer, or experiment data. Otherwise, the tool doing the work also gets to decide whether the work succeeded.

Someone still has to own the result

Even with the right metrics in place, somebody has to be responsible for the outcome.

Only 17% said their organization names an owner for AI business outcomes. It was the least common of the eight practices measured in the study.

That role is different from owning the platform or the workflow. The platform owner makes sure the tool works. The workflow owner makes sure the process runs. The outcome owner answers for whether the AI produced the desired result.

Organizations using a named outcome owner were able to quantify results 46% of the time, versus 23% of those without one. They also used controlled tests and financial reporting more often. The study doesn’t establish that ownership caused better measurement, but the association is substantial.

Better measurement can make AI look worse

Teams that measure more carefully may also find more things going wrong.

Organizations using three or more of the study’s measurement practices reported more AI-to-employee handoff problems, adoption issues, and data-quality friction than those using zero or one. It isn’t clear if the increased measurement exposed more problems or if more complicated deployments had more problems to find. Either way, the operational lesson is useful.

A mature AI program can look messier because somebody is finally checking what happens after the automation fires. That means reviews need to cover more than output and speed. Look at where handoffs break, where employees ignore recommendations, where customers repeat themselves, and where a metric moves in the wrong direction.

Decide what would make you stop

Only 28%  said their organization uses agreed criteria to decide whether to scale, change, or stop an AI application. Just 37% review AI results on a regular schedule.

That leaves plenty of AI running without a clear rule for what happens if the direct metric improves but the business result doesn’t.

For one application, define the outcome, baseline, success threshold, and comparison before the next review. Then write down what result would justify scaling it, what would require a change, and what would cause the team to stop.

AI can make a martech stack much more capable. It can also make weak measurement much harder to unwind once the system is running.

AI is moving beyond helping marketers write copy, summarize research, and complete individual tasks. Increasingly, AI agents can take on multistep assignments, coordinate activities and make decisions that previously required human intervention.

Designing workflows for human-AI collaboration

The Betsy Ross House: A Home Woven into the American Story

Built around 1740, the Betsy Ross House offers a glimpse into the determination, ingenuity and entrepreneurial spirit of a working woman during America’s founding.

The Philadelphia home is associated with Betsy Ross, the upholsterer, entrepreneur and Revolutionary-era flag maker whose story became intertwined with the creation of the first American flag. While historians continue to debate parts of the familiar legend, records show Ross was an accomplished tradeswoman who made flags for the government for decades.

The home’s remarkable story extends beyond the Revolutionary era. When it faced demolition in the late 19th century, Americans from across the country came together to preserve it, contributing through 10-cent memberships. It was an early demonstration of the collective pride and community spirit that continue to give historic places their meaning. 

Its Redfin listing-style page looks beyond the legend to explore Ross as a business owner, working mother and survivor who built an extraordinary life within two small, rented rooms. 

1892 Bishop’s Palace: A Remarkable Gilded Age Home

Built between 1887 and 1892, Bishop’s Palace was designed by Nicholas J. Clayton, Galveston’s most influential architect, for lawyer and congressman Walter Gresham, his wife, Josephine, and their seven children. The home spans 52 rooms and 19,086 square feet. It became the residence of Bishop Byrne after the Catholic Church purchased it in 1923.

Each detail of the home was custom-chosen, from hand-carved woodwork throughout to a dining room ceiling mural painted by Josephine herself. At the heart of the home, an octagonal mahogany rotunda staircase rises beneath French stained glass, surrounded by finishes from Venice, Holland and North Africa.

The property also includes a few conversation pieces, among them Josephine’s china collection, the original bed, Walter’s desk and Bishop Byrne’s player piano. It sits in Galveston’s East End Historic District, a neighborhood of colorful historic homes and lush landscaping, with the Gulf only a few blocks south. 

Today, Bishop’s Palace stands as a one-of-a-kind blend of elaborate craftsmanship, Texan influence and Gilded Age ambition.

'A wave can hide a lot of weaknesses': Dems question if they'll learn anything

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Democrats are poised for a good night next Tuesday. That doesn’t mean they’ve figured out how to win.

More than a dozen lawmakers, campaign operatives, strategists and pollsters warned in interviews that national Democrats risk drawing the wrong conclusions if they take back control of at least one chamber of Congress next week.

Those figures from across the party worry that the midterms, which have become a referendum on President Donald Trump, have done little to improve the Democratic Party’s brand. They caution that a favorable political environment has papered over persistent problems for a party still grappling with why it lost the 2024 presidential election. And they admit they are still sharply divided over what its message should be heading into 2028.

“Don't confuse them losing with us winning,” said Morgan Jackson, the senior adviser to Democrat Roy Cooper’s Senate campaign in North Carolina. Cooper has consistently led in the polls by a wide margin in a state Trump won three times.

“This is not an affirmation of the Democratic Party in any which way,” said one veteran Democratic pollster working on Senate campaigns, who was granted anonymity to speak candidly. “It is very much a rejection of Trump.”

The Democratic Party’s approval rating plummeted in the aftermath of the 2024 presidential election, and remains relatively weak. That’s even as voters tell pollsters they are more likely to trust Democrats than Republicans on several fronts, including addressing the cost of living, which voters consistently say is their top issue this cycle.

Democrats’ quest for a coherent, positive pitch to answer rising prices and perceived corruption in Washington has at times been stymied by infighting between the party’s progressive and moderate factions over the best way to respond to the Trump administration.

Democrats fought over whether to prolong government shutdowns to protest funding for Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the slashing of health care subsidies in late 2025 and early 2026. They were quick to attack their own upon release of the Democratic National Committee’s formal autopsy on where the party went wrong in 2024.

But as Americans soured on Trump’s agenda and the Iran war drove up costs, Democrats zeroed in on a broad “costs, chaos and corruption” message that blamed the White House — without having to resolve their own internal tensions and disorder.

“When your opponent is destroying themselves," said Matt Barron, a Democratic strategist who has done work across the rural Midwest and Rust Belt, "just get out of the way and let it happen.”

Nabbing wins in a midterm election with a historically unpopular president is one thing. Building a winning brand for a party that was soundly defeated at the polls just two years ago is another.

“If all you do is say the other people are terrible, that only gets you so far,” said Democratic donor Oliver Libby, who has advised Democratic House candidates Cait Conley in New York and Johnny Garcia in Texas this cycle. “You can have a minimum viable product of a midterm with criticism. You definitely don't win a presidential year with just criticism.”

In a new POLITICO Poll conducted by PF Global, more than half of Americans said if Republicans lose control of both the House and the Senate, it would be either because of Trump’s actions since he became president, or because Republicans have run weak campaigns. Just one in four said a Democrat-controlled Congress would be because the party had run a strong campaign.

“There are people who are going to vote in this election who are going to vote against Republicans,” said Antjuan Seawright, a Democratic strategist and adviser to House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries. “That does not mean they are in love with Democrats. It just means they are voting against the people in charge.”

Democratic strategist Lis Smith, an architect of Pete Buttigieg's 2020 presidential campaign, said the Democratic candidates performing well in historically deep-red territory are the ones distancing themselves from the national party.

“That should tell party leaders something,” she said.

Smith helped found the moderate groups Majority Democrats and The Bench, and is advising Democratic Senate candidates like Adam Hamilton in Kansas, James Talarico in Texas and Josh Turek in Iowa — all of whom have taken on that tack.

In Kansas, Hamilton publicly asked former Vice President Kamala Harris to stay away from his campaign, and said he would vote against Chuck Schumer remaining the party’s leader in the Senate. In Texas, Talarico vowed to challenge the party’s “establishment” and “change the Democratic Party.” And in Iowa, Turek says he rarely thinks about the party, while Rob Sand, the Democratic gubernatorial candidate in the state, has said he wants to end the two-party system altogether.

“The reality is that a lot of very average and even mediocre candidates get across the line in a wave election,” Smith said. “A wave can hide a lot of weaknesses, and so we need to be able to make sure that the party can survive when the wave recedes.”

Assuming Democrats take back control of at least one chamber of Congress next week, top party operatives see an opportunity for their side to deliver for voters who have wanted more checks on the Trump administration’s expansive use of executive power. But many caution that Democrats on Capitol Hill should avoid leaning too hard into an anti-Trump message next year, while others argue that the anti-corruption message will continue to galvanize the electorate.

"The thing that I would be concerned about, if you go from 2026 to 2028, is that people think that corruption, that elite control over our economy, is just a Trump issue," said Bill Neidhardt, who worked on Bernie Sanders’ 2020 presidential campaign and is now advising left-leaning Democratic candidate Will Lawrence and working to boost Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic candidate for Senate in Michigan. “What they need to focus on is that corporate power, those billionaire elites who are dictating the terms of our economy, that's what people are upset with."

William Garcia, the co-chair of Wisconsin’s La Crosse County Democratic Party, has been canvassing extensively for his party in the state’s rural-heavy 3rd district, where voters swung toward Trump in recent years. Democrats are now trying to claw back that support.

“I think we’re going to have a great night on Nov. 3. And then all these Democrats are going to have to deliver on all of these things we’re talking about,” Garcia said. “If we get to the end of 2027 summer and we haven’t passed bills to make a substantial difference in people’s lives, that’s my biggest fear. We gotta deliver.”

Others acknowledge that voters may hold the party to a higher bar if they succeed in taking back control of both chambers of Congress — and they’ll be ready to punish Democrats in 2028 if they don’t follow through on their campaign promises to hold the Trump administration to account and bring down the cost of living.

It’s a slippery slope for a party that has historically struggled to convince voters that it is well equipped to handle the economy. In 2024, Trump persuaded the majority of voters they would be financially better off with him in the White House, while painting Democrats as a party more concerned with social issues.

“If we start getting back into a party that cares more about non-economic issues, then we haven't learned the lesson,” warned Pete Giangreco, a veteran Democratic strategist who has worked on House, Senate and gubernatorial campaigns this cycle. “People just are going to keep hitting the change button until the economy starts working for them again.”

Samuel Benson and Meredith Lee Hill contributed reporting.

Barcelona targeting January move for Tottenham Hotspur defender

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Tottenham could face growing interest in one of their key players ahead of the January transfer window, with Barcelona president Joan Laporta reportedly keen on bringing Pedro Porro to the Nou Camp.

The Spanish right-back has attracted attention following his performances for Spain at the World Cup, but Spurs have several reasons to feel confident about keeping hold of the defender.

Barcelona Identify Pedro Porro as January Target

According to Catalan broadcaster 3Cat, Porro is one of Laporta’s preferred defensive targets as Barcelona consider whether to strengthen their backline in January.

The Catalan club have been dealing with a series of defensive injuries involving Eric Garcia, Jules Kounde, Andreas Christensen and Xavi Espart. However, Barcelona have not made a final decision on whether to bring in another defender, with the club waiting to assess the injured players’ recoveries.

Laporta’s admiration for Porro reportedly grew during Spain’s World Cup campaign. The Tottenham defender became an important part of the national team, developing a strong understanding with Lamine Yamal on the right flank and scoring twice during the tournament, including in the semi-final against France.

Laporta watched that semi-final in Dallas and was reportedly impressed by Porro’s performance. Barcelona had also gathered information about the defender towards the end of the previous summer transfer window, but their enquiry did not progress into a formal proposal to either the player or Tottenham.

Porro’s contractual situation gives Spurs a strong negotiating position. The 27-year-old renewed his contract shortly before the World Cup, committing his future to the north London club until 2031.

Reports have also indicated that Tottenham would demand a significant fee to consider allowing the defender to leave. Real Madrid have also been linked with an interest in the Spain international, potentially creating further competition for his signature.

What This Means for Tottenham

Barcelona’s admiration for Porro is a reminder of the defender’s standing in European football, but there is currently no confirmed bid or formal approach.

Tottenham will be keen to retain a player who remains an important part of their plans. His long-term contract also means the club are under no immediate pressure to negotiate a sale.

The situation could develop if Barcelona decide their defensive injury problems require January reinforcements. For now, however, Spurs remain in a strong position, and any interested club would likely have to make a substantial offer to test their resolve.

World Anti-Doping Agency’s Latest Banned List Sends a Warning to Athletes Looking for ‘Loopholes’

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There is a certain kind of athlete who treats the World Anti-Doping Agency’s prohibited list like a treasure map. He reads it, finds something that isn’t printed there, and figures he’s discovered a loophole. The molecule has a name that sounds like a Wi-Fi password, the vial says “research use only,” and some guy with a podcast swears it repairs tendons, melts fat, or turns you into a gazelle. Perfect. What could possibly go wrong?

Well, WADA just made that little game harder. Its 2027 Prohibited List, which takes effect Jan. 1, now explicitly mentions peptides in the category for non-approved substances. The U.S. Anti-Doping Agency put out an advisory explaining the change and warning athletes not to assume an experimental peptide is legal simply because its particular alphabet soup isn’t spelled out on the list. That’s a useful distinction, because the peptide business has become a sprawling mess of legitimate medicine, promising research, questionable claims, and little bottles of who-the-hell-knows-what.

What Changes Are Included in WADA’s 2027 Prohibited List?

Before anybody starts screaming that WADA banned every peptide on earth, it didn’t. Peptides are chains of amino acids, and some are established medicines. Insulin is a peptide. The relevant category, called S0, covers pharmacological substances without approval for human therapeutic use by a government health authority anywhere in the world, provided they aren’t already covered by another prohibited class. Some peptides fall into other categories. The word “peptides” appearing in S0 is a clarification, not a declaration that every peptide drug is forbidden.

The important point is that experimental substances weren’t necessarily fair game yesterday. BPC-157, for example, was already prohibited under the rules for non-approved substances. Others, including certain growth-hormone-related compounds, are covered elsewhere. What changed is the language, making it even harder to pretend that an unlisted research chemical gets a free pass. And if you compete in a tested sport, “I couldn’t find it on Google” ranks right up there with road rage. No one is gonna buy it.

There are some other interesting additions as well. WADA names two experimental metabolic modulators, SLU-PP-332 and SLU-PP-915, as examples in its hormone and metabolic modulators category. The list also adds examples of diuretics and masking agents, including eplerenone and finerenone, drugs that have perfectly legitimate medical uses. That’s another reality athletes forget: the prohibited list isn’t just a catalog of things somebody invented to cheat. It can include ordinary prescription medications, depending on the drug and circumstances.

Meanwhile, semaglutide and tirzepatide, the active ingredients in two widely used families of weight-loss and diabetes treatments, remain on WADA’s monitoring program for 2027. Monitoring is not banning. WADA is collecting information about their use in sport; it has not put them on the prohibited list. The same distinction matters with caffeine, which is monitored in competition but not prohibited. Which makes me feel good that the people making the rules recognize a difference between watching something and convicting it.

While they were at it, they also tuned up the cannabis language. WADA clarified that psychoactive THC-related cannabinoids, including certain synthetic and semi-synthetic versions, are prohibited in competition. CBD itself isn’t prohibited, but a CBD product containing undeclared THC can still create a problem. Meaning, you run the risk of failing a drug test.

It doesn’t matter if you’re an Olympian, a fighter, a weightlifter, or some local competitor. Under anti-doping rules, athletes are responsible for what’s in their bodies. What’s missing off a label is not an alibi. Neither is a coach or a doctor. You’re on your own. So play the game wisely.

I am 100% for researching new performance drugs. That’s how some useful things eventually get discovered. But research is not approval, and a promising animal study is not proof in humans. None of that really matters to many athletes because they usually rely on anecdotal evidence. They will do it anyway—if they think it works and can find a way of not getting caught. Which, for the most part, in my view of things, they do. But if your competitive career matters to you, you need to be very careful, as peptides create a very broad gray area. Make sure you check the actual status of the substance before you put it in your body—and remember, just because WADA has banned it doesn’t mean that there’s a test for it. January isn’t far away, and WADA is making one thing abundantly clear: They’re always a step behind. I guess it never occurs to them to switch hats and discover the new stuff before we do.

Manchester City favourites to sign Borussia Dortmund star after formal approach

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Manchester City are continuing to invest in young talent as they look to strengthen their squad for the future, with another exciting prospect emerging on their transfer radar.

Pep Guardiola’s side have reportedly moved into pole position for one of Borussia Dortmund’s most highly rated youngsters.

United States international Mathis Albert has attracted interest from several European clubs, but City are currently considered the favourites to secure his signature.

With the teenager’s first-team opportunities limited in Germany, the Premier League champions could look to take advantage of the situation.

Manchester City Lead Mathis Albert Chase

According to TEAMtalk, Manchester City are the frontrunners to sign Albert after making a formal approach during the summer transfer window.

The report claims City are prepared to offer the 17-year-old lucrative personal terms as they step up their pursuit. Their interest is serious, with the club previously prepared to meet a valuation in the region of €20 million (£16.7m).

Chelsea are also long-term admirers of the youngster and were reportedly willing to spend a similar amount last winter. Paris Saint-Germain previously made contact with Dortmund in 2025, while Arsenal and Liverpool have more recently been monitoring his situation.

However, City are currently viewed as being in the strongest position should Dortmund soften their stance.

Albert joined Borussia Dortmund from the LA Galaxy academy in 2024 and has quickly established himself as one of the most exciting young American talents in European football. He has already made Bundesliga appearances and earned senior international recognition with the United States.

Despite his potential, opportunities under Niko Kovac have been limited this season. That lack of regular first-team football has encouraged several clubs to monitor his development, although Dortmund remain determined to keep hold of their young attacker.

The Bundesliga side have reportedly rejected approaches, including loan enquiries, and continue to view Albert as an important part of their long-term plans. They are also preparing a contract extension offer, with his current agreement running until June 2027.

There is another important factor for City. Premier League regulations prevent English clubs from signing Albert permanently until he turns 18 in May, meaning any potential transfer would be geared towards next summer rather than an immediate move.
