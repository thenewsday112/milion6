This isn’t the Newcastle United team I expected

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Watching this Newcastle United team has so far raised more questions than answers.

Many more.

I am not writing off any of the newcomers, whether that is Matthias Jaissle or any of the signings.

History tells us that snap judgements are often proved wrong with the benefit of time, so personally I will give all of them a proper chance to show what they can, or can’t, do.

To be honest, I admit I hadn’t previously heard of Matthias Jaissle or any of the eight players that ended up coming in this summer, with the exception of Nico Gonzalez. Even then I confess that the sum total of my knowledge was that he is a midfielder and wasn’t a regular first choice Manchester City player. I couldn’t have described what his strengths and weaknesses were but of course once it became clear we were going to sign him, I then became a Nico Gonzalez expert like the rest of you, as we searched for info and footage of him online.

On the other hand, when it comes to the players in this Newcastle United team who hadn’t just arrived this summer, I/we know them well. Their strengths, their weaknesses…

This was the Newcastle United team on Thursday night at the Hill Dickinson Stadium…

Hornicek, Dedic, Schar, Burn, Hall, Gonzalez, Willock, Ramsey, Barnes, Murphy, Osula

Back in say February, if you had asked me to name the Newcastle United team I hoped would be first choice in the 2026-27 season I would have been proved wrong on ten counts. The honourable exception is of course the brilliant Lewis Hall.

The 2026-27 Newcastle United team I would have had in mind back in February…

Trafford, Livramento, Thiaw, new left-side centre-back, Hall, Bruno, Tonali, new midfielder, Gordon, Elanga, new striker

Needless to say, the eight months that have followed February 2026 haven’t turned out as I expected, as I hoped. The same for pretty much all of you I imagine.

Many factors have contributed to the changes that have happened at St James’ Park, including what the Newcastle United team is now, compared to what I’d expected, hoped for.

The severe pressures brought about by the UEFA SCR situation, on top of all the existing disadvantages the likes of Newcastle United and Aston Villa have when it comes to the ability to pay the wages and transfer fees, compared to the financially dominant six. You have then had a change internally at NUFC whereby the Sporting Director leads on who is signed and sold, Eddie Howe deciding to leave and a new face to the Premier League arriving, then so many ins and outs in the transfer window, plus so many injuries and players struggling to get back to full match fitness.

This Newcastle United team we now have. It is what it is.

Photo by Matt Busby Said via The Mag

Going through that Newcastle United team that faced Everton…

Lukas Hornicek

We were always going to be getting a new first choice keeper and Lukas Hornicek was no doubt on the club’s radar. Though I do think James Trafford was who Eddie Howe clearly wanted and it had looked as though he’d finally come to Tyneside. That didn’t happen and to be fair to Hornicek I think he looks a good signing and somebody who will improve as he adjusts to the step up from the weak Portuguese league.

Amar Dedic

A similar story to our goalkeeper as Dedic gets used to the Premier League after moving from Portugal. The club made clear he was already on their radar and not just Matthias Jaissle then arriving and recommending a player he’d already managed. If things had turned out the way I’d hoped/expected they would for this season, then Amar Dedic may well still have been signed, especially as he can play on the right and left, but I think he would have been on the bench more than in the first team and that may still happen if Tino ever manages to stay fit and available.

Fabian Schar

Has been a brilliant player for Newcastle United but he turns 35 during this season and I expected him to be a back-up central defender this season.

Dan Burn

See above.

Lewis Hall

Levels above all of our other players.

Nico Gonzalez

I don’t think the club were thinking of buying him from Manchester City but Bruno’s departure pushed this signing.

Joe Willock

Assumed he would have left this past summer.

Jacob Murphy

See above.

Jacob Ramsey

Improved as last season went on but still had it to prove that he could/should be first choice. I expected a new signing coming in to play alongside Bruno and Tonali and Ramsey as main back-up.

Harvey Barnes

I like Harvey Barnes but didn’t expect him to be first choice this season. Even with all the injuries last season he only started 19 Premier League games and remarkably came off the bench in 18 of the other 19. For me, I expected him to be back-up this season but instead he is automatic first choice as things stand.

William Osula

By the end of February 2026 the Danish striker had scored a total of two Premier League goals in his entire career. An excellent late run of six Premier League goals began with his superb solo effort that defeated Manchester United in March despite NUFC going a man down. I expected him to be the main back-up striker this season but due to that late run of goals last season and by default (not signing a new clear first choice striker), Will Osula is Newcastle’s first choice striker, when fit.

I hope Matthias Jaissle proves to be the long-term answer for Newcastle United, only time will tell.

What I do know though is at the moment I am bemused when so many fans are…bemused when this Newcastle United team aren’t doing far better, why they are so up and down, why they look so fragile at times once conceding, why individual errors happen, why chances are missed and goals conceded, why they rarely dominate the opposition.

Photo by Matt Busby Said via The Mag

This Newcastle United team is currently one made up of players who I/we expected to have left by now or be squad players, supplemented by new signings who to greater or lesser extents are still integrating and not helped by the fact that so much experience and quality was lost this summer.

Hopefully the injury and fitness situation will significantly improve and help the Newcastle United team to do the same. With most players available there will hopefully be enough quality that can be knitted together to bring relative success this season, though that relative success has a relatively low glass ceiling of potential compared to the Newcastle United team I envisaged this season, that I hoped for.

Best of luck every time, every season, to whichever 11 players take to the pitch for Newcastle United. At the moment though I will be hoping for the best but fearing the worst, as this looks a difficult season ahead and my expectations have been significantly lowered.

Billy Sharp makes surprise retirement U-turn to play one final game in FA Cup for former club

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Billy Sharp will return to football less than a month after announcing his retirement.

The 40-year-old will come back for one game only, having quit football on October 11, to help out former club Rushden & Diamonds.

Sharp scored 299 goals across his career – now he can make it 300Alamy

Eighth-tier AFC Rushden & Diamonds, the phoenix club following Rushden & Diamonds demise in 2011, announced the signing of Sharp on Sunday.

The Northern Premier League Division One Midlands side reached the first round proper of the FA Cup for the first time.

They won five qualifying rounds to reach the first round and will take on Burton.

In a statement, the club said: “After 21 years away, AFC Rushden & Diamonds are excited to announce that Billy Sharp becomes a Diamond once again.

“Billy scored the first of 299 goals for Rushden & Diamonds back in 2005.

“The man who put the ball in the Cobblers’ net, has 299 career goals spanning over 22 years.

“Featuring for RDFC, Sheffield United Doncaster Rovers, Southampton, & Scunthorpe to name a few!

“Billy comes in to help the club with James Clifton being suspended and we can’t wait to see him pull on the shirt one final time as we go to Burton Albion in the FA Cup 1st Round Proper for the first time ever in AFC Rushden & Diamonds history.”

Billy Sharp retirement

Sharp scored 299 senior goals in an extensive playing career that saw him feature from League Two through to the Premier League.

This included three spells at Sheffield United, as well as successful stints at Scunthorpe, Doncaster and Nottingham Forest.

Sharp also had a brief stint in the MLS with LA Galaxy in 2023Getty

His first taste of Premier League football came with Southampton in the 2012/13 season, before returning six years later with the Blades.

Sharp is also the Championship’s record goalscorer having hit 130 in the second tier.

Speaking to talkSPORT, Sharp was asked if it was a difficult decision to make, and replied: “No. I’ve been planning it, I had plenty of time to think about it.

“I’m just really satisfied that I got a choice to retire and it’s not been taken away from me by injury or something else. It’s a big wrench to make that decision.

“I know I could have played on and scored goals, but I thought the time was right. I’ve just had a new addition to the family and time away with the family, which I’ve not really been able to have.

“It’s always been football. Football has given me a lot of highs, a lot of great memories, and it was there for me when I lost a child. I’ll be forever grateful and in debt to that.

“I’ve had a good career, scored a lot of goals, and I’ve got so many memories to take away from it.”

Billy Sharp's career in numbers

  • Sheffield United – 377 games, 129 goals, 42 assists
  • Doncaster Rovers – 198 games, 71 goals, 20 assists
  • Scunthorpe United – 94 games, 56 goals, 5 assists
  • Nottingham Forest – 40 games, 11 goals, 5 assists
  • Leeds United – 35 games, 5 goals, 1 assist
  • Southampton – 18 games, 10 goals, 3 assists
  • Rushden & Diamonds – 16 games, 9 goals, 1 assist
  • Hull City – 13 games, 0 goals, 0 assists
  • LA Galaxy – 12 games, 6 goals, 1 assist
  • Reading – 10 games, 2 goals, 0 assists

Total: 814 games, 299 goals, 78 assists

Sharp had also joined Baller League team N5, led by former Arsenal and Germany goalkeeper Jens Lehmann.

The striker has also completed his coaching badges and may have one eye on management in the future.

The Nike Alphafly is the flagship marathon super shoe from the company that invented the super shoe—and arguably the most iconic racing shoe ever made. It was on the feet of Eliud Kipchoge in 2019 when he became the first human to break two hours in the marathon, and again in 2022 when he ran his 2:01:09 official world record. It stood atop the podium in Chicago in 2023 when Kelvin Kiptum broke Kipchoge’s record, and again in 2024 when Ruth Chepngetich ran 2:09:56 to break the women’s world record. Over the past six years and three iterations, the Alphafly has consistently carried elites and competitive runners to course records, podium finishes, and PRs.

But despite this consistent success, the Alphafly has struggled to maintain a consistent ride and reliable feel. The original was incredibly bouncy but extremely unstable. The Alphafly 2 fixed the stability issue, but many found it lost its bounce. The Alphafly 3 introduced a continuous sole and repositioned the Air Zoom units, bringing back the bounce in a lighter overall package.

As a competitive masters athlete and full-time running coach, I have a few trusted super shoes I personally race in and recommend to my clients. The Nike Alphafly 3 is one of them; the Alphafly 2 was not. So when I heard rumors of the Alphafly 4 launch, I got anxious about what Nike would change and whether it would be a step back for the stellar shoe.
