Di Lorenzo: ‘Napoli struggle with consistency, I was forced to go off’

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Giovanni Di Lorenzo confirms he was ‘forced’ to come off for Napoli at half-time in the 2-0 defeat to Juventus, but was glad to reunite with his old coach Luciano Spalletti.

The captain and Kevin De Bruyne went off at half-time with injuries, the Belgium international turning his ankle, and the team never quite stepped up the performance in the same way.

Di Lorenzo suffers new Napoli setback

NAPLES, ITALY – MAY 11: Giovanni Di Lorenzo of SSC Napoli celebrates after scoring his side first goal during the Serie A match between SSC Napoli and Bologna FC 1909 at Stadio Diego Armando Maradona on May 11, 2026 in Naples, Italy. (Photo by Francesco Pecoraro/Getty Images)

“We have many games weighing on us and I was forced to go off, which disappointed me, as I really wanted to keep going,” Di Lorenzo told DAZN Italia.

“We had that big chance to take the lead with Lucca, but we were unable to maintain the same level after the restart and the game slowly drifted into Juve’s hands. It’s a pity, because this is a blow for our position in the table and we are falling behind.”

TURIN, ITALY – NOVEMBER 01: Federico Gatti of Juventus is challenged by Scott McTominay of SSC Napoli during the Serie A match between Juventus and SSC Napoli at Allianz Stadium on November 01, 2026 in Turin, Italy. (Photo by Valerio Pennicino/Getty Images)

Napoli are eight points worse off than at this stage last season, when after 10 rounds they were alone in first place, and currently sit in eighth position alongside Bologna.

“I think we are lacking consistency, because we have some really good performances and alternate them with negative ones, while even over the course of 90 minutes we struggle to stay at the same level. We have to stay united and try to improve our position in the table.”

TURIN, ITALY – NOVEMBER 01: Nick Woltemade of Juventus is challenged by Giovane of SSC Napoli during the Serie A match between Juventus and SSC Napoli at Allianz Stadium on November 01, 2026 in Turin, Italy. (Photo by Valerio Pennicino/Getty Images)

Stanislav Lobotka, Benoit Badiashile, Leonardo Spinazzola, David Neres, Alisson Santos, Costantino Favasuli and Alessandro Buongiorno were already out of action, and they seemed to be fatigued after the midweek 4-1 win over Monza with very little room for squad rotation.

“If we are in this situation, then we all have to raise the bar. We are aware it’s a tough moment and we’re trying to work hard to improve, but the results are deficient right now. The team will do everything to turn it around.”

There was a reunion with Juventus boss Spalletti, who won the Scudetto with Di Lorenzo at Napoli in 2023.

“He asked me how my family was doing, I have a strong rapport with the coach, we had two incredible years together at Napoli with the Scudetto, and I will always remember him with pleasure,” concluded the defender.

Collins accuses Schumer of pushing attack ads filled with 'outright lies' in tight Maine race

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Sen. Susan Collins on Sunday blasted Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer and the Democratic Party over what she described as a wave of false attack ads targeting her and other Republican candidates, saying she has never seen a campaign with "so many outright lies."

She made the remarks during an interview with Fox News on Sunday, just two days before Election Day in Maine, where she is seeking reelection in a closely watched race against Democratic rival Troy Jackson. According to the latest Fox News poll, the two candidates are tied at 49%.

Collins accused Democrats of following "Chuck Schumer's playbook," pointing to attack ads she said were nearly identical to those targeting other Republican lawmakers.

MANCHIN CALLS SCHUMER 'CRAZY' FOR TRYING TO OUST GOP SENATOR IN CRUCIAL SENATE SHOWDOWN

"One of the outrageous, completely false ads against me had to do with Jeffrey Epstein. And it turns out, in talking to two of my colleagues who are in tough races, that they had virtually identical ads run against them. So this is part of Chuck Schumer's playbook," she said.

"There are a lot of differences between my opponent and me, but I've never seen a campaign with so many outright lies. It's very frustrating," she added.

Collins further accused Democrats of trying to shift the focus away from local issues and toward divisive national political battles.

"It's certainly an attempt by my opponent and Chuck Schumer and their allies to nationalize this race," she said.

SENATE CONTROL IS A COIN FLIP — AND DEMOCRATS’ PATH RUNS THROUGH TRUMP COUNTRY

Turning to her own campaign, Collins portrayed herself as a bipartisan lawmaker willing to work across party lines, arguing that the Senate needs more centrists as moderates increasingly leave Washington.

"Unfortunately, a lot of them have given up and retired or decided they just didn't want to run again. And that's one reason I am running again," she said.

"We need more people who are willing to work across party lines. That is really important," she said.

FOX NEWS POLL: NAIL-BITER IN MAINE SENATE RACE

She also described herself as the "most independent senator on either side of the aisle," pointing to her voting record and saying her decisions are based on what she believes is right, rather than who occupies the White House or what President Donald Trump supports.

"There's this phony figure out there that I voted with President Trump 95% of the time. That's based on a sample of only 30% of the votes. If you look at a broader sample of 90% of the vote, that drops significantly to like 72%. And I voted against the president's policies 138 times," she said.

While Jackson previously told Fox News that he believes he has a "good shot" at defeating Collins, the longtime Republican senator touted strong voter support and said she is more optimistic than ever about her reelection chances.

"I'm feeling very optimistic based on the great response I get in visiting communities all over the state," she said.

Vikings quarterback Kyler Murray developed hamstring tightness sometime late in the fourth quarter. He said it was late enough that it didn't affect him in Sunday's 19-15 loss to the Lions.

But Murray will undergo further evaluation on Monday.

"We’ll look at it, obviously, and do what we need to do," Murray said, via Kevin Seifert of ESPN.

Murray went into the sideline medical tent after throwing a touchdown pass with 8:44 remaining. He was back on the field for the next drive.

It is unclear whether that was related to the hamstring issue or something else.

"Kyler Murray just [had] some hamstring tightness, and he was able to return late in the football game," coach Kevin O'Connell said.

Murray completed 17 of 29 passes for 210 yards with two touchdowns and an interception. He ran for 35 yards on four carries.
