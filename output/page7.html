Fantasy Football: Key offseason narratives take shape on Sunday in Week 8

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Every Sunday, I'm somehow surprised and not surprised at the same time. The action keeps me on the edge of my seat. A tipped pass here, a missed tackle (or many) there and my mood swings with every snap. In the moment, it all feels random.

But once the dust settles, I can usually trace the path. The signs were there. A target share creeping up in September. A run game that never looked right in camp. An offensive line we all worried about in August. We just didn't know when those threads would finally come together or fall apart. 

That's where we are after Week 8. Some of the biggest storylines from Sunday felt like shocks while they were happening. But if we look back at a few of the narratives we were debating in the offseason and September, they shouldn't have caught us off guard. So, let's check in on those early takes and see which ones are paying off.

The Cardiac Cats are Back

Here's a quick peek behind the curtain. During the first window of Sunday games, I'm taking notes and going back and forth with the crew on what will go into this piece and the theme. I couldn't concentrate after about 4 p.m. ET.

Joe Burrow takes it himself to strike first in OT!

Almost any other week, Joe Burrow leading a game-winning drive in OT would only lower my life expectancy by a year or two. Finally, there's a positive data point in the "is Burrow clutch" argument. Throwing for 462 yards and zipping five TD passes to his pass-catchers isn't bad either. But Burrow threw some of the gas on the fire with multiple turnovers, causing me to yell incoherently at my office wall.

However, the largest problem was on the other side of the ball.

  • Pressure Rate: 37.0% (Weeks 1-7), 19.0% (against TEN)

  • Rushing Yards per Game Surrendered: 80.5, 117.0

  • Passing Success Rate Allowed: 52.0%, 66.0%

To be fair, a couple of the offseason defensive additions showed up when the situation looked bleak. DT Dexter Lawrence sacked Cam Ward, taking the Titans out of FG range and he almost had a scoop-and-score at the start of the second half. DE Boye Mafe set up the turnover. But Tennessee hadn't scored more than 35 points in a game since Week 17 of '21. Ward's highest single-game passing yardage total was 265 until he came to Paycor Stadium. The idea that the Bengals could turn around their defense in a single year now seems, in Step Brothers terms, a little messed up. And Cincinnati's response on offense looked all too familiar.

Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins were eating from all different alignments at all levels of the field. pic.twitter.com/Rxk77n9r9i

— Goodberry (@JoeGoodberry) November 1, 2026

As I mentioned on Friday, unless DC Al Golden can bring his unit back to even close to league average, just give me more pass-catchers for Burrow. Seven of Chase Brown's 12 carries went for three or fewer yards. Samaje Perine averaged 2.5 YPC while playing 58.8% of the red-zone snaps. All while playing against a defense that came into Week 8 allowing 104.9 YPG to opposing RBs. 

Meanwhile, the Bengals used three-WR sets at a season-high 64.3% rate, and got their young pass-catchers into the mix. Rookie TE Jack Endries got his first TD, and WR Dohnte Meyers ran a route on 66.0% of Burrow's dropbacks, picking up a key first down to keep them alive.

Instead of mortgaging its future to fix its ground game, Cincinnati should invest in what makes them a problem for opposing defenses and make Burrow the MVP candidate he's looked like for years.

Klint Kubiak Will Fix the Raiders

The Raiders are in a constant "it's so over — we're so back" loop. They brought Klint Kubiak after he orchestrated Seattle's Super Bowl run. But Brock Bowers, their best offensive weapon, needed a meniscus trim before playing a regular-season snap. Kirk Cousins goes on a three-game winning streak, then a trio of losses put us on "Mendoza Watch." Sure, facing the Jets is a get-right spot for most teams. But I don't remember Cousins showing this same type of swagger last year.

Maybe swagger is the wrong descriptor. I'm not completely sure because I don't know what Cousins is doing after his second TD pass. If there's a name for his celly, somebody let me know. Anyway, Cousins operating Kubiak's scheme to near perfection isn't a revelation, after the two worked together in Minnesota. Cousins hit a season-high play-action rate (40.6%), and it was the third straight outing with the personnel grouping requiring at least two TEs on the field. He averaged 1.06 EPA per attempt to the MoF. And seeing the guy responsible for most of the production also isn't much of a shock.

Brock Bowers caught 6 passes for 87 yards and 2 touchdowns aligned as an outside receiver today.

Betting on Kubiak was a bet on him making his top pass-catcher an unguardable weapon. He's lived up to the expectation so far. Bowers just getting to 70 yards for the sixth consecutive game was an accolade on its own. But his utility to the offense goes past how far Bowers moves the ball.

Since coming back to the lineup in Week 3, Bowers ranks 11th amongst all pass-catchers in first downs per route run (12.6%). He sits right between Amon-Ra St. Brown and Ja'Marr Chase in that metric. The "TE-WR" is also just inside the top 12 in YPRR ahead of Chase and CeeDee Lamb. And this is all while running just eight fewer routes from the outside than Tee Higgins. A change in coaching is working out for the passing game as we envisioned.

Vegas' rushing attack? Not so much.

Even when he’s covered, he’s open.

That last sentence had most folks scratching their head well into the second quarter. Jeanty's output as a rusher has been a cause for concern since Week 1. After cracking the century mark against the Dolphins, Jeanty hasn't crossed 60 yards in a game since, and his yards on the ground have receded each week. He's continued to redeem himself as a receiver, but Mike Washington Jr. continues to steal snaps and carries. Washington's moved the chains more often and has a higher success rate on early downs than Jeanty. With his weekly workload rising, Washington will have FLEX appeal in this expanded version of the Raiders' backfield.

It's Not Just Wilson and Hall in New York Anymore

We weren't targeting any of the Jets' skill players until the third or fourth round for a couple of reasons. First, we assumed the team would lag behind the division, but bringing back Geno Smith would stabilize the situation. Second, and more importantly, the idea was that the targets and touches would consolidate on Garrett Wilson and Breece Hall. I get that Hall's quad injury has kept him off the field. But even if Hall were active, he might be in the same situation as Jeanty. 

Braelon Allen just put together the best rushing performance of his NFL career.

21 CAR, 147 RUSH YDS, 1 TD, 2 REC, 6 REC YDS

With Breece Hall sidelined, Allen has now surpassed 100 scrimmage yards in each of his first 7 career starts. pic.twitter.com/aqcqSZbVkY

Braelon Allen has averaged 22 touches per game with Hall out. He's churned out more than 100 scrimmage yards in four of five starts. That's while opposing defenses have put eight or more in the box on 46.2% of his carries (fifth-highest rate among active starters). It's also after defenders have met Allen at or behind the LOS on 48 of his totes (only Bucky Irving and Derrick Henry have more). And still, you'll find Allen's name in the rank where we'd expect to see Hall's.

  • Rushing Success Rate: 48.4%, 3rd (out of 19 qualifiers – min. 50% team rushing rate)

  • Adj. Yards After Contact per Att.: 4.6, 2nd 

  • Explosive Play Rate: 11.8%, 4th 

Smith's return has done more for their aerial attack than anticipated, too. Luckily, a sideline exchange and spending time with his QB after the game got Wilson's target rate back up over 20%. But during each contest, a few extra names have come into the picture.

  • Obvious Passing Situations: 14.8% (Mitchell), 11.5% (Taylor), 9.8% (Sadiq)

  • Downfield: 27.6% (Mitchell), 13.8% (Williams), 10.6% (Cooper)

  • Red Zone: 16.7% (Williams), 16.7% (Taylor), 16.7% (Sadiq)

Adonai Mitchell is the latest contestant on the "Who's the Jets' WR2" game show. After Omar Cooper Jr. took the title in Week 7, the former Colt turned nine targets into 142 yards. But Mitchell's 25-yard aDOT should have anyone reconsidering slotting him into their WR3 spot in Week 9. While Smith has been willing to let the ball fly downfield, a positive result isn't always in the cards. Plus, with the Jets facing the Chiefs, Wilson should lead the Jets in targets yet again.

It Was All About The Young Boys at RB for Broncos and Chiefs

The battle for the AFC West totaled 543 passing yards and five TDs. Patrick Mahomes was the first QB to complete three TD passes against Denver. Bo Nix was ripping passes to Jaylen Waddle downfield. The game came down to the final play.

But the two players that stood out were the rookies, not the vets.

Rookie Emmett Johnson had a breakout performance, scoring on the Chiefs' longest touchdown play since Tyreek Hill in 2021.

5 REC, 128 REC YDS, 1 REC TD, 1 CAR, 15 RUSH YDS

Well, I did say last Friday that Mahomes needed another receiver. And granted, the Broncos defense isn’t the best place to find out if you’re on the same page as your QB. But both Andrew Armstrong and Jalen Royals ran fewer routes on Sunday than when they played the Seahawks. Cyrus Allen got in some more cardio, but earned just one more target. In short, the environment was conducive to more RB targets, but instead of Kenneth Walker III doing most of the damage, Emmett Johnson became The Engine of the offense.

  • (Johnson) Air Yards per Target: 1.2

  • Yards per Route Run: 12.8

  • YAC per Reception: 24.4

I know a single game can skew rate stats. But there was more to Johnson’s 187-yard outburst than taking a screen pass to the house. The first-year rusher broke six tackles across his six touches on the day. His lone carry went for 15 yards. While we can’t expect half of his touches to turn into explosive gains every week, establishing himself as the ideal backup to Walker (with a rushing and receiving skill set) should put him on everyone’s radar as a bench stash.

But for Denver, if you got its RB1 off waivers, you’ve found an every-week starter.

If I’ve preached anything, it’s about applying context before reacting. Jonah Coleman reaching 100 yards and finding the box in his second game would have anyone declaring him a solid RB2 for the rest of the season. But let me at least take a look under the hood regarding his utilization before we pin “league winner” to his name.

  • Routes per Team Dropback: 52.9%, 11.8%

The Broncos ran the ball five times from inside Kansas City’s 5-yard line. Not only did Dobbins stay off the field for all of those plays, but Coleman handled four of the carries (Nix took the other). With RJ Harvey on IR, Coleman took all of the third-down plays, too. The situation is lining up for the rookie to take over as the starter as Denver looks to maintain their spot atop the division. And with the Panthers up before their bye, Coleman has a chance to force the decision sooner rather than later.

Trump administration’s social media posts ahead of elections criticized for being inflammatory

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

The Trump administration’s online accounts are circulating images of a pumpkin carved with a sombrero design and a fake Halloween-themed background around a Black man apparently accused of food assistance fraud, in social media posts slammed by critics as inflammatory rhetoric.

Only days before the midterm elections, the posts coincide with the government’s sweeping campaign to show its aggressive immigration enforcement, and highlight efforts to crack down on fraud to save taxpayers money when rising healthcare costs and other economic stressors have made affordability a top voter concern.

Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins posted Saturday on the social platform X a photo of the Black man standing in front of stacks of what appear to be U.S. currency with the headline, SNAP FRAUDSTER, an apparent reference to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, formerly known as food stamps.

He is holding aloft what appears to be a bill set aflame.

“Some costumes come with a price tag. This one comes with a prison sentence,” it says to the backdrop of fake Spirit Halloween costume packaging.

Another photo posted by the White House on X on Thursday showed a pumpkin with the shape of a sombrero carved across it and the words “Spooky szn is here.”

The post by Rollins, who was nominated by Trump to serve as the U.S. Secretary of Agriculture in November 2024, appears to be taken from an Instagram post from September 2025 by an account under the name Shawty Dred.

Earlier this month, Mercedes Reshard New, identified by the Justice Department as a social media personality also known as “Shawty Dred,” was arrested on federal charges for fraudulently obtaining more than $4 million in Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program benefits in Georgia.

It’s not the first time the White House has been accused of posting inflammatory images on official government social media accounts.

Just over a year ago during the government shutdown, the White House revived a deepfake video posted by Trump of House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries in a fake mustache and sombrero to accuse the Democrats of acting on behalf of illegal immigrants instead of U.S. citizens. Jeffries described the meme as bigoted. They played it on repeat in the White House briefing room, though Vice President JD Vance promised that the “sombrero memes will stop” when Democrats agreed to end the shutdown.

The NAACP did not respond to an email seeking comment, nor did the White House or Rollins.

The Trump administration in recent weeks has ratcheted up its anti-immigrant rhetoric and accusations of fraud, including against U.S. Rep. Ilhan Omar, whose Washington, D.C., home was searched by federal agents earlier this month. Federal authorities are investigating potential fraud in her family’s immigration paperwork. Omar, who was born in Somalia, is a naturalized U.S. citizen.

It also has taken a series of punitive steps linked to allegations of fraud in mostly Democratic-led states, including California and Minnesota whose governors accused the federal government of targeting their states for political reasons.

Federal prosecutors said in a news release Oct. 21 that the influencer, New, controlled more than 350 fraudulent SNAP accounts under stolen identities between January 2020 and July 2026. He allegedly applied for his company, Shawty Dred LLC, to become an authorized SNAP retailer and used EBT debit cards to extract about $4 million from the accounts.

New’s Instagram account, which has more than two million followers, has flaunted his extravagant lifestyle, federal prosecutors said. Posts depict him flying on private jets, posing with Rolls-Royce vehicles in front of his “super mansion,” and handling massive stacks of cash.

“Instead of this money feeding families in need, New, the social media personality known as ‘Shawty Dred,’ allegedly used the cash to bankroll a life of luxury,” Special Agent in Charge of FBI Atlanta Marlo Graham said in the release.

New was indicted by a federal grand jury Oct. 7 on dozens of counts of mail fraud and aggravated identity theft, and one count each of SNAP fraud and illegal transactions with access devices. He was arrested Oct. 13 and remains in federal custody.

According to Fulton County jail records, New has been arrested multiple times on suspicion of crimes including identity theft, insurance fraud, financial transaction card fraud, forgery and simple assault.

A federal defender listed in court records as representing New, Joseph Austin, did not return a phone message.

Gabby Williams leads Valkyries to WNBA championship in second season, beating Dream 68-63 in Game 7

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

SAN FRANCISCO (AP) — A fresh WNBA CHAMPIONS T-shirt over her uniform, Gabby Williams cradled the shiny MVP trophy in one arm and gave it a kiss. She held up a No. 1 with her left hand, then hugged and high-fived her supporters all the way to Golden State’s locker room, where she pulled on some goggles for the celebration.

Williams made her way back to the Bay Area where she cherishes so many youth basketball memories exactly for a chance to do something like this — to win it all and be part of building something special.

And she has done that all right.

“I just walked into a group full of love,” Williams said. “And that just changed the entire trajectory of my career.”

Williams scored her team’s final 11 points of the third quarter and dazzled through the fourth on the way to 30 points, and the Valkyries captured a championship in just their second season by beating the Atlanta Dream 68-63 in a sensational first WNBA Finals Game 7 on Saturday night.

“Gabby is our closer; she stayed patient, she stayed with it,” coach Natalie Nakase said. “Closing with 30, that is incredible!”

Williams joined Penny Taylor in 2007 for Phoenix as the only players in WNBA history to score 30 or more points in a winner-take-all Finals game.

Jordin Canada scored 18 points, Allisha Gray 16 and Angel Reese had eight points and 12 rebounds for the Dream, denied another championship in the franchise’s fourth try.

Gray made two free throws with 11 seconds left then Atlanta forced a jump ball — Gray against Williams — and Veronica Burton corralled it and converted two free throws with 5.1 seconds to go.

“I’m just so blessed, this is insane!” said Burton, cut by Dallas two years ago.

Rhyne Howard’s layup tied it at 57 with 2:41 left only for Williams to answer once more with a clutch 3-pointer. She converted two free throws with 1:40 remaining, then Kiah Stokes scored a late basket. Stokes — now a four-time WNBA champion — pulled down 13 rebounds and blocked two more shots to cap her spectacular postseason.

Williams left Seattle with a great feeling about joining Golden State and its close-knit culture, returning to the Bay Area determined to prove herself as a WNBA star after all her international success — and now she’s a postseason icon. Fans chanted “Gabby! Gabby!” throughout the fourth quarter.

“All it took was good people,” she said. “All we heard last year was we don’t have no stars. We don’t have no talent. Yeah right! We got good people. We got good people. We got winners. We got people who are ready to put their bodies on the floor to win a game.”

When the final buzzer sounded, the violet-clad sellout crowd at Chase Center went into a frenzy as players sprinted to the middle of the floor to hug and celebrate as confetti fell from the rafters.

“Ballhalla” had become “Boohalla” for this far-but-haunted Halloween night in the raucous arena. Now, with a women’s championship to its name right alongside the one earned by the Golden State Warriors in 2022.

“We gotta build a bigger trophy case,” co-owner Peter Guber said.

The team that led at halftime in the first six matchups had won every game so far, and the Dream were ahead 30-24 at the break. Atlanta also had been 16-0 this season when Reese made at least one 3. The Dream’s DeWanna Bonner became the first player in WNBA history to reach 600 career postseason defensive rebounds.

Nakase insisted to Williams at halftime the game would open up for her.

“Nat told me to stay patient, I trust her with my life,” Williams said. “I stayed patient.”

Nakase changed her starting lineup for Game 4 in a move that paid huge dividends after Golden State fell behind 2-1, inserting Kaila Charles and Tiffany Hayes and bringing Kayla Thornton and Cecilia Zandalasini off the bench.

The 37-year-old Hayes, who came out of retirement two years ago and spent 10 seasons with the Dream, scored 11 points in the winner-take-all Game 7 and brought energy all series that made Nakase want to win for the veteran guard as much as anybody.

Valkyries owner Joe Lacob guaranteed a championship within the first five years for this franchise three years ago when the Bay Area was granted an expansion team, and in improbable, joyful fashion, his team accomplished it in Year 2. That after becoming the first expansion organization to reach the playoffs in its inaugural year in 2025.

During the victory ceremony, Lacob shouted out the team and its leaders, while Nakase, general manager Ohemaa Nyanin and President Jess Smith hugged. And Lacob thanked the fans who have sold out Chase Center for every game since Day 1.

“Ballhalla!” Lacob hollered.

“These ladies deserve all the credit in the world for the incredible resilience, every last one of them, and our coach,” he said later. “I can’t even say how proud I am. I don’t care men, women, whatever, it doesn’t matter, that is an incredible year, an incredible performance, not only tonight but the whole playoffs.”
