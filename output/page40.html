📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Noni Madueke avoided a red card against Liverpool despite appearing to headbutt Alexis Mac Allister.

The Arsenal winger was involved in a flashpoint during the second half at Anfield on Sunday.

Madueke avoided a red card despite appearing to headbutt Mac AllisterGetty

Madeuke and Mac Allister clashed after the Liverpool midfielder had fouled Martin Odegaard.

Jurrien Timber also confronted the Argentina international following the foul.

However, it was Madueke who leaned in towards Mac Allister before he fell to the floor.

Madueke avoids red card

Both Mac Allister and Timber were subsequently booked by referee Andy Madley.

Madueke avoided punishment, though, despite moving his head towards his opponent.

The incident was reviewed by VAR, who deemed the contact not to be forceful enough to warrant a red card.

As a result, VAR official Stuart Attwell stuck with the referee’s on-field decision not to dismiss Madueke.

The Premier League Match Centre provided an explanation for the decision on social media.

A post on X read: “The referee’s call of no violent conduct by Madueke on Mac Allister was checked and confirmed by VAR.

“VAR: ‘All of the situations have been checked and cleared. There is some contact as Madueke and Mac Allister square-up. In my opinion, it’s not forceful, not violent, not brutal.'”

Discussing the incident with Majestic, the talkSPORT co-host said: “No, I’m not having it, I’m not having it.

“Your head has been touched a little bit. If I touch your head now with that much force, you ain’t going down like that.

“By the way, he’s the one that’s going to Odegaard and having a moan about him going down.

“Which was a foul, definitely, and then he’s dropping on the floor. What are we doing now?”

Madueke had already been on a yellow card after being cautioned by Madley in the 55th minute.

Arsenal end Anfield wait

DOJ set to appeal ruling in Alaska Senate race dispute over ‘Decoy Dan’

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

A federal judge on Sunday dismissed the Justice Department’s lawsuit seeking to delay certification of Alaska’s Senate election amid the "Decoy Dan" controversy, rejecting the Trump administration’s bid two days before the midterm elections.

The DOJ said it would appeal.

The lawsuit centered on two candidates with similar names: Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan and challenger Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. The DOJ lawsuit sought to delay or block certification while investigating whether voter confusion may have affected the outcome.

DEM BUCKS PARTY OVER ALLEGED ALASKA VOTE-SIPHONING EFFORT: 'NEITHER PARTY SHOULD BE DOING IT'

U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason said the government had not shown how the requested investigation could identify voters’ intent while protecting ballot secrecy. She also said the relief sought would intrude on Alaska’s responsibility to run its elections.

The DOJ said it would appeal, arguing the judge dismissed the case on procedural grounds without addressing its claim that Sullivan Jr. entered the race to confuse voters. The case now heads to the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals.

The dispute has roiled the campaign in recent days. The New York Times reported that people connected to Democratic nominee Mary Peltola’s campaign helped recruit Sullivan Jr. to run as a spoiler. Peltola has denied involvement, and Sullivan Jr. has denied coordinating with her campaign.

Sen. Sullivan said Sunday he would accept the results of Tuesday’s election, even as the Trump administration pursues a criminal investigation into Sullivan Jr.’s candidacy. Sullivan accused the challenger of trying to confuse voters.

Alaska Gov. Mike Dunleavy, a Republican, has suggested he may not certify a Peltola victory. Sullivan declined to say whether he would support efforts to refuse certification.

The Senate race could help determine which party controls the chamber. Alaska uses ranked-choice voting, under which lower-performing candidates are eliminated in rounds if no one wins a majority in the first count.
